## Axis A — Mechanism: can AGN/SMBH feedback remove, heat, or deplete star-forming gas?

**Status (enum):** ejective side: widely_supported - maintenance side: contradicted_or_model_dependent (per-side; pending verification)

In selected massive or AGN-host galaxies, AGN activity can drive gas outflows capable of removing or depleting star-forming fuel — a capability claim scoped to the systems where it is observed, not a universal one. ⟨s7_A01⟩ In one massive z~2 sample, measured mass-outflow rates span 3-100 solar masses per year. ⟨s7_A02⟩ One z=3 galaxy, GS-10578, shows that ejective black-hole feedback can occur, with a neutral outflow rate about ten times the star-formation rate; it is a single case, and it cannot anchor any prevalence fraction. ⟨s7_A03⟩ In simulations, a second channel — maintenance heating via chaotic cold accretion and dual jet/heating modes — is distinct from ejective outflows; in this corpus that channel is supported in models only, and its certainty is capped at model-dependent. ⟨s7_A04⟩ The cap has a recorded reason: observational maintenance evidence (X-ray cavities and bubbles) is absent from the approved corpus, and filling that gap — queued, not executed in this run — is the single move that could lift this side off model-only status. ⟨s7_A05⟩ The corpus also holds a direct countercase to the ejective side: in typical low-redshift galaxies, cold-gas outflows may be driven by star formation rather than AGN activity — so the ejective claim survives only because it is scoped to selected systems, not typical ones. ⟨s7_A06⟩ Status: the ejective side is labeled widely_supported and the maintenance side contradicted_or_model_dependent — no single axis-level label exists because the sides sit at different enum levels, and both labels bind to pending-verification entries. ⟨s7_A07⟩

## Axis B — Prevalence: how often do AGN-associated outflow signatures occur?

**Status (enum):** emerging_sample_limited (pending verification)

Across selected AGN or massive-galaxy samples, observed AGN-associated outflow signatures occur in substantial but sample-dependent fractions — not universally. ⟨s7_B01⟩ In the MOSDEF z=1.4-3.8 AGN sample, ionized outflows are detected in 17% of 159 AGNs. ⟨s7_B02⟩ In one JWST sample of 113 massive (log M*/Msun > 10) z~2 galaxies, 46% show excess Na I D absorption, and half of the absorption profiles are blueshifted by at least 100 km/s. ⟨s7_B03⟩ These two fractions carry a do-not-average guard: they measure different tracers (ionized versus neutral gas) on different selections (AGN-selected versus mass-selected) in different redshift windows, so the 17%-46% spread is at least partly definitional, not a measurement scatter on one quantity. ⟨s7_B04⟩ The GS-10578 case shows that ejective feedback can occur at z=3, but its risk flag (CASE_ROW_NOT_PREVALENCE_ANCHOR) is binding: it is excluded from every fraction quoted here. ⟨s7_B05⟩ Status: emerging_sample_limited across all five entries (pending verification); a matched-tracer, matched-selection census across redshift is what would turn the tracer-confounded 17%-46% spread into a real dispersion on defined quantities. ⟨s7_B06⟩

## Axis C — Driver/dominance: which quenching channel dominates, and where?

**Status (enum):** actively_debated (pending verification)

Whether AGN feedback dominates quenching is actively debated: AGN feedback is one important axis, but its dominance relative to stellar feedback, gas retention, strangulation, stripping, halo/environment, and satellite channels remains debated and context-dependent. ⟨s7_C01⟩ Strangulation, environmental stripping, and cold-gas pathway differences remain mandatory alternative or qualifying quenching channels. ⟨s7_C02⟩ Behind that position: strangulation appears primary for local galaxies below 10^11 solar masses on a roughly 4 Gyr timescale, stripping is reported as ubiquitous among satellites, and central galaxies defy simple pathway assignments. ⟨s7_C03⟩ Central properties — bulge mass, central velocity dispersion, or black-hole mass — are a real quenching-predictor axis; the black-hole-mass machine-learning result joins this position only as a qualifier, because it is simulation-comparison-based. ⟨s7_C04⟩ Halo, environment, and satellite-quenching channels are also real quenching axes and must remain separate from central/BH predictors. ⟨s7_C05⟩ On that side of the ledger: mass-driven and environment-driven quenching stay separable out to z~1, star formation has ceased in 30%-70% of satellites, and satellite quenching is reported as dominant for satellites below 10^10 solar masses, with a delayed-then-rapid history — a 2-4 Gyr delay, then an e-folding time under 0.8 Gyr. ⟨s7_C06⟩ The central and halo/environment positions bound each other by construction: their ledger links are same-axis, and each asserts a complementary 'real axis' claim, not dominance over the other. ⟨s7_C07⟩ The direct countercase sits in the corpus: in a 456-galaxy sample of typical z<0.2 galaxies, cold-gas outflows may be driven by star formation rather than AGN — a single-sample result, and the ledger's only direct contradicts-stance observational countercase. ⟨s7_C08⟩ The alternatives position is not unopposed either: it is held in tension by the ejective-mechanism evidence of Axis A, while simulation entries enter this debate only as qualifiers, never as observed-frequency evidence. ⟨s7_C09⟩ No cross-channel dominance fraction is licensed: the per-channel numbers above do not combine, and dominance wording stays blocked until a position can name the mass, redshift, and central-versus-satellite scope in which its channel is shown dominant with the other axes' entries represented. ⟨s7_C10⟩ Status: actively_debated (pending verification) — and per Step 6, actively debated is a valid reader-facing result, not a failure. ⟨s7_C11⟩

## Axis D — Gas fate: does quenching require gas removal?

**Status (enum):** actively_debated (pending verification)

Does quenching require gas removal? Here the evidence is mixed and the question is actively debated, with the two sides separated by physical scale. ⟨s7_D01⟩ Gas retention and low star-formation efficiency can qualify simple gas-removal accounts: SFR-matched AGN hosts show no molecular-gas difference, some z~0.7 quenched galaxies retain large molecular reservoirs, and some massive quiescent disks retain substantial atomic hydrogen. ⟨s7_D02⟩ That retention evidence enters the ledger as a qualifier by construction — 'not a refutation of ejective cases'. ⟨s7_D03⟩ On the other side, some local AGN-host central regions show molecular gas fractions about a factor of 2 lower than star-forming regions — a central-kpc scoped result, resting most strongly on four best-statistics galaxies, and not a global quenching claim. ⟨s7_D04⟩ The two sides are scale-separated — global reservoirs versus the central kiloparsec — and the current corpus contains no joint measurement connecting them; that joint measurement is what would settle the axis. ⟨s7_D05⟩ Status: actively_debated at axis level, carried by the highest-information entry, with the central-depletion qualifier at emerging_sample_limited inside it (both pending verification). ⟨s7_D06⟩
