This study systematically explores the dependence of reionization photon budget on key parameters like ionizing efficiency, SFRD normalization, clumping factor, and escape fraction proxies. By evaluating various systematic corners and propagating uncertainties through Monte Carlo simulations, it demonstrates that differing conclusions in the literature (i.e., "photon-budget crisis" vs. sufficiency of star-forming galaxies) stem from varying assumptions about these inputs. The study acknowledges limitations, such as reliance on indirect proxies for escape fractions and use of a maintenance criterion rather than full reionization history integration. While additional direct measurements are needed to resolve uncertainties, the work provides a nuanced understanding of how assumptions affect conclusions. The results are well-caveated and transparently presented, making this an acceptable contribution to the field.
