## Method 1 — packet-gated paper-to-wiki reconciliation

**What it is:** the broadest, most heavily annotated page. 30 inline claim chips spanning all 9 sections (IDs {2905–2923, 2925, 2926, 2929–2936, 2946}), 0 numeric citations, plus four provenance panels (watch-layer trust table, 27 baseline-preserved chips, a rejected/NO-GO boundary table, and a citation-backing note).

- **Reader-facing clarity:** High as a *sourced encyclopedia*. Every section carries specific, ID-bound factual statements (halo masses at cosmic noon, depletion timescales, the FMR 0.1-dex stability, S0 formation, reionization sources). The connective prose is genuinely good and repeatedly re-frames the chips as "boundary conditions, not universal recipes" and "coupled diagnostics rather than independent switches." Chip density is the trade-off: superscript IDs and badges interrupt reading more than M3.
- **Caution / overclaim discipline:** Good overall, and uniquely *auditable* — it is the only page that shows its rejection reasoning inline (2924 recast→2946; 2298/2299/2948 NO-GO; 2546 `"0.5"` bucket held). Contested claims carry visible badges (2931 debated, 2929 unverified, 2946 reported). The gap: the **27 baseline-preserved chips render with no visible trust badge**, so they read as uniformly authoritative even though their underlying trust varies; the page defers that to "the claim layer," which a reader of the static page cannot see.
- **Structure / readability:** Strong. The hero + article + provenance panels layout is well-organized and makes the method's discipline legible. Densest of the three but navigable.
- **Differentiation:** Its distinctive contribution is **breadth × provenance + an explicit audit trail**: it is the only page that both covers the whole topic *and* binds ~30 claims to IDs while showing what it excluded and why.
- **Red-flags (evaluation notes, not fix requests):** (a) 27 chips lack a visible per-chip trust signal — flattened authority; (b) a few chips read declaratively for still-active science — 2914 ("…results in the formation of S0 galaxies"), 2921 (central density "linked to mass quenching"), 2926 (faint-galaxy contribution "secondary to proto-globular clusters"); surrounding prose caveats them, but the chip text itself sounds settled.

## Method 2 — source-first paper adjudication

**What it is:** the most rigorously sourced and most cautious page, but deliberately narrow. 6 chips {2942–2947}, all in the AGN / gas / stellar-feedback cluster, each followed by numeric evidence badges (22 distinct 28xxx IDs) with per-badge paper + adjudicated-role tooltips; a 22-row cited-positions table; and a 14-row exclusions ledger (2 adjudication-held + 12 rejected, never cited).

- **Reader-facing clarity:** Clear and honest, but *narrower and more self-referential*. Only 4 of 9 sections carry claims; the other 5 are prose that often describes the **method** rather than the science ("the Method2 source set does not authorize broad new morphology claims"; "a source position marked background-only by Lana's S2 erratum is not cited here"). A reader wanting to learn galaxy evolution gets the least topical coverage; a reader wanting to know exactly *why each sentence is allowed* gets the most.
- **Caution / overclaim discipline:** **Strongest of the three.** Verb discipline is exemplary — "can," "in some systems," "scoped," "model-dependent," "review-synthesis," "limited support," plus explicit anti-overclaim lines ("not…a guarantee that every active nucleus quenches its host"; "without making the pathway universal"). It visibly separates the 2 full-`accepted` rows (28141, 28095) from `accepted_limited`, preserves the 28060 positive-feedback caution *outside* any chip, and lists every excluded row with a reason. This is the cleanest overclaim posture.
- **Structure / readability:** Good, but the exclusion tables and process vocabulary make it read more like an adjudication report than a wiki article in places.
- **Differentiation:** Its distinctive contribution is **per-claim auditability to a specific paper + adjudicated role**, with an explicit accepted-vs-limited distinction and a full exclusion record — the highest evidentiary transparency of the three.
- **Red-flags:** (a) coverage is an *under-claim* limitation, not an overclaim one — 5 sections are effectively "we are not authorized to say more here"; (b) methodology leaks into reader-facing prose ("ratified ledger," "S2 erratum"); (c) "direct quasar-outflow" (28141) and "ultra-fast-outflow detection" (28148) are the strongest verbs on the page — both inside the ratified accepted/limited envelope (consistent with the prior overclaim review), so not a defect, just the two spots carrying the most weight.

## Method 3 — debate-map-to-wiki rebuild (P2)

**What it is:** the best-written page and the only one with **no** claim or citation markers at all (P2 by design; binding deferred to P3). Light, theme-aware typography; a meta box; clean article; footer. No provenance panels.

- **Reader-facing clarity:** **Highest.** Uninterrupted, essay-quality prose. It is the best at representing *debates as debates* — "One line of analysis argues…; another treats…," named competing positions on the halo-vs-central question and on the reionization photon budget, explicit redshift scoping (z∼0–2.3, z>6 vs z∼3 He II), and careful mechanism disambiguation (internal starvation vs environmental stripping vs strangulation). Pedagogically the strongest.
- **Caution / overclaim discipline:** Excellent, achieved through *language* rather than badges — "genuinely debated," "should carry their redshift scope," "neither upgraded to observed fact nor dismissed as contradicted," "prefaced with 'in simulations' or 'in this model'." Discipline is comparable to M2 in spirit.
- **Structure / readability:** Cleanest reading experience of the three.
- **Differentiation:** Its distinctive contribution is **narrative quality + explicit multi-position debate framing** — the "debate map" heritage is visible and is a genuinely different product from M1/M2 (which share much verbatim connective prose over a differing claim layer).
- **Red-flags:** (a) **Zero provenance binding** — no statement is individually traceable to evidence; by P2 design and intentional, but it means the page is not auditable as a sourced wiki page in its current state; (b) **internal inconsistency**: the opening blockquote states "Highlighted claim chips mark statements with provenance… used sparingly here," yet the page has *no chips* — stale shared-skeleton boilerplate that the meta box then contradicts (meta box correctly says no markers). This is the clearest reader-facing inconsistency across all three pages.

## Overall ranking (with reasons)

Weighted as *candidate provenance-backed wiki pages* (i.e., balancing coverage + auditability + caution + readability, since that is what these artifacts are):

1. **Method 1 — best-balanced.** The only page that covers the entire topic *and* binds its claims to IDs *and* shows an auditable rejection ledger. It reads as a finished sourced article. Costs: flattened trust on 27 baseline chips; a few declarative chips.
2. **Method 2 — most rigorous, narrowest.** Best per-claim discipline and evidence traceability of any page; choose it if rigor and auditability outweigh breadth. Costs: only the AGN cluster is sourced; prose is partly about its own method.
3. **Method 3 — best-written, least-sourced.** The strongest prose and debate framing and the cleanest layout, but no provenance binding (P2) and a self-contradictory chip blockquote. Ranks third *for this purpose only*; on pure readability it is first.

**Short comparative summary for choosing:**
- Want a complete, source-annotated wiki article with an audit trail → **M1**.
- Want maximum per-claim rigor and the strongest guard against overclaiming, and can accept narrow scope → **M2**.
- Want the most readable, debate-forward narrative and treat provenance as a later step → **M3**.
The methods produced genuinely distinct pages (M1 and M2 differ mainly in provenance philosophy over a largely shared prose base; M3 is a distinct rewrite), so the choice is a real editorial trade-off between **breadth**, **auditable rigor**, and **narrative readability** — not a case of one page dominating on every axis.
