\author{NebulaMind Research Autopilot}

\begin{abstract}
We revise the simulation-validation pilot by turning the target vector into a selection-flagged observational product rather than a model-comparison claim.  The current artifact contains 15 mass--redshift cells from the capped 60,000-row SDSS four-line emission sample.  Across cells, the observed quenched fraction spans approximately 0.001--0.856 and the BPT optical AGN fraction spans approximately 0.001--0.610, but two cells have cached $N<500$ and should not drive precise headline claims.  Public SDSS checks show that the small cached cells correspond to larger four-line S/N$\geq3$ eligible counts: 300 cached of 1,252 eligible for $8.0<\log M_\star<9.5$, $0.08<z<0.12$, and 390 cached of 1,553 eligible for $11.0<\log M_\star<12.5$, $0.02<z<0.05$.  No simulation is accepted, rejected, or ranked until mocks are passed through matching SDSS/MaNGA/ALMA/X-ray/radio selection, aperture, and noise models.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{galaxies: evolution --- galaxies: active --- surveys --- methods: data analysis --- methods: statistical}

\section{Scope and status}\label{sec:scope}
This Lana revision addresses the main overclaim risk in the M3 P3 pilot.  The batch paper framed the output as a validation vector, but the safe object is an observed SDSS target vector with selection flags.  It is useful for future forward modelling; it is not itself a simulation comparison.

The full proposal requires simulated catalogs passed through the same SDSS spectroscopic, line-S/N, mass, redshift, aperture, and noise filters, plus analogous MaNGA/ALMA/X-ray/radio selection functions if those observables are compared.  Without those mocks, differences between a simulation and Table~\ref{tab:m3p3-vector} would mix physics, selection effects, aperture effects, and measurement definitions.

\section{Data and definitions}\label{sec:data}
The source sample is the cached public SDSS DR17 four-line emission sample: 60,000 galaxies at $0.02<z<0.12$ with finite stellar mass, finite catalog specific SFR, and S/N$\geq3$ in H$\alpha$, H$\beta$, [O~III]$\lambda5007$, and [N~II]$\lambda6584$.  The corresponding public SDSS four-line S/N$\geq3$ eligible count is 249,917, so the cache covers 24.0\% of the strict eligible set and is capped by SpecObjID order.

The table uses the Goru definitions recovered from the analysis code: $f_Q$ is the fraction with catalog $\log {\rm sSFR}<-11.0$; $f_{\rm BPT\,AGN}$ is the fraction classified as optical AGN by the BPT criterion; and $f_{\rm high\,exc.}$ requires BPT AGN plus $\log([\mathrm{O\,III}]/\mathrm{H}\beta)>0.25$.  The vector is intentionally compact, so cell-level flags are carried in a separate column rather than hidden in prose.

\section{Discussion outline and scope guards}\label{sec:discussion}
The main contribution is a reproducible target-vector shape: low-mass/high-sSFR cells have low $f_Q$ and low optical AGN incidence, while high-mass cells in this emission-line sample have larger low-sSFR and BPT-AGN fractions.  That pattern is observationally useful, but it is not an explanation.  It cannot identify whether feedback prescriptions, halo quenching, morphology, dust, aperture effects, or line-selection cuts drive the trends.

Safe wording for integration: ``The pilot writes a 15-cell observed SDSS vector that future mocks must reproduce under the same selection.''  Unsafe wording to avoid: ``the SDSS vector validates a feedback model,'' ``the high-mass cells prove AGN feedback,'' or ``a mismatch would falsify a simulation'' without forward-modelled mock catalogs.

Topic-specific simulation-validation bibliography remains a gap.  It should be inserted only after the Literature Wave-2 source packet classifies forward-modelling and feedback-prescription sources; this revision does not invent citation support.
