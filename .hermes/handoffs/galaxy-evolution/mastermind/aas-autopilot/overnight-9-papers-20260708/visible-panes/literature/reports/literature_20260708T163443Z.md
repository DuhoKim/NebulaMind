### 1. Rapid early quenching / ultra-massive quiescent galaxies
- **Title:** *The JWST EXCELS survey: Too much, too young, too fast? Ultra-massive quiescent galaxies at 3 < z < 5*
- **Authors:** A. C. Carnall et al. (24 authors)
- **Year / venue:** 2024, MNRAS (accepted); arXiv:2405.02242
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.02242
- **Grounds:** four ultra-massive quiescent galaxies at z = 3.19–4.62; high stellar metallicities + α-enhancement imply intense, rapid star formation at z ≈ 8–10; no fundamental ΛCDM conflict only under extreme (near-100%) baryon-conversion efficiency.
- **Supports → INTERPRETATION** (chemical-abundance evidence for a rapid-formation-then-quench history), with a secondary **method** contribution (SED + stellar-metallicity/α-enhancement fitting to date quenching).

### 2. AGN feedback as the quenching driver
- **Title:** *The fundamental signature of star formation quenching from AGN feedback: A critical dependence of quiescence on supermassive black hole mass not accretion rate*
- **Authors:** Asa F. L. Bluck, Joanna M. Piotrowska, Roberto Maiolino
- **Year / venue:** 2023, ApJ (accepted); arXiv:2301.03677
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.03677
- **Grounds:** across EAGLE, Illustris, IllustrisTNG and SDSS+CANDELS data, **black-hole mass** (integrated feedback), not current accretion rate/AGN luminosity, is the most predictive parameter of central-galaxy quenching at all epochs.
- **Supports → INTERPRETATION and METHOD** (interpretation: identifies the causal feedback variable; method: prescribes M_BH rather than L_AGN as the observational quenching predictor — directly usable to frame a paper's AGN-feedback claims).

### 3. Environment vs. morphology sequence
- **Title:** *Star formation quenching precedes morphological transformation in COSMOS-Web's richest galaxy groups*
- **Authors:** Z. Ghaffari et al. (84 authors)
- **Year / venue:** 2026, A&A vol. 708, A129; arXiv:2601.06297
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06297
- **Grounds:** in COSMOS-Web groups, quenching precedes shape change (contrasts the classical morphology–density relation); high-mass galaxies (M* > 10^10.5 M☉) quench in ~1 Gyr and become spheroidal, lower-mass systems quench more slowly; AGN feedback partly drives the rapid high-mass quenching.
- **Supports → INTERPRETATION** (temporal ordering of quenching vs. morphology; also corroborates source #2's AGN-driven high-mass quenching).
