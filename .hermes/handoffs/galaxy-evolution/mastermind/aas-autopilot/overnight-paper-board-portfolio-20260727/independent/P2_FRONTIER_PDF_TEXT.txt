                                                        ABSTRACT
          We ask whether star-forming galaxies alone can supply the ionizing photons required to reionize
        the Universe, and show that the answer is governed almost entirely by two adopted quantities—the
        ionizing-photon production eﬀiciency ξion and the cosmic star-formation-rate density (SFRD)—rather
        than by the escape fraction itself. Using the standard reionization-maintenance criterion, we solve for
        the Lyman-continuum escape fraction fesc required of star-forming galaxies to balance recombinations
        across 5 ≤ z ≤ 12, propagating a grid of systematic assumptions (ξion , IGM clumping, LzLCS proxy
        calibration, and SFRD normalization) by Monte Carlo. The required fesc rises steeply with redshift
        and crosses the indirect-proxy-inferred value (≈ 6%) at a “crisis-onset” redshift that ranges from z ≈ 5
        to z ≈ 8.75 across defensible assumptions; it exceeds the physical ceiling fesc = 1 at z ≈ 8.5 in the most
        pessimistic corner, but never within 5 ≤ z ≤ 12 in the most optimistic. Under fiducial assumptions
        the budget closes to z ≈ 6.5 and becomes unphysical by z ≈ 10.75. We conclude that the apparent
        “photon-budget crisis” is not a robust observational result but a restatement of the adopted ξion and
        SFRD priors: the same data admit both closure and a severe shortfall. This is a descriptive, automated
        study; it has not been validated by a human referee, uses no new survey data, and is not peer-reviewed.
