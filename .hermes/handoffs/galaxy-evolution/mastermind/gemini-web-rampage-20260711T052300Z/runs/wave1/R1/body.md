Card 1 — Isolating the causal contribution of AGN feedback to central-galaxy quenching
1.1 Prior-study findings (with source links)

The evolutionary trajectory of central galaxies, particularly their transition from the actively star-forming blue cloud to the quiescent red sequence, represents a foundational inquiry in modern extragalactic astrophysics. Within this framework, isolating the precise physical drivers responsible for halting star formation remains a complex challenge. Extensive theoretical and observational efforts have increasingly focused on the regulatory role of active galactic nucleus (AGN) feedback.

Piotrowska et al. (2022)  deployed advanced machine learning techniques, specifically Random Forest classification architectures, to disentangle the highly covariant parameter space governing galaxy evolution. By analyzing vast datasets derived from the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS)  alongside mock observations generated by the IllustrisTNG  cosmological hydrodynamical simulations, their analysis indicates that supermassive black hole mass is the overwhelmingly dominant predictive parameter for central galaxy quiescence. This statistical relationship supports a physical model wherein a combination of preventative feedback and the continuous injection of turbulence regulates star formation within the host galaxy's extended halo.   

Building upon these algorithmic findings, Bluck et al. (2023)  utilized similar statistical datasets to identify a critical dependence of star formation quiescence on supermassive black hole mass, notably asserting that the contemporary, instantaneous black hole accretion rate offers minimal predictive power. This theoretical result carries substantial implications for observational strategies, cautioning that the current active state of an AGN, typically traced by its bolometric luminosity, is an inadequate proxy for the cumulative, integrated impact of AGN feedback over cosmic time.   

Furthermore, Goubert et al. (2024)  integrated the EAGLE  simulation framework with SDSS  observations to assess the role of large-scale environment in the quenching process. Their work suggests that AGN feedback extends its thermodynamic and kinetic influence far beyond the immediate host galaxy. This non-local feedback impacts neighbouring satellite systems in a non-uniform manner that is highly dependent on radial distance, halo mass, and ambient gas content.

1.2 What remains unknown

Despite the robust statistical associations highlighted by contemporary machine learning frameworks, the exact physical mechanisms governing the transfer of energy from the supermassive black hole's event horizon to the extended circumgalactic medium remain shrouded in profound theoretical uncertainty. While black hole mass acts as a powerful integrator of past AGN activity and a robust predictor of central galaxy quenching, the temporal sequence and physical modality of the energy injection are largely unconstrained by pure correlation analyses.

The Random Forest methodologies successfully identify the hierarchy of predictive features, yet they do not inherently elucidate whether the suppression of star formation is driven predominantly by sudden, explosive kinetic outflows (quasar mode) during early evolutionary phases or by a prolonged, steady thermal maintenance mode operating at lower Eddington ratios. Additionally, while the integrated history of active galactic nucleus activity correlates with black hole mass, the variance in duty cycles across different host morphologies and varying dark matter halo topographies is not fully resolved. It remains unknown exactly how the kinetic energy transported by relativistic radio jets physically couples with the multiphase gas in the interstellar medium to drive the specific scales of turbulence required to halt molecular cloud fragmentation and subsequent star formation.

Furthermore, the non-local impacts reported in recent cosmological hydrodynamical simulations raise substantial questions regarding the boundary conditions of feedback. The precise physical radius at which an active galactic nucleus ceases to influence satellite galaxies, versus the dominant role of environmental ram-pressure stripping as galaxies infall into massive clusters, is still subject to significant modeling degeneracies and lacks definitive observational calibration.

1.3 Recommended data/survey families

To disentangle the degeneracies between cumulative feedback history and instantaneous accretion state, future investigations require a multifaceted, wide-field observational approach. Large-scale, spatially resolved integral field spectroscopy surveys, such as the Mapping Nearby Galaxies at Apache Point Observatory (MaNGA)  and the upcoming Hector  survey, are highly recommended. These facilities possess the capability to map the internal stellar population ages, spatially resolved metallicities, and kinematic structures of both central and satellite systems across vast cosmological volumes, providing the necessary spatial granularity to trace feedback signatures.   

These optical integral field spectroscopy surveys must be rigorously cross-matched with deep, wide-field X-ray surveys, such as those conducted by the extended Roentgen Survey with an Imaging Telescope Array (eROSITA), to trace the hot halo gas and measure the macroscopic thermodynamic properties of the circumgalactic medium. Bridging the gap from local environments to high-redshift centrals necessitates the selective deployment of the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST), utilizing its Near Infrared Spectrograph (NIRSpec) capabilities to trace the earliest phases of massive central quenching during cosmic noon, where the initial scaling relations between black holes and host galaxies were forged.

1.4 Test/decision-criteria realism (JWST/ALMA or stated alternative)

JWST and ALMA marginal here — the relevant capability is the massively multiplexed optical integral field spectroscopy and wide-field X-ray instrument families.

The sample sizes required to build statistically robust machine learning training sets and validate complex cosmological hydrodynamical simulations often encompass tens to hundreds of thousands of individual objects, matching the volumetric coverage of the Sloan Digital Sky Survey. These requirements are fundamentally incompatible with the targeted, narrow-field, high-resolution nature of ALMA and JWST observing programs. Realistically, executing decision criteria based on population-level parameter distributions and large-scale environmental correlations requires facilities optimized for sheer survey-speed and wide-area mapping. Consequently, the eROSITA X-ray observatory, alongside next-generation optical multi-object spectrographs like the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) or the 4-metre Multi-Object Spectroscopic Telescope (4MOST), represent the realistic, indispensable platforms necessary to map halo masses, environmental densities, and black hole scaling relations across statistically significant cosmological volumes.

1.5 Overclaim risks

A paramount overclaim risk in this domain involves the epistemological leap from statistical predictability to explicit physical causation. Relying on Random Forest feature importance metrics to assert a direct, singular mechanism for quenching, without physically resolving the kinetic or thermal energy transfer physics in the multiphase interstellar medium, risks vastly overstating the algorithmic output.

Additionally, asserting that satellite quenching is primarily governed by non-local central active galactic nucleus feedback based solely on radial distance correlations risks conflating the genuine feedback influence with the underlying dark matter halo topology and standard environmental pre-processing. When reporting the severity of star formation suppression, comparing the absolute median star formation rates of heavily quenched, high-mass systems against matched-control differences in specific star formation rates measured in local dwarf populations must be explicitly labeled non-commensurable. Furthermore, extrapolating scaling relations derived from massive, low-redshift centrals to low-mass or high-redshift systems without rigorously accounting for the cosmological evolution of the fundamental mass-metallicity and mass-size planes presents a severe vulnerability to analytical overclaim.

Card 2 — A tracer-resolved, common-denominator census of AGN-driven outflows
2.1 Prior-study findings (with source links)

Galactic winds and large-scale outflows are widely considered to be the primary physical vectors for regulating the supply of gas available for star formation and redistributing metals into the circumgalactic medium. Veilleux et al. (2020)  delivered a comprehensive, highly cited literature review encompassing cool outflows in galaxies, reporting that neutral-atomic and molecular outflows are a ubiquitous occurrence operating over the full spatial extent of their galaxy hosts. Their systematic assessment indicates that these dense molecular components often dominate the total mass and momentum budget of galactic winds, eclipsing the mass carried by the hot, highly ionized X-ray emitting plasma.   

Fluetsch et al. (2021)  conducted a targeted, high-resolution investigation of the properties of multiphase outflows in local ultraluminous infrared galaxies using the Multi Unit Spectroscopic Explorer (MUSE). By analyzing a carefully selected sample of 31 galaxies , they report that the ionized phase of the outflows exhibits an average electron density roughly three times higher than the surrounding quiescent galactic disc. Specifically, their spectral diagnostic modeling derived electron densities of approximately 500 inverse cubic centimeters in the outflowing material, compared to 145 inverse cubic centimeters in the unperturbed disc. This significant density enhancement suggests that physical cloud compression within the turbulent outflow exceeds the rate of cloud dissipation.   

Extending the molecular outflow census to extreme redshifts, Spilker et al. (2020a, 2020b)  leveraged the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA)  to report that clumpy molecular outflows, robustly traced by far-infrared hydroxyl (OH) absorption features, are ubiquitous in massive dusty star-forming galaxies at redshifts greater than four. Their findings indicate that within the reionization era, the measured molecular outflow rates scale almost linearly with the host galaxy's star formation rate, mirroring the empirical scaling relations routinely observed in the local universe.

2.2 What remains unknown

Despite the increasingly routine detection of widespread multiphase outflows across cosmic time, the precise physical coupling mechanisms that entrain cold molecular gas into high-velocity winds—without utterly destroying the fragile molecular bonds via strong shocks and intense ultraviolet radiation—remain intensely debated. It is largely unknown whether the massive molecular gas components observed in these winds form in situ within the expanding outflowing shell via rapid cooling instabilities condensing out of the hot phase, or if pre-existing, dense giant molecular clouds residing in the galactic disc are physically accelerated outward by a combination of ram pressure and radiation pressure.

Furthermore, the detailed mass loading factors across the discrete phases—ionized, neutral atomic, and cold molecular—within individual, spatially resolved systems lack a universally calibrated, systematic census. The ultimate thermodynamic fate of this outflowing material is also unconstrained; the precise fractions of molecular mass that achieve true escape velocity, permanently enriching the intergalactic medium, versus the fraction of material that merely stalls in the halo and fountains back to fuel subsequent generations of star formation, remain elusive parameters. Finally, the degree to which episodic, highly luminous active galactic nucleus driving versus continuous, distributed supernova feedback governs the long-term kinetic energy budget of these multiphase winds presents a critical, unsolved boundary condition in galaxy evolution models.

2.3 Recommended data/survey families

Achieving a true tracer-resolved, common-denominator census requires simultaneous, spatially resolved mapping across multiple electromagnetic bands. The Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) is highly recommended for tracing the cold molecular phase, utilizing low-J carbon monoxide transitions, atomic carbon fine-structure lines, and deep hydroxyl absorption features against the far-infrared dust continuum.

This millimeter-wave mapping must be meticulously coupled with high-resolution near-infrared and mid-infrared integral field spectroscopy provided by the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) Near Infrared Spectrograph (NIRSpec) and Mid-Infrared Instrument (MIRI). These instruments are required to map the shock-heated warm molecular hydrogen and high-ionization tracers like Paschen-alpha and coronal lines. Complementary optical integral field spectroscopy from ground-based facilities like the Multi Unit Spectroscopic Explorer (MUSE) is continuously required to constrain the spatially resolved electron densities, internal dust extinction, and complex kinematics of the extended ionized gas halos.

2.4 Test/decision-criteria realism (JWST/ALMA or stated alternative)

ALMA and JWST are exquisitely realistic and represent the premier, indispensable capabilities for executing this specific scientific vision. ALMA currently possesses the unparalleled interferometric sensitivity and baseline resolution to map the sub-kiloparsec structural morphology of cold molecular outflows in both local ultraluminous infrared galaxies and gravitationally lensed high-redshift systems.

Concurrently, the JWST integral field units provide the precise mid-infrared spectroscopic diagnostics necessary to penetrate the exceptionally thick dust columns characteristic of merger-driven starbursts and deeply embedded active galactic nuclei, allowing observers to resolve the kinematics of the warm molecular phases that optical telescopes cannot reach. However, the realism of securing a comprehensive "common-denominator census" across statistically massive samples (hundreds of multiphase targets) is severely restricted by the heavy integration time investments required for deep interferometric and space-based integral field observations. Thus, executing these tests on carefully selected, highly representative sub-samples remains the most realistic near-term operational strategy.

Card 3 — Distinguishing reservoir removal from inefficient star formation
3.1 Prior-study findings (with source links)

The physical distinction between a galaxy that has ceased star formation due to the violent physical ejection of its gas reservoir versus a galaxy that retains a substantial gas mass rendered completely inert remains a critical juncture in quenching models. Saintonge & Catinella (2022)  published an exhaustive Annual Review  analyzing the cold interstellar medium of galaxies in the local universe. Their synthesis highlights that the depletion time of molecular gas (the inverse of star formation efficiency) is not a universal constant, but rather varies systematically with internal galaxy structural properties. Crucially, their review points out that a significant fraction of molecular mass resides in carbon monoxide-dark gas, particularly in low-metallicity and highly irradiated environments , complicating traditional gas census efforts.   

The ALMA-MaNGA QUEnching and STar formation (ALMaQUEST)  survey yielded critical insights into star formation scaling relations by resolving galaxies into sub-kiloparsec spaxels. Ellison et al. (2020)  reported that while the absolute, integrated star formation rates are primarily driven by the total available molecular gas mass, the scatter in the resolved star formation main sequence is predominantly driven by local, internal variations in the star formation efficiency itself, rather than variations in the local gas fraction. Conversely, Thorp et al. (2022) , in a dedicated ALMaQUEST study of 31 merging galaxies, found that merger-induced starbursts can be powered by a variety of distinct physical mechanisms. Their spatially resolved observations indicate that some regions within a merging system exhibit significantly enhanced star formation efficiency, while other regions are driven simply by an anomalous abundance of molecular gas fuel, demonstrating that no single, monolithic channel dictates merger-driven activity.

3.2 What remains unknown

The fundamental physical processes that tip the localized thermodynamic balance between gas reservoir removal (via bulk kinetic ejection from supernovae or active galactic nuclei) and the suppression of star formation efficiency (via turbulent heating, magnetic tension, or morphological quenching) remain poorly differentiated in integrated, whole-galaxy measurements. It is unknown exactly how low-level active galactic nucleus maintenance feedback dynamically couples with the dense, star-forming giant molecular clouds to halt their gravitational collapse without necessarily accelerating them to escape velocities and expelling them from the galactic potential well.

The precise contribution of carbon monoxide-dark molecular gas—gas where carbon is primarily in the form of neutral atomic carbon or ionized carbon due to pervasive far-ultraviolet radiation—to the total mass reservoir in the outskirts of recently quenched or post-starburst galaxies presents a major observational blind spot. Furthermore, the timeline and evolutionary track over which a system transitions from a state of wildly enhanced star formation efficiency during a major merger to a fully quenched, gas-depleted, or gas-inert relic is not mapped continuously across the multi-dimensional parameter space of galaxy evolution.

3.3 Recommended data/survey families

To accurately differentiate between a physically removed gas reservoir and a present but dynamically inert reservoir, multi-tracer millimeter and submillimeter interferometry is mandatory. The Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA), utilizing multiple transitions of carbon monoxide, neutral atomic carbon fine-structure lines, and high-density tracers such as hydrogen cyanide and formyl cation, must be deployed in tandem to measure both the total gas mass and the critical dense gas fraction. Expanding systematic, spatially resolved surveys like ALMaQUEST to include a significantly wider dynamic range of active galactic nucleus bolometric luminosities, environmental densities, and post-starburst ages is highly recommended to track the evolution of the Kennicutt-Schmidt relation on resolved kiloparsec scales across the full quenching timeline.

3.4 Test/decision-criteria realism (JWST/ALMA or stated alternative)

ALMA offers highly realistic, state-of-the-art capabilities for mapping the molecular denominator necessary for calculating star formation efficiency and depletion times on sub-galactic scales. Its ability to leverage alternative mass tracers, such as optically thin dust continuum emission or atomic carbon, allows observers to bypass the traditional, highly uncertain carbon monoxide conversion factor assumptions in extreme environments. JWST is equally realistic and vital for mapping the numerator of the efficiency equation; its mid-infrared photometric and spectroscopic capabilities can accurately measure deeply obscured star formation rates at unprecedented spatial resolutions, rendering the combined, synergistic ALMA-JWST observational axis the absolute optimal suite for distinguishing reservoir removal from mere inefficiency.

3.5 Overclaim risks

Asserting that a global reduction in low-J carbon monoxide luminosity equates to the absolute removal or destruction of the molecular gas reservoir severely overclaims the data, as it ignores phase transitions into carbon monoxide-dark molecular gas or the dense atomic phase driven by intense local radiation fields.

In comparative analyses, setting the absolute median star formation rates of merging systems against matched-control differences in gas fractions derived from isolated disk galaxies must be explicitly labeled non-commensurable. Additionally, calculating star formation efficiencies utilizing a single, static conversion factor across violently interacting merger systems or powerful active galactic nucleus hosts risks artificially manufacturing the very variations in efficiency the study purports to measure, conflating chemical and excitation state changes with true mass variations.

Card 4 — An observational determination of the maintenance-heating duty cycle
4.1 Prior-study findings (with source links)

The maintenance-mode feedback paradigm posits that long-lived, low-accretion-rate active galactic nuclei periodically inject mechanical energy into their surrounding halos, preventing the catastrophic cooling of the intracluster or circumgalactic medium. Eckert et al. (2021)  analyzed active galactic nucleus feedback across diverse, massive environments utilizing the X-ray Galaxy Groups and Clusters Project (X-GAP). Their thermodynamic analysis indicates that in morphologically disturbed clusters, the measured mechanical cavity power—derived from X-ray surface brightness depressions—more than compensates for the radiative cooling losses of the intracluster medium. This activity delivers substantially more energy than is strictly required to halt the cooling flow, suggesting significant energy may be channeled into lifting the intracluster medium at large radii.   

Concurrently, studies leveraging the unprecedented sensitivity of the LOFAR Two-metre Sky Survey (LoTSS)  Data Release Two have deeply probed the radio active galactic nucleus duty cycle across cosmic time. Best et al. (2023)  and Smith et al. (2025)  formulated highly complete radio luminosity functions. Their findings indicate that a large population of massive quiescent galaxies currently lacking active, instantaneous radio emission have historically been heavily influenced by past episodes of radio feedback. This periodic, cyclic injection of relativistic jets and subsequent energy thermalization is central to the viability of the maintenance-heating paradigm in cosmological models.

4.2 What remains unknown

The precise, empirical mathematical formulation of the radio maintenance-heating duty cycle—specifically the absolute duration of the active jet phase relative to the quiescent, non-accreting phase—remains exceptionally challenging to calibrate observationally. While the detection of large-scale X-ray cavities provides a vital historical calorimetric measure of past mechanical energy injection, it is unknown how isotropically this directed jet energy is ultimately distributed. The specific physical fraction of the mechanical energy that efficiently thermalizes in the cooling core, versus the fraction that merely displaces gas and generates weak shocks at large virial radii without heating the dense central regions, is poorly constrained.

Furthermore, the specific physical mechanisms that trigger the reignition of the central supermassive black hole engine after a prolonged quiescent phase remain elusive. How these central accretion triggers correlate with the micro-scale, multiphase cooling instabilities and precipitation rates of the surrounding hot halo represents an unresolved, highly complex feedback loop that evades current analytical characterization.

4.3 Recommended data/survey families

To accurately constrain the absolute timescales of the duty cycle, ultra-wide-field, low-frequency radio interferometric surveys are essential to detect the faint, steep-spectrum synchrotron emission characteristic of old, expanding, and dying radio lobes. The LOFAR Two-metre Sky Survey (LoTSS) and the upcoming Square Kilometre Array (SKA) are the primary, indispensable recommended survey families for this specialized task.

These comprehensive radio measurements must be rigorously combined with deep, high-spatial-resolution X-ray mapping from the Chandra X-ray Observatory or the XMM-Newton satellite. The X-ray data is strictly required to visually identify the physical boundaries of X-ray surface brightness depressions (cavities), map shock fronts, and calculate the associated mechanical PdV work and buoyancy timescales of the bubbles.

4.4 Test/decision-criteria realism (JWST/ALMA or stated alternative)

JWST and ALMA marginal here — the relevant capability is the low-frequency radio and high-resolution X-ray instrument families.

The physical scales relevant for maintenance-mode heating via relativistic radio jets typically extend from tens to hundreds of kiloparsecs, scales that operate well beyond the optimized, highly targeted fields of view of ALMA and JWST. Moreover, the hot plasma tracing the primary radiative cooling losses emits entirely in the X-ray regime, and the relic, aged electron populations mapping the history of the duty cycle emit predominantly at megahertz radio frequencies. Thus, relying on low-frequency arrays like LOFAR and X-ray observatories like Chandra to define the duty cycle decision criteria and measure integrated energy budgets is the only realistic, physically appropriate observational strategy.

4.5 Overclaim risks

Calculating precise duty cycles strictly from radio spectral aging models carries significant overclaim risks, as these mathematical models depend heavily on assumed, often highly unconstrained, local magnetic field strengths and complex expansion energy losses.

In the context of reporting feedback efficiency, comparing single-object absolute cavity powers derived from localized X-ray observations to simulation medians of time-averaged energy injection must be explicitly labeled non-commensurable. Additionally, associating the mere spatial presence of a radio bubble or a weak shock front with the complete, uniform, and immediate prevention of gas cooling throughout the entire, multi-phase cluster core overclaims the efficiency of the feedback coupling, ignoring the highly localized nature of energy deposition.

(2020)  published a seminal review on the Chaotic Cold Accretion framework, outlining a mechanism where extended warm and cold multiphase filaments rapidly condense out of a hot, turbulent intracluster plasma. Their theoretical synthesis indicates that this precipitation occurs primarily when the ratio of the local cooling time to the turbulent eddy turnover time approaches unity. This precipitation forms a complex, rain-like "cosmic weather" that tightly regulates the feeding and subsequent feedback cycles of supermassive black holes.

These forward-modeled, idealized simulations show that baroclinicity can trigger fresh, sustained turbulence in stratified cluster atmospheres, directly linking macro-scale mechanical feedback from the AGN to the micro-scale, multiphase accretion physics.

ALMA and JWST are highly realistic and absolutely essential tools for this micro-scale validation. ALMA currently possesses the sub-arcsecond spatial resolution and high spectral precision required to explicitly test the turbulent velocity power spectra of the cold molecular gas against the synthetic mock interferometric data cubes generated by hydrodynamical simulations.

Similarly, the JWST Near Infrared Spectrograph offers unprecedented realism for testing the warm molecular and ionized emission features (such as forbidden iron II and ro-vibrational molecular hydrogen lines) in deeply obscured active galactic nucleus environments. This unparalleled infrared sensitivity enables direct, pixel-by-pixel kinematic and thermodynamic comparisons between the empirical high-redshift data and the theoretical mock outputs of sub-grid feedback recipes.

5.5 Overclaim risks

A substantial risk in the theoretical community lies in claiming that a cosmological simulation successfully captures the fundamental physics of AGN feedback based purely on qualitative, visual morphological similarities between the mock synthetic images and empirical observations, rather than relying on rigorous, quantitative statistical diagnostics like two-point correlation functions or kinematic structure functions.

When conducting comparative validation, setting the absolute simulation medians of condensing gas mass directly against observational per-tracer mass fractions mapped by highly specific, ionization-dependent single emission lines must be explicitly labeled non-commensurable. Furthermore, validating a sub-grid active galactic nucleus feedback prescription based on its performance in a simulated massive cluster core does not guarantee its physical accuracy or scaling validity when applied down to low-mass dwarf galaxy halos, presenting a severe risk of extrapolative overclaim.

The highly accelerated, rapid timeline required to assemble ultra-massive quiescent galaxies within the first billion years of cosmic history severely challenges current hierarchical structure formation models and standard dark matter halo accretion histories. It is entirely unknown whether the abrupt cessation of star formation observed in these extreme early systems, such as the mini-quenched galaxy at redshift 7.3, represents a permanent, irreversible evolutionary endpoint onto the red sequence, or merely a temporary, localized lull in a broader, highly bursty and rejuvenation-prone star formation history.

Furthermore, the physical origin of the anomalous chemical abundance patterns, particularly the unexpected, extreme nitrogen enhancements observed alongside low overall oxygen abundances at high redshift, remains a significant theoretical puzzle. It is currently unknown whether these anomalies are driven by rapid enrichment from massive Wolf-Rayet stars, exotic massive binary interactions, the presence of supermassive stars in dense proto-globular clusters, or highly top-heavy initial mass functions that deviate entirely from the locally calibrated standard.

6.3 Recommended data/survey families

To systematically rebalance the structural, kinematic, and chemical evidence base at the high-redshift frontier, deep, high signal-to-noise continuum spectroscopy is absolutely mandatory. Medium-resolution grating surveys with the JWST Near Infrared Spectrograph (NIRSpec) are recommended to robustly measure faint stellar absorption features, map rest-frame optical breaks (such as the Balmer break), and resolve critical, closely blended outflow tracers like the sodium doublet and ionized magnesium.

Expanding the observational footprint of specialized surveys like JADES and EXCELS to target substantially larger statistical samples of rare post-starburst galaxies is essential. Additionally, spatially resolved chemical mapping via JWST integral field units is highly recommended to physically trace metallicity gradients and isolate the spatial origins of anomalous abundance ratios within the earliest assembling galactic structures.

6.4 Test/decision-criteria realism (JWST/ALMA or stated alternative)

JWST is exceptionally realistic and historically unprecedented for this domain; it currently stands as the sole astronomical facility capable of securing the necessary high signal-to-noise rest-frame optical spectroscopy required to derive direct-method T

ALMA provides highly realistic and strictly necessary complementary decision criteria. It possesses the sub-millimeter sensitivity required to detect faint thermal dust continuum and aggressively search for residual cold molecular gas reservoirs inside these early purportedly quenched galaxies. Mapping this invisible mass with ALMA is the only realistic method for verifying their absolute passivity and confirming that the gas reservoir has been truly exhausted or expelled, rather than merely rendered temporarily inefficient.

6.5 Overclaim risks

Deriving precise mass outflow rates and total kinetic energy budgets from single, often saturated absorption line profiles, such as the sodium doublet, in faint, low-resolution high-redshift spectra introduces severe, frequently underreported systematic errors. These errors are heavily related to unknown geometric gas covering fractions and highly uncertain ionization state corrections.

When evaluating the evolution of feedback power, comparing single-object absolute outflow rates measured at redshift four to matched-control differences measured in broad, local universe samples must be explicitly labeled non-commensurable. Additionally, declaring a high-redshift dwarf galaxy permanently and irreversibly quenched based solely on a temporary suppression of its rest-frame ultraviolet continuum risks overstating the finality of early-universe active galactic nucleus feedback, ignoring the well-documented prevalence of cosmic gas accretion and subsequent stellar rejuvenation.
