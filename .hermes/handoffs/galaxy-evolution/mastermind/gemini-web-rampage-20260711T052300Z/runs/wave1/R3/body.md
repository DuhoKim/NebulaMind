What no study provides

Despite the accumulation of spatially resolved outflow data from cutting-edge observatories like ALMA and JWST, the literature lacks the comprehensive datasets and unified methodologies required to execute a truly commensurable multi-phase feedback measurement. Explicit gaps remain unaddressed by current campaigns.

GAP: A single sample featuring all four outflow gas phases (hot X-ray, warm ionized, neutral atomic, and cold molecular) observed simultaneously at matched depth and identical spatial resolution.
Current data fall short because observatory capabilities dictate disparate physical scales. ALMA and NOEMA CO(1-0) interferometry, which is necessary to resolve cold molecular gas, operates at sensitivity thresholds and angular resolutions that do not map seamlessly onto JWST or SINFONI integral-field spectroscopy capturing [O III] or Na I D winds. Furthermore, X-ray Fe K absorption tracking UFOs via XMM-Newton or Chandra probes sub-parsec, accretion-disk scales that are fundamentally inaccessible to galactic-scale mapping efforts [arXiv:2606.05312, 10.1051/0004-6361/201937175]. Consequently, the literature is forced to rely on combining disjointed catalogs—such as merging WISSH X-ray targets with completely separate local ULIRG CO data—creating cross-family compilations that are inherently non-commensurable due to unmatched observational depths.

GAP: Unified mass-loading factor calculations simultaneously utilizing spatially resolved, timescale-matched SFR denominators across all observed gas phases within a single galaxy.
Current data fall short because different physical phases are historically married to specific SFR proxies. Ionized outflows are frequently normalized by instantaneous, localized star formation mapped via H$\alpha$, whereas molecular mass-loading factors depend almost exclusively on galaxy-integrated FIR luminosities calibrated by Kennicutt relations. Meanwhile, neutral gas studies increasingly resort to SED-averaged stellar masses or historical SFRs [10.1093/mnras/stac190, 10.1051/0004-6361/201937175]. No single study currently derives η for the molecular, ionized, and neutral phases by tracking the exact spatial location of the multiphase gas and dividing it by an SFR matched to that precise spatial bin and the equivalent temporal launching epoch.
