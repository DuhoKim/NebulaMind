Per-observable discrimination power

A central pursuit of the galaxy formation literature is identifying which specific synthetic observables genuinely possess the discriminatory power to differentiate between the highly complex, parameterized sub-grid physics models governing supermassive black hole feedback (e.g., SIMBA's explicitly decoupled bipolar kinetic jets, TNG's dual-mode high-accretion quasar state versus low-accretion kinetic winds, and EAGLE's stochastic thermal heating model). The literature emphasizes that not all observables are equally sensitive to the underlying algorithmic choices of the simulation builders.

Nebular line ratios, when combined with specific star formation rates (sSFR), provide immense discriminatory leverage. Gawade et al. [arXiv:2512.22268] report that BPT-mapped nebular line ratios (comparing collisionally excited forbidden lines to hydrogen recombination lines) effectively discriminate between feedback architectures, particularly within the evolutionary transition zone known as the green valley. According to their rigorous forward-modeling pipeline, EAGLE’s stochastic thermal feedback models portray a broad, continuous distribution of specific star formation rates that smoothly span across both the star-forming and composite loci of the synthetic BPT diagnostic diagram. This structural output indicates that thermal heating models support a slower, more gradual decline in galactic star formation as the active nucleus slowly heats the surrounding halo. In stark contrast, the application of TNG’s kinetic mode (which relies on impulsive mechanical kicks to the gas cells) results in simulated analogues that concentrate heavily and unnaturally in a LINER-like, extremely low-sSFR regime. This discrete locus clustering suggests that the kinetic sub-grid models drive an efficient, near-binary shutdown of star formation, a behavioral divergence that allows researchers to discriminate between the efficacy of thermal versus kinetic implementations relative to local active host observations. Known failure modes

The transition from theoretical absolute values derived from a computational mesh to observationally restricted estimands is fraught with methodological perils. The literature documents several critical failure modes where a raw-statistics comparison and a rigorous forward-modeled comparison of the identical physical quantity disagreed profoundly, leading to severe potential misinterpretations of the hydrodynamic simulation's underlying physical validity.

1. Outflow Column Density Underestimation (The Instrumental Smoothing Failure)
In evaluating mass outflow rates in the FIRE-2 zoom-in simulations, Carr et al. [arXiv:2503.22312] isolated a severe failure mode residing within the application of Empirical Partial Covering Models (PCMs). When measuring the true, raw column density of circumgalactic outflows directly from the simulation's gas cells, the physical values were significantly higher than the values recovered when the PCM analytical framework was applied to the forward-modeled synthetic Si II spectra.

Attributed quote/explanation: "The PCM systematically underestimates the column density by 1.3 (± 1.3) dex on average, with the discrepancy increasing at higher column densities. We measure an average bias of 1.3 (± 1.3) dex with an uncertainty of 1.3 (± 1.3) dex... We attribute this bias to instrumental smoothing. Since the PCM underestimates column densities, it also underestimates flow rates." [arXiv:2503.22312]. The failure mode dictates that the mathematical assumptions inherent in the observational model regarding how light is smoothed by spectrographs fundamentally fail to capture the true, clumpy, multi-phase optical depth of the simulated wind.

2. Quenched Fraction Misinterpretation (The Volume-Limited vs Selection-Matched Failure)
Donnari et al. [arXiv:2008.00004] identify a persistent and highly impactful failure mode in comparing simulated quiescent fractions to immense optical surveys like the Sloan Digital Sky Survey. A raw-statistics comparison evaluating all available three-dimensional spatial data in a volume-limited simulation box yields completely different temporal gradients and spatial clustering metrics than a forward-modeled comparison subjected to two-dimensional cylindrical geometry and synthetic aperture limits.

Attributed quote/explanation: "Proper consideration of the stellar and host mass distributions is crucial: naive comparisons to volume-limited samples from simulations lead to misinterpretation of the quenched fractions as a function of z by up to 20 (± UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) percent... Taking projected rather than 3D distances for satellites decreases the quenched fractions by up to 10 (± UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) percent due to field contamination." [arXiv:2008.00004].

Note on Commensurability: Simulation absolute medians vs observed selection-shaped distributions are unlike estimands: label non-commensurable unless the source itself matched selections. Consequently, the authors conclude that a three-dimensional, non-aperture-restricted, raw-simulation, unweighted fraction of 80 (± UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) percent is inherently non-commensurable with SDSS parameters unless strict geometrical forward modeling is applied to mimic the telescope's line of sight.

3. X-ray Cavity Enthalpy Overestimation (The Midpoint Pressure Assumption Failure)
Jennings et al. [arXiv:2407.14415] outline a critical failure mode regarding the thermodynamics of active galactic nucleus feedback in the SIMBA and Hyenas models. When extracting the energetic properties of synthetic X-ray cavities utilizing the standard, accepted observational technique (which relies heavily on measuring the midpoint pressure of the underdense bubble to calculate total enthalpy), the observationally derived energy severely disagreed with the known, raw energetic kinetic output explicitly injected by the simulation's theoretical AGN jet event algorithm.

Attributed quote/explanation: "Bubble enthalpy as measured with the usual midpoint pressure typically exceeds the energy released by the most recent jet event, hinting that the mechanical work is done predominantly at a lower pressure against the IGrM." [arXiv:2407.14415]. This failure mode indicates that standard observational assumptions regarding isotropic expansion and pressure equilibrium fail to capture the true thermodynamic work done against the intracluster medium during the highly dynamic inflation phase of the cavity.

Gaps

Despite immense computational advances in forward-modeling techniques and synthetic observatories, significant lacunae remain within the published literature regarding the direct comparison of these specific cosmological simulations to physical observations.

GAP: Forward-modeled, instrument-convolved Chandra or eROSITA mock comparisons explicitly intended for characterizing the detailed X-ray cavity and associated intracluster shock populations specifically within the ROMULUS (Romulus25 and RomulusC) simulations (NONE_FOUND – while the Romulus model features highly specific thermal energy injections with uniquely engineered shut-off cooling mechanics intended to produce cavities, deep mock-observational pipelines like the MOXHA suite or pyXSIM have seemingly only been published for the SIMBA and IllustrisTNG volumes, leaving Romulus without a commensurate observational mapping in the current literature).
