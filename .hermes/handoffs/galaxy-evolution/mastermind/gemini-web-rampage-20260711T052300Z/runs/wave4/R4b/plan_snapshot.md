 Galaxy Quenching: Depletion vs. Inefficiency 
Research Websites
(1) Search scholarly databases for publications from 2018 onwards analyzing gas reservoirs in quenched, quenching, green-valley, and post-starburst galaxies.
(2) For each study, extract the sample selection criteria, molecular gas tracers used, conversion assumptions, measured gas fractions, star-formation efficiencies, stated uncertainties, and the authors' conclusions regarding depletion versus suppressed star-formation efficiency.
(3) Identify and extract data on how studies decompose specific star formation rate offsets into gas fraction changes versus star formation efficiency changes, including discussions on systematics like conversion factors and aperture effects.
(4) Find analyses within these papers that distinguish central-kpc gas depletion from global galaxy-wide gas loss, noting the measured radii and any extrapolations made by the authors.
(5) Extract statistical population fractions of gas-poor versus gas-rich but inefficient quenched systems, capturing the specific tracer, selection criteria, denominator, redshift range, and selection biases for each fraction.
(6) Find published claims and tests within the literature that associate Active Galactic Nucleus presence or history with either the gas depletion or the suppressed efficiency scenarios.
(7) Identify proposed future discriminating measurements and telescope observations suggested by the literature to resolve the depletion versus efficiency debate, including authors' stated feasibility limits.
(8) Compile all findings with rigorous citation tracking to support the required structured deliverables without assuming missing uncertainties or drawing definitive conclusions.
