Matched-depth census feasibility

Establishing a multi-phase active galactic nucleus (AGN) outflow census at matched depth requires bridging observational constraints across the ionized, neutral, and molecular gas phases, primarily targeting the peak epoch of galaxy assembly within the z ≈ 0–2 window. The feasibility of securing homogeneous, statistically significant sample sizes across these disparate phases rests on the integration of optical/near-infrared integral field spectroscopy with sub-millimeter interferometry. The available published record indicates that matching the sensitivities of these distinct tracers is observationally expensive but currently being executed in sample sizes ranging from approximately 30 to 200 galaxies (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE).

To characterize the neutral gas phase, recent studies rely heavily on Na I D absorption signatures, a feature notoriously difficult to isolate from stellar continuum contamination. Deep spectroscopic campaigns utilizing the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) Near Infrared Spectrograph (NIRSpec) have successfully charted this phase at cosmic noon. In an analysis of the mass-complete Blue Jay survey, JWST/NIRSpec R ~ 1000 slit spectra were utilized to examine 113 galaxies (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) situated between redshifts 1.7 < z < 3.5 (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE). This program detected excess Na I D absorption—beyond the expected stellar contribution—in 46% (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) of the massive galaxy sample, defined as possessing stellar masses log(M_star / M_sun) > 10 (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) (https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17939). Crucially, half of these observed absorption profiles exhibited blueshifts of at least 100 km s^-1 (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE), providing unambiguous signatures of neutral gas outflows. Using these data, researchers derived conservative mass outflow rates ranging from 3 to 100 M_sun yr^-1 (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) (https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17939). Further constraints on the neutral and ionized phases simultaneously are provided by the EXCELS survey, which leveraged JWST/NIRSpec medium-resolution observations to study 24 massive quiescent galaxies (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) with log(M_star / M_sun) > 10 (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) at 1 < z < 3 (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE). The EXCELS program reported evidence for weak AGN activity in 4 out of the 8 galaxies (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) that yielded robust emission line detections based on line ratio diagnostics, and utilized Na D absorption lines to characterize the incidence of outflows in a subset of 13 post-starburst and quiescent galaxies (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) (https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18278). The depth of these observations allows for the estimation of quenching times spanning 0.06 to 1.75 Gyr (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) (https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18278).

The ionized phase of AGN-driven winds is traditionally mapped via rest-frame optical emission lines, notably [OIII] and H-alpha. The KMOS3D survey provides a vast benchmark, representing a near-infrared integral field spectroscopic survey of over 700 galaxies (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) spanning 0.7 < z < 2.7 (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) (https://www.mpe.mpg.de/ir/KMOS3D). Within a specific redshift slice of 1.4 <= z <= 2.7 (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE), the survey detected broad-component outflow signatures in 190 out of 599 galaxies (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE), yielding an overall ionized outflow detection fraction of 32% (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) (https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07621v2). Among these detected outflows, 54% (103 galaxies) (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) were classified as AGN-driven winds, while the remaining 46% (87 galaxies) (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) were attributed to star-formation-driven winds (https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07621v2).

To achieve matched multi-phase depth, specifically targeting the synergy between ionized and molecular outflows, the SINFONI Survey for Unveiling the Physics and Effect of Radiative feedback (SUPER) offers the most stringent feasibility test case. The SUPER program executed a blind survey of 39 X-ray selected AGN (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) at z ~ 2 (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE), utilizing adaptive optics-assisted integral field unit (IFU) observations to achieve high spatial resolutions of ~ 2 kpc (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) (https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04858). The survey mapped outflow morphologies via the broad component of the [OIII] 5007 line and star formation via the narrow component of H-alpha. The target sample spanned stellar masses from 4 x 10^9 to 2 x 10^11 M_sun (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE), star formation rates of 25 to 680 M_sun yr^-1 (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE), and AGN bolometric luminosities of 2 x 10^44 to 8 x 10^47 erg s^-1 (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) (https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04858).

Crucially, the molecular gas content of the SUPER targets was subsequently targeted with the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) to complete the multi-phase picture. In a dedicated follow-up, 41 luminous unobscured quasars (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) from the Sloan Digital Sky Survey at z ~ 2 (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) were targeted using CO(3-2), CO(4-3), and CO(7-6) transitions. The main sample of 32 targets (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) observed in CO(3-2) yielded 15 detections (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE), alongside 5 detections (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) from an archival sub-sample of 9 quasars (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) (https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/540/1/1163/8128274). The median molecular gas mass for the 20 CO-detected quasars (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) was reported as 8.0 ± 1.5 x 10^9 M_sun (stated 1-sigma uncertainty), with corresponding gas fractions ranging from 0.02 to 0.32 (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) (https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/540/1/1163/8128274). Interestingly, six of these quasars (15% of the sample, UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) also showed evidence of companion galaxies detected in CO at the same redshift, indicating overdense environments characterized by a quasar-galaxy cross-correlation length of 9.81 + 2.22 / - 2.05 h^-1 cMpc (stated uncertainties, UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) (https://arxiv.org/html/2606.30742v1). However, bridging these multi-phase measurements introduces significant systematic complexities; for instance, the calculation of mass outflow rates depends heavily on the optical depth (tau_0,r), which is inversely correlated with the covering fraction (Cf) of the gas (https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/528/3/4976/7598247). These parameters must be carefully constrained to yield reliable energy injection rates. Overall, the literature supports the feasibility of matched-depth multi-phase censuses for sample sizes of approximately 40 to 120 targets, provided substantial allocations on highly sensitive facilities such as JWST, VLT/MUSE, and ALMA are secured.

Resolved gas in quenched hosts

Determining the residual molecular gas and dust reservoirs in high-redshift quiescent and post-starburst galaxies remains a demanding observational challenge. Because the interstellar medium in these systems is intrinsically dim, containing gas fractions typically ~100 times lower than those of local star-forming galaxies (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE), the literature reveals a stark methodological divide. Studies must rely either on deep integration of strongly gravitationally lensed individual targets or the extensive stacking of wide-field (sub)millimeter interferometric maps to reach the required sensitivities (https://research.rug.nl/files/918902403/aa45977-23.pdf). These divergent approaches have occasionally yielded conflicting measurements regarding the true volume of cold gas retained by massive galaxies following the quenching of star formation.

Stacking analyses leverage large ensembles of galaxies to detect the faint average continuum emission that falls below the formal detection threshold of individual pointings. A recent analysis utilized ALMA Band 6 observations (probing the 1.1 mm rest-frame dust continuum) in the GOODS-South field. By stacking the emission of moderately massive quiescent galaxies—characterized by log(M_star / M_sun) = 10.7 ± 0.2 (stated 1-sigma uncertainty) at a mean redshift of z ~ 1.5 (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE)—across a 72.42 arcmin^2 (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) map, researchers reported a tentative detection (https://nbi.ku.dk/english/theses/masters-theses/david-blnquez-ses/David_Blanquez-Serse_Master_Thesis.pdf). This stacked signal yielded a dust mass equivalent of log(M_dust / M_sun) = 7.47 ± 0.13 (stated 1-sigma uncertainty) and an inferred molecular gas mass of log(M_gas / M_sun) = 9.42 ± 0.14 (stated 1-sigma uncertainty). These figures translate to an average gas fraction (f_gas) of 5.3 ± 1.8 % (stated 1-sigma uncertainty) (https://research.rug.nl/files/918902403/aa45977-23.pdf). Older stacking experiments utilizing far-infrared Herschel data (e.g., Gobat et al. 2018) have previously suggested higher gas fractions, but current high-resolution interferometric stacking appears to stabilize the population average near the ~5% mark for cosmic noon quiescent systems.

In stark contrast, deep targeted ALMA observations of individual gravitationally lensed quiescent galaxies paint a picture of severe gas depletion. Leveraging the natural magnification provided by foreground galaxy clusters, studies such as the REQUIEM survey investigated massive quiescent galaxies at 1.5 < z < 3.2 (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE). For a subset of these strongly lensed sources lacking detectable dust continuum emission, stringent upper limits on the dust-to-stellar mass ratio were placed at < 10^-4 (upper limit, UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) (https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/527/4/9529/7457738). Even more severe dust deficiencies were recorded in ultra-massive quiescent galaxies at 3 < z < 4 (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE), where ratios spanning 2 x 10^-4 to 5 x 10^-3 (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) were identified, indicating rapid destruction or removal of dust shortly after quenching (https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Low-dust-masses-for-quenched-galaxies-Measurements-of-fdust-for-distant-lensed-quiescent_fig2_354776949). Similarly, targeted CO(2-1) transition observations of extremely massive quiescent galaxies (log(M_star / M_sun) > 11.55, UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) situated at 1.2 < z < 1.5 (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) point toward negligible remaining molecular gas reservoirs, registering limits of f_gas < 1 % (upper limit, UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) (https://pure.rug.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/918902403/aa45977-23.pdf).

The literature heavily debates the origin of this discrepancy between the ~5% stacking averages and the <1% individual targeted limits. Part of the tension arises from the conversion factors utilized to transform observable dust continuum into total gas mass. Calculations hinge on an assumed gas-to-dust ratio (GDR), typically fixed at values between 92 and 100 (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) depending on the study (https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/527/4/9529/7457738). Adjusting the dust temperature (T_dust) from 25 K to 20 K (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) only increases the inferred dust mass by approximately 40% (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE), which is insufficient to bridge the order-of-magnitude gap between the populations (https://arxiv.org/html/2507.16914v2). Furthermore, assumptions regarding the Initial Mass Function (IMF) inject additional scatter; converting from a Salpeter to a Chabrier IMF in stellar mass estimations alters the ratio by approximately 0.21 dex (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) (https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/527/4/9529/7457738).

Moving forward, pushing detection limits for resolved gas in quenched hosts is increasingly reliant on alternative tracers. Observations of [CII] fine-structure emission in ALMA high-frequency Bands 9 and 10 offer a pathway to probe molecular gas fraction limits significantly deeper than achievable with dust continuum or low-J CO transitions at fixed integration times, particularly for galaxies at 3 < z < 6 (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) (https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09347v1). Concurrently, the NOEMA observatory is leveraging dense gas tracers (HCN, HCO+, HNC) to map reservoirs. The PRUSSIC III survey detected 34 dense-gas transitions (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) across 11 primarily lensed dusty star-forming galaxies (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) at z = 1.6 to 3.2 (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE), calculating dense-gas depletion times of a median 23 Myr (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) and uncovering dense-gas fractions (HCN/CO ratios) ranging from 0.01 to 0.15 (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) (https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/prussic-iii-alma-and-noema-survey-of-dense-gas-in-high-redshift-s/). Such deep spectroscopic capabilities are required to untangle the true gas depletion mechanisms operating within quiescent halos.

Hot-phase limits vs halo mass

The detection and characterization of the hot-phase circumgalactic medium (CGM) and X-ray cavities—the primary physical signatures of mechanical AGN feedback—are fundamentally constrained by the scaling of X-ray luminosity with halo mass. Because the thermal bremsstrahlung and inverse Compton emission of the hot gas drops precipitously at lower masses, mapping these structures toward the group and individual galaxy scale (M_halo ~ 10^11 to 10^13 M_sun, UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) requires either exceptionally deep targeted observations or the massive statistical power of all-sky survey stacking.

Wide-field stacking analyses have been revolutionized by the eROSITA telescope on board the Spektrum-Roentgen-Gamma (SRG) observatory, which boasts a field of view of approximately 1 degree (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) and reaches a sensitivity of 10^-13 erg cm^-2 s^-1 (1 day exposure, point source, UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) in the soft 0.2–10 keV band (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) (https://www.spiedigitallibrary.org/journals/Journal-of-Astronomical-Telescopes-Instruments-and-Systems/volume-11/issue-4/042008/XRISM-Xtend-transient-search--system-design-and-results-during/10.1117/1.JATIS.11.4.042008.pdf). Leveraging the eROSITA Final Equatorial-Depth Survey (eFEDS), which covers 140 deg^2 (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) and overlaps deep optical/NIR surveys such as HSC Wide and KIDS-VIKINGS, researchers successfully stacked the X-ray events around ~ 16,000 central galaxies (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) selected from the GAMA survey and ~ 1,600 massive galaxies (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) from SDSS (https://arxiv.org/html/2401.17308v3). This extensive stacking methodology allowed for the detection of weak X-ray emission from the outer CGM, extending sensitivity limits to recover the average X-ray scaling relations of halos down to a mass of M_200 ~ 10^12.3 M_sun (survey average, UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) (https://indico.nbi.ku.dk/event/1774/contributions/13478/attachments/4040/6371/NTruong_Slides.pdf). The primary limitation of this technique is the eROSITA Point Spread Function (PSF), which measures ~15 to 26 arcsec (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE); to properly resolve the extended hot CGM, the physical size of the eROSITA PSF strictly limits the maximum redshift within which galaxies can be stacked (https://arxiv.org/html/2401.17308v3).

While stacking unveils population averages, the resolution of individual AGN-inflated X-ray cavities and local shocks relies almost exclusively on deep Chandra integrations, utilizing its superior 0.5 arcsec on-axis spatial resolution (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) (https://www.mpe.mpg.de/455799/instrument). The detection of these cavities—surface brightness depressions representing bubbles of low-density relativistic plasma displacing thermal gas—provides direct calorimetric measurements of AGN mechanical feedback. For example, a deep 105 ks (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) Chandra observation of the radio-loud galaxy group 3C 88 revealed an immense X-ray cavity with a diameter of ~ 50 kpc (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) situated ~ 28 kpc (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) from the nucleus (https://uva.theopenscholar.com/files/craig-sarazin/files/stz229.pdf). Calculations indicate the total enthalpy of this cavity is 3.8 x 10^58 erg (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE), requiring an average inflation power of ~ 2.0 x 10^43 erg s^-1 (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE). Furthermore, the observation detected a weak shock with a Mach number of M = 1.4 ± 0.2 (stated uncertainty), carrying a shock energy estimated at 1.9 x 10^59 erg (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) (https://uva.theopenscholar.com/files/craig-sarazin/files/stz229.pdf). Similar massive observational investments are required to map thermodynamics in other low-mass systems; the NGC 5813 group required an exceptional 650 ks (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) Chandra observation, and the HCG 62 compact group required a 120 ks (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) pointing to constrain the presence of thermal gas inside the cavities and quantify the total power budget of the AGN outburst (https://cxc.harvard.edu/target_lists/cycle10/CLUSTERSOFGALAXIES10.html).

The empirical measurement of hot halo limits continues to expose severe tensions with cosmological hydrodynamical simulations. Theoretical models such as EAGLE, IllustrisTNG, and SIMBA predict drastically different physical states for the CGM, particularly at low halo masses (M_halo < 10^12.5 M_sun, UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) (https://arxiv.org/html/2506.17440v1). For instance, the kinetic mode AGN feedback in SIMBA is far more efficient at ejecting halo gas than the thermal mode implemented in TNG, resulting in significantly lower CGM mass fractions (f_CGM) at fixed black hole mass (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.13889). Hot gas densities at 20 to 50 kpc (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) radii from M_star = 10^10.2 to 10^11.2 M_sun (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) galaxies are predicted to be an order of magnitude higher in EAGLE and TNG compared to values inferred from pressure equilibrium arguments based on cool UV absorber data, suggesting the X-ray CGM could be dimmer than current simulations predict (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.13889).

Future constraints on the dynamics of the hot phase will rely on the XRISM Resolve microcalorimeter, which offers unprecedented spectral resolution of < 7 eV at 6 keV (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) combined with an effective area of 180 cm^2 at 6 keV (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) (https://www.spiedigitallibrary.org/journals/Journal-of-Astronomical-Telescopes-Instruments-and-Systems/volume-11/issue-4/042023/Resolve-instrument-onboard-XRISM--design-integration-and-instrument-test/10.1117/1.JATIS.11.4.042023.pdf). Simulated 100 ks (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) observations of outflow regions in nearby systems like M87 demonstrate that Resolve is capable of resolving individual spectral lines (such as the Fe L line complex, Si, and S K lines) and tracing gas motions directly through Doppler broadening and line energy shifts, providing robust measurements of the chemical composition, multi-temperature structure, and turbulent velocity of the hot gas (https://heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov/docs/xrism/about/XRISM_White_Paper.pdf).

Program-allocation reality

Executing multi-phase matched-depth surveys and high-resolution spatial mapping requires massive allocations of observing time across the world's premier astronomical facilities. The published statistics of accepted programs reveal the sheer scale of the observing commitments necessary to produce decisive constraints on galaxy evolution and AGN feedback.

To facilitate ambitious scientific projects that necessitate panchromatic coverage, major observatories have established extensive Joint Proposal agreements. Through a single peer-reviewed submission, the ALMA observatory can allocate substantial time on partner telescopes. For each cycle beginning with Cycle 10, an ALMA proposal can secure up to 115 hours (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) of JWST time, up to 50 hours (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) on the ESO Very Large Telescope (VLT), and up to 5% (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) of the available time on the Karl G. Jansky Very Large Array (VLA) (https://www.almaobservatory.org/en/announcements/four-telescopes-for-the-price-of-one/). Reciprocal agreements allow JWST to allocate up to 115 hours (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) of ALMA time (across the 12-m, 7-m, and Total Power Arrays), while the VLA and VLT can each allocate up to 50 hours (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) of ALMA time per year (https://almascience.eso.org/news/alma-announces-joint-proposal-agreements-for-jwst-vla-and-the-vlt). The overall availability of time on these flagship space observatories is fiercely contested; for instance, JWST's Cycle 4 allocated a total of approximately 8,500 hours (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) across all selected cosmic science programs (https://astro.cornell.edu/news/cornell-astronomers-secure-over-350-hours-jwst-cycle-4-time-groundbreaking-cosmic-research).

A review of completed and ongoing large surveys illustrates the typical time blocks awarded to achieve statistical significance. The SINFONI Survey for Unveiling the Physics and Effect of Radiative feedback (SUPER), which mapped ionized outflows in a sample of 39 AGN, required a dedicated allocation of 280 hours (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) of VLT/SINFONI time (https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04858). Similarly, the KMOS3D near-infrared integral field spectroscopic survey, which traced H-alpha, [NII], and emission line maps across 700 galaxies, was executed via a 75-night (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) Guaranteed Time Observations program (https://www.mpe.mpg.de/ir/KMOS3D). For deep targeted programs, such as the JWST EXCELS survey aiming to constrain early extragalactic continuum and emission lines, observation configurations utilizing the NRSIRS2RAPID readout mode required specific exposure architectures to reach necessary depths for priority targets, which included 25 to 30 massive quiescent galaxies (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) at 1 < z < 2.5 (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) (https://www.stsci.edu/jwst-program-info/download/jwst/pdf/3543/). Finally, characterizing the hot phase via high-resolution X-ray imaging routinely consumes enormous single-target allocations, with deep Chandra observations of individual galaxy groups ranging from 105 ks to 650 ks (UNCERTAINTY_NOT_QUOTED_BY_SOURCE) (https://uva.theopenscholar.com/files/craig-sarazin/files/stz229.pdf) to successfully map thermodynamic parameters and confirm the presence of AGN-inflated cavities.
