# Galaxy Evolution — PGR Wiki Enrichment (Reviews 09–11)

**Method note.** This file is *supplementary* content produced by Packet-Gated Paper-to-wiki Reconciliation (PGR). It extends — it does not replace — the existing Method-1 galaxy-evolution wiki. Every claim below is packet-backed: a specific finding traced to a specific paper (or set of papers) carrying the trust judgment recorded in three newly DR-verified review packets — Förster Schreiber & Wuyts (2020) on the structure and kinematics of star-forming galaxies at cosmic noon; Conroy (2013) on stellar population synthesis; and Wechsler & Tinker (2018) on the galaxy–halo connection. Claims that multiple independent packets or papers support are marked **established**; claims where the packets record genuine, unresolved disagreement are recorded as **debates**, with no winner chosen. Where new material belongs in a section the existing wiki already carries, the section title is reused so the claims merge rather than fork; those additions are flagged inline. Temporal and methodological boundaries recorded in the packets (IMF choice, tracer, aperture, redshift, calibration, resolution, halo-mass definition) are retained as caveats. The Förster Schreiber & Wuyts packet is bounded to non-AGN physics at z~1–3; the Conroy packet to work through 2013; the Wechsler & Tinker packet to the statistical galaxy–halo connection through 2018.

## New section — Structural and Kinematic Evolution at Cosmic Noon

The existing wiki treats the *global* main sequence, gas content, and cosmic star-formation history, but its structural coverage was thin — essentially the size–mass relation and a mergers-versus-instabilities note. The Förster Schreiber & Wuyts (2020) packet lets that coverage be strengthened with resolved, spatially and kinematically detailed observations of massive star-forming galaxies at the peak of cosmic assembly.

A central established result is that a large fraction of resolved, massive star-forming galaxies at z~1–3 are **rotation-dominated but dynamically hot, turbulent disks** in emission-line integral-field spectroscopy (Förster Schreiber & Wuyts 2020; Wisnioski 2015; Genzel 2008; Förster Schreiber 2009). These disks are not the settled thin disks of the local Universe: their **intrinsic ionized-gas velocity dispersions are elevated at high redshift and decline toward low redshift**, with sigma_0 ~ 50 km/s at z~2.3 falling to ~25 km/s at z~0.9 in mass-selected samples (Förster Schreiber & Wuyts 2020; Wisnioski 2015; Übler 2019). They are also structurally more compact: **at fixed stellar mass, star-forming galaxies are smaller at higher redshift**, with rest-optical effective radius scaling roughly as (1+z)^-0.75 for late types, and more steeply (~(1+z)^-1.48) for quiescent galaxies, over 0 < z < 3 (Förster Schreiber & Wuyts 2020; van der Wel 2014). This quantitative, population-specific scaling refines the qualitative "different size–mass evolution" statement already in the wiki's structural section.

Two further established features characterize these systems internally. **Kiloparsec-scale star-forming clumps** with inferred masses of order 10^7–10^9 solar masses are common in resolved images and line maps of gas-rich high-redshift disks (Förster Schreiber & Wuyts 2020; Genzel 2011; Förster Schreiber 2011; Elmegreen 2009) — with the strong caveat that PSF blending and SED assumptions can inflate the masses. And in dynamical models the **inner regions (within roughly one effective radius) are baryon dominated**, with low inferred central dark-matter fractions (median f_DM ~ 8% for a Chabrier IMF in some samples; Förster Schreiber & Wuyts 2020; Genzel 2017; Wuyts 2016; Price 2016). Broad ionized-gas emission components also reveal **galaxy-scale outflows in normal, non-AGN-selected star-forming samples** at cosmic noon, common in incidence even where the mass flux is poorly constrained (Förster Schreiber & Wuyts 2020; Förster Schreiber 2019; Newman 2012). Finally, **bulge growth, dense central cores, and compact star-forming systems are observationally linked to structural pathways toward quiescence** (Förster Schreiber & Wuyts 2020; Lang 2014; Barro 2013; Barro 2017), although the progenitor matching is statistical rather than object-by-object. Taken together, these scaling relations, gas reservoirs, disks, and outflows are read by the packet as consistent with a regulated, equilibrium-growth picture of accretion, star formation, feedback, and recycling — a framework-level synthesis rather than a validation of any single physical implementation.

The packet is equally explicit about what remains unresolved, and these are carried as debates. Whether **most massive main-sequence galaxies are ordered rotating disks or whether disturbed systems and mergers form a comparably important fraction** cannot be pinned to a universal number, because resolution, depth, kinematic classification, and pair timescales all shift the census (Förster Schreiber & Wuyts 2020; Wisnioski 2015; Genzel 2008). The **origin of the elevated disk turbulence** is contested between gravitational instability plus accretion and stellar-feedback energy and momentum injection (Förster Schreiber & Wuyts 2020; Übler 2019; Burkert 2010). The **longevity and migration of giant clumps** — long-lived bound structures that migrate inward to build bulges, versus short-lived features disrupted by feedback — is unsettled and depends on resolution and feedback prescriptions (Förster Schreiber & Wuyts 2020; Genzel 2011; Bournaud 2014). The **reality of the low inner dark-matter fractions** is itself debated: genuine baryon dominance, or an artifact of pressure support, IMF and gas-mass assumptions, halo contraction or cores, and selection, given that rotation curves rarely extend far beyond the luminous disk (Förster Schreiber & Wuyts 2020; Genzel 2017; Genzel 2020; Lang 2017). And whether galaxies quench by **central compaction before quenching or by gradual inside-out growth** cannot be decided from cross-sectional populations alone (Förster Schreiber & Wuyts 2020; Barro 2013; Wuyts 2012).

## Additions to existing section — The Star-Forming Main Sequence and Quenching

Two packet-backed claims extend the existing main-sequence section rather than starting a new one. First, an established result: **main-sequence star formation dominates the cosmic-noon star-formation-rate budget**, with strong starburst outliers contributing only a minority of the total (Förster Schreiber & Wuyts 2020; Rodighiero 2011). Second, a debate the existing section did not carry: the **slope and normalization of the main sequence** — near-linear versus bent with a high-mass flattening, and how both evolve with redshift — remain unsettled because SFR indicators, dust corrections, IMF, selection, stacking, and bulge contamination all move the fits (Förster Schreiber & Wuyts 2020; Speagle 2014; Whitaker 2014; Schreiber 2015).

## Addition to existing section — Gas Content, Depletion, and Accretion

The existing gas section records that molecular-gas fractions rise toward cosmic noon while depletion time evolves weakly. The Förster Schreiber & Wuyts packet sharpens the cosmic-noon endpoint: **massive main-sequence galaxies at z~1–3 host large molecular reservoirs with depletion times of order 0.7–1.0 Gyr**, shorter than local disks, subject to CO excitation and alpha_CO or dust-to-gas calibration systematics (Förster Schreiber & Wuyts 2020; Tacconi 2013; Tacconi 2018).

## New section — Stellar Population Synthesis and Stellar-Mass Systematics

Nearly every quantity in the existing wiki — stellar masses, SFRs, ages, the main sequence, the mass–metallicity relation — is *inferred* from galaxy light through stellar population synthesis (SPS). The Conroy (2013) packet supplies the methodological foundations and, crucially, the systematic-uncertainty envelope that the rest of the wiki implicitly assumes. This is largely new territory for the wiki.

The architecture is the starting point: **an SPS prediction combines an IMF, stellar-evolution isochrones, and stellar spectra into simple stellar populations, then convolves those with a star-formation history, enrichment, and dust to form composite populations**, so that every inferred quantity inherits the adopted ingredients and priors (Conroy 2013; Bruzual & Charlot 2003; Conroy 2009). Within that architecture, **stellar mass is among the more robust SED-derived properties**, but it remains a model-dependent mass-to-light scaling rather than a direct observation (Conroy 2013; Bell 2003; Kauffmann 2003). Several specific systematics are established. The **low-mass normalization of the IMF strongly controls mass-to-light ratios while contributing little integrated light**, so roughly 60–80% of stellar mass can hide in low-mass stars nearly invisible in the SED (Conroy 2013; Kroupa 2001; Chabrier 2003). Old populations suffer a **strong age–metallicity degeneracy**, in which an age change of about a factor of three mimics a metallicity change of a factor of two (the Worthey 3/2 rule), broken only partially by high-S/N full spectra (Conroy 2013; Worthey 1994; Thomas 2003). The **thermally pulsing AGB phase drives large near-infrared differences between SPS codes for intermediate-age (~0.1–2 Gyr) populations**, while optical color-based masses are comparatively insensitive to the TP-AGB prescription (Conroy 2013; Maraston 2005; Marigo 2008; Bruzual & Charlot 2003). **Extinction and attenuation laws are neither interchangeable nor universal**, because geometry and stellar age alter the effective attenuation across Milky-Way sightlines, starbursts, and normal galaxies (Conroy 2013; Cardelli 1989; Calzetti 2001; Charlot & Fall 2000). And **UV-continuum, recombination-line, and infrared SFR indicators average star formation over different timescales and dust sensitivities** — UV over roughly the last ~100 Myr, Halpha over the last <~10 Myr under a steady-state IMF assumption (Conroy 2013; Kennicutt 1998; Salim 2007).

The packet records four live debates that bound how far these inferences can be trusted. The **strength of the TP-AGB contribution** at intermediate ages is contested — TP-AGB-heavy versus lighter near-IR prescriptions — because cluster calibrators, stochasticity, metallicity, SFH, and circumstellar dust permit competing fits (Conroy 2013; Maraston 2005; Bruzual & Charlot 2003; Kriek 2010). **IMF universality** is unresolved: a Milky-Way-like universal IMF versus systematic variation with velocity dispersion or abundance, with gravity-sensitive spectra, dynamics, and lensing of massive early-type galaxies pointing to heavier normalizations but remaining degenerate with abundance responses and dark matter (Conroy 2013; van Dokkum & Conroy 2012; Cappellari 2012; Treu 2010). **Flexible versus parametric star-formation histories** trade bias against prior dependence and time-bin covariance (Conroy 2013; Ocvirk 2006; Cid Fernandes 2005). And the **absolute accuracy of stellar masses** — roughly factor-of-two versus larger SPS/IMF/SFH systematics — is debated, because formal fit errors omit model mismatch and unseen old or low-mass populations, and extreme SFH choices can shift recovered mass by up to ~0.6 dex (Conroy 2013; Bell 2003; Kauffmann 2003). This IMF-and-SED-systematics theme also underlies the mass and SFR caveats attached to the cosmic-noon structural results above.

## New section — The Galaxy–Halo Connection

The existing wiki's galaxy-formation section carries a single galaxy–halo result: that abundance matching places peak stellar-conversion efficiency near ~10^12 solar-mass halos. The Wechsler & Tinker (2018) packet turns that one line into a developed topic, connecting the baryonic galaxy-evolution physics elsewhere in the wiki to the dark-matter halos that host it.

The backbone is the **stellar-to-halo mass relation (SHMR) for central galaxies, which is strongly non-linear**, with maximum integrated galaxy-formation efficiency near a Milky-Way-scale halo of about 10^12 solar masses (Wechsler & Tinker 2018; Behroozi 2013; Moster 2013; Guo 2010). That peak is quantitatively bounded: **the integrated efficiency M*/(f_b M_h) stays below about 20% at z~0** (~0.15–0.20 near the pivot; Wechsler & Tinker 2018; Behroozi 2013; Moster 2013; Moster 2010). Around it, the relation is tight and structured. **Intrinsic scatter in central stellar mass at fixed halo mass above the pivot is small**, generally below ~0.2 dex and usually modeled as lognormal after measurement errors are removed (Wechsler & Tinker 2018; Leauthaud 2012; Behroozi 2010; Zu & Mandelbaum 2016). **Below the pivot, central stellar mass falls steeply** with decreasing halo mass, and the slope and scatter grow increasingly uncertain toward dwarfs (Wechsler & Tinker 2018; Behroozi 2013; Moster 2013); **above it, at group and cluster masses, central stellar mass grows much more slowly than halo mass**, roughly as M_h^(1/3), once BCG photometry and intracluster light are handled (Wechsler & Tinker 2018; Guo 2010; Zu & Mandelbaum 2016).

The statistical machinery behind these results is itself established. **HOD and CLF models require separate central and satellite occupation distributions** to reproduce galaxy abundance and scale-dependent clustering, with the one-halo/two-halo transition producing measurable departures from a single clustering power law (Wechsler & Tinker 2018; Yang 2003; Peacock & Smith 2000; Zehavi 2011). **Abundance matching reproduces abundance and clustering by rank-ordering galaxies against halos and subhalos** using a chosen proxy with scatter, under assumptions about monotonicity, proxy choice, orphans, disruption, and stellar-mass systematics (Wechsler & Tinker 2018; Vale & Ostriker 2004; Conroy 2006; Reddick 2013). At fixed halo mass, **halo clustering depends on formation history and concentration in collisionless simulations — halo assembly bias** (Wechsler & Tinker 2018; Gao 2005; Wechsler 2002; Wechsler 2006). And connecting to the wiki's quenching material, **satellite galaxies' star formation and color differ from comparable centrals**, with reconstructed infall histories implying a delayed then relatively rapid quenching (delay ~2–4 Gyr, e-folding <~0.8 Gyr) for local massive satellites (Wechsler & Tinker 2018; Wetzel 2013; Wetzel 2012).

The packet carries four debates. **Galaxy assembly bias** — whether mass-only halo occupation suffices or galaxy properties must be coupled to halo formation history or concentration — is unresolved, since halo masses and secondary properties are noisy and correlated and environment can mimic the signal (Wechsler & Tinker 2018; Gao 2005; Hearin & Watson 2013; Zentner 2014). The **origin of SHMR scatter** — merger and accretion history, concentration, spin, baryonic stochasticity, or measurement error — is contested, because inferred intrinsic scatter depends on deconvolution and cross-correlated observables (Wechsler & Tinker 2018; Leauthaud 2012; Behroozi 2010; Reddick 2013). The **optimal abundance-matching proxy** — present Vmax or mass, peak values, infall values, or concentration-sensitive compounds — is unsettled because satellite stripping, disruption, resolution, and orphans change small-scale clustering (Wechsler & Tinker 2018; Reddick 2013; Vale & Ostriker 2004; Lehmann 2017). And the **physical mechanism of satellite quenching** — starvation/strangulation, ram pressure, tidal effects, and preprocessing — remains open, since demographics constrain delay and fade times better than any single mechanism (Wechsler & Tinker 2018; Wetzel 2013). The packet is emphatic on a boundary the wiki should respect: HOD, SHAM, weak lensing, and group catalogs each recover halo masses only conditional on definitions, selection, and model assumptions, and none delivers a method-independent halo truth.
