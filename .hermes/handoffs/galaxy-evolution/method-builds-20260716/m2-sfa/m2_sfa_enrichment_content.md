# Galaxy Evolution — SFA Enrichment (Review Packets 09, 10, 11)

**Method note.** This file supplements the existing Source-First Adjudication (SFA) wiki with three newly DR-verified review packets: Förster Schreiber & Wuyts (2020, structure and kinematics at cosmic noon), Conroy (2013, stellar population synthesis), and Wechsler & Tinker (2018, the galaxy–halo connection). As in the base wiki, each point was first extracted from its primary sources, then adjudicated by corroboration strength: statements carried by several mutually independent measurements are marked **established** with the sources named and counted; contested points, where sources measuring the same quantity disagree, are recorded as **debate** with competing sources foregrounded. Packet 09 chiefly *strengthens* the structural/kinematic material already in the base wiki and adds the inner dark-matter budget; packets 10 and 11 open two largely new topics. Cross-links to existing sections are flagged explicitly. Everything below is grounded in the three packets; no outside facts are introduced.

## Structural and Kinematic Evolution at Cosmic Noon

*Relation to the base wiki: the existing "Structural Evolution and Feedback" section already establishes that high-z disks are turbulent, clumpy, rotationally supported (σ0 ≈ 40–80 km/s, v_rot/σ0 ≈ 2–6) with giant clumps that can migrate to bulges, and that the size–mass relation evolves since z ≈ 3. Those claims are not repeated here. This section adds the redshift decline of dispersion, quantified size scaling, the inner dark-matter budget, non-AGN outflow demographics, and several debates the base wiki did not foreground. It also corroborates the base "Main Sequence, Gas Content" section through shared Tacconi/Speagle/Whitaker sources.*

**Established — the cosmic-noon frame.** Cosmic noon (z ≈ 2, lookback ≈ 10 Gyr) is the peak of cosmic star formation and the main assembly epoch of today's massive galaxies; Förster Schreiber & Wuyts (2020) synthesize scaling relations, gas reservoirs, resolved kinematics, and outflows into a regulated equilibrium-growth picture, echoing the cold-stream/unstable-disk simulations of Dekel et al. (2009) and Ceverino et al. (2010). This independently corroborates the base wiki's Cosmic Star-Formation History section (which peaks the SFRD near z ≈ 1.9 from Madau & Dickinson 2014).

**Established — dispersion declines with cosmic time (new dimension).** Intrinsic ionized-gas velocity dispersion in rotation-dominated massive disks is elevated at high z and declines toward low z: KMOS3D forward-modeled Hα gives σ0 ≈ 50 km/s at z ≈ 2.3 falling to ≈ 25 km/s at z ≈ 0.9 (Wisnioski et al. 2015; Übler et al. 2019), with disks sitting near marginal Toomre stability Q ≈ 1 (Genzel et al. 2008; Burkert et al. 2010; Swinbank et al. 2017). The base wiki gave only the static σ0 range; the *decline* is the new, multi-survey-corroborated result.

**Established — quantified size evolution (sharpens an existing claim).** At fixed stellar mass, star-forming galaxies are smaller at higher z, with rest-optical R_eff ∝ (1+z)^-0.75 for late types versus ≈ (1+z)^-1.48 for quiescent galaxies over 0 < z < 3 (van der Wel et al. 2014). This single-source measurement quantifies the qualitative size–mass evolution already stated in the base wiki.

**Established — noon gas reservoirs (corroborates base gas-content claim).** Massive main-sequence galaxies at z ≈ 1–3 hold large molecular reservoirs with depletion times of only ≈ 0.7–1.0 Gyr, much shorter than local disks (Tacconi et al. 2010, 2013, 2018; Daddi et al. 2010). These are the same PHIBSS/Tacconi sources the base wiki uses for the ΔMS depletion-time scaling, so this is strong cross-review corroboration with an added noon-specific value.

**Established — inner disks are baryon-dominated (new topic).** Within ~1 R_eff, dynamical models of many massive cosmic-noon disks are baryon-dominated, with low inferred central dark-matter fractions (median f_DM(<R_eff) ≈ 8% for a Chabrier IMF, MOSDEF z ≈ 2) and falling outer rotation curves. Five independent efforts back this: Genzel et al. (2017), Lang et al. (2017), Wuyts et al. (2016), Price et al. (2016), and Burkert et al. (2016). This dark-matter budget is absent from the base wiki and is a principal new contribution of packet 09.

**Established — outflows in normal SFGs (new demographic).** Broad ionized-gas components reveal galaxy-scale outflows in normal, non-AGN star-forming galaxies across z = 0.6–2.7 (Förster Schreiber et al. 2019, KMOS3D; Newman et al. 2012, SINS/zC-SINF). The base wiki treats outflows only in the feedback-model and CGM contexts; here the point is the observed *incidence* across the main-sequence population. Mass-loading remains low-to-moderate confidence.

**Established — compaction linked to quenching.** Bulge growth, dense central cores, and compact star-forming systems are observationally tied to pathways toward quiescence, connecting compact SFGs to compact quiescent descendants (Lang et al. 2014; Barro et al. 2013, 2017). The base wiki notes compact quiescent galaxies exist at z ≈ 2 (Barro 2013); the *correlation with bulge growth/compaction* is the added element.

**Debate — main-sequence slope and normalization.** Whether the main sequence is near-linear or bends/flattens at the high-mass end, and how its slope and normalization evolve, is unresolved: SFR indicators, dust, IMF, selection, stacking, and bulge contamination move the fits (Speagle et al. 2014; Schreiber et al. 2015; Whitaker et al. 2014; Daddi et al. 2007). The base wiki states the main sequence as established with ~0.3 dex scatter; packet 09 foregrounds the residual slope debate over the same Speagle/Whitaker/Daddi sources.

**Debate — disks versus mergers.** Most massive main-sequence SFGs show ordered rotation, yet a significant disturbed/merging fraction persists; resolution, depth, kinematic classification, pair timescales, and selection prevent a universal fraction (Wisnioski et al. 2015, 2019; Genzel et al. 2006, 2008).

**Debate — giant-clump longevity and migration.** Long-lived bound clumps that migrate inward to build bulges versus short-lived clumps disrupted by feedback: inferred clump ages, masses, and gradients are resolution/model-dependent, and simulations differ in feedback strength (Genzel et al. 2011; Bournaud et al. 2007, 2014; Förster Schreiber et al. 2018). The base wiki asserts clump→bulge migration as established; packet 09 records the longevity question as genuinely open.

**Debate — origin of low inner dark-matter fractions.** Genuine baryon dominance versus an artifact of pressure support, IMF/gas-mass assumptions, halo contraction versus cored profiles, and selection; rotation curves rarely extend far beyond the luminous disk and mass components are degenerate (Genzel et al. 2017, 2020; Lang et al. 2017; Price et al. 2016; Burkert et al. 2016). Note Genzel et al. (2020) reads the same data as evidence for cored halos. Low central dark matter is *not* absence of a halo.

**Debate — compaction versus inside-out growth.** Central compaction before quenching versus gradual inside-out disk growth with bulge buildup; cross-sectional populations do not uniquely reconstruct tracks, and resolved SEDs are dust/model-sensitive (Barro et al. 2013, 2017; Lang et al. 2014; Wuyts et al. 2012).

*(The turbulence-driver debate — feedback versus gravitational instability/accretion — is already a debate in the base wiki's structural section, backed by Krumholz & Burkert and Dekel et al.; packet 09's D03 corroborates it and is not restated as a new claim.)*

## Stellar Population Synthesis and Stellar-Mass Systematics

*Relation to the base wiki: this is a largely new topic. The base wiki uses IMF return fractions/yields (Salpeter, Chabrier) in its SFH section and mentions SED/dust systematics in passing, and gives the Gallazzi stellar mass–metallicity relation. Conroy (2013) supplies the underlying inference machinery and its systematics — the axis on which nearly every stellar mass, age, SFR, and metallicity in the rest of the wiki is measured.*

**Established — SPS architecture.** An SPS prediction combines an IMF, stellar-evolution isochrones, and stellar spectra into simple stellar populations, then convolves them with a star-formation history, enrichment, and dust for composite populations; every inferred quantity inherits the adopted ingredients and priors (Bruzual & Charlot 2003; Maraston 2005; Conroy, Gunn & White 2009; foundational framing in Tinsley 1980).

**Established — stellar mass is robust but model-dependent.** Stellar mass is among the more robust SED-derived properties, yet it is a model-dependent mass-to-light scaling, not a direct observation, and is sensitive to the IMF and to hidden old populations (Bell et al. 2003; Kauffmann et al. 2003; Zibetti et al. 2009).

**Established — the SFH-outshining systematic.** Normal-SFH statistical mass errors are ~0.3 dex, but SFH flexibility can move mass, age, and past SFR by up to ~0.6 dex because young stars outshine older mass (Bell et al. 2003; Kauffmann et al. 2003; Ocvirk et al. 2006; Tojeiro et al. 2007; Cid Fernandes et al. 2005). **This directly feeds two other wiki sections:** the stellar-mass axis of the cosmic-noon main sequence and inner dark-matter fractions (packet 09) and the scatter of the stellar-to-halo mass relation (packet 11).

**Established — age–metallicity degeneracy.** Old-population optical colors and indices suffer a strong age–metallicity degeneracy — the Worthey 3/2 rule (an age factor ~3 mimics a metallicity factor ~2) — worst for ages > 5 Gyr, reduced but not erased by full spectra at S/N ≳ 100/Å (Worthey 1994; Thomas, Maraston & Bender 2003; Schiavon 2007).

**Established — TP-AGB drives the near-IR.** TP-AGB treatment drives large near-IR SPS differences for intermediate-age (~0.1–2 Gyr) populations, while optical color M/L estimates are less TP-AGB-sensitive than near-IR ones (Maraston 2005; Marigo et al. 2008; Bruzual & Charlot 2003; Kriek et al. 2010; Zibetti et al. 2009).

**Established — the IMF sets the mass scale.** Low-mass IMF normalization strongly controls M/L while contributing little light; ~60–80% of an old population's stellar mass can be in low-mass stars that add little optical/NIR luminosity (Salpeter 1955; Kroupa 2001; Chabrier 2003).

**Established — heavier IMF in massive early-types.** By 2013 gravity-sensitive spectra, dynamics, and lensing gave independent evidence that massive early-type galaxies may have heavier IMF normalizations than the Milky Way, though abundance/dark-matter/dynamical degeneracies remain (van Dokkum & Conroy 2010, 2012; Cappellari et al. 2012; Treu et al. 2010) — three methodologically independent lines of evidence.

**Established — attenuation is not universal; energy balance links UV to IR.** Extinction and attenuation laws are not interchangeable because geometry and stellar age alter effective attenuation; panchromatic energy-balance models nonetheless tie attenuated UV-optical starlight to IR dust emission given UV-to-IR coverage (Cardelli, Clayton & Mathis 1989; Calzetti 2001; Charlot & Fall 2000; da Cunha, Charlot & Elbaz 2008; Draine & Li 2007; Meurer, Heckman & Calzetti 1999).

**Established — nebular contamination and library limits.** Nebular lines/continuum can materially contaminate broadband SEDs of high-equivalent-width galaxies and bias stellar parameters if omitted (strongest for high-sSFR, low-Z, high-z systems); empirical and theoretical stellar libraries carry complementary parameter/abundance-coverage limits (Charlot & Longhetti 2001; Pickles 1998; Le Borgne et al. 2003; Sánchez-Blázquez et al. 2006; Coelho et al. 2005).

**Debate — TP-AGB strength.** TP-AGB-heavy versus lighter near-IR contributions at intermediate ages; cluster calibrators, stochasticity, metallicity, SFH, and dust permit competing fits (Maraston 2005 vs Bruzual & Charlot 2003; Marigo et al. 2008; Kriek et al. 2010).

**Debate — IMF universality (cross-cutting).** Universal MW-like IMF versus systematic variation with velocity dispersion/abundance; features, dynamics, lensing, abundances, remnants, and dark matter are degenerate (Chabrier 2003; Kroupa 2001; van Dokkum & Conroy 2012; Cappellari et al. 2012; Treu et al. 2010). Because the IMF sets absolute stellar masses, this debate propagates into the main-sequence normalization (packet 09) and the SHMR normalization and scatter (packet 11) — a single systematic threading three sections.

**Debate — flexible versus parametric SFHs.** Simple exponential/rising histories versus nonparametric burst-capable reconstructions: flexibility reduces bias but raises prior dependence and time-bin covariance (Ocvirk et al. 2006; Tojeiro et al. 2007; Cid Fernandes et al. 2005).

**Debate — absolute stellar-mass accuracy.** ~Factor-two practical estimates versus larger SPS/IMF/SFH systematics, since formal errors omit model mismatch and unseen low-mass/old populations (Bell et al. 2003; Kauffmann et al. 2003; Zibetti et al. 2009).

**Debate — SFR calibration and timescale.** UV, Hα, IR, and SED-fit SFRs need not agree for bursty/dusty populations; each tracer weights different ages (UV ~100 Myr, Hα ≲ 10 Myr) and dust-heating sources (Kennicutt 1998; Brinchmann et al. 2004; Salim et al. 2007; Meurer, Heckman & Calzetti 1999). This underpins the SFR indicators used throughout the base wiki's SFH and main-sequence sections.

## The Galaxy–Halo Connection

*Relation to the base wiki: largely new. The base wiki has a single claim that baryon-conversion efficiency peaks near M_h ≈ 10^12 M_sun (~20%), citing Behroozi and Moster — exactly the sources Wechsler & Tinker (2018) synthesize. This section deepens that into the full statistical galaxy–halo framework (SHMR shape and scatter, HOD/CLF, abundance matching, clustering/lensing, assembly bias, satellite quenching). The satellite-quenching material cross-links to the base wiki's quenching-mechanism debate.*

**Established — the SHMR pivot and efficiency ceiling (deepens base claim).** The central SHMR is strongly non-linear, peaking near a Milky-Way-scale halo (pivot M_h ≈ 10^12 M_sun) where integrated efficiency M*/(f_b M_h) stays below ~20% (≈ 0.15–0.20) at z ≈ 0 (Behroozi, Wechsler & Conroy 2013; Moster, Naab & White 2013; Moster et al. 2010; Guo et al. 2010). This is the same efficiency-peak result the base wiki carries, now with four corroborating abundance-matching analyses and the explicit non-linear shape.

**Established — small scatter above the pivot.** Intrinsic scatter in central stellar mass at fixed halo mass above the pivot is small, ≲ 0.2 dex (~0.16–0.20), usually lognormal and inferred jointly from abundance + clustering + lensing after removing measurement error (Leauthaud et al. 2012; Behroozi, Conroy & Wechsler 2010; Zu & Mandelbaum 2016). The "measurement error" to be removed is precisely the SPS stellar-mass systematic of packet 10.

**Established — steep low-mass decline.** Below the pivot, M* falls steeply with halo mass and the slope/scatter grow uncertain toward dwarfs, dominated by incompleteness, reionization, stellar-mass estimates, stripping, and central/satellite definitions (Behroozi, Wechsler & Conroy 2013; Moster, Naab & White 2013; Bullock & Boylan-Kolchin 2017).

**Established — slow central growth in groups/clusters.** At group/cluster masses central stellar mass grows much more slowly than halo mass (~M*_cen ∝ M_h^1/3), once BCG photometry, intracluster light, and central-versus-total mass are separated (Guo et al. 2010; Leauthaud et al. 2012; Zu & Mandelbaum 2016).

**Established — HOD/CLF need centrals and satellites.** HOD and CLF models require separate central and satellite occupation distributions to reproduce abundance and scale-dependent clustering, the satellite term following a near-power-law set by the subhalo mass function (Yang, Mo & van den Bosch 2003; Peacock & Smith 2000; Zehavi et al. 2011; Kravtsov et al. 2004; Scoccimarro et al. 2001).

**Established — abundance matching works empirically.** Subhalo abundance matching reproduces abundance and clustering by rank-ordering galaxies and halos/subhalos via a chosen proxy with scatter, underpinned by N-body-calibrated halo mass functions and bias relations (Tinker et al. 2008, 2010; Sheth & Tormen 1999) (Vale & Ostriker 2004; Conroy, Wechsler & Kravtsov 2006; Reddick et al. 2013; Behroozi, Conroy & Wechsler 2010; Lehmann et al. 2017).

**Established — clustering and lensing constrain occupation.** The one-halo/two-halo transition produces measurable departures from a single power law in clustering, and galaxy–galaxy lensing plus clustering jointly constrain mean halo mass, satellite fraction, and occupation (Zehavi et al. 2004, 2011; Scoccimarro et al. 2001; Leauthaud et al. 2012; Zu & Mandelbaum 2016).

**Established — halo assembly bias (in simulations).** Dark-matter halo clustering depends on formation history and concentration at fixed halo mass, with amplitude/sign depending on halo mass, secondary property, environment, and halo finder (Gao, Springel & White 2005; Wechsler et al. 2002, 2006).

**Established — satellite quenching demographics (cross-links base quenching debate).** Satellites differ systematically in SF and color from comparable centrals, and infall histories imply delayed-then-rapid quenching locally (delay ~2–4 Gyr, then e-folding ≲ 0.8 Gyr for M* > 5×10^9 M_sun) (Wetzel, Tinker & Conroy 2012, 2013). This adds an environmental axis to the base wiki's quenching-mechanism debate, which considered strangulation/ejection/efficiency/morphology for centrals.

**Debate — galaxy assembly bias.** Mass-only occupation versus explicit coupling of galaxy properties to halo formation history/concentration; halo masses and secondary properties are noisy and correlated, and environment can mimic the signal (Gao, Springel & White 2005; Wechsler et al. 2006; Hearin & Watson 2013; Zentner, Hearin & van den Bosch 2014; Lehmann et al. 2017).

**Debate — origin of SHMR scatter.** Merger/accretion history, concentration, spin, baryonic stochasticity, and residual measurement error as dominant contributors; inferred intrinsic scatter depends on deconvolution and cross-correlated observables — including the SPS stellar-mass systematics of packet 10 (Leauthaud et al. 2012; Wechsler et al. 2002; Behroozi, Conroy & Wechsler 2010; Rodríguez-Puebla et al. 2016).

**Debate — optimal abundance-matching proxy.** Present Vmax/M versus peak V/M versus infall values versus concentration-sensitive proxies; satellite stripping/disruption, resolution, and orphan galaxies change small-scale clustering (Reddick et al. 2013; Vale & Ostriker 2004; Lehmann et al. 2017).

**Debate — satellite quenching mechanism.** Starvation/strangulation, ram pressure, tidal effects, and preprocessing with different timing; demographics constrain delay/fade times better than they isolate a unique mechanism (Wetzel, Tinker & Conroy 2012, 2013).

**Debate — method-dependence of halo mass.** Clustering/HOD, abundance, groups, weak lensing, satellite dynamics, X-ray, and SZ carry different degeneracies, and dark-matter-only halo statistics may need baryonic corrections (contraction/expansion/redistribution) at survey precision, so no method recovers a model-free halo mass (Zehavi et al. 2011; Leauthaud et al. 2012; Zu & Mandelbaum 2016; Tinker et al. 2012; Heitmann et al. 2010).

## Cross-source adjudication summary

- **Strongest corroboration (4–5 independent sources per claim):** the SHMR pivot/efficiency (packet 11, 4 abundance-matching analyses, and matching the base wiki's own efficiency claim); inner baryon dominance at cosmic noon (packet 09, 5 dynamical studies); the HOD central+satellite structure and the clustering/lensing constraints (packet 11).
- **Cross-review corroboration with the existing wiki:** packet 09's noon gas depletion reuses the base wiki's Tacconi sources; packet 09's main-sequence debate reuses Speagle/Whitaker/Daddi; packet 11's efficiency peak reuses Behroozi/Moster. These are independent reviews landing on the same primary literature — high-confidence agreement, not new assertion.
- **The IMF as a spine through all three packets and the base wiki:** Conroy (2013) shows the IMF sets absolute stellar masses (packet 10); those masses set the main-sequence normalization and inner dark-matter fractions (packet 09) and the SHMR normalization and scatter (packet 11); the base wiki already uses the IMF to set the SFRD normalization and yields. The IMF-universality debate is therefore recorded once (packet 10) and referenced from the others rather than duplicated.
- **Genuine conflicts left open (not resolved by fiat):** inner dark-matter fraction (baryon dominance vs cored halos vs modeling artifact, with Genzel et al. 2017 and 2020 reading overlapping data differently); clump longevity; compaction vs inside-out growth; TP-AGB strength; IMF universality; abundance-matching proxy choice; satellite-quenching mechanism; and method-dependence of halo mass.
