# Galaxy Evolution — A Source-First Adjudicated Wiki (Method 2: SFA)

**Method note.** This wiki is built by Source-First Adjudication (SFA). Every point below was first extracted from individual primary sources on their own terms, then adjudicated across sources: statements are organized by how strongly they are *corroborated*. A claim backed by several mutually independent measurements is marked **established** and the corroborating sources are named and counted; where sources measure the same thing and disagree, or where a single source stands alone on a contested point, the item is recorded as a **debate** with the competing sources foregrounded. Corroboration strength — not any single review's framing — is the organizing principle. All content is grounded in the DR-verified review packets (Kennicutt & Evans 2012; Madau & Dickinson 2014; Somerville & Davé 2015; Naab & Ostriker 2017; Maiolino & Mannucci 2019; Péroux & Howk 2020; Tumlinson, Peeples & Werk 2017; Tacconi, Genzel & Sternberg 2020; and the area1–3 chemical/gas packets); no outside facts are introduced.

## Star-Formation Laws and Efficiency

**Strongly corroborated.** On galaxy-integrated scales, star-formation-rate surface density follows a super-linear power law of total (HI+H2) gas surface density with index N ≈ 1.4, spanning normal disks and starbursts. This is the benchmark Schmidt–Kennicutt law, rooted in Schmidt's (1959) original parameterization and measured by Kennicutt (1998); the same slope reappears in the reviews of Kennicutt & Evans (2012) and Naab & Ostriker (2017) and in the high-z compilations (Genzel et al. 2010) — a claim carried independently by four+ sources.

**Strongly corroborated (4 sources).** At sub-kiloparsec resolution the star-formation rate correlates tightly with molecular gas (CO/H2) and is essentially uncorrelated with atomic HI. This decoupling was first mapped by Bigiel et al. (2008) and Leroy et al. (2008) and confirmed into atomic-dominated regimes by Schruba et al. (2011) and by IFU mapping (Blanc et al. 2009).

**Corroborated (3+ sources).** Atomic-hydrogen surface density saturates near ~9–10 M_sun pc^-2, and star-formation efficiency is severely suppressed in the HI-dominated outer disks and low-surface-brightness systems above that transition. Established by Bigiel et al. (2008), Leroy et al. (2008), Bigiel et al. (2010), Wyder et al. (2009) and Schruba et al. (2011).

**Corroborated.** The molecular-gas depletion time in normal local disks is of order 1–2 Gyr (≈2.3 Gyr under a standard conversion factor), i.e. roughly constant within the inner molecular disk. Measured by Leroy et al. (2008), Bigiel et al. (2011) and reviewed by Kennicutt & Evans (2012).

**Corroborated (galaxies → clouds).** Star-formation rate scales approximately linearly with dense-gas (HCN) luminosity, a proportionality that holds from whole galaxies down to individual Milky Way clumps. Established by Gao & Solomon (2004), Wu et al. (2005) and Lada et al. (2010, 2012).

**Corroborated.** Molecular clouds convert gas to stars inefficiently — of order 1–2% per free-fall time — so cloud depletion times (~82 Myr) vastly exceed the free-fall (~1.4 Myr) and crossing (~5.5 Myr) times. Measured by Evans et al. (2009), Lada et al. (2010) and Utomo et al. (2018), and framed theoretically by Krumholz et al. (2012).

**Corroborated.** The CO-to-H2 conversion factor (alpha_CO) is not universal: it rises steeply toward low metallicity and depends on the radiation field, diverging below 12+log(O/H) ≈ 8.4. Established by Bolatto et al. (2013), Sandstrom et al. (2013), Narayanan et al. (2012) and Accurso et al. (2017), with resolved evidence for CO-dark gas from gamma-rays and dust (Abdo et al. 2010; Pineda et al. 2010).

**Debate — one global law vs. regime-dependent laws.** Whether star formation obeys a single super-linear total-gas law or a near-linear molecular law with distinct regimes is unresolved: Kennicutt (1998) fits N ≈ 1.4 on integrated total gas, while resolved molecular measurements (Bigiel et al. 2008; Leroy et al. 2008) find a near-linear H2 relation plus a sharply suppressed HI regime. The relations differ with spatial averaging.

**Debate — disk/starburst bimodality vs. a continuous sequence.** Genzel et al. (2010) and Daddi et al. (2010) argue that mergers/starbursts occupy a separate, more efficient sequence from secular disks; continuous conversion-factor and dynamical-state variation (Tacconi et al. 2018; Saintonge et al. 2017; Krumholz et al. 2012) can instead reproduce a single sequence, and the apparent bimodality is sensitive to alpha_CO.

**Debate — is H2 causal or merely co-located?** Whether molecular hydrogen is physically necessary for star formation, or simply co-located with shielded, self-gravitating cold gas, is contested: the precursor interpretation (Bigiel et al. 2008; Leroy et al. 2008) versus a shared environmental cause via shielding/turbulence (Glover et al. 2010; Krumholz et al. 2012). A decisive test requires very low-metallicity regimes.

## Cosmic Star-Formation History

**Strongly corroborated.** The comoving cosmic star-formation-rate density rises from early times to a broad maximum near z ≈ 1.9 and declines thereafter, well described by the Madau & Dickinson (2014) analytic fit ψ(z) = 0.015 (1+z)^2.7 / [1 + ((1+z)/2.9)^5.6]. The rise-and-fall shape is corroborated across UV and IR tracers by Lilly et al. (1996), Gruppioni et al. (2013) and the Hopkins & Beacom (2006) compilation.

**Corroborated.** Roughly half of today's stellar mass had formed by z ≈ 1.3, about one quarter above z ≈ 2, and less than ~1% at z > 6. Established by integrating the fit (Madau & Dickinson 2014) against the stellar-mass-density history of Dickinson et al. (2003), Muzzin et al. (2013) and Cole et al. (2001).

**Corroborated (5+ sources).** Dust-obscured star formation is a major and near-dominant contributor around cosmic noon, so far-infrared tracers are essential. Established by Sanders et al. (2003), Gruppioni et al. (2013), Magnelli et al. (2011, 2013) and the obscured populations of Daddi et al. (2005).

**Corroborated.** The rest-frame ultraviolet luminosity functions at high redshift have steep faint-end slopes, α ≈ -2 at z ≈ 7 (α = -2.01 ± 0.21). Established by Bouwens et al. (2011, 2012), Schenker et al. (2013) and Reddy & Steidel (2009).

**Corroborated (with residual).** The stellar-mass-density history is broadly consistent with the time-integrated star-formation-rate density once mass return is included, though a residual normalization offset persists. Supported by Dickinson et al. (2003), Cole et al. (2001), Li & White (2009), with systematic caveats from Conroy et al. (2009) and Maraston (2005).

**Established (single-source measurement, uncontested).** The initial mass function materially sets the star-formation-rate normalization, the stellar-mass return fraction (R ≈ 0.27 Salpeter, 0.41 Chabrier) and the net metal yield (y ≈ 0.016 Salpeter, 0.032 Chabrier). Quantified by Madau & Dickinson (2014) using Salpeter (1955) and Chabrier (2003).

**Debate — physical high-z decline vs. incompleteness, and the reionization budget.** Whether the steep z > 6 decline is intrinsic or amplified by galaxies below the detection limit is unsettled (Bouwens et al. 2011, 2012; Schenker et al. 2013; Coe et al. 2013); relatedly, whether faint galaxies supply enough ionizing photons for reionization depends on an unknown escape fraction (Robertson et al. 2013; Haardt & Madau 2012).

**Debate — the supernova-rate cross-check.** The observed core-collapse supernova rate falls short of the rate predicted from the integrated star-formation-rate density: Horiuchi et al. (2011) flag the discrepancy against the rate census of Dahlen et al. (2004), leaving an unresolved normalization tension.

## Star-Forming Main Sequence, Gas Content, and Quenching

**Strongly corroborated.** Star-forming galaxies occupy a tight star-formation-rate–stellar-mass "main sequence" with ~0.3 dex scatter that persists from z ≈ 0 to z ≈ 4, its normalization rising with redshift. Established by Noeske et al. (2007), Daddi et al. (2007), Speagle et al. (2014), Whitaker et al. (2014) and Wuyts et al. (2011).

**Corroborated.** Galaxies are bimodally distributed into a star-forming blue cloud and a quiescent red sequence, and stellar mass has built up on the red sequence since z ≈ 1. Established by Baldry et al. (2004), Bell et al. (2004) and Muzzin et al. (2013).

**Corroborated (3+ sources).** The efficiency of converting baryons into stars peaks near a halo mass of ~10^12 M_sun (~20% for L* halos) and falls on both sides. Established by abundance-matching analyses of Behroozi et al. (2010, 2013) and Moster et al. (2013).

**Corroborated.** Molecular gas fractions were high at cosmic noon (μ_gas ≈ 0.3–0.5) and decline to the present, while the depletion time scales with the offset from the main sequence: t_dep ∝ ΔMS^-0.44 and μ_gas ∝ ΔMS^+0.53. Established by Tacconi et al. (2010, 2013, 2018), Genzel et al. (2015) and Decarli et al. (2019).

**Corroborated (revises the "constant" picture).** The galaxy-integrated molecular depletion time is *not* constant; it varies systematically with specific star-formation rate and main-sequence offset. Established by Saintonge et al. (2011, 2017) and Tacconi et al. (2018), qualifying the ~2 Gyr inner-disk value.

**Corroborated.** Sustaining the observed star-formation history requires net gas accretion, because the declining HI+H2 reservoirs cannot supply ongoing stellar-mass growth by themselves. Inferred by Walter et al. (2020), Bauermeister et al. (2010) and the regulator models of Bouché et al. (2010) and Lilly et al. (2013).

**Debate — quenching mechanism.** How galaxies quench is contested along two axes. On *what is removed*: strangulation/starvation of the gas supply (Peng, Maiolino & Cochrane 2015; Trussler et al. 2020; Looser et al. 2024) versus rapid ejective removal. On *what changes locally*: suppressed star-formation efficiency at fixed gas content (Ellison et al. 2020) versus reduced gas fraction (Saintonge et al. 2017), with morphological stabilization by a stellar bulge as a third channel (Martig et al. 2009; Walter et al. 2014).

**Debate — critical quenching halo mass.** A characteristic halo mass ~10^12 M_sun, above which stable virial shocks heat infalling gas and cold accretion is suppressed, is proposed as a quenching threshold by Birnboim & Dekel (2003), Dekel & Birnboim (2006) and Kereš et al. (2005), but whether this alone quenches massive galaxies (vs. requiring AGN preventive feedback) is debated.

**Debate — preventive massive-halo feedback.** Reproducing the bright end of the luminosity/mass function requires suppressing cooling flows in massive halos beyond what stellar feedback provides, attributed to radio-mode AGN feedback by Bower et al. (2006) and Croton et al. (2006); whether this uniquely identifies the mechanism, versus tuned recipes, remains open.

**Debate — main-sequence offset: reservoir vs. efficiency.** Whether displacement above/below the main sequence is driven mainly by gas fraction or by star-formation efficiency is unresolved: Saintonge et al. (2017) and Tacconi et al. (2018) emphasize the reservoir, while resolved kpc-scale analysis (Ellison et al. 2020) finds efficiency variations dominate.

## Mass–Metallicity Relation and the Fundamental Metallicity Relation

**Strongly corroborated.** Local star-forming galaxies follow a gas-phase mass–metallicity relation that rises with stellar mass, flattens above a turnover near ~10^10.5 M_sun, and has ~0.1 dex scatter. Established from ~53,000 SDSS galaxies by Tremonti et al. (2004), reproduced analytically by Zahid et al. (2014), and confirmed on a temperature-based scale by Andrews & Martini (2013) and Curti et al. (2020).

**Corroborated.** Absolute abundance calibrations disagree by up to ~0.7 dex, so mass–metallicity normalizations are comparable across studies only when the underlying scales are matched. Quantified by Kewley & Ellison (2008) and corroborated by Curti et al. (2017, 2020).

**Corroborated (4 sources).** At fixed stellar mass, gas-phase metallicity anticorrelates with star-formation rate, and a mass–SFR–metallicity surface (the Fundamental Metallicity Relation) reduces the residual scatter to ~0.05 dex locally. Established by Mannucci et al. (2010), Lara-López et al. (2010), Bothwell et al. (2013) and Curti et al. (2020).

**Established (distinct relation).** Mean stellar metallicity also rises with stellar mass, forming an integrated-history "stellar" mass–metallicity relation distinct from the gas-phase one. Established by Gallazzi et al. (2005).

**Corroborated.** The gas-phase mass–metallicity relation shifts to lower metallicity at higher redshift, with a measured decline dlog(O/H)/dz ≈ -0.11 ± 0.02 out to z ≈ 3.3. Established by Erb et al. (2006), Maiolino et al. (2008), Troncoso et al. (2014) and Sanders et al. (2021).

**Corroborated (JWST extension).** JWST/NIRSpec extends the relation to low-mass galaxies at z ≈ 4–10, which are less enriched than local galaxies at fixed mass (~0.9 dex lower at z ≈ 8). Established by Nakajima et al. (2023), Curti et al. (2024) and Langeroodi et al. (2023).

**Corroborated (resolved origin).** In local disks, local gas-phase metallicity correlates positively with local stellar surface mass density, and this resolved relation can reproduce aspects of the global relation and radial gradients. Established by Barrera-Ballesteros et al. (2016), with gradient support from Sánchez et al. (2014).

**Debate — is the FMR redshift-invariant?** Mannucci et al. (2010) and Sanders et al. (2021) find no evolution of the surface out to z ≈ 3.3, whereas Curti et al. (2024) measure a ~0.5 dex offset at z > 6 and Garcia et al. (2024) predict an evolving "weak" secondary SFR dependence rather than a strictly invariant relation.

**Debate — does the high-z low-mass slope evolve?** Sanders et al. (2021) find a slope near 0.30 unchanged to z ≈ 3.3, while Curti et al. (2024) measure a shallower 0.17 ± 0.03 at z = 3–10 and Langeroodi et al. (2023) report a similar or slightly shallower slope.

**Debate — which abundance scale transfers to high redshift?** Direct-temperature and photoionization-model calibrations disagree in absolute normalization (Kewley & Ellison 2008; Curti et al. 2020), and simulations show that applying local calibrations to early galaxies can bias inferred oxygen abundance downward by up to ~1 dex (Hirschmann et al. 2023).

**Debate — what physically sets the relation?** The mass–metallicity relation alone cannot uniquely separate the roles of metal-loaded outflows, pristine inflow, star-formation efficiency and gas retention; models remain degenerate (Finlator & Davé 2008; Peeples & Shankar 2011; Zahid et al. 2014; Sanders et al. 2021).

## Chemical Evolution and Nucleosynthesis

**Strongly corroborated.** Closed-box chemical evolution fails — most sharply through the G-dwarf problem — so galaxy enrichment requires open-box gas exchange (inflow, outflow, recycling). Established by Tinsley (1980) and formalized in gas-regulator/analytic models by Lilly et al. (2013), Chiappini et al. (1997), Weinberg et al. (2017) and Matteucci (2021).

**Strongly corroborated.** Two nucleosynthetic channels dominate the alpha-vs-iron pattern: core-collapse supernovae deliver prompt alpha-elements, while Type Ia supernovae deliver delayed iron. Established by Nomoto et al. (2013), Maoz et al. (2012, 2014) and Thomas et al. (2005).

**Established (measurement).** The Type Ia supernova delay-time distribution is a continuously declining power law of index ≈ -1.07 from a few hundred Myr to over 10 Gyr, with a time-integrated production efficiency ≈ 1.3×10^-3 SNe Ia per solar mass formed. Measured by Maoz, Mannucci & Brandt (2012) and reviewed by Maoz, Mannucci & Nelemans (2014).

**Corroborated.** Low- and intermediate-mass stars on the asymptotic giant branch are major sources of carbon, secondary nitrogen and roughly half of the slow-neutron-capture (s-process) elements, on mass-dependent delays. Established by Karakas & Lattanzio (2014), Henry et al. (2000), with the r-process shown to require a distinct high-neutron-flux site by Cowan et al. (1991).

**Corroborated.** The nitrogen-to-oxygen ratio shows a low-metallicity plateau near log(N/O) ≈ -1.6 (primary nitrogen) that rises steeply at high oxygen abundance (secondary nitrogen). Established by Vincenzo et al. (2016), Henry et al. (2000), Izotov & Thuan (1999) and Garnett et al. (1995).

**Corroborated.** Local star-forming disks show negative radial oxygen-abundance gradients — enriched centers, metal-poorer outskirts, ~-0.1 dex per effective radius — consistent with inside-out assembly; stellar radial migration subsequently flattens and blurs stellar gradients. Established by Sánchez et al. (2014), Belfiore et al. (2017), Zaritsky et al. (1994) and Bresolin et al. (2009), with migration from Sellwood & Binney (2002).

**Debate — is [alpha/Fe] a clean star-formation clock?** High [alpha/Fe] is read as a short formation timescale by Thomas et al. (2005), but a top-heavy IMF, metallicity-dependent yields or burst timing could raise [alpha/Fe] independently of the star-formation history (Nomoto et al. 2013; Johnson & Weinberg 2020; Matteucci 2021).

**Debate — early-Universe nitrogen enrichment.** The supersolar N/O in GN-z11 at z = 10.6 (Cameron et al. 2023) is in tension with standard delayed nitrogen enrichment (Vincenzo et al. 2016; Henry et al. 2000), prompting competing explanations and questions about high-z line-diagnostic reliability.

**Debate — high-redshift metallicity-gradient evolution.** Whether z ≈ 1–3 disks have steeper, flat or inverted radial gradients is unresolved, since observed gradients are diverse and may reflect genuine cold-flow accretion (Cresci et al. 2010; Jones et al. 2013) or beam-smearing and geometry (Stott et al. 2014; Wuyts et al. 2016; Sánchez et al. 2014).

## The Cosmic Baryon and Metal Cycle

**Strongly corroborated.** More than ~90% of present-day baryons are gaseous rather than locked in stars, and the total baryon density (Omega_b ≈ 0.0455) is concordant between Big-Bang nucleosynthesis and the CMB. Established by Driver et al. (2018), Planck Collaboration (2016) and Cooke et al. (2018).

**Corroborated.** The cosmic neutral-atomic-hydrogen density Omega_HI evolves only weakly from z ≈ 5 to 0, while the cosmic molecular-gas density rises to a peak near cosmic noon and then declines, tracking the star-formation-rate density. Established by Péroux et al. (2003) and Bird et al. (2017) for HI, and Decarli et al. (2019, 2020), Riechers et al. (2019) and Bauermeister et al. (2010) for H2.

**Strongly corroborated.** Only ~20–25% of the metals a galaxy has produced are retained in its stars, ISM and dust; the majority reside in the circumgalactic medium or have been expelled. Established by Peeples et al. (2014), Werk et al. (2014) and Tumlinson et al. (2011).

**Corroborated.** There is no severe global "missing-metals" problem in the modern census: the metals in cold (T < 10^4 K) gas at z > 2.5 approach the expected produced amount, and submillimeter galaxies contribute less than ~15% of the cosmic metal budget. Established across the Bouché et al. (2005, 2006, 2007) missing-metals series and Becker et al. (2011).

**Corroborated.** Both the dust-to-gas and dust-to-metals ratios decrease toward lower metallicity. Established by Aller et al. (2014) and Berg et al. (2016).

**Debate — HI census vs. absorber selection.** Whether Omega_HI is genuinely near-constant or biased by column-density thresholds and dust obscuration of high-column damped systems is debated: Péroux et al. (2003) vs. Bird et al. (2017), with obscuration concerns from Aller et al. (2014) and Bolmer et al. (2019).

**Debate — molecular census systematics.** The cosmic H2 density inferred from CO luminosity functions depends on excitation and the metallicity-dependent CO-to-H2 conversion, so its amplitude and peak redshift are uncertain: Decarli et al. (2019, 2020) and Riechers et al. (2019) vs. the conversion-factor caveats of Bolatto et al. (2013).

**Debate — observed vs. simulated phase partition.** Simulations can match integrated baryon and metal totals while assigning them to different temperatures, ionization states and radii than observations imply, and ionization corrections in the CGM/LLS are model-dependent. Sources: Chisari et al. (2018), Bird & Haehnelt (2015), Churchill et al. (2015), Chen et al. (2017), Wotta et al. (2019).

## The Circumgalactic Medium

**Strongly corroborated.** The circumgalactic medium (disk radius < r ≤ virial radius) is multiphase, spanning cold photoionized gas, warm transition gas and hot coronal plasma along single sightlines. Established by Chen et al. (2010), Tripp et al. (2008) and Werk et al. (2013).

**Corroborated.** Cool neutral/low-ionized hydrogen is ubiquitous out to ~150 kpc around both star-forming and passive L* galaxies. Established by Tumlinson et al. (2013), Thom et al. (2012) and Chen et al. (1998).

**Corroborated.** Strong O VI absorption surrounds star-forming L* galaxies but is markedly weaker around passive ones, a dichotomy reproduced in simulations where the O VI peak coincides with the L* halo virial temperature. Established by Tumlinson et al. (2011) and Oppenheimer et al. (2016).

**Corroborated (independent PIE models).** The cool CGM holds a large ionized-gas reservoir — ≈6.5×10^10 M_sun (Werk et al. 2014) to 9.2 ± 4.3×10^10 M_sun (Prochaska et al. 2017) for L* galaxies — comparable to the galaxy's ISM. Two independent photoionization analyses agree at the ~10^10.8 M_sun level.

**Corroborated.** Hot X-ray coronae are detected around only a handful of very massive spirals and fall well short of closing the baryon budget (the Milky Way hot halo is ≤(0.5–1.5)×10^10 M_sun, only 2–5% of the missing baryons). Established by Anderson & Bregman (2010, 2011), Anderson et al. (2013), Bogdán et al. (2013) and Dai et al. (2012).

**Corroborated (geometric signature).** Low-ionization metal absorption is statistically concentrated along galaxy minor axes (biconical outflows), while co-rotating absorption aligns with the major axis (accretion). Established by Bordoloi et al. (2011), Kacprzak et al. (2012) and Bouché et al. (2013); dust is also detected in the CGM by Ménard et al. (2010).

**Debate — cool-cloud survival.** Whether cool CGM clouds are rapidly shredded by Kelvin–Helmholtz instabilities or survive and even grow via cooling, conduction suppression and thermal-instability precipitation (when t_cool/t_ff ≲ 10) is contested: Armillotta et al. (2017) and Bland-Hawthorn et al. (2007) vs. Maller & Bullock (2004) and McCourt et al. (2012).

**Debate — the origin of O VI and intermediate ions.** Whether O VI arises in ~10^5.5 K collisionally ionized gas or in photoionized/non-equilibrium cooling interfaces and turbulent mixing layers is unresolved: Tumlinson et al. (2011) and Oppenheimer et al. (2016) vs. Tripp et al. (2008), Faerman et al. (2017) and Edgar & Chevalier (1986).

**Debate — metallicity as an origin tag.** A bimodal cool-CGM metallicity distribution has been read as separating pristine accretion from enriched recycled outflows, but mixing and ionization overlap blur the interpretation: Lehner et al. (2013) and Prochaska et al. (2017) vs. the simulation mixing of Ford et al. (2014).

**Debate — feedback-model dependence of the CGM.** Different wind prescriptions yield different CGM column densities and phase masses, so CGM observables constrain feedback only within model assumptions: Muratov et al. (2015), Finlator & Davé (2008) and Oppenheimer & Davé (2008).

## Structural Evolution and Feedback in Galaxy-Formation Models

**Strongly corroborated.** Galaxy formation proceeds in two stages — collisionless dark-matter halo assembly followed by dissipative gas cooling — within the ΛCDM hierarchy. Established by White & Rees (1978), White & Frenk (1991) and Blumenthal et al. (1984).

**Strongly corroborated.** Without feedback, gas cools catastrophically ("overcooling"), overproducing central mass and stars and losing angular momentum; this failure mode motivates all feedback prescriptions. Established by White & Frenk (1991), Balogh et al. (2001), Katz et al. (1996) and Navarro & Benz (1991).

**Corroborated.** Cosmological simulations cannot resolve star formation and feedback directly and must use subgrid recipes calibrated to observed relations. Established by Katz (1992), Springel & Hernquist (2003) and Schaye et al. (2015, EAGLE).

**Corroborated (theory + high-resolution experiments).** A supernova remnant transfers energy and momentum through free-expansion, Sedov–Taylor, shell-formation and momentum-conserving stages, injecting a terminal radial momentum of ~2–3×10^5 M_sun km/s per event near n_H ≈ 1 cm^-3 (per ~10^51 erg). Established by Cioffi et al. (1988), Blondin et al. (1998), Kim & Ostriker (2015) and Martizzi et al. (2015).

**Corroborated.** Depositing supernova thermal energy at insufficient numerical resolution causes it to radiate away artificially ("numerical overcooling"), a core reason feedback is hard to model. Established by Agertz et al. (2013), Dalla Vecchia & Schaye (2008) and Kim & Ostriker (2015).

**Corroborated.** Stellar/supernova-driven outflows are the central regulator that lowers baryon-conversion efficiency in low-mass galaxies and transports metals, and their mass loading scales inversely with halo circular velocity; ejected gas need not escape permanently — galactic fountains recycle it. Established by Dekel & Silk (1986), Larson (1974), Muratov et al. (2015), Pettini et al. (2001) and Governato et al. (2010); recycling from Fraternali & Binney (2008), Marinacci et al. (2011) and Brook et al. (2011).

**Corroborated.** High-redshift star-forming disks are turbulent, clumpy and rotationally supported, with velocity dispersions σ0 ≈ 40–80 km/s and v_rot/σ0 ≈ 2–6; violent disk instabilities form giant (~10^8–10^9 M_sun) clumps that can migrate to build bulges. Established by Förster Schreiber et al. (2009, 2018), Wisnioski et al. (2015), Genzel et al. (2011), Ceverino et al. (2010) and Bournaud et al. (2007).

**Corroborated.** The galaxy size–mass relation evolves since z ≈ 3, with star-forming and quiescent populations following different size evolution, and compact quiescent galaxies already exist at z ≈ 2. Established by van der Wel et al. (2014) and Barro et al. (2013); baseline disk sizes trace halo angular momentum (Fall & Efstathiou 1980; Mo, Mao & White 1998; Peebles 1969).

**Debate — mergers vs. internal processes for structural growth.** Whether spheroids and compact cores form mainly through major/minor mergers (Barnes 1988, 1992; Cox et al. 2006) or through internal violent disk instabilities and secular evolution (Ceverino et al. 2010; Bournaud et al. 2007; Zolotov et al. 2015) is unresolved, and morphology alone does not identify the quenching mechanism (Bell et al. 2012).

**Debate — the source of high-z disk turbulence.** Whether the elevated velocity dispersions of cosmic-noon disks are powered by stellar feedback or by gravitational instability and accretion is contested: Krumholz & Burkert (2010) and Dekel et al. (2009) vs. feedback-driven accounts.

**Debate — subgrid/calibration degeneracy (highest-trust caution).** Reproducing observed stellar masses, star-formation rates and mass functions does not prove the underlying mechanism is correct: distinct subgrid implementations (thermal vs. kinetic feedback; effective equation of state vs. explicit channels) match the same calibrated statistics while diverging in secondary predictions. Sources: Agertz et al. (2013), Dalla Vecchia & Schaye (2008), Springel & Hernquist (2003) and Schaye et al. (2015).

**Debate — numerical vs. physical convergence.** Higher resolution need not converge to the true feedback coupling if new resolved phases change the effective problem, so tuned population agreement is not physical validation; independent same-initial-condition, multi-code tests and out-of-sample gas/CGM/structure predictions are required. Sources: Kim & Ostriker (2015), Martizzi et al. (2015), Schaye et al. (2015).
