# Galaxy Evolution: A Debate Map — Enrichment

**Method: DMW (Debate-Map-to-Wiki). Supplementary sections.** This file extends the existing debate map with three new areas drawn from newly-available DR-verified review syntheses: Förster Schreiber & Wuyts (2020) on the structural and kinematic evolution of star-forming galaxies at cosmic noon; Conroy (2013) on stellar population synthesis and the systematics of inferring stellar mass, age, metallicity, and star-formation rate; and Wechsler & Tinker (2018) on the statistical connection between galaxies and their dark-matter halos. As with the rest of the wiki, each section is organized around live tensions, separating what is genuinely **settled** from what remains **contested**, and the same meta-tension recurs: an observed correlation or a calibrated model can be reproduced by more than one physical mechanism.

## Section 11 — How did massive galaxies build their structure and kinematics at cosmic noon? Disk settling, dispersion support, and the inner dark-matter budget

**Central tension:** Resolved spectroscopy shows that most massive star-forming galaxies at the peak of cosmic star formation are rotating disks — but turbulent, compact, clumpy ones whose settling history, dark-matter content, and evolutionary endpoint are each read differently depending on how the kinematics are modeled and how the sample is selected.

### Settled
A large fraction of resolved, massive star-forming galaxies at cosmic noon (z ≈ 1–3) are rotation-dominated but turbulent disks in emission-line integral-field data, rather than a population dominated by ongoing major mergers (Förster Schreiber & Wuyts 2020; Wisnioski et al. 2015, 2019; Förster Schreiber et al. 2009). Their intrinsic ionized-gas velocity dispersions are elevated at high redshift and decline toward the present — from about 50 km/s at z ≈ 2.3 to about 25 km/s at z ≈ 0.9 in mass-selected KMOS3D samples — once beam smearing and unresolved rotation are forward-modeled out (Übler et al. 2019). At fixed stellar mass, these galaxies are more compact at higher redshift, with rest-optical effective radius scaling roughly as (1+z)^−0.75 for late types and more steeply, about (1+z)^−1.48, for quiescent galaxies over 0 < z < 3 (van der Wel et al. 2014). In dynamical models the inner regions (within roughly one effective radius) of many massive cosmic-noon disks are baryon-dominated, with low inferred central dark-matter fractions — for example a median enclosed dark-matter fraction near 8 percent for a Chabrier IMF in z ≈ 2 MOSDEF samples (Wuyts et al. 2016; Price et al. 2016; Genzel et al. 2017).

### Contested
**Rotation-versus-dispersion support and the timing of disk settling.** Whether most massive main-sequence galaxies at cosmic noon are already dynamically settled rotating disks, or whether disturbed, dispersion-dominated, and merging systems remain a major channel, is unresolved. Kinematic classification, resolution, depth, inclination, and pair-timescale assumptions prevent a selection-complete disk-versus-merger fraction across mass and redshift (Wisnioski et al. 2015; Genzel et al. 2008; Förster Schreiber et al. 2009). **Baryon dominance versus modeling bias in the inner dark-matter budget.** Whether the low inferred inner dark-matter fractions reflect genuinely baryon-dominated centers, or are partly an artifact of pressure support, IMF and gas-mass assumptions, and halo contraction versus cored profiles, is unresolved — rotation curves rarely extend far beyond the luminous disk and the mass components are strongly degenerate, with the same data read as evidence for baryon dominance (Genzel et al. 2017) and for cored halos (Genzel et al. 2020; Lang et al. 2017; Price et al. 2020). A low central dark-matter fraction is not the absence of a dark halo. **Compaction versus inside-out growth.** Whether present-day massive galaxies quench through an early phase of central compaction into a dense star-forming core, or through gradual inside-out disk growth with progressive bulge buildup, is unresolved: cross-sectional populations and dust-sensitive resolved SEDs cannot uniquely reconstruct individual evolutionary tracks (Barro et al. 2013, 2017; Wuyts et al. 2012). **Clump longevity, migration, and measurement inflation.** Whether the kiloparsec-scale clumps in gas-rich high-redshift disks are intrinsic, long-lived, in-situ structures formed by violent disk instability that migrate inward to build bulges, or are largely short-lived, feedback-disrupted, and inflated in mass by PSF blending and SED assumptions, is unresolved because clump ages, masses, and radial gradients are strongly resolution- and model-dependent (Genzel et al. 2011; Förster Schreiber et al. 2018; Bournaud et al. 2014). **Main-sequence slope and high-mass flattening.** Whether the star-forming main sequence is near-linear or bends and flattens at the high-mass end, and how its normalization evolves, remains contested because SFR indicators, dust corrections, IMF, stacking, and bulge contamination shift the fitted slope and zero-point (Speagle et al. 2014; Schreiber et al. 2015; Whitaker et al. 2014).

## Section 12 — Can we trust the stellar masses, ages, and star-formation rates we infer? Stellar-population-synthesis systematics

**Central tension:** Almost every quantity in extragalactic astrophysics — stellar mass, age, star-formation rate, metallicity — is not measured but *inferred* by fitting stellar-population-synthesis (SPS) models to light. Those models rest on assumed ingredients and priors, and the deepest debates are about which inferences survive the model dependence.

### Settled
An SPS prediction combines an assumed IMF, stellar-evolution isochrones, and stellar spectra to build simple stellar populations, then convolves them with a star-formation history, enrichment, and dust for composite populations; every inferred property inherits the adopted ingredients and priors and is not a direct observation (Bruzual & Charlot 2003; Conroy, Gunn & White 2009). Stellar mass is among the more robust SED-derived properties given adequate wavelength coverage and normal histories, but it remains an SPS-dependent mass-to-light scaling whose absolute value is set by the IMF and by hidden old populations (Bell et al. 2003; Kauffmann et al. 2003; Zibetti, Charlot & Rix 2009). Old-population optical colors and many absorption indices suffer a strong age-metallicity degeneracy — approximately the Worthey 3/2 rule, in which a factor-three change in age mimics a factor-two change in metallicity — most severe for populations older than about 5 Gyr with limited optical information (Worthey 1994; Thomas, Maraston & Bender 2003). The low-mass normalization of the IMF strongly controls mass-to-light ratios while contributing little light, so that roughly 60–80 percent of the stellar mass of an old population can reside in low-mass stars nearly invisible in optical and near-infrared SEDs (Salpeter 1955; Kroupa 2001; Chabrier 2003). UV, recombination-line, and infrared SFR indicators average star formation over different timescales and dust sensitivities — the UV continuum over roughly 100 Myr, Hα recombination over less than about 10 Myr — and every calibration assumes an IMF, metallicity, SFH duration, and dust decomposition (Kennicutt 1998; Brinchmann et al. 2004; Salim et al. 2007).

### Contested
**IMF universality and its effect on stellar masses.** Whether the IMF is universal and Milky-Way-like or varies systematically with velocity dispersion or abundance is unresolved: gravity-sensitive spectra, dynamics, and lensing by 2013 indicated that massive early-type galaxies may have heavier IMF normalizations, but abundance-response, dark-matter, and dynamical-modeling degeneracies prevent proof of a single universal trend (van Dokkum & Conroy 2010; Cappellari et al. 2012; Treu et al. 2010). Because the IMF sets the mass-to-light ratio, this debate propagates directly into every stellar mass. **Absolute stellar-mass accuracy.** Whether absolute masses are accurate to about a factor of two or are limited by larger SPS/IMF/SFH systematics is disputed, because formal fit errors of order 0.3 dex omit model mismatch and unseen old or low-mass populations, and extreme SFH choices alone can shift recovered mass by up to about 0.6 dex (Conroy, Gunn & White 2009; Kauffmann et al. 2003). **Parametric versus nonparametric SFHs.** Whether simple exponential or rising histories or flexible, burst-capable nonparametric reconstructions better recover mass, age, and past SFR is unresolved: flexible models reduce bias but increase prior dependence and time-bin covariance, and young stars outshine older mass (Ocvirk et al. 2006; Tojeiro et al. 2007; Cid Fernandes et al. 2005). **TP-AGB strength.** The thermally pulsing asymptotic-giant-branch contribution drives large near-infrared differences between SPS models for intermediate-age populations (≈0.1–2 Gyr), and whether heavier or lighter prescriptions are correct is contested because cluster calibrators, stochasticity, metallicity, SFH, and circumstellar dust permit competing fits (Maraston 2005; Marigo et al. 2008; Kriek et al. 2010). **Dust law and geometry.** Whether a universal foreground-like attenuation law applies or effective attenuation varies with mixed star-dust geometry and stellar age is unresolved, because extinction and attenuation laws are not interchangeable and UV slope, IRX, inclination, and composition covary (Calzetti 2001; Charlot & Fall 2000; Meurer et al. 1999). **Optical-only versus panchromatic inference.** Whether optical spectra alone suffice or panchromatic UV-to-infrared energy-balance data are required is contested, because adding bands supplies information on young and obscured stars but also introduces model systematics, and dust emission cannot be inferred from optical-only fits (da Cunha, Charlot & Elbaz 2008; Charlot & Longhetti 2001).

## Section 13 — How do galaxies connect to their dark-matter halos? Abundance matching, scatter, and assembly bias

**Central tension:** The statistical mapping between galaxies and halos is now precise enough to anchor both galaxy-formation physics and cosmology, but the mapping is conditional on halo-mass definitions, proxy choices, and model assumptions — and whether galaxy properties depend on more than halo mass is the field's defining open question.

### Settled
The central-galaxy stellar-to-halo mass relation (SHMR) is strongly non-linear, with integrated galaxy-formation efficiency peaking near a Milky-Way-scale halo of about 10^12 solar masses and falling steeply toward both lower and higher halo mass (Behroozi, Wechsler & Conroy 2013; Moster, Naab & White 2013; Guo et al. 2010). At z ≈ 0 the peak integrated central efficiency — central stellar mass divided by the cosmic baryon allotment of the halo — remains below about 20 percent, so even the most efficient halos convert only a minority of their baryons into central stars (Behroozi, Wechsler & Conroy 2013; Moster, Naab & White 2013). The intrinsic scatter in central stellar mass at fixed halo mass above the pivot is small — generally below about 0.2 dex, often assumed lognormal — when inferred jointly from abundance, clustering, and lensing with measurement errors removed (Leauthaud et al. 2012; Behroozi, Conroy & Wechsler 2010; Zu & Mandelbaum 2016). Halo-occupation-distribution (HOD) and conditional-luminosity-function (CLF) models require separate central and satellite occupation distributions to reproduce both abundance and scale-dependent clustering, with the one-halo to two-halo transition producing measurable departures from a single power law (Yang, Mo & van den Bosch 2003; Zehavi et al. 2011; Kravtsov et al. 2004). Subhalo abundance matching reproduces abundance and clustering by rank-ordering galaxies against halos and subhalos using a chosen proxy with scatter (Vale & Ostriker 2004; Conroy, Wechsler & Kravtsov 2006; Reddick et al. 2013). And in collisionless N-body simulations, halo clustering depends on formation history and concentration at fixed halo mass — halo assembly bias (Gao, Springel & White 2005; Wechsler et al. 2002, 2006).

### Contested
**Galaxy assembly bias.** Whether galaxy occupation depends only on halo mass, or galaxy properties are explicitly coupled to halo formation history or concentration to produce observable galaxy assembly bias, is unresolved: halo masses and secondary properties are noisy and correlated, environment can mimic the signal, and ignoring it can bias mass-only cosmological inference (Hearin & Watson 2013; Zentner, Hearin & van den Bosch 2014; Lehmann et al. 2017). **Origin of SHMR scatter.** The dominant source of scatter — halo merger and accretion history, concentration, spin, baryonic stochasticity, or residual measurement error — is contested, because the inferred intrinsic scatter depends on the deconvolution and on cross-correlated galaxy observables (Leauthaud et al. 2012; Behroozi, Conroy & Wechsler 2010; Reddick et al. 2013). **Optimal abundance-matching proxy.** Which halo proxy best links galaxies to halos — present maximum circular velocity, peak velocity or mass, infall values, or compound concentration-sensitive proxies — is unresolved, because satellite stripping and disruption, resolution, and orphan galaxies change small-scale clustering (Reddick et al. 2013; Vale & Ostriker 2004; Lehmann et al. 2017). **Satellite quenching mechanism.** The mechanism that quenches satellites — starvation and strangulation, ram-pressure stripping, tidal effects, or group preprocessing — is unresolved: satellite demographics constrain a delayed-then-rapid pattern (delays of roughly 2–4 Gyr followed by rapid fading below about 0.8 Gyr for local massive satellites) better than they identify a unique physical cause (Wetzel et al. 2013; Wetzel, Tinker & Conroy 2012). **Low-mass SHMR.** The slope and scatter of the SHMR below the pivot and toward dwarfs are uncertain, with steep abundance-matching extrapolation competing against broader, more scattered dwarf occupation and incomplete luminous samples, because ultra-faint completeness, reionization, tidal histories, and dynamical-mass conversion remain poorly constrained (Behroozi, Wechsler & Conroy 2013; Bullock & Boylan-Kolchin 2017). **Halo-mass inference method and baryonic modification.** Because clustering, abundance, group catalogs, weak lensing, satellite dynamics, X-ray, and SZ constraints each carry different degeneracies, no method recovers a definition-independent halo mass, and whether dark-matter-only mass, profile, and bias calibrations remain adequate or must include baryonic contraction, expansion, and redistribution at survey precision is unresolved (Zu & Mandelbaum 2016; Tinker et al. 2008; Leauthaud et al. 2012).

## Enrichment meta-tension

These three areas reinforce the wiki's recurring structural problem from three new directions. In cosmic-noon kinematics, the same rotation curve is read as baryon dominance or as a cored halo depending on the pressure-support and IMF priors. In stellar population synthesis, the same broadband SED yields different masses and ages depending on the IMF, SFH parameterization, and TP-AGB prescription — the inference is model-conditioned all the way down. In the galaxy–halo connection, the same clustering signal is reproduced by a mass-only occupation or by an assembly-bias model, and no halo-mass proxy is definition-independent. In every case progress depends on breaking a specific degeneracy: extended rotation curves with independent gas and pressure constraints; mock-tested nonparametric SFHs with abundance-sensitive spectra and dynamical cross-checks on the IMF; and joint clustering-plus-lensing measurements with controlled secondary-property selections and realistic mocks that expose assembly bias directly.
