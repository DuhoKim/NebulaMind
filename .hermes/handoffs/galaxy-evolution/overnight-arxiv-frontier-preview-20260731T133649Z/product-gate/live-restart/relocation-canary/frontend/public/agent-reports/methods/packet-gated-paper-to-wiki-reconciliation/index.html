When to use it

Use when

When old claim chips, source-position rows, trust badges, or page artifacts already exist and may overstate, duplicate, hide, or mislabel what the papers support.

Do not mistake it for

Not the fastest way to discover new science from the corpus; it is a safety/reconciliation method for legacy or already-derived paper-to-wiki artifacts.

Method details

Inputs

  • Many papers already distilled into claim rows, source positions, evidence bindings, status labels, trust receipts, and page/chip display state.
  • A target slice such as 2298/2299/2924/2572 or another claim family.
  • Fresh before-state receipts, hashes, counts, and rollback requirements before any future write packet.

Work

  • Compare each existing claim chip against the papers and current debate map.
  • Classify each artifact as keep, recast, retire, hide, re-parent, relabel, or defer to a later trust recompute.
  • Prepare exact docs/static packet manifests; do not mutate product state without a separate fresh gate.

Outputs

  • A claim disposition table tied back to evidence and source positions.
  • A safe reconciliation packet proposal for later review.
  • Wiki-readiness notes: what prose may say, what must stay caveated, and what product chips would contradict the final page if left unchanged.

Stop gates

  • Any claim lacks a clear paper/evidence basis or current before-state snapshot.
  • The packet would make a trust/display change without explaining the science status.
  • The method starts treating cleanup as final wiki completion; it is only one support lane.

Safety / non-execution notice

NO ACTIVE EXECUTION PHRASE. This page explains a method. It does not authorize a database write, SQL, trust recompute, page publish, service restart, deploy, git action, or product mutation. Any future mutation still needs a fresh exact packet, fresh before-state capture, and separate user approval at that time.

Evaluation-first readiness and display-safety hygiene are overlays that apply to every method; they are not separate peer methods for completing the paper-to-wiki Baseline.
