## AGN Feedback & Quenching

Quenching is not a single event. It can be rapid or slow, central or environmental, preventive or ejective, and it can leave different signatures in stellar populations, gas reservoirs, and morphology. The scoped conclusion is deliberately modest: <!--claim:2942-->AGN or SMBH feedback is a real but scoped quenching pathway in galaxy evolution, not a universal explanation for why galaxies stop forming stars.<!--/claim:2942-->

For the ejective channel, the evidence supports selected systems rather than a population-wide rule. <!--claim:2943-->AGN activity can drive molecular, ionized, or neutral gas outflows capable of removing or depleting star-forming fuel in selected massive or AGN-host galaxies, with detection rates remaining tracer- and sample-dependent.<!--/claim:2943--><!--cite:30760,30759,30758,30757,30756,30755,30754--> The citation set deliberately mixes molecular, ionized, neutral, and high-redshift examples; it does not by itself prove that every massive galaxy quenches through AGN-driven expulsion.

That caveat matters because the competing axes are real, not cleanup noise. <!--claim:2944-->AGN feedback is one quenching axis among several; central/bulge/black-hole predictors, halo/environment/satellite pathways, strangulation, stripping, gas retention, low star-formation efficiency, and star-formation-driven outflows remain alternatives or qualifiers.<!--/claim:2944--><!--cite:30765,30764,30763,30762,30769,30768,30767,30766--> A readable synthesis should therefore compare AGN-linked mechanisms against central-structure and environmental channels instead of turning AGN into the default cause.

Gas-reservoir evidence is a useful check on overly simple wording. <!--claim:2945-->Gas-reservoir language requires caution: central-kiloparsec depletion can occur in some AGN hosts, while other quenched systems retain large gas reservoirs or show low star-formation efficiency, so gas removal alone cannot explain every quenching pathway.<!--/claim:2945--><!--cite:30779,30778,30777,30776--> This keeps central depletion, retained gas, and inefficient star formation in the same interpretive frame.

Finally, preventive feedback is distinct from ejective outflows. <!--claim:2946-->Preventive or maintenance feedback is distinct from ejective outflows; current maintenance/heating support in this source set remains model-dependent or simulation-bounded rather than a measured prevalence result.<!--/claim:2946--><!--cite:30785,30784,30783,30782,30781,30780--> The section should therefore treat hot-halo or radio-mode heating as a model-bounded maintenance pathway unless a later packet adds stronger observational prevalence support.
