Galaxy Evolution — Research proposal agenda (Method 3)

Evidence-aware study proposals: each states what the current research-status synthesis already reports, what is still unknown, the exact measurement proposed, the named data that would supply it, and the test that would decide the result.

Please read. These are proposed studies — hypotheses and research questions for future work, not accepted results or citation-bound evidence. "What studies already show" summarizes the current method synthesis in plain words; where support is model-only or single-galaxy, that is stated. Survey/instrument names are data a study would use, not datasets that already prove a claim. Confidence wording ("actively debated", "emerging", "model-dependent") reflects how settled the science is and is specific to this method's perspective.

1. When does AGN feedback dominate galaxy quenching?

Research question: In which regime of halo mass, environment, and redshift is AGN feedback the leading quenching channel, rather than halo, environmental, or stellar-feedback channels?

What studies already show

  • Central black-hole, bulge, and velocity-dispersion properties correlate with central-galaxy quenching in the current synthesis.
  • AGN feedback is one channel among several; the synthesis explicitly declines to name it "the dominant cause."
  • Countercases (environmental strangulation, satellite processing, gas retention, stellar feedback) remain active alternatives.

What remains unknown

Which channel is causally responsible at fixed halo mass, environment, and stellar mass — the correlations do not break the degeneracy between AGN power and host structure.

Survey/data plan

  • DESI, SDSS/GAMA: mass-, environment-, and redshift-matched parent samples and quenched-fraction denominators.
  • SDSS/MaNGA or MUSE: resolved star-formation histories and central structure to fix host properties.
  • Chandra/XMM/eROSITA + VLA/LOFAR: X-ray and radio AGN power/state per galaxy.
  • IllustrisTNG / HORIZON-AGN-style simulations: counterfactual runs with and without AGN feedback, as priors to test — not as evidence.

Analysis/test

At fixed halo mass, environment, and stellar mass, regress quenched fraction on AGN power; compare the residual quenching to the with/without-AGN simulation counterfactuals to isolate the AGN-attributable share.

Expected result or decision point

A conditional threshold (e.g., a halo-mass/AGN-power regime above which AGN-attributable quenching exceeds the alternative channels), or a demonstration that the channels remain degenerate at current precision.

Caveats

AGN activity and host structure are selection-coupled; time-variable AGN power blurs instantaneous indicators; simulation feedback prescriptions differ.

Provenance: axes dominance_debate, mechanism_ejective_feedback, alternatives_countercases; local basis clc_agn2299_003, clc_agn_009, clc_agn_010.

2. A comparable-denominator census of AGN-driven outflows

Research question: What is the incidence of AGN-driven outflows as a function of tracer and selection, once a single denominator is applied?

What studies already show

  • The synthesis records outflow-incidence fractions from different samples — for example a ~17% ionized-outflow rate in one cosmic-noon AGN sample and a ~46% neutral-outflow rate in a massive-galaxy sample.
  • It explicitly warns these are tracer- and selection-specific and must not be merged into one number.
  • Molecular, ionized, and neutral phases are observed in selected AGN hosts, but under heterogeneous selections.

What remains unknown

The population-wide outflow frequency under a common selection and denominator, and how it varies by gas phase and redshift — current fractions share no denominator.

Survey/data plan

  • MOSDEF, JWST/NIRSpec: rest-optical ionized-gas outflow detection at cosmic noon and beyond.
  • Na I D absorption (optical spectra): neutral-phase outflows in the same parent sample.
  • ALMA CO/[C II]: cold molecular-outflow phase and mass loading.
  • COSMOS/CANDELS, DESI: a single mass/redshift-selected parent sample to define the denominator.

Analysis/test

Apply one selection function and redshift grid; measure per-tracer detection fractions at identical sensitivity thresholds; report tracer-resolved prevalence with explicit denominators rather than a merged rate.

Expected result or decision point

A tracer-resolved prevalence curve with controlled selection — the reference needed before "AGN outflows are common/rare" can be stated.

Caveats

Sensitivity varies by tracer and distance; phase mismatch (ionized vs neutral vs molecular) can double-count or miss outflows; single striking cases are not prevalence anchors.

Provenance: axis outflow_prevalence_frequency (emerging / sample-limited); local basis clc_agn_002a (MOSDEF ~17% ionized), clc_agn_002b (JWST ~46% neutral).

3. Gas reservoirs and star-formation efficiency in quenching galaxies

Research question: Are quenching galaxies gas-poor (removed reservoirs) or gas-retaining but inefficient, and where do AGN hosts fall along that axis?

What studies already show

  • The synthesis reports that some quenched systems show central kiloparsec-scale molecular depletion.
  • Others retain substantial gas but form stars inefficiently (low star-formation efficiency), so low star formation is not equivalent to low gas.
  • Expulsion of central gas does not by itself imply a galaxy-wide reservoir loss.

What remains unknown

The relative frequency of "removed" vs "retained-inefficient" quenching across the population, and whether AGN activity tracks central depletion specifically rather than global loss.

Survey/data plan

  • ALMA CO/[C II]: resolved molecular gas mass and depletion time (central vs galaxy-wide).
  • SDSS/MaNGA or MUSE: spatially resolved star-formation efficiency and stellar populations.
  • X-ray/radio AGN-stage indicators: order galaxies along a duty cycle to link reservoir state to AGN phase.

Analysis/test

Compare resolved gas depletion time vs AGN stage and radius; classify systems as depleted / retained-inefficient / intermediate; test whether AGN hosts concentrate in the central-depletion class specifically.

Expected result or decision point

A population split (fraction removed vs retained-inefficient) and a yes/no on whether AGN activity predicts central rather than global depletion.

Caveats

Diffuse and atomic gas can be missed; depletion time depends on the star-formation tracer; central vs galaxy-wide must be kept distinct.

Provenance: axis reservoir_response (actively debated); local basis clc_agn_005 (retention / low-SFE), clc_agn_006 (central-kpc depletion).

4. An observational program for maintenance (preventive) heating

Research question: Does radio-mode/maintenance heating balance cooling across the population, as an observed duty cycle rather than a model expectation?

What studies already show

  • In the current synthesis, maintenance/preventive heating is rendered model-dependent — supported in simulations, not established as an observed galaxy-scale prevalence.
  • It is kept distinct from ejective outflow feedback and is not rendered as "contradicted" either.

What remains unknown

Whether observed heating power (X-ray cavities, radio duty cycles) statistically balances cooling luminosity across a controlled halo-mass range — the observational duty cycle is not yet measured here.

Survey/data plan

  • Chandra/XMM/eROSITA: X-ray cavity energetics, cooling luminosity, hot-halo thermodynamics.
  • VLA/LOFAR/MeerKAT: radio jet power, morphology, and duty cycle (the heating engine's on/off history).
  • Group/cluster samples spanning halo mass: to test the balance as a function of halo mass.

Analysis/test

In a halo-mass-controlled sample, compare cavity+radio heating power to X-ray cooling luminosity, per system and time-averaged over the duty cycle.

Expected result or decision point

A measured heating/cooling balance (with a range) that promotes maintenance heating from model-supported to observationally bounded — or an upper bound if balance is not achieved.

Caveats

Duty-cycle averaging is uncertain; cavity energetics carry systematic factors; lowest-mass halos are hardest; model expectation must not be read as observation.

Provenance: axis maintenance_heating_prevention (model-dependent in this corpus); local basis clc_agn_004.

5. Validating simulation predictions against unbiased observations

Research question: Which simulation-predicted outflow rates, heating signatures, and quenching outcomes survive a forward-modeled comparison to unbiased survey samples?

What studies already show

  • The synthesis labels simulation-only statements "in simulations / in this model" and treats them as model-dependent.
  • Simulations demonstrate what feedback mechanisms can produce under their assumptions, not an observed prevalence.

What remains unknown

Which specific model predictions match observations once the survey selection is applied to the simulation — untested predictions may live only in unselected simulation space.

Survey/data plan

  • IllustrisTNG / HORIZON-AGN-style simulations: mock catalogs of outflow/heating/quenching observables (clearly a model to test).
  • DESI, SDSS, JWST deep fields: unbiased observed comparison samples with defined selection functions.

Analysis/test

Apply each survey's selection to the simulated galaxies (forward-modeling); compare predicted vs observed outflow, heating, and quenching statistics; flag predictions only visible without selection.

Expected result or decision point

A validated/invalidated list of simulation predictions — a rule for which model behaviors are observationally supported.

Caveats

Forward-model fidelity limits the test; sub-grid feedback differs between simulations; a match under one selection may fail under another.

Provenance: axis simulation_model_scope (model-dependent); local basis clc_agn_011.

6. Filling the lightly-covered channels: chemical, structural, and high-redshift

Research question: Which under-mapped channels (chemical enrichment/timing, morphology/structure, halo regulation, reionization frontier) carry enough evidence to weigh against the AGN-feedback channel, and which need new measurements?

What studies already show

  • The synthesis reports the mass–metallicity relation with modest scatter at cosmic noon and a fundamental-metallicity relation roughly stable (to ~0.1 dex) from the local universe out to z~2.3.
  • The reionization frontier is framed as open debates — the ionizing-photon budget (star-forming galaxies vs faint AGN) and the JWST z>10 "too-massive/too-early" tension — not settled findings.
  • Halo-regulation and morphology/structural-growth sections are flagged as scoped/lightly-covered, resting on largely unverified rows.

What remains unknown

Whether these channels' evidence is strong enough to rank against AGN feedback, and their redshift-resolved behavior beyond the currently scoped ranges.

Survey/data plan

  • JWST/NIRSpec (JADES/AURORA-style programs), SDSS/MaNGA: mass–metallicity and stellar-age measurements across redshift.
  • HST–JWST deep imaging, COSMOS/CANDELS: size–mass and morphology evolution.
  • DESI + wide imaging (weak lensing/clustering): halo-mass regulation of quenching.
  • JWST spectroscopy: reionization-era ionizing-photon and stellar-mass constraints.

Analysis/test

Build redshift-resolved scaling relations (mass–metallicity, size–mass) and halo-regulation measures on consistent selection; compare their evidential weight to the AGN-feedback channel to rebalance the multi-channel map.

Expected result or decision point

A ranked evidence gap across channels — which under-mapped channel most needs new data before the multi-channel picture is balanced.

Caveats

Each relation carries its own redshift scope and must not be generalized beyond it; unverified source rows are not settled claims.

Provenance: scoped coverage sections (Dark Matter Halos & Structure Formation; Environment, Morphology & Structural Growth; Chemical Enrichment & Cosmic Timing; High-Redshift & Reionization Frontier).
