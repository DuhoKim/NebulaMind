Proposal 1

How permanently do AGN outflows deplete star-forming gas? A removal-versus-recycling census

Research question

What fraction of AGN-driven outflowing gas escapes the halo permanently versus falls back within a few hundred kiloparsecs, and how does that fraction change with stellar mass and redshift?

What studies already show

The current method source basis reports direct observations of quasar-driven outflows removing star-forming gas in some high-redshift systems. It also cautions that outflowing gas in massive galaxies can fall back before travelling beyond about 100 kpc, and that winds in low-redshift, low-mass systems may be too weak to expel gas at all. Most of the removal support is a single class of detection or is model-based, not a measured escape fraction.

What remains unknown

The escaped-versus-recycled fraction itself. No study in the source basis ties outflow velocity to halo escape velocity across a mass- and redshift-matched sample, so an observed outflow is not yet an established permanent loss of the star-forming reservoir.

Survey/data plan

MUSE / MaNGA: resolved ionized-outflow velocity fields and spatially resolved quenching. ALMA CO and [C II]: molecular reservoir mass, depletion time, and any cold recycling gas. JWST/NIRSpec: high-redshift ionized-outflow velocities. DESI / Mg II absorption: cool circumgalactic gas around low-mass hosts, i.e. the recycling reservoir. (All proposed data to use.)

Analysis/test

Per galaxy, compare outflow velocity with halo escape velocity, and outflow kinetic energy with binding energy; classify each outflow as escaping or bound-and-recycling; bin the escaped fraction by stellar mass and redshift, against mass-matched controls.

Expected result or decision point

A calibrated escaped-fraction(mass, redshift) relation that converts outflow detections into reservoir-loss estimates, and a threshold mass/redshift above which permanent removal dominates recycling.

Caveats

Multiphase gas is only partly traced; escape is inferred, not directly observed; samples are biased toward luminous nuclei.

Method2 evidence basis: claim 2943 removal support (incl. the quasar-outflow row 28141) and the claim 2945 recycling/low-mass cautions (28066, 28075).

Proposal 2

Does AGN heating balance cooling in hot halos? An observed maintenance-heating census

Research question

Does the mechanical power that AGN deposit into group- and cluster-scale hot atmospheres balance their radiative cooling, and over what duty cycle?

What studies already show

The source basis supports maintenance heating mainly through cosmological simulations that require AGN feedback to avoid over-producing massive galaxies. Its one direct observational anchor is X-ray cavities inflated in hot atmospheres. Direct observations are otherwise sparse, so the balance is largely simulation-inferred.

What remains unknown

Whether observed cavity enthalpy actually matches cooling luminosity across a mass-selected sample, and how episodic that balance is. The heating/cooling ledger has not been measured across the mass function.

Survey/data plan

Chandra / XMM-Newton: cavity enthalpy, hot-halo temperature and density profiles, cooling luminosity. eROSITA: a mass-selected hot-atmosphere parent sample and denominator. VLA / LOFAR: jet power and duty cycle that tie cavities to active episodes. ALMA / optical IFU: residual central cooling and star formation. (Proposed data to use.)

Analysis/test

Compute heating power (cavity enthalpy divided by buoyancy time, times jet duty cycle) against cooling luminosity, per halo, across the mass function; test for balance and for time-variability between active and quiescent phases.

Expected result or decision point

An observed heating-to-cooling ratio, with scatter, as a function of halo mass — an observational bound that no longer depends on simulation-only support; if consistent with balance, a rule to promote maintenance heating from model-dependent to observation-supported.

Caveats

Cavity detection is sensitivity-limited; samples shrink at high redshift; the balance is time-variable and orientation-dependent.

Method2 evidence basis: claim 2946 (model-bounded rows 28089, 28123; X-ray-cavity observation 28158).

Proposal 3

How efficiently do radio jets couple to galaxy gas? A coupling-efficiency measurement

Research question

What is the distribution of efficiencies with which radio jets deposit their mechanical power into the surrounding galaxy gas, and how does it depend on environment?

What studies already show

The source basis supports a kinetic, radio-mode channel through review-level synthesis plus one radio-mode observation in massive radio galaxies, while cautioning that jet-to-gas coupling can be weak. A second line from the same review was deliberately set aside to avoid over-reliance on one source. No coupling-efficiency distribution has been measured.

What remains unknown

The coupling-efficiency distribution across a radio-selected sample: how often jets actually disturb the gas versus pass through it, and how strongly that depends on environment and jet power.

Survey/data plan

VLA / LOFAR / MeerKAT: jet power, morphology, and duty cycle. Chandra / XMM: jet-inflated cavities as a mechanical-work calorimeter. MaNGA / MUSE: jet-interstellar interaction, shocked-gas line ratios, and local star-formation suppression. IllustrisTNG- / HORIZON-AGN-style simulations: coupling-efficiency priors to test against, clearly labeled as simulations. (Proposed data to use.)

Analysis/test

Per galaxy, correlate jet power with gas kinematics and star-formation suppression; derive coupling efficiency as the ratio of energy deposited in the gas to jet mechanical power; compare the observed distribution with the simulation priors.

Expected result or decision point

A coupling-efficiency distribution (median and scatter) grounded in independent detections, and a decision on whether radio-mode feedback is generically effective or gated by environment.

Caveats

Radio-mode activity is episodic and environment-dependent; matched multi-wavelength samples are observationally expensive.

Method2 evidence basis: claim 2947 (review row 28095, radio-mode observation 28131, cautions 28108, 28062).

Proposal 4

Is M51-scale feedback (including positive feedback) common? A population-frequency test

Research question

Do the low-power kinetic mode and the locally positive, star-formation-triggering feedback characterized in M51 occur across the nearby active-galaxy population, and at what frequency?

What studies already show

Several scoped results come from one galaxy, M51 — including a preserved caution that AGN feedback can be locally positive, compressing gas and triggering star formation. These are single-galaxy findings that may not generalize, and positive feedback presently has no dedicated place in the claim set.

What remains unknown

The population frequency of low-power kinetic and positive feedback: whether M51 is typical or peculiar, and whether positive feedback is common enough to warrant its own claim.

Survey/data plan

PHANGS-style ALMA + MUSE, and MaNGA: resolved molecular gas and star-formation maps of nearby active galaxies, to find M51-like compression-triggered star formation at jet/outflow interfaces. Matched inactive controls: to isolate the nucleus's role. (Proposed data to use.)

Analysis/test

Define M51 diagnostic signatures (a low-power kinetic mode; localized compression with enhanced star formation at outflow interfaces); search a resolved nearby-galaxy sample; measure the fraction showing local positive versus negative feedback, relative to controls.

Expected result or decision point

A population frequency for positive feedback, and a promotion rule: if it exceeds a set frequency with clear diagnostics, positive feedback becomes its own claim rather than a single-galaxy caution.

Caveats

Resolved multiphase data are limited to nearby galaxies; positive and negative feedback can coexist in one system; single-galaxy priors may not transfer.

Method2 evidence basis: M51-scoped rows (28074, 28091, 28155) and the no-current-target positive-feedback caution (28060).

Proposal 5

Where does stellar feedback stop being enough? Locating the stellar-to-AGN transition mass

Research question

At what stellar or halo mass does stellar feedback cease to be able to quench a galaxy on its own, so that AGN feedback becomes necessary?

What studies already show

The source basis shows stellar feedback driving strong outflows and a baryon deficiency in low-mass galaxies, scaling with star-formation rate. It also reports that stellar feedback alone is generally insufficient to fully quench high-mass galaxies. Only these two endpoints are stated; the crossover mass is not measured.

What remains unknown

The transition mass or regime where stellar-only feedback budgets fail and AGN feedback becomes required. The boundary between the low-mass (stellar-sufficient) and high-mass (AGN-needed) statements is unmeasured.

Survey/data plan

DESI + MOSDEF: outflow mass-loading and quenched fractions across the stellar-mass function. JWST/NIRSpec: high-redshift massive quiescent galaxies. ALMA: cold-gas depletion times versus mass. GAMA / COSMOS: mass- and redshift-matched parent samples for denominators. Simulations: feedback-budget priors. (Proposed data to use.)

Analysis/test

Measure outflow mass-loading factor and quenched fraction versus stellar and halo mass; find the mass at which the stellar-feedback energy/momentum budget falls below what is needed to quench; test whether AGN indicators rise above that mass.

Expected result or decision point

A transition mass, with uncertainty, that separates the stellar-sufficient regime from the AGN-required regime.

Caveats

Stellar and nuclear contributions are degenerate; the result depends on careful mass/redshift sample matching.

Method2 evidence basis: claim 2944 (low-mass stellar-outflow rows 28069, 28073; high-mass-insufficiency row 28088).

Proposal 6

Strengthening the evidence base: citation-linking, full-text verification, reconsideration criteria (methods appendix)

Research question

Which parts of the underlying evidence would most change the conclusions if better verified or citation-linked, and under what criteria would set-aside evidence be reconsidered?

What studies already show

The source basis is candid that much of its support was judged from abstracts rather than full papers, that its local source judgments are not yet matched to citable literature records, and that a set of positions was set aside as off-topic, duplicate, or wrong in scale. All three are documented rather than hidden.

What remains unknown

Which abstract-only positions would change a conclusion if fully verified, and which set-aside positions would flip under new evidence. This prioritization has not been done.

Survey/data plan

NASA ADS plus the local bibliography: match each local source judgment to a citable record (a future citation-binding step; metadata only, not observational evidence). The existing local corpus: rank abstract-only positions for full-text verification. No new observations. (Documentation-only inputs.)

Analysis/test

Build a proposed source-judgment-to-citable-record mapping table; rank abstract-only positions by how much a full-text upgrade would shift a conclusion; pair each set-aside reason with the specific evidence that would overturn it.

Expected result or decision point

A prioritized full-text-verification queue, explicit reconsideration criteria, and a ranked evidence-gap list.

Caveats

Citation-binding and any change of verification status are separate, gated steps not performed here; this uses catalogue metadata only.

Method2 evidence basis: the abstract-only accepted-limited positions, the currently unlinked local source judgments, and the twelve set-aside positions.
