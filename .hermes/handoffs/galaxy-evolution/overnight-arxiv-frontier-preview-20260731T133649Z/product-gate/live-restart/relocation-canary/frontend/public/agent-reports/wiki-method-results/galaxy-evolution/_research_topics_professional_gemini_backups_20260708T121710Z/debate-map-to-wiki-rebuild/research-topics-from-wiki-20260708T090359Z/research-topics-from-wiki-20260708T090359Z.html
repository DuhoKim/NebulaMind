Galaxy Evolution: A Research Prospectus from a Debate-Map Synthesis (Method 3)

Six proposed studies addressing unresolved questions in galaxy quenching. For each, prior evidence is stated with links to the underlying Method 3 evidence basis, the remaining uncertainty is specified, and a data and analysis plan defines a decision criterion.

Status. This is a static, docs-only prospectus. The proposals are hypotheses for future work, not accepted results, and no product citations are bound. Each evidence statement links to the corresponding section of the local Method 3 evidence basis, which lists the underlying source identifiers; survey and instrument names denote data a study would acquire, not datasets that already establish a result.

1. Isolating the causal contribution of AGN feedback to central-galaxy quenching

Research question. At fixed halo mass, environment, and stellar mass, what fraction of central-galaxy quenching is attributable to active galactic nucleus (AGN) feedback rather than to halo, environmental, or stellar-feedback channels?

Prior evidence and constraints

  • Central black-hole, bulge, and velocity-dispersion properties are reported to correlate with central-galaxy quenching, though as coupled predictors rather than isolated causal channels evidence basis §4.
  • The source synthesis classifies AGN dominance as actively debated and does not designate a single dominant cause §4.
  • Environmental and internal alternative channels (strangulation, stripping, gas retention, stellar feedback) are recorded as established, required context §5, §3.

Remaining uncertainty

The causal partition among channels at fixed host properties is unresolved; existing correlations do not separate AGN power from the host structure with which it co-varies.

Data and measurement plan

  • DESI, SDSS/GAMA — mass-, environment-, and redshift-matched parent samples defining quenched-fraction denominators.
  • SDSS/MaNGA, MUSE — spatially resolved star-formation histories and central structural measures.
  • Chandra/XMM/eROSITA, VLA/LOFAR — per-object X-ray and radio AGN power and state.
  • IllustrisTNG, HORIZON-AGN-class simulations — with/without-AGN counterfactuals used as priors to be tested against observations, not as evidence.

Analysis and decision criterion

Regress quenched fraction on AGN power within matched host bins and compare the AGN-attributable residual to the simulation counterfactuals. Support for the hypothesis requires an AGN-attributable quenching excess that exceeds the combined alternative channels at defined significance; failure to exceed it refutes AGN dominance in that regime.

Limitations

AGN activity and host structure are selection-coupled; AGN power is time-variable; feedback prescriptions differ between simulations. Non-binding, docs-only status.

Provenance: axes dominance_debate, mechanism_ejective_feedback, alternatives_countercases; source identifiers clc_agn2299_003, clc_agn_009, clc_agn_010.

2. A tracer-resolved, common-denominator census of AGN-driven outflows

Research question. Under a single selection function and denominator, what is the incidence of AGN-driven outflows as a function of gas-phase tracer and redshift?

Prior evidence and constraints

  • The synthesis records tracer-specific outflow-incidence fractions, including an approximately 17 per cent ionized-outflow rate in one cosmic-noon AGN sample and an approximately 46 per cent neutral-outflow rate in a massive-galaxy sample, and states these must not be combined evidence basis §4.
  • Molecular, ionized, and neutral outflows are reported in selected AGN hosts, but under heterogeneous selections; outflow prevalence is classified as emerging and sample-limited §4.

Remaining uncertainty

No common denominator links the reported fractions; the population-wide incidence by gas phase and redshift is unconstrained.

Data and measurement plan

  • MOSDEF, JWST/NIRSpec — rest-optical ionized-outflow detection across redshift.
  • Na I D absorption spectroscopy — neutral-phase outflows in the same parent sample.
  • ALMA CO/[C II] — molecular-outflow mass and loading factor.
  • COSMOS/CANDELS, DESI — one mass- and redshift-selected parent sample fixing the denominator.

Analysis and decision criterion

Apply a single selection and redshift grid, measure per-tracer detection fractions at matched sensitivity, and report tracer-resolved prevalence with explicit denominators. The result is decisive when phase-resolved incidence is measured on a common sample; a merged single rate is treated as uninformative.

Limitations

Sensitivity varies by tracer and distance; phase mismatch can double-count or omit outflows; individual cases are not prevalence anchors. Non-binding, docs-only status.

Provenance: axis outflow_prevalence_frequency; source identifiers clc_agn_002a (ionized fraction), clc_agn_002b (neutral fraction).

3. Distinguishing reservoir removal from inefficient star formation in quenching galaxies

Research question. What is the population fraction of quenching galaxies that have lost their gas reservoirs versus those that retain gas at low star-formation efficiency, and does AGN activity track central depletion specifically?

Prior evidence and constraints

  • Central, kiloparsec-scale molecular depletion is reported in some quenched systems evidence basis §3.
  • Other quiescent systems are recorded as retaining substantial gas at low star-formation efficiency, so suppressed star formation does not imply a depleted reservoir §3.
  • The synthesis states that central-gas expulsion does not by itself imply galaxy-wide reservoir loss §4.

Remaining uncertainty

The relative frequency of removed versus retained-inefficient quenching is unmeasured, and whether AGN activity predicts central rather than galaxy-wide depletion is untested.

Data and measurement plan

  • ALMA CO/[C II] — resolved molecular gas mass and depletion time, central versus galaxy-wide.
  • SDSS/MaNGA, MUSE — resolved star-formation efficiency and stellar populations.
  • X-ray/radio AGN-stage indicators — duty-cycle ordering linking reservoir state to AGN phase.

Analysis and decision criterion

Compare resolved depletion time against AGN stage and galactocentric radius; classify systems as depleted, retained-inefficient, or intermediate. The hypothesis that AGN drives central depletion is supported if AGN hosts concentrate in the central-depletion class at fixed mass, and refuted if depletion is independent of AGN stage.

Limitations

Diffuse and atomic gas may be missed; depletion time depends on the star-formation tracer; central and galaxy-wide measures must be kept distinct. Non-binding, docs-only status.

Provenance: axis reservoir_response; source identifiers clc_agn_005 (retention / low efficiency), clc_agn_006 (central-kpc depletion).

4. An observational determination of the maintenance-heating duty cycle

Research question. Across a halo-mass-controlled population, does observed radio-mode heating power balance X-ray cooling luminosity when averaged over the AGN duty cycle?

Prior evidence and constraints

  • Maintenance (preventive) heating is classified in the synthesis as model-dependent — supported in simulations but not established as an observed galaxy-scale prevalence evidence basis §4.
  • Simulation-based statements are recorded as model-scope, not observed prevalence, and are kept distinct from ejective feedback §8.

Remaining uncertainty

The observed heating-to-cooling balance, and its dependence on halo mass, has not been established from cavity and duty-cycle measurements in this synthesis.

Data and measurement plan

  • Chandra/XMM/eROSITA — X-ray cavity energetics, cooling luminosity, hot-halo thermodynamics.
  • VLA/LOFAR/MeerKAT — radio jet power, morphology, and duty cycle.
  • Halo-mass-spanning group and cluster samples — to resolve the balance as a function of halo mass.

Analysis and decision criterion

Compare duty-cycle-averaged heating power to X-ray cooling luminosity per system across the controlled sample. A statistically balanced heating budget promotes maintenance heating from model-supported to observationally bounded; a deficit sets an upper bound on its role.

Limitations

Duty-cycle averaging and cavity energetics carry systematic uncertainty; the lowest-mass halos are hardest to constrain; model expectation must not be read as observation. Non-binding, docs-only status.

Provenance: axis maintenance_heating_prevention; source identifier clc_agn_004.

5. Forward-modeled validation of simulation feedback predictions against unbiased surveys

Research question. Which simulation-predicted outflow, heating, and quenching statistics survive comparison with unbiased survey samples once the survey selection function is applied to the simulation?

Prior evidence and constraints

  • The synthesis labels simulation-only statements as model-dependent and treats them as demonstrations of what feedback can produce under assumptions, not as observed prevalence evidence basis §8.

Remaining uncertainty

Which specific model predictions are observationally supported after selection is applied is untested; some may exist only in unselected simulation space.

Data and measurement plan

  • IllustrisTNG, HORIZON-AGN-class simulations — mock catalogs of outflow, heating, and quenching observables (the model under test).
  • DESI, SDSS, JWST deep fields — unbiased observed comparison samples with defined selection functions.

Analysis and decision criterion

Forward-model the simulations into each survey's selection and compare predicted and observed distributions. A prediction is validated when it matches the selected observations within stated tolerance, and invalidated when it appears only without selection.

Limitations

Forward-model fidelity limits the test; sub-grid feedback differs between simulations; a match under one selection may fail under another. Non-binding, docs-only status.

Provenance: axis simulation_model_scope; source identifier clc_agn_011.

6. Rebalancing the multi-channel evidence base: chemical, structural, and high-redshift constraints

Research question. Which under-mapped channels — chemical enrichment and cosmic timing, morphological and structural growth, halo regulation, and the reionization frontier — carry sufficient evidence to weigh against AGN feedback, and which require new measurements?

Prior evidence and constraints

  • The mass–metallicity relation is reported with modest intrinsic scatter at cosmic noon, and the fundamental metallicity relation approximately stable to about 0.1 dex from the local universe to redshift ~2.3 evidence basis §6.
  • The reionization frontier is framed as open debate, including the ionizing-photon budget and the JWST high-redshift (z>10) high-stellar-mass tension, not as settled findings §7.
  • Halo regulation and morphological/structural growth are recorded as scoped, lightly-covered sections resting largely on unverified rows §2, §5.

Remaining uncertainty

Whether these channels' evidence can be ranked against AGN feedback, and their redshift-resolved behavior beyond the currently scoped ranges, is unresolved.

Data and measurement plan

  • JWST/NIRSpec (JADES/AURORA-class programs), SDSS/MaNGA — mass–metallicity and stellar-age measurements across redshift.
  • HST–JWST deep imaging, COSMOS/CANDELS — size–mass and morphology evolution.
  • DESI with weak-lensing and clustering — halo-mass regulation of quenching.
  • JWST spectroscopy — reionization-era ionizing-photon and stellar-mass constraints.

Analysis and decision criterion

Construct redshift-resolved scaling relations and halo-regulation measures on a consistent selection and compare their evidential weight to the AGN-feedback channel. The output is a ranked evidence gap identifying which channel most requires new data before the multi-channel account is balanced.

Limitations

Each relation carries its own redshift scope and must not be extrapolated; unverified source rows are not treated as settled evidence. Non-binding, docs-only status.

Provenance: scoped coverage sections — Dark Matter Halos & Structure Formation, Environment/Morphology & Structural Growth, Chemical Enrichment & Cosmic Timing, High-Redshift & Reionization Frontier.
