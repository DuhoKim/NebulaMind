Overview: Galaxy Evolution as a Regulated Baryon Cycle

framing · multi-channelEvidence basis →

Galaxy evolution is the coupled history of stars, gas, supermassive black holes, dark matter halos, and environments, not the action of any single master cause — a regulated baryon cycle in which halos assemble and draw in gas, galaxies convert only part of it into stars, stellar and black-hole feedback return energy and enriched material, and a set of quenching processes eventually slows or halts star formation. Quenching is a central, multi-channel event; which channel dominates depends on sample selection, redshift, and how quenching is measured.

Dark Matter Halos & Structure Formation

scoped: halo-mass/assemblyhalo-vs-central OPENEvidence basis →

Halos set the gravitational stage, but halo mass alone does not fix baryon-to-star efficiency; halo mass and assembly history both regulate accretion and later suppression, and a feedback mechanism beyond stellar feedback appears important above the mass of peak star-formation efficiency. Whether halo mass or central/black-hole properties is the primary predictor of central-galaxy quenching is an open debate. This section is scoped to halo-mass regulation and assembly; broad cosmological structure formation is not resolved here.

Gas Supply, Star Formation & Feedback

reservoir: actively debatedalternatives: supportedEvidence basis →

Star formation tracks the usable cold-gas supply and its replenishment timescale; stellar feedback bridges local star formation to galaxy-scale regulation, especially in lower-mass halos. Reservoir evidence is mixed: a shortage of star formation is not a shortage of gas — some quenched systems show central kiloparsec-scale depletion, others retain gas but form stars inefficiently. Central depletion is not galaxy-wide gas absence.

AGN Feedback & Quenching

mechanism: supportedoutflow prevalence: emergingdominance: debatedmaintenance: model-dependentEvidence basis →unmatched: 2915/2921/2913 (P3)

In selected systems, AGN/SMBH feedback can regulate or reduce star formation via jets, winds, turbulence, circumgalactic heating, and internal starvation (kept distinct from environmental strangulation); the corpus does not support generic quenching. Outflows are the strongest observational grounding but their frequencies are tracer-, selection-, and redshift-specific — not population-wide rates. Ejective feedback is distinct from preventive/maintenance heating, which is best rendered as model-dependent. Whether AGN feedback dominates is genuinely debated; central observables are correlational predictors, not causal switches.

Environment, Morphology & Structural Growth

environment: supportedmorphology: scopedEvidence basis →unmatched: 2133→2605.22497 (P3)

Environment reshapes galaxies by stripping, starving, perturbing, and merging; after infall, satellites can quench via ram-pressure/tidal stripping and strangulation (distinct from AGN starvation), but removal is not automatic and the dominant channel is system-dependent. Morphological transformation and structural growth — mergers, bulge growth, size evolution — accompany quenching without proving a cause; black-hole/bulge correlations are predictors, not demonstrated causal channels.

Chemical Enrichment & Cosmic Timing

scoped: MZR/FMR, z~0–2.3Evidence basis →

Metallicity scaling relations act as clocks and regulators. The mass–metallicity relation links stellar mass to gas-phase oxygen abundance with modest scatter at cosmic noon; the fundamental metallicity relation is roughly stable to ~0.1 dex from the local universe out to $z\sim2.3$; the MZR normalization evolves with redshift and its scatter drivers are debated. These carry their redshift scope (z ~ 0–2.3), not a broad universal chemical-evolution narrative. Stellar-age distributions in quenched galaxies indicate a variety of histories.

High-Redshift & Reionization Frontier

cosmic noon: sample/model-dependentreionization: OPEN frontierEvidence basis →unmatched: 2374 seeding (P3)

Two regimes must not be blurred. At cosmic noon (z ~ 1.5–3) some massive galaxies shut down quickly, but selected cases are not a universal high-redshift rule. The reionization-era frontier is a set of open debates with named positions: the ionizing-photon budget (star-forming galaxies vs faint AGN); helium II reionization near z ~ 3 kept distinct from hydrogen reionization at z > 6; and the JWST z > 10 "too-massive/too-early" tension (top-heavy IMF vs enhanced baryon-conversion efficiency vs bursty star formation), presented as unresolved.

Observational Evidence & Surveys

The evidence base is multi-wavelength and redshift-dependent, each mode reducing a specific ambiguity: rest-frame optical/NIR spectra for stellar populations and metallicities; submillimetre/radio for gas and obscured star formation; X-ray/radio for AGN power and hot atmospheres; IFU for resolved outflows and quenching. Simulation-based statements are labeled "in simulations / in this model." Specific survey/instrument enumeration is deferred to the P3 binding gate.

Synthesis & Open Tensions

synthesis · all 7 axesEvidence basis →

Current evidence supports a context-dependent, multi-channel account rather than a single-cause story: halo growth sets the stage, accretion and recycling provide fuel, stellar and AGN feedback regulate efficiency, and environment alters boundary conditions after infall. The open frontier is when each mechanism dominates and how strongly it couples to observable gas phases — outflow prevalence, AGN dominance, reservoir response, and maintenance heating all remain actively debated. Uncertainty is reported as a useful result; sourced binding of claims is the P3 next step.
