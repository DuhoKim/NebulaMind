## Overview: Galaxy Evolution as a Regulated Baryon Cycle

**Trust framing:** multi-channel synthesis; anchored to the widely supported alternatives/countercases axis.  
**Local provenance:** coverage map §overview; prior evidence basis §1.

Galaxy evolution is the coupled history of stars, gas, supermassive black holes, dark matter halos, and environments. A useful page should not read as though one mechanism explains all galaxies. It should read as a regulated baryon cycle: halos assemble and acquire gas, galaxies turn only part of that gas into stars, stellar and black-hole feedback return energy and enriched material to surrounding reservoirs, and quenching processes eventually slow or halt star formation.

That cycle gives the page its causal structure. Gas supply, star-formation efficiency, feedback, halo growth, environment, and cosmic time all matter, but they matter differently by mass range, epoch, and measurement strategy. Quenching is therefore not a single switch. It can involve internal mass-linked processes, central structure, black-hole feedback, environment-linked stripping or strangulation, low star-formation efficiency, and retained reservoirs that complicate simple gas-loss stories.

The Method3 stance is to make those interactions visible and to state uncertainty as part of the result. The page distinguishes mechanisms that are well supported in scoped contexts from questions that remain sample-limited, actively debated, or model-dependent. This is why the evidence/trust layer is shown as a debate-map overlay rather than as product claim chips.

## Dark Matter Halos & Structure Formation

**Trust framing:** scoped coverage extension; halo mass and assembly are locally supported, but broad cosmological structure formation is not fully rebuilt here.  
**Local provenance:** coverage map §halos; prior evidence basis §2.

Dark matter halos set the gravitational stage for galaxy formation. Their mass and assembly history affect how gas is acquired, retained, heated, or converted into stars. But halo mass alone does not determine a galaxy's visible outcome. Similar halo masses can host different stellar populations because gas supply, merger history, metallicity, environment, feedback timing, and central structure redirect the baryon cycle.

The live debate is not whether halos matter. It is how halo effects rank against central predictors such as velocity dispersion, bulge mass, and black-hole mass when explaining central-galaxy quenching. One local thread supports halo-mass regulation and assembly history; another treats the halo-mass correlation as secondary to central properties. A careful wiki page keeps those positions separate rather than declaring a winner.

This section is intentionally scoped. It can support a reader-facing account of halo regulation, halo assembly, and the halo-versus-central-predictor debate. It should not be stretched into a broad, standalone explanation of cosmological structure formation unless a later source pass adds and binds that material.

## Gas Supply, Star Formation & Feedback

**Trust framing:** reservoir response actively debated; non-AGN alternatives widely supported and required context.  
**Local provenance:** coverage map §gas; prior evidence basis §3.

The gas cycle links accretion, the interstellar medium, the circumgalactic medium, star formation, feedback, and recycling. Star formation follows usable cold gas, but "gas present" is not the same as "stars forming efficiently." Some systems can retain gas and still form stars slowly, while others show central depletion or suppressed inflow. A reader needs those outcomes separated because they imply different mechanisms.

Stellar feedback remains one of the clearest routes from local star formation to galaxy-scale regulation. It can drive outflows, stir surrounding gas, enrich the circumgalactic medium, and influence how much material later returns. In lower-mass halos, this feedback can strongly shape the baryon cycle. In more massive systems, the same vocabulary can be misleading if it hides the role of hot halos, black-hole feedback, or environmental processing.

The reservoir question is an active debate. Central kiloparsec depletion, galaxy-wide gas absence, suppressed inflow, turbulent support, morphological stabilization, and stripping in dense environments are not interchangeable. Method3 keeps these cases apart so that later evidence binding can attach the right observation to the right physical claim.

## AGN Feedback & Quenching

**Trust framing:** AGN/SMBH mechanism widely supported in selected systems; outflow prevalence emerging and sample-limited; dominance actively debated; maintenance heating model-dependent in this corpus.  
**Local provenance:** coverage map §agn; prior evidence basis §4.

Active galactic nuclei and central supermassive black holes are real regulatory channels, but the defensible wording is conditional. In selected systems, AGN and SMBH feedback can affect gas through jets, winds, turbulence, circumgalactic heating, and starvation. The sign and strength of those effects depend on feedback mode, gas phase, host conditions, and measurement scale. Locally positive feedback can also occur, so the page should not imply that every AGN episode quenches its host.

Outflows provide concrete observational grounding, but prevalence remains difficult to generalize. Molecular, ionized, and neutral outflows are observed in selected AGN-host and massive-galaxy samples. Their frequencies depend on tracer, selection, redshift, and detection threshold. A single striking case is an example, not a population anchor, and two tracer-specific fractions should not be merged into one universal rate.

The physical modes also need separation. Ejective or outflow feedback is not the same as preventive or maintenance heating. In this corpus, maintenance and preventive heating are best treated as model-dependent or bounded: simulations and models can show how such mechanisms work under assumptions, but they do not by themselves establish observed population prevalence. The page can say these channels are plausible and important in models without upgrading them to settled observational facts.

The central question is dominance. AGN feedback may be important, especially in massive systems and hot halos, but it sits alongside gas supply, halo growth, environment, stellar feedback, central structure, and observational selection. Central black-hole, bulge, and velocity-dispersion correlations should be described as coupled predictors unless a later bound source supports a narrower causal statement.

## Environment, Morphology & Structural Growth

**Trust framing:** environmental pathways supported; morphology and structural growth are scoped coverage extensions with correlational guards.  
**Local provenance:** coverage map §environment; prior evidence basis §5.

Environment changes galaxies by stripping, starving, perturbing, and merging them. After infall into groups or clusters, satellite galaxies can undergo quenching through ram-pressure stripping, tidal processing, strangulation, or combinations of several channels. Environmental strangulation is the gradual cutoff of fresh gas supply and should remain distinct from AGN-internal starvation in the previous section.

Dense environments do not act through one universal pathway. Some galaxies lose gas rapidly; others retain reservoirs but form stars inefficiently; still others undergo structural transformation while quenching proceeds. The useful synthesis is system-dependent rather than one-channel. It should distinguish groups, clusters, satellites, isolated massive galaxies, and cosmic-web context when the source basis allows.

Morphology and structural growth belong in the map, but with caution. Bulge growth, central stellar mass density, mergers, size evolution, and black-hole growth often accompany quenching, yet accompaniment is not proof of an independent cause. Method3 therefore treats black-hole and bulge relations as correlational predictors and reads morphology as part of a coupled evolutionary state.

## Chemical Enrichment & Cosmic Timing

**Trust framing:** scoped coverage extension; strongest local basis is mass-metallicity/fundamental-metallicity relation and stellar-age/abundance timing.  
**Local provenance:** coverage map §chemical; prior evidence basis §6.

Chemical enrichment records the integrated history of star formation, inflow, outflow, and recycling. Metallicity scaling relations act as clocks and regulators because they encode how much gas has been processed into stars, how much enriched material has been lost, and how much lower-metallicity gas has diluted the interstellar medium.

The local source basis supports a scoped account of the mass-metallicity relation and the fundamental metallicity relation. It supports redshift-aware language: the relation between stellar mass, gas-phase oxygen abundance, and star-formation rate is discussed around the local universe through cosmic noon, with a rough scope of z ~ 0 to 2.3 for the strongest FMR stability statement. It does not support a sweeping chemical-evolution history across all epochs.

Cosmic timing adds a second dimension. Stellar-age distributions and spatial gradients show that galaxies can reach similar present-day classes through varied histories. Some massive systems have older central populations and younger outer regions; recently quenched systems can preserve different formation timescales. The page should use that distributional language rather than imply one shared timeline.

## High-Redshift & Reionization Frontier

**Trust framing:** cosmic-noon shutdown sample/model-dependent; reionization and z > 10 tensions are open frontier debates.  
**Local provenance:** coverage map §high-redshift; prior evidence basis §7.

High redshift covers more than one regime. At cosmic noon, roughly z ~ 1.5 to 3, massive galaxies can show rapid shutdown and AGN-linked quenching is discussed as a fast channel. Those claims are important but sample- and model-dependent. They should not become a universal high-redshift rule.

The reionization-era frontier is even more open. One debate concerns the ionizing-photon budget: whether star-forming galaxies supplied enough photons, whether faint AGN contributed significantly, and how strong emission-line galaxies near z ~ 7 affect the accounting. A separate lower-redshift question concerns helium II reionization near z ~ 3; it should not be conflated with hydrogen reionization at z > 6.

JWST-era z > 10 massive-galaxy tensions also belong here, but as tensions rather than conclusions. High inferred stellar masses can be discussed alongside possible explanations such as a top-heavy initial mass function, enhanced baryon-conversion efficiency, bursty star formation, dust and modeling effects, and halo-abundance expectations. The page should present the tension as an active constraint on models, not as a simple falsification of the cosmological frame.

## Observational Evidence & Surveys

**Trust framing:** evidence-method framing; simulations model-dependent; detailed survey enumeration remains future binding work.  
**Local provenance:** coverage map §observational; prior evidence basis §8.

The evidence base is multi-wavelength and redshift-dependent. Rest-frame optical and near-infrared spectra constrain stellar populations, nebular lines, metallicities, and dust. Submillimetre and radio data trace molecular and atomic gas plus dust-obscured star formation. X-ray and radio signatures identify AGN power, hot atmospheres, and cavities. Integral-field spectroscopy maps outflows, rotation, turbulence, and spatially resolved quenching. Wide surveys connect individual mechanisms to population statistics and large-scale environment.

The point is not to list facilities for their own sake. Each observing mode reduces a specific ambiguity: whether gas is absent or inefficient, whether an outflow is local or galaxy-scale, whether a quenching signature is central or environmental, and whether a result is a single system or a population trend. This section should remain a guide to evidence types until P3 binds particular facilities, papers, and evidence IDs.

Simulations belong beside observations, not inside them. A simulation can test a mechanism under assumptions, explore feedback coupling, or show what maintenance heating can do in a model. It does not automatically measure observed prevalence. Method3's trust layer therefore labels simulation-backed claims as model-dependent unless a later product binding supplies direct observational support.

## Synthesis & Open Tensions

**Trust framing:** synthesis across all seven debate axes; open tensions are carried forward rather than hidden.  
**Local provenance:** coverage map §synthesis; prior evidence basis §9.

Current evidence supports a context-dependent, multi-channel account of galaxy evolution. Halo growth creates the gravitational setting; gas accretion and recycling provide fuel; stellar and AGN feedback regulate conversion efficiency; and groups, clusters, satellites, and cosmic-web geometry alter boundary conditions after infall. No one mechanism can be promoted to universal cause without losing the structure of the evidence.

The open frontier is not whether any single mechanism matters. It is when each mechanism dominates, how strongly it couples to observable gas phases, how those answers change with mass and redshift, and how selection effects shape the sample. AGN outflow prevalence, AGN dominance relative to other quenching channels, reservoir response, maintenance heating, z > 10 mass tensions, cold-stream survival, simulation resolution, stripping versus starvation, and reionization source budgets all remain live areas for careful binding.
