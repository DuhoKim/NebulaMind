Overview: Galaxy Evolution as a Regulated Baryon Cycle

framing · multi-channel rests on the widely-supported alternatives axis

Galaxy evolution is best read not as a single mechanism but as a regulated baryon cycle: dark-matter halos assemble and draw in gas, galaxies convert only part of it into stars, stellar and black-hole feedback return energy and enriched material, and quenching processes eventually slow or halt star formation. The pivot the page circles is quenching — the transition from star-forming to passive — presented as a genuinely multi-channel process, where which channel dominates depends on sample selection, redshift, and how quenching is measured.

Dark Matter Halos & Structure Formation

scoped: halo mass / assembly halo-vs-central OPEN

A galaxy's halo sets the gravitational stage, but halo mass alone does not fix baryon-to-star efficiency. Halo mass and assembly history together regulate accretion and later suppression, and a feedback mechanism beyond stellar feedback appears important above the mass of peak star-formation efficiency. Because assembly history — not just present-day mass — shapes galaxy properties, similar halos can reach different outcomes. Whether halo mass or central/black-hole properties is the primary quenching predictor is a real, unresolved debate.

Supported by claims 2572, 2557, 2573, 2233, 2570, 2912, 2338; sources 2512.16290v1, 2401.12953, 2508.11846v3, 1804.07798v2 (atlas Physical Mechanisms: 4 accepted / 3 challenged / 7 reported / 16 unverified).   Limited by scoped to halo-mass/assembly; broad structure formation out of scope.   Unbound here no product cite IDs; halo-vs-central left open. basis →

Gas Supply, Star Formation & Feedback

reservoir: actively debated alternatives: supported

Star formation tracks the usable cold-gas supply and its replenishment timescale; stellar feedback bridges local star formation to galaxy-scale regulation, especially in lower-mass halos. The key caution: a shortage of star formation is not a shortage of gas — some quenched systems show central kiloparsec-scale depletion, others retain gas but form stars inefficiently. Central depletion is not galaxy-wide gas absence.

AGN Feedback & Quenching

mechanism: supported outflow prevalence: emerging dominance: debated maintenance: model-dependent

AGN and their central black holes are a real regulatory channel, but the careful statement is conditional. In selected systems, AGN/SMBH feedback can regulate or reduce star formation via jets, winds, turbulence, circumgalactic heating, and internal starvation — kept distinct from environmental strangulation. The corpus does not support generic quenching. Outflows are the strongest grounding, but their frequencies are tracer-, selection-, and redshift-specific, so one selection's fraction is not a population rate, and a single case is not a prevalence anchor. Ejective feedback is distinct from preventive/maintenance heating (model-dependent). Whether AGN feedback dominates is genuinely debated; central observables are correlational predictors, not causal switches.

Supported by focus-claim 2929; axes mechanism_ejective_feedback, outflow_prevalence_frequency (clc_agn_002a_mosdef_17pct_ionized_outflows, clc_agn_002b_jwst_46pct_neutral_naid_outflows), dominance_debate, maintenance_heating_prevention; claims 2917, 2924 (atlas AGN Feedback & Quenching: 2 accepted / 3 debated / 2 reported / 1 unverified).   Limited by outflow fractions cited as tracer/selection-specific, never merged.   Unmatched claim IDs 2915, 2921, 2913 are body-only (not in the atlas snapshot) — re-resolve at P3. basis →

Environment, Morphology & Structural Growth

environment: supported morphology: scoped

Environment reshapes galaxies by stripping, starving, perturbing, and merging them — channels that belong in the explanation, not a footnote. After infall, satellites can quench via ram-pressure/tidal stripping and environmental strangulation (distinct from internal AGN starvation), but removal is not automatic and the dominant channel is system-dependent. Morphological transformation and structural growth — mergers, bulge growth, size evolution — accompany quenching without proving a cause; black-hole/bulge correlations are predictors, not demonstrated causal channels.

Chemical Enrichment & Cosmic Timing

scoped: MZR/FMR, z ≈ 0–2.3

Metallicity scaling relations act as both clocks and regulators. The mass–metallicity relation links stellar mass to gas-phase oxygen abundance with modest scatter at cosmic noon; the fundamental metallicity relation is roughly stable to ~0.1 dex from the local universe to z ≈ 2.3; the normalization evolves with redshift and its scatter drivers are debated. These carry their redshift scope — not generalized to all epochs, and not a broad universal chemical-evolution narrative. Stellar-age distributions in quenched galaxies indicate a variety of histories, not one timeline.

Supported by claims 2731, 2725, 2738, 2426, 2427, 2656, 2728, 2253, 2338, 2227, 2579, 2580, 2234; sources 2512.16989v1 (AURORA), 2606.11345 (JADES), 2606.05284, 2605.29623, 2605.25557, 2605.27555, 2604.03503, 2605.13966.   Limited by scoped to MZR/FMR + resolved ages, z ≈ 0–2.3.   Unbound here no product cite IDs. basis →

High-Redshift & Reionization Frontier

cosmic noon: sample/model-dependent reionization: OPEN frontier

Two regimes must not be blurred. At cosmic noon (z ≈ 1.5–3) some massive galaxies shut down quickly, but selected cases are not a universal high-redshift rule. The reionization-era frontier is presented as open debates with named positions: the ionizing-photon budget (star-forming galaxies vs faint AGN); helium II reionization near z ≈ 3 kept distinct from hydrogen reionization at z > 6; and the JWST z > 10 "too-massive/too-early" tension (top-heavy IMF vs enhanced baryon-conversion efficiency vs bursty star formation), presented as unresolved.

Supported by claims 2836, 2798, 2736, 2754, 2735, 2698, 2812, 2811, 2805, 2619, 2618, 2625, 2235; sources 2606.05323, 2605.24112 (Lumina), 2605.26209, 2604.13866, 2606.02738, 2512.16981v1, 2605.20698 (atlas Open Questions: 3 accepted / 2 challenged / 1 debated / 4 reported / 59 unverified).   Limited by competing positions, none settled.   Unmatched claim 2374 (EoR seeding) has garbled text — repair/drop at P3; cold-gas part (2235) is supported. basis →

Observational Evidence & Surveys

evidence framing simulations: model-dependent

The evidence base is multi-wavelength and redshift-dependent, each mode reducing a specific ambiguity: optical/near-IR spectra for stellar populations and metallicities; submillimetre/radio for gas and obscured star formation; X-ray/radio for AGN power and hot atmospheres; integral-field spectroscopy for resolved outflows and quenching. Simulation-based statements are labeled as such ("in simulations / in this model"), because a simulation-only result establishes what a model can produce, not an observed frequency.

Synthesis & Open Tensions

synthesis · all 7 axes

Current evidence supports a context-dependent, multi-channel account rather than a single-cause story: halo growth sets the stage, accretion and recycling provide fuel, stellar and AGN feedback regulate efficiency, environment alters boundary conditions after infall. The open frontier is not whether a mechanism matters but when each dominates and how strongly it couples to observable gas phases. Outflow prevalence, AGN dominance, reservoir response, and maintenance heating all remain actively debated.
