## Claim 2942 — AGN feedback is scoped, not universal  
_Trust: qualified_

<!--claim:2942-->AGN feedback is best read as a context-dependent regulator rather than a universal switch that shuts down every galaxy. Everything supporting this framing is accepted-limited: a review-level synthesis stressing that AGN feedback operates through many interacting modes, a group-scale study finding its influence strongest in intermediate-mass systems, and two rows from the well-studied M51 galaxy where models both require AGN feedback and reveal one specific low-power kinetic mode. ## Claim 2944 — Stellar feedback is a real but limited alternative  
_Trust: qualified_

<!--claim:2944-->Not every galaxy-scale outflow is AGN-driven. Spectroscopic (DESI/Mg II) observations tie strong outflows and baryon deficiency in low-mass galaxies to their star-formation rate — a genuine non-AGN channel. But the same evidence base is honest about its ceiling: a high-redshift simulation result finds stellar feedback alone is generally insufficient to fully quench high-mass galaxies. All three supporting rows are accepted-limited.<!--/claim:2944--> <!--cite-unmatched:Method2 source-adjudication evidence 28069 (arXiv:2512.05584), 28073 (arXiv:2512.05584), 28088 (arXiv:2605.03008); unresolved to product cite ID--> [source positions & adjudication](../p1-source-position-ledger.html)

Evidence & trust:
  - **LIMITED** supports `e:28069` (arXiv:2512.05584) — DESI/Mg II: stellar feedback drives strong outflows in low-mass galaxies
  - **LIMITED** supports `e:28073` (arXiv:2512.05584) — outflow rate and mass-loading scale with star-formation rate
  - **LIMITED** supports `e:28088` (arXiv:2605.03008) — stellar feedback alone is insufficient to fully quench high-mass galaxies

## Claim 2945 — Gas removal competes with recycling  
_Trust: caution_

<!--claim:2945-->Observing an outflow is not the same as permanently losing the star-forming reservoir. Two accepted-limited cautions travel with any outflow claim: outflowing gas can fall back before it escapes the halo, and winds are less effective at expelling gas in low-redshift, low-mass systems. These rows exist to stop an outflow detection from being over-read as quenching.<!--/claim:2945--> <!--cite-unmatched:Method2 source-adjudication evidence 28066 (arXiv:2512.05584), 28075 (arXiv:0901.1880); unresolved to product cite ID--> [source positions & adjudication](../p1-source-position-ledger.html)

Evidence & trust:

  - **LIMITED** caution `e:28075` (arXiv:0901.1880) — winds are less effective at expelling gas in low-z, low-mass systems

## Claim 2947 — A kinetic, radio-mode channel operates in massive systems  
_Trust: qualified_

<!--claim:2947-->AGN can act through a kinetic, radio-mode channel where jets mechanically couple to the surrounding gas.
