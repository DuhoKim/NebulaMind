# Paper B — human-review-ready draft abstract
**Title:** The z≈4–6 massive-galaxy abundance is consistent with IllustrisTNG once the stellar-mass systematic budget is accounted for

**Abstract.** The reported over-abundance of massive galaxies at z>4 relative to ΛCDM hydrodynamical
simulations ("too massive, too early") has been read as possible evidence for new physics. We test it by
confronting IllustrisTNG (TNG100-1) cumulative number densities n(>M⋆, z) against JWST-era observations on a
like-for-like stellar-mass basis. At z≈5–6 the observed n(>10^10.5 M⊙) (Weibel et al. 2024, ≈3×10⁻⁵ Mpc⁻³)
exceeds TNG (1.1×10⁻⁵ Mpc⁻³ at z=5) by a factor ≈2.7; but given the steep massive-end slope of the simulated
SMF (dlog n/dlog M⋆ ≈ −1.6), this excess is fully erased by a downward stellar-mass shift of only ≈0.28 dex —
well inside the stellar-mass systematic budget, which the literature places near ~1 dex once SED-fitting priors,
IMF assumptions, nebular-emission and AGN ("Little Red Dot") contamination, and Eddington bias are included.
We therefore find no robust tension at z≈5–6: the abundance is consistent with TNG within systematics. The
larger apparent excess at z≈7–9 (Labbé et al. 2023 candidates, ≈13×) needs ≈0.44 dex — still within a plausible
budget — but rests on unconfirmed photometric masses. We flag as a distinct, harder residual the
spectroscopically-confirmed quiescent galaxies at z>6 (e.g. RUBIES-UDS-QG-z7), whose ~2 dex excess is not
dissolved by these systematics and warrants dedicated follow-up.

**Honest label:** descriptive confrontation of simulation predictions with observations on a matched
stellar-mass basis — NOT a validated measurement. Non-circular by construction (independent sim vs obs).
**Data:** TNG100-1 (smf_results.json); Weibel+2024, Labbé+2023; systematic budget per DR2 (68 sources).
