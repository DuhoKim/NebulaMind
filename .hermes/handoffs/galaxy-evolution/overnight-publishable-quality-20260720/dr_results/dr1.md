# The Epoch of Reionization Chemical Frontier: Independent Mass-Metallicity Relation Anchors at $z > 7$ in the Post-2024 JWST Era

## Executive Summary and Bottom-Line Answer

The successful deployment and continuous operation of the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) have fundamentally disrupted the established paradigms of cosmic chemical evolution. Prior to 2022, the mass-metallicity relation (MZR)—the fundamental correlation between a galaxy’s stellar mass and its gas-phase oxygen abundance, representing the integrated history of star formation and gas exchange—was largely restricted to $z \lesssim 3.3$. Higher-redshift inferences relied heavily on highly uncertain photometric proxies, uncalibrated strong-line (SL) diagnostics, or theoretical extrapolations from cosmological simulations<sup>[[1]](#source-1)</sup>. The post-2024 literature (specifically papers and preprints published in 2025 and 2026) marks a transition from the initial "discovery phase" of JWST to an era of rigorous, statistically robust spectroscopic characterization deep into the Epoch of Reionization (EoR) at $z > 7$.

**Bottom-Line Answer:** Are there genuinely independent, unlensed MZR anchors at $z > 7$, and what abundance scales do they use? Yes. The 2025–2026 literature provides a robust framework of unlensed, field-galaxy anchors that securely define the $z > 7$ MZR. These independent anchors utilize a mix of direct electron-temperature ($T_e$) measurements derived from auroral lines, carefully recalibrated stacked strong-line diagnostics, and rigorous ISM photoionization model grids, thereby bypassing the severe systematic uncertainties associated with gravitational lensing.

**Confidence Labels for Current MZR Constraints:**

- **High Confidence:** The $z > 7$ MZR exists, is mathematically definable, and exhibits a progressively lower normalization compared to the local Universe, with metal depletion dropping by $\approx 0.3$ to $0.5$ dex at fixed stellar mass relative to $z=0$ baselines<sup>[[1]](#source-1)</sup>.

- **High Confidence:** The "Auroral Bias" systematically skews individual direct-$T_e$ measurements toward lower metallicities. Stacked spectra of non-auroral emitters reveal that the true, bulk MZR normalization is $0.2-0.3$ dex higher than initially reported by early JWST studies relying exclusively on individual auroral detections<sup>[[4]](#source-4)</sup>.

- **Medium Confidence:** The Fundamental Metallicity Relation (FMR)—which posits an invariant equilibrium between gas inflow, star formation, and outflows—begins to break down at $z > 7$. Galaxies at these epochs operate in a regime of "chemical chaos" driven by bursty star formation and massive, stochastic pristine gas infall<sup>[[3]](#source-3)</sup>.

- **Medium-Low Confidence:** The absolute slope of the $z > 7$ MZR at the extreme low-mass end ($M_* < 10^{8} M_\odot$). While field surveys provide vital anchor points, the exact steepness of the slope remains contested due to the scarcity of ultra-low-mass unlensed targets and the high scatter inherent in pristine gas accretion events<sup>[[1]](#source-1)</sup>.

## Search Strategy and Methodological Filtering

To definitively answer the query and construct a reference-grade literature report, a rigorous search and filtering strategy was employed to isolate genuinely independent $z > 7$ MZR anchors from the post-2024 literature. The strategy was executed across primary astrophysical databases and preprint servers, targeting literature designated as public in 2025 or 2026, with late-2024 work included exclusively for establishing the independence or context of specific samples.

The search protocol utilized boolean combinations of the following parameters: `("James Webb Space Telescope" OR JWST)` AND `("mass-metallicity relation" OR MZR OR "gas-phase metallicity" OR "direct-Te" OR "auroral line")` AND `(z > 7 OR "redshift > 7" OR "Epoch of Reionization")` AND `(2025 OR 2026)`.

To ensure the highest fidelity of the resulting MZR anchors, strict exclusion and quarantine principles were applied to the initial results:

1.  **Exclusion of Photometric Proxies and Simple Detections:** Studies inferring metallicities solely from broadband spectral energy distribution (SED) fitting, photometric colors, or single-line detections without line-ratio context were rejected. Only spectroscopic datasets covering multiple rest-frame optical nebular emission lines (e.g., \[OII\] $\lambda\lambda 3726, 3729$, \[NeIII\] $\lambda 3869$, H\$\gamma\$, \[OIII\] $\lambda 4363$, H\$\beta\$, \[OIII\] $\lambda\lambda 4959, 5007$) or far-infrared fine-structure lines (e.g., \[OIII\] $88\mu\text{m}$) were retained for abundance calculations.

2.  **Exclusion of Simulation and Forecast Data:** Purely theoretical MZR predictions derived from cosmological hydrodynamical models (e.g., COLIBRE, IllustrisTNG, EAGLE, SIMBA) or semi-analytical forecasts were utilized solely for theoretical context and comparative analysis, not as observational anchor points<sup>[[9]](#source-9)</sup>.

3.  **Strict Quarantine of Lensed Samples:** Gravitationally lensed galaxies were categorically quarantined from the primary anchor list. While cluster lensing (e.g., Abell 2744, SMACS 0723) provides unparalleled magnification to probe intrinsic masses down to $10^{6} M_\odot$, it introduces critical systematic uncertainties. Specifically, "differential magnification" occurs because the highly ionized HII regions emitting the \[OIII\] $\lambda 4363$ auroral line are often physically compact and spatially offset from the older, more extended stellar continuum. Applying a uniform magnification factor ($\mu$) to both the nebular gas and the stellar continuum distorts the stellar mass ($M_*$) and specific star formation rate (sSFR) estimates required to anchor the MZR accurately<sup>[[7]](#source-7)</sup>.

## The Hazard of Magnification: Quarantined Lensed Datasets

Before detailing the independent field anchors, it is imperative to address the high-profile gravitationally lensed samples that populate the pre-2025 and 2025 literature. Gravitational lensing by foreground galaxy clusters (such as Abell 2744 and SMACS 0723) acts as a cosmic telescope, boosting the flux of background galaxies and allowing JWST to probe the extreme low-mass end of the galaxy luminosity function down to $10^{6} M_\odot$ at $z > 7$. However, relying on these sources as fundamental anchors for scaling relations like the MZR is hazardous.

The primary issue is differential magnification. A galaxy is not a uniform point source; it possesses complex internal structure. The older, less massive stellar populations that dictate the bulk of the stellar continuum are typically more spatially extended than the highly active, compact HII star-forming regions responsible for nebular emission lines such as the \[OIII\] $\lambda 4363$ auroral line. When a lensed galaxy crosses a caustic curve, these different spatial components are magnified by vastly different factors. Applying a single, luminosity-weighted magnification factor ($\mu$) across the entire spectral energy distribution artificially distorts the ratio between the nebular emission and the stellar continuum. This directly corrupts the calculation of both the stellar mass ($M_*$) and the specific star formation rate (sSFR)<sup>[[7]](#source-7)</sup>.

A prominent example of this is the UNCOVER survey cohort behind Abell 2744. Chemerynska et al. (2024) presented a sample of 8 highly magnified galaxies during the EoR at $z=6-8$. They reported extreme low oxygen abundances ranging from $12+\log(\text{O/H}) = 6.7$ to $7.8$ for stellar masses between $10^6$ and $10^8 M_\odot$. Based on this sample, they proposed a significantly steepened low-mass MZR slope expressed as $12+\log(\text{O/H}) = -0.076 \times (\log(M_*))^2 + 1.61 \times \log(M_*) - 0.26$<sup>[[8]](#source-8)</sup>. While this work pushes the boundaries of detection, the derived stellar masses are inextricably tied to highly complex parametric lens mass models. Consequently, the steepened slope cannot be accepted as a pristine, independent field anchor for the MZR.

Similarly, in the spatially resolved NIRSpec-IFU study by Koller et al. (2026), the target SMACS0723_4590 at $z=8.45$ is featured<sup>[[7]](#source-7)</sup>. Because this system is situated behind the SMACS 0723 lensing cluster, its intrinsic spatial scale, physical mass, and the calculated slope of its metallicity gradient are subject to source-plane reconstruction uncertainties. Therefore, while Koller et al.’s unlensed field galaxies (such as SXDF_NB1006-2) are retained in this report as independent anchors, SMACS0723_4590 and similar lensed sources are strictly quarantined from the absolute anchor catalog<sup>[[18]](#source-18)</sup>. Furthermore, the AMORE6 system at $z=5.725$, while hailed as an extremely metal-poor candidate, is multiply imaged by a foreground cluster and is thus excluded both due to its lensing status and its lower redshift<sup>[[23]](#source-23)</sup>.

## The Genuinely Independent MZR Anchors at $z > 7$

The following studies represent the vanguard of unlensed, field-galaxy metallicity measurements in the Epoch of Reionization. Each utilizes distinct, highly advanced methodologies to overcome the intrinsic faintness of high-redshift nebular emission and construct reliable anchors for the gas-phase mass-metallicity relation.

### Anchor 1: The $z \approx 10$ PRISM Cohort (Pollock et al., 2026)

The most significant population-level anchor for the early Universe MZR is provided by Pollock et al. (2026), who compiled a pristine cohort of 11 unlensed, star-forming galaxies at redshifts ranging from $z = 9.3$ to $10.0$<sup>[[3]](#source-3)</sup>. This sample includes notable sources such as CAPERS-EGS-25297, which sits at $z = 9.9381$<sup>[[11]](#source-11)</sup>.

The critical technical innovation in this work is a custom reduction of archival JWST/NIRSpec Prism data. The standard ESA/JWST pipeline typically enforces a wavelength cutoff at $5.3 \mu\text{m}$. Pollock et al. deliberately extended the wavelength calibration out to $5.5 \mu\text{m}$. This seemingly minor extension is scientifically profound: it enables the detection of the rest-frame optical \[OIII\] $\lambda\lambda 4959, 5007$ doublet and the crucial H\$\beta\$ line for galaxies pushing to $z = 10.0$, lines that previously fell off the edge of the detector<sup>[[3]](#source-3)</sup>.

By successfully resolving the temperature-sensitive auroral \[OIII\] $\lambda 4363$ emission line from the adjacent H\$\gamma\$ line at these extreme redshifts, the authors were able to bypass strong-line calibrations entirely. They employed the direct electron-temperature ($T_e$) method, calculating oxygen abundances ranging from $12+\log(\text{O/H}) = 7.1$ to $8.3$, which corresponds to $3\%$ to $50\%$ of the solar value<sup>[[3]](#source-3)</sup>.

The stellar masses for these 11 systems, derived via comprehensive spectro-photometric SED modeling assuming a standard Initial Mass Function, are generally typical for the epoch, hovering around $10^8 M_\odot$. However, their specific star formation rates are extraordinarily high, with a median sSFR of $38 - 58 \text{ Gyr}^{-1}$, indicating intense, bursty star formation episodes oscillating on 10 Myr versus 100 Myr timescales<sup>[[3]](#source-3)</sup>.

As an MZR anchor, the Pollock et al. cohort definitively establishes that the mass-metallicity normalization at $z \approx 10$ is universally suppressed. At a fixed stellar mass, the oxygen abundance sits approximately a factor of 3 ($0.5$ dex) lower than the established plateau observed in galaxies throughout the majority of cosmic time from $z=0$ to $3$<sup>[[3]](#source-3)</sup>. Data indicates a notable universal depression in the MZR, confirming that the progressive chemical enrichment of the interstellar medium is a relatively late-blooming phenomenon in cosmic history.

### Anchor 2: The Extreme Low-Metallicity Boundary: EXCELS-63107 (Cullen et al., 2025)

While population cohorts define the average trend, understanding the variance and extreme limits of the MZR requires isolated boundary anchors. Cullen et al. (2025) provide this with the discovery and characterization of EXCELS-63107, an unlensed star-forming galaxy at $z = 8.271$, observed as part of the Early eXtragalactic Continuum and Emission Line Science (EXCELS) survey<sup>[[14]](#source-14)</sup>.

EXCELS-63107 is an exceptionally compact system, with an effective radius ($r_e$) of less than 200 parsecs, making it physically consistent with the scale of local star-forming cluster complexes<sup>[[16]](#source-16)</sup>. Despite its diminutive size, it exhibits an extremely steep, blue UV continuum slope ($\beta = -3.3 \pm 0.3$) as measured by JWST/NIRCam, indicating an environment virtually devoid of dust attenuation and dominated by young, hot stars<sup>[[14]](#source-14)</sup>.

The deep, medium-resolution NIRSpec G395M spectrum of EXCELS-63107 is extraordinary for its prominent \[OIII\] $\lambda 4363$ auroral-line emission relative to the \[OIII\] $\lambda 5007$ forbidden line. Utilizing direct-$T_e$ calculations coupled with advanced Cloudy photoionization models, Cullen et al. determined an oxygen abundance of $12+\log(\text{O/H}) = 6.89^{+0.26}_{-0.21}$<sup>[[14]](#source-14)</sup>. At approximately $1.6\%$ of solar metallicity, this is one of the lowest directly constrained oxygen abundances measured in any galaxy to date, sitting roughly a factor of 10 lower than is typical for $z \simeq 8$ galaxies with a similar stellar mass ($\log_{10}(M_*/M_\odot) = 8.6$)<sup>[[14]](#source-14)</sup>.

The profound insight derived from EXCELS-63107, cementing its status as a critical boundary anchor, is the failure of standard stellar physics to explain its internal thermodynamics. The strength of the auroral line indicates that the ionized gas is being heated to a volume-averaged electron temperature of $T_e \simeq 34,000 \text{ K}$. Cullen et al. exhaustively demonstrate that stellar population models assuming standard, locally derived initial mass functions (such as a Salpeter or Chabrier IMF) are fundamentally incapable of producing the extreme ionizing radiation field ($T_{\text{eff}} \gtrsim 80,000 \text{ K}$) required to maintain this gas temperature, nor is there evidence of Active Galactic Nucleus (AGN) heating<sup>[[16]](#source-16)</sup>. The existence of EXCELS-63107 strongly implies that the nearly pristine environments of the EoR precipitate a transition to a top-heavy IMF. To reproduce the observed spectrum, theoretical models must invoke a $10 - 30\times$ excess of massive stars ($M > 50 M_\odot$) relative to local star-forming regions<sup>[[14]](#source-14)</sup>.

### Anchor 3: Resolving Spatial Gradients and Satellite Mixing (Koller et al., 2026)

The majority of JWST PRISM and Multi-Object Spectrograph (MSA) observations provide integrated, single-aperture measurements, yielding a galaxy's bulk properties. However, mapping the baryon cycle requires understanding how metals are distributed internally. Koller et al. (2026) achieved this by utilizing the NIRSpec Integral Field Unit (IFU) to conduct a spatially resolved study of metallicity gradients in seven low-metallicity systems at $z = 7.2 - 9.5$<sup>[[7]](#source-7)</sup>.

The unlensed main sample (which includes targets like SXDF_NB1006-2, JADES_10058975, and JADES_8013) spans stellar masses of $\log(M_*/M_\odot) \sim 7.8 - 9.5$ and features gas-phase metallicities ranging from $4\%$ to $13\% Z_\odot$<sup>[[7]](#source-7)</sup>. Through IFU mapping of emission lines from Ly\$\alpha\$ through \[OIII\] $\lambda 5007$, Koller et al. calculated an average radial metallicity gradient across the sample of $-0.03 \pm 0.04 \text{ dex kpc}^{-1}$, indicating that early galaxies generally lack the steep, negative metallicity gradients (metal-rich cores, metal-poor outskirts) common in mature disk galaxies at $z=0$<sup>[[17]](#source-17)</sup>.

The IFU maps revealed the primary mechanism behind these flat gradients: early-epoch interactions. Within the sample, the researchers identified three ultra-low metallicity satellite galaxies ($3 - 4\% Z_\odot$) physically interacting with the primary halos<sup>[[17]](#source-17)</sup>. The flattest metallicity gradients were definitively linked to these confirmed satellite systems. This provides direct kinematic evidence that tidal forces, minor mergers, and interactions drive rapid radial mixing, physically homogenizing the interstellar medium and preventing the establishment of steep gradients<sup>[[17]](#source-17)</sup>. Conversely, in one un-merged source, the IFU data detected a positive metallicity gradient (metal-poor core, metal-rich outskirts). This acts as a distinct physical anchor, providing direct observational evidence of pristine, zero-metallicity gas from the circumgalactic medium funnelling directly into the galactic core to fuel new star formation, temporarily diluting the central metallicity<sup>[[17]](#source-17)</sup>.

### Anchor 4: The JADES Stacked Calibrations (Isobe et al., 2026)

While individual direct-$T_e$ measurements provide vital anchors, establishing a canonical MZR for the general population requires massive statistical samples. Isobe et al. (2026) provided the definitive statistical anchor by analyzing gas-phase metallicities of star-forming galaxies at $z = 1 - 10$ using deep JWST/NIRSpec spectra from the JADES full data release, Dark Horse, and OASIS programmes<sup>[[4]](#source-4)</sup>.

Rather than relying solely on individual targets with prominent auroral lines, Isobe et al. stacked approximately 1,500 medium-resolution (R1000) spectra. This technique vastly improved the signal-to-noise ratio, yielding secure detections of the faint \[OIII\] $\lambda 4363$ auroral line in the stacked spectra down to a remarkably low metallicity of $12+\log(\text{O/H}) = 7.0$<sup>[[4]](#source-4)</sup>. From these $T_e$-anchored stacks, they derived a new suite of stack-based strong-line calibrations valid over the critical range of $12+\log(\text{O/H}) = 7.0 - 8.7$<sup>[[4]](#source-4)</sup>.

Applying these bespoke high-redshift calibrations, the authors established a canonical MZR out to $z=10$. They confirmed a global decrease in metallicity normalizations from $z \sim 0$ to $z \sim 4 - 10$, but notably found no significant change in the fundamental power-law slope of the relation<sup>[[4]](#source-4)</sup>. Furthermore, they identified 50 robust candidates for Extremely Metal-Poor Galaxies (EMPGs), possessing metallicities of $1-4\%$ solar ($12+\log(\text{O/H}) = 6.7 - 7.3$) spanning $z = 1.2$ to $9.1$<sup>[[4]](#source-4)</sup>.

### Anchor 5: The Absolute Redshift Limit: JADES-GS-z14-0 (Carniani et al., 2025; Schouws et al., 2025)

The absolute temporal boundary for chemical enrichment measurements is currently anchored by JADES-GS-z14-0. With a highly precise spectroscopic redshift of $z = 14.1793 \pm 0.0007$, this galaxy is observed as it existed a mere 300 million years after the Big Bang<sup>[[19]](#source-19)</sup>.

Because the rest-frame optical \[OIII\] lines are shifted deeply into the mid-infrared at this redshift, standard NIRSpec direct-$T_e$ methods are impossible. Instead, independent teams (Carniani et al., 2025; Schouws et al., 2025) achieved a groundbreaking $6.6\sigma$ detection of the far-infrared fine-structure line \[OIII\] $88\mu\text{m}$ using the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA)<sup>[[20]](#source-20)</sup>.

By coupling the ALMA-measured line luminosity ($L_{\text{[OIII]}88} = 2.1 \times 10^8 L_\odot$) with JWST/MIRI photometric constraints on the rest-optical \[OIII\] $\lambda\lambda 4959, 5007$ and H\$\beta\$ lines, the researchers utilized extensive Cloudy ISM model grids to derive the metallicity<sup>[[20]](#source-20)</sup>. The results were astonishing: despite its extreme youth, JADES-GS-z14-0 is already surprisingly metal-enriched, possessing an oxygen abundance of $Z \sim 0.05 - 0.2 Z_\odot$ (5% to 20% of solar)<sup>[[19]](#source-19)</sup>. This anchor definitively proves that multiple generations of massive stars had already lived, died as supernovae, and chemically seeded the interstellar medium within the first 300 Myr of cosmic time, drastically compressing the timeline for early galactic evolution<sup>[[19]](#source-19)</sup>.

## Abundance Scale Incompatibilities and the "Auroral Bias"

A central challenge in defining the post-2024 MZR is navigating the severe incompatibilities between historical abundance scales and the physical realities of the early Universe. Historically, strong-line (SL) diagnostics—such as $R_{23}$ (the ratio of \[OII\]+\[OIII\] to H\$\beta\$), $O_{32}$ (\[OIII\]/\[OII\]), and $N_2$ (\[NII\]/H\$\alpha\$)—were empirically calibrated against samples of local ($z \sim 0$) HII regions and galaxies with direct-$T_e$ measurements<sup>[[28]](#source-28)</sup>.

Applying these $z = 0$ SL calibrations to $z > 7$ galaxies is fundamentally flawed. Galaxies in the EoR exhibit radically different interstellar medium conditions: they possess harder ionizing radiation fields, highly elevated ionization parameters ($U$), higher electron densities, and significant deviations in secondary abundance ratios (such as elevated N/O ratios)<sup>[[28]](#source-28)</sup>. Using local calibrations on high-redshift spectra systematically biases the derived oxygen abundances downward by as much as 1.0 dex, creating the false illusion of a massive, rapid drop in cosmic metallicity<sup>[[28]](#source-28)</sup>.

However, the 2025–2026 literature has uncovered an even more insidious systematic error originating within the high-redshift JWST datasets themselves: the **Auroral Bias**.

The most secure method of measuring metallicity is the direct electron-temperature ($T_e$) method, which requires the detection of the faint auroral emission line \[OIII\] $\lambda 4363$. The emissivity of this line is exponentially dependent on the gas temperature; hotter gas emits significantly stronger $\lambda 4363$ flux. In astrophysics, gas temperature anti-correlates with metallicity, as heavy elements like oxygen act as primary coolants for the ISM via fine-structure emission<sup>[[5]](#source-5)</sup>.

Therefore, when researchers require a secure ($>3\sigma$) detection of the \[OIII\] $\lambda 4363$ line to calculate an individual galaxy's metallicity, they are systematically filtering their sample. This requirement artificially isolates the hottest, most highly ionized, and most severely metal-poor HII regions, discarding the cooler, more metal-rich bulk of the galaxy population where the auroral line is too faint to detect<sup>[[5]](#source-5)</sup>.

Giménez-Alcázar et al. (2026) quantified this bias in a comprehensive study of 286 star-forming galaxies across $z=1-9$<sup>[[5]](#source-5)</sup>. By stacking the spectra of galaxies that *lacked* individual auroral-line detections, they discovered that the resulting MZR was shifted $0.2$ to $0.3$ dex *higher* in metallicity at fixed stellar mass compared to the MZR constructed solely from individual auroral detections<sup>[[5]](#source-5)</sup>. The undetected, stacked populations appeared significantly more chemically evolved and closer to fundamental equilibrium relations<sup>[[5]](#source-5)</sup>.

This phenomenon was rigorously corroborated by Isobe et al. (2026) using the JADES dataset. They found that at a fixed metallicity, their stacked spectra exhibited \[OIII\] $\lambda 5007$/H\$\beta\$ ($R_3$) and \[OIII\]/\[OII\] ($O_{32}$) values generally lower than the calibrations derived from high-$z$ individual auroral-line emitters<sup>[[4]](#source-4)</sup>. The data indicates that the true bulk population sits 0.2 to 0.3 dex higher in metallicity than the extreme, low-metallicity tail isolated by individual auroral detections.

Consequently, to convert or compare older JWST datasets (2022–2024) to the modern standard, one must account for the fact that studies relying exclusively on individual $\lambda 4363$ detections systematically underestimated the true chemical enrichment of the early Universe. The true, unbiased $z > 7$ MZR normalization is higher than initial estimates suggested, though it remains significantly depleted relative to $z=0$ baselines<sup>[[4]](#source-4)</sup>.

## Second and Third-Order Insights into the Epoch of Reionization

The establishment of independent MZR anchors at $z > 7$ yields profound second- and third-order insights that extend far beyond simply plotting oxygen abundance against stellar mass. These data points force a fundamental rewriting of galactic gas-regulator models and early supermassive black hole formation mechanisms.

### 1. The Breakdown of the Fundamental Metallicity Relation (FMR)

In the local Universe, the mass-metallicity relation is not merely a 2D line, but a projection of a tighter 3D plane linking stellar mass, metallicity, and star formation rate, known as the Fundamental Metallicity Relation (FMR)<sup>[[1]](#source-1)</sup>. The physical basis of the FMR is the "gas-regulator" or "bathtub" model. This model assumes galaxies exist in a quasi-equilibrium state: metal production from steady star formation is perfectly balanced by the dilution of steady, metal-poor gas inflows from the intergalactic medium and the ejection of enriched gas via supernovae outflows<sup>[[32]](#source-32)</sup>. Under the FMR, a galaxy that experiences a sudden influx of gas will see its SFR rise and its metallicity drop; as the gas is consumed, SFR drops and metallicity rises, moving the galaxy along a predictable track<sup>[[2]](#source-2)</sup>.

The independent anchors from the post-2024 literature definitively show that this equilibrium model shatters at $z > 7$. The Extremely Metal-Poor Galaxies (EMPGs) characterized by Isobe et al. (2026) display a massive scatter in the MZR. More critically, in direct contradiction to the local FMR—where higher SFRs correlate with lower metallicity—many $z > 7$ EMPGs with the lowest metallicities paradoxically exhibit *lower* specific SFRs<sup>[[4]](#source-4)</sup>. Pollock et al. (2026) confirm this macro-trend, noting a distinct break in the applicability of the FMR beyond $z \approx 3$<sup>[[3]](#source-3)</sup>.

This breakdown suggests that the Epoch of Reionization is an era defined by "chemical chaos." At $z > 7$, the timescale for massive, stochastic pristine gas infall from the cosmic web is drastically shorter than the internal mixing and feedback equilibrium timescales<sup>[[6]](#source-6)</sup>. Galaxies operate in violent, bursty cycles. A single massive accretion event of pristine gas can temporarily crash the overall gas-phase metallicity. However, if this cold gas has not yet shocked, compressed, and triggered star formation, the galaxy will display both low metallicity and low SFR simultaneously, violating the FMR. The $z > 7$ MZR is therefore not an equilibrium state, but a snapshot of turbulent, non-steady-state gas dynamics.

### 2. The Paradox of Little Red Dots (LRDs) and Black Hole Seeding

A highly scrutinized sub-population of the metal-poor systems at $z > 7$ are the "Little Red Dots" (LRDs). These are exceptionally compact, heavily reddened sources displaying broad Balmer emission lines (indicative of dense, fast-moving gas) and spectral breaks consistent with heavily obscured, actively accreting supermassive black holes (SMBHs)<sup>[[4]](#source-4)</sup>.

A major outstanding question in cosmology is how these SMBHs grew so massive, so quickly. A leading theory is the "Direct Collapse Black Hole" (DCBH) model, which requires massive gas clouds of completely pristine, zero-metallicity gas to collapse directly into a massive seed black hole without fragmenting into individual stars (since metals cool the gas and promote fragmentation)<sup>[[33]](#source-33)</sup>.

However, spectroscopic analysis of LRDs by teams including Nikopoulos et al. (2026) and Maiolino et al. (2025) reveals a striking paradox: LRDs maintain a remarkably stable, narrow range of sub-solar metallicities across cosmic time. They are not pristine<sup>[[29]](#source-29)</sup>. The presence of substantial heavy elements in LRD host galaxies poses a severe challenge to DCBH models that require formation via pristine gas collapse<sup>[[29]](#source-29)</sup>. The MZR anchors of LRDs imply that prior generations of stars (Population III or early Population II) must have already lived, died, and chemically enriched the nuclear gas *before* the rapid accretion phase of the SMBH began. This shifts the theoretical preference toward models where black hole seeds grew via aggressive, super-Eddington accretion within dense, already-enriched young nuclear star clusters, rather than from isolated pristine clouds.

### 3. The Dust-Blind Chemical Dawn

The ALMA detection of the \[OIII\] $88\mu\text{m}$ fine-structure line in JADES-GS-z14-0, coupled with the absolute lack of any detectable dust continuum emission, highlights a stark temporal disconnect in the early cosmic baryon cycle<sup>[[20]](#source-20)</sup>.

Chemical enrichment and dust formation operate on vastly different timescales. Oxygen and other $\alpha$-elements are synthesized rapidly by high-mass, short-lived stars and are released into the ISM upon core collapse, typically within 5 to 10 million years of a starburst<sup>[[20]](#source-20)</sup>. Conversely, the production of significant dust mass requires much longer timescales. Dust is primarily produced in the cool outflows of intermediate-mass Asymptotic Giant Branch (AGB) stars (requiring $>100$ Myr timescales) or via slow grain growth within the ISM itself<sup>[[29]](#source-29)</sup>. Furthermore, any early dust produced by supernovae is highly susceptible to destruction by the violent reverse shocks of the very explosions that created it<sup>[[35]](#source-35)</sup>.

The $z > 7$ MZR anchors confirm this paradigm. The Universe enriched chemically at an astonishing pace, achieving $5\%$ to $20\%$ of solar metallicity within the first 300 million years<sup>[[19]](#source-19)</sup>. However, because dust production mechanisms lagged behind rapid oxygen synthesis, the early Universe remained largely "dust-blind." This physical reality was crucial for cosmic evolution: the lack of a mature dust reservoir allowed the hard, ionizing Lyman-continuum photons produced by these early, metal-enriched starbursts to escape their host galaxies unchecked, providing the necessary radiation to drive the Epoch of Reionization.

## Conclusion

The 2025–2026 literature unequivocally establishes that genuinely independent, unlensed anchors for the mass-metallicity relation exist deep into the Epoch of Reionization at $z > 7$. By discarding heavily magnified, mass-ambiguous cluster targets and correcting for the systematic "auroral bias" that artificially skewed early JWST findings toward extreme metal deficiency, a coherent picture of early chemical evolution has emerged. Utilizing advanced IFU spatial mapping, deep stacked strong-line diagnostics, and high-precision ALMA/MIRI cross-calibrations, the current MZR anchors demonstrate that early galaxies enriched at a remarkably rapid pace. However, these galaxies existed in a state of chemical chaos driven by stochastic pristine gas infall, fundamentally breaking the equilibrium models that govern the local Universe and setting the stage for the rapid, dust-free reionization of the cosmos.
