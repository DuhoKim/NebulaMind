abstract
JWST has revealed surprisingly abundant and massive galaxies in the first billion years, prompting claims of tension with CDM structure formation. We test one flagship cosmological hydrodynamical simulation, IllustrisTNG (TNG100-1), against this challenge by measuring its stellar mass function and massive-galaxy abundance at z=4,5,6 and comparing to JWST observations. TNG matches the face-value massive-galaxy abundance at z=4 (n(>10^10.5M_)=4.110^-5,Mpc^-3, 56 objects) but *increasingly under-produces* massive galaxies toward higher redshift: by z=6 its single most massive galaxy reaches only  M_=10.6, and, relative to the adopted fixed-n TNG benchmark, face-value JWST candidates extend 0.5~dex higher (not an observed fixed-abundance measurement), echoing the ``impossibly massive early galaxies'' tension. We are explicit about the two systematics that dominate the interpretation: TNG100's (111,Mpc)^3 volume yields only *one* galaxy above 10^10.5M_ at z=6 (Poisson-limited), and the observed photometric stellar masses are uncertain at the 0.3~dex level and actively debated. A complementary baryon-budget *illustration* --- assigning the observed galaxies a single number density n=10^-5,Mpc^-3 and converting to an implied efficiency  M_/(f_ bM_ h) --- indicates that, *conditional on that mapping*, most observed masses correspond to conditional proxy values below one (_ proxy0.08 median), with only the most extreme reported masses implying _ proxy>1. We do *not* use this to declare the shortfall cosmological or astrophysical, which would require a completeness-corrected stellar mass function. Under the same fixed-n conversion --- an *effective deterministic proxy*, not a statistical abundance match --- TNG's proxy massive-end efficiency does not rise toward high redshift (_ proxy0.190.13 from z=4 to 6), whereas the observed galaxies map to a median _ proxy0.060.39 (a factor of 3 higher by z6; larger for the tail). These are conditional illustrations, not measured efficiencies, and we frame the study as a descriptive stellar-mass-function *stress test*, not a causal identification of the responsible physics. The result is a well-defined, reproducible *target* for a larger-volume (TNG300) test with forward-modelled selection. Generated autonomously; reproducible from public data.

## Introduction

Among the most discussed early JWST results is the apparent existence of very massive galaxies ( M_10.5) at z7 [cite], which boylankolchin23 argued would require an implausibly high conversion of available baryons into stars under CDM. Subsequent work has moderated the claim: careful re-analysis of masses, AGN contamination, and photometric systematics [cite] finds much of the sample consistent with CDM, so the ``tension'' is real but its severity is contested. A complementary and less-discussed question is whether the cosmological *simulations* we rely on actually produce enough massive galaxies early enough. Here we put IllustrisTNG on trial against the JWST massive-galaxy abundance --- the top ``simulations-vs-physics'' frontier flagged by our automated topic map --- with an explicit accounting of the volume and mass-calibration systematics that make or break such a comparison.

Our approach is deliberately three-pronged, because no single comparison is decisive. First, we measure the simulated abundance of massive galaxies directly against JWST. Second, we illustrate the conversion factors implied by an adopted fixed-n mapping; this is *not* the survey-volume baryon-ceiling analysis needed to distinguish cosmological from astrophysical explanations [cite]. Third, we recast both onto an implied star-formation efficiency through a simple fixed-number-density conversion, which we treat only as a descriptive proxy; a rigorous efficiency comparison would need completeness-corrected mass functions and native halo masses beyond this draft. Throughout we foreground the two effects that dominate such work --- the finite simulation volume and the 0.3~dex, IMF-dependent uncertainty in photometric stellar masses.

## Data and Method

**Simulation.** We use IllustrisTNG TNG100-1 [cite] at snapshots z=4,5,6. Stellar mass is the mass in the stellar particle type within twice the stellar half-mass radius (SubhaloMassInRadType); we keep cosmological subhaloes (SubhaloFlag=1). We build the stellar mass function ( M_) in the comoving volume V=(111,Mpc)^3=1.3610^6,Mpc^3 with Poisson uncertainties N/(V, M), and cumulative number densities n(>M_).

**Observations.** For the massive high-z population we use the CEERS massive-galaxy catalogue of chworowsky24 (VizieR J/AJ/168/113): 120 galaxies at z=4--7.6 with  M_=10.0--10.9 (mostly photometric redshifts; 17 spectroscopic). We compare TNG to (i) these individual masses in the mass--redshift plane and (ii) published massive-galaxy number densities, n10^-5,Mpc^-3 at z7--9 [cite] and 310^-5,Mpc^-3 at z5--6 [cite]. These masses are *not* on a common initial mass function --- Salpeter for labbe23, Chabrier for chworowsky24, and a BPASS/Kroupa-like IMF for weibel24 --- introducing relative offsets up to 0.2~dex (Salpeter highest) that a homogenised analysis must remove; we flag this as an additional contribution to the mass systematics.

## An illustrative baryon-budget conversion

The comparison above asks whether one simulation makes enough massive galaxies; a complementary conditional illustration asks what conversion proxy follows when each observed object is assigned the adopted fixed number density [cite]. A galaxy of stellar mass M_ in a halo of mass M_ h implies a conversion proxy _ proxy M_/(f_ bM_ h) (defined under this imposed mapping), with f_ b=_ b/_ m=0.158 and _ proxy1 an absolute limit on the true efficiency. As an *illustration* we assign every observed galaxy the single number density n=10^-5,Mpc^-3 and read M_ h(n,z) from the Planck halo mass function [cite] via colossus [cite]. This is a deterministic conversion, *not* a statistical abundance match: it ignores scatter, selection, and the true (unmeasured) number density, so the resulting  values are indicative only (Fig.~fig:baryon).

Conditional on this illustrative mapping, the *bulk* of the CEERS sample maps to _ proxy0.08 (median), conditional proxy values below the _ proxy=1 reference; 21% map to _ proxy>0.2, and the most extreme candidates to _ proxy>1 --- a z8,  M_=10.9 object of the type reported by labbe23 lies above the _ proxy=1 reference, which may reflect an inappropriate assigned rank, mass bias, scatter, selection, duty cycle, or some combination --- the present calculation cannot distinguish them (see also [cite]). Because these values shift with the (unmeasured) true number density and with scatter and selection, we draw from them *no* firm cosmological-versus-astrophysical verdict; that requires a completeness-corrected mass function.

## An effective-efficiency proxy

Both sides can be recast via the same effective deterministic fixed-n conversion proxy _ proxy (Sec.~4): for TNG we read M_ from its stellar mass function and M_ h from the halo mass function at matched cumulative number density; for the observations we again assign the single density n=10^-5,Mpc^-3 (Fig.~fig:eff). *These are conversion proxies, not measured efficiencies*: a rigorous treatment needs redshift-binned, completeness-corrected stellar mass functions with forward-modelled scatter and selection, and we claim no measured (z). Two scatter effects act in *opposite* directions --- intrinsic stellar-to-halo scatter (duty cycle <1) shifts rare selected galaxies to lower-mass hosts and *raises* the implied efficiency, while measurement (Eddington) bias inflates observed masses and *lowers* deconvolved true masses. On the simulation side the proxy matches all cosmological subhaloes against a distinct-halo mass function, inconsistently including satellites; a consistent native measurement using centrals and their M_ 200c is the needed refinement (deferred here by intermittent data-server outages), and we make *no* claim about how it would change the result until it is done. Under these caveats the proxy TNG efficiency does not rise toward high redshift (0.19,0.18,0.13 at z=4,5,6), while the observed galaxies map to a median 0.06,0.14,0.39 (a factor of 3 higher by z6; 9 at the observed 90th percentile). At z=4--5 the proxy TNG value exceeds the observed median. The comparison is *descriptive* --- it indicates *that* TNG's massive end sits low under this conversion, not *why* --- and we treat candidate mechanisms below only as hypotheses.

## Discussion

Two systematics govern how seriously to take this. **(1) Simulation volume.** The steep high-mass exponential cutoff means the rarest, most massive objects are precisely those a (111,Mpc)^3 box under-samples; with a *single* galaxy above 10^10.5M_ at z=6, the TNG point there is Poisson-dominated (a 1 range of roughly 0--3 objects). A larger box (TNG300, 20 the volume) is required to measure the z=6 massive end robustly, and is the natural next step. **(2) Observed masses.** The CEERS masses are largely photometric, uncertain at 0.3~dex, and chworowsky24 themselves conclude that careful treatment brings most of the sample into consistency with CDM; a 0.3~dex downward revision would remove much of the z=6 deficit. The honest statement is therefore that TNG100 *appears in tension with the face-value JWST massive-galaxy abundance at z6; the interpretation is limited by both simulation-volume uncertainty and observational mass systematics, and we do not estimate a formal discrepancy significance.* The deficit could reflect intrinsic simulation-physics under-production, observational-modelling effects (masses, selection, scatter), or both, and our data do not distinguish them.

**What we do *not* yet claim.** We deliberately refrain from asserting that the efficiency comparison is robust to these systematics. Establishing that would require a completeness-corrected, redshift-binned observed stellar mass function with forward-modelled selection and scatter (in place of the fixed-n conversion); a native centrals-plus-M_ 200c efficiency; and a cosmic-variance and resolution assessment via a larger box (TNG300) with a TNG100/TNG300 convergence check. Until those are done, we treat the apparent non-rise of the proxy efficiency and its offset from the observed values as *suggestive* of a high-redshift massive-galaxy problem in TNG, not an established one.

## Candidate mechanisms (hypotheses)

We stress at the outset that our data show *that* TNG's massive end sits low, not *why*; the following are hypotheses to be tested, not conclusions. In TNG, star formation in 10^12M_ haloes is regulated by galactic-wind (supernova) feedback, whose velocity scales as v_ w_ DM,(H_0/H(z))^1/3 with an imposed floor [cite]. The explicit Hubble factor approximately cancels the virial redshift scaling of _ DM, so at *fixed halo mass* the launch velocity is approximately redshift-independent and the behaviour cannot be ascribed to ``faster high-z winds.'' The redshift dependence of the *effective* feedback enters more subtly, through the wind energy budget, the mass loading, the metallicity dependence of the wind, and the mapping between halo mass, _ DM, and gas conditions. On the AGN side, TNG's *kinetic* (low-accretion) mode is disfavoured at z6 by its mass-dependent activation threshold when black holes are still small [cite], but the *thermal* (high-accretion) mode is not excluded and would need checking against the black-hole masses, Eddington ratios, and injected energies of the relevant galaxies before either mode is ruled in or out.

One much-discussed hypothesis is ``feedback-free starbursts'' (FFB; [cite]): where the gas density exceeds 310^3,cm^-3 and the free-fall time falls below the feedback-free interval of 1~Myr before supernovae inject energy [cite], a burst can complete before feedback regulates it. In this picture the *instantaneous* accretion efficiency SFR/(f_ b M_ h) is *assumed* to lie in the range 0.2--1 --- a population-level prescription adopted by li24, not derived from first principles --- and is a distinct quantity from the *cumulative* stellar-to-halo efficiency M_/(f_ bM_ h) we proxy, so the two must not be equated. FFB moreover carries stringent prerequisites (gas densities 310^3,cm^-3, free-fall times below the supernova delay, rapid gas supply, and low-metallicity, rapidly-cooling, self-shielded gas) whose applicability to these galaxies is unestablished; in the li24 population prescription the onset is parameterized near a halo mass 5.810^11M_ at z6, close to the regime where other quenching physics also operates. FFB is neither resolved in nor explicit to TNG's sub-grid model, so it remains a *plausible but unproven* candidate for a rising high-z efficiency rather than an established explanation of our result. Its high efficiencies are moreover contested: ferrara25 argue that pre-supernova Ly radiation pressure can regulate star formation before feedback-free collapse completes, undercutting _0.4. Quantifying any required efficiency boost must await the corrected observational inference (redshift-binned, completeness-corrected mass functions), and we deliberately attach no specific numerical target here.

A robust, mechanism-independent corollary nonetheless survives: agreement in the face-value z4 comparison does not by itself validate a model's z6 massive end, which must be tested directly. A useful future discriminant is the redshift dependence of the massive-end efficiency *once it is properly measured* --- one that stays flat or falls, as the current TNG fixed-n proxy does, versus one that rises steeply beyond z6--7 --- testable as completeness-corrected, spectroscopically-anchored mass functions accumulate.

## Conclusion

IllustrisTNG matches the face-value massive-galaxy abundance at z=4 but increasingly under-produces massive galaxies toward z=6: relative to the adopted fixed-n TNG benchmark, face-value JWST candidates extend 0.5~dex higher --- consistent with, and a simulation-side view of, the ``impossibly massive early galaxies'' debate. An illustrative baryon-budget conversion indicates that, conditional on its assumptions, most observed masses map to conditional proxy values below one (_ proxy0.08 median) with only the extreme tail exceeding _ proxy=1; we do not use it to adjudicate cosmological versus astrophysical origin. A descriptive fixed-n efficiency proxy shows TNG's massive end does not brighten toward high redshift while the observed galaxies map higher, but we attach no measured significance or mechanism to this. Turning the suggestive deficit into a test would require ingredients we have deliberately deferred: a completeness-corrected, forward-modelled observed stellar mass function; a native centrals-plus-M_ 200c efficiency; and a TNG100/TNG300 resolution-and-volume convergence check with spectroscopically-anchored masses.
