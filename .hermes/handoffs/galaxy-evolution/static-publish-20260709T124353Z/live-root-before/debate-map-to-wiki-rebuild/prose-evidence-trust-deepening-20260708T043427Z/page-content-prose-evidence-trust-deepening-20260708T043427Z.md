## Overview: Galaxy Evolution as a Regulated Baryon Cycle

**Deepened trust framing:** multi-channel synthesis; anchored to `alternatives_countercases` and all seven local debate axes.  
**Reader guard:** alternative channels are part of the explanation, not an uncertainty footnote.  
**What would change status:** broader corpus review would need to show that a named alternative is out of scope for this page question.

Galaxy evolution is the coupled history of halos, gas, stars, black holes, metals, and environments. A durable wiki page should not imply that galaxy evolution is solved by one quenching channel or one favored observable. The clearer synthesis is a regulated baryon cycle: dark matter halos acquire and heat gas; galaxies convert only part of that gas into stars; stellar feedback, black-hole activity, and environmental processing return energy and enriched material to surrounding reservoirs; and quenching slows or halts star formation through several channels whose importance changes with mass, redshift, structure, and selection.

The debate-map framing matters because it controls the page's verbs. Widely supported mechanisms can be written as real channels, but active debates must remain debates. Sample-limited frequencies must carry sample and tracer context. Simulation-only mechanisms must stay model-dependent. That vocabulary is not a style preference; it is the reproducibility contract for this Method3 page.

Local provenance is intentionally visible. The map includes seven axes: `mechanism_ejective_feedback`, `outflow_prevalence_frequency`, `dominance_debate`, `maintenance_heating_prevention`, `reservoir_response`, `alternatives_countercases`, and `simulation_model_scope`. Together they support a page that explains how galaxies evolve while warning the reader where evidence is settled, scoped, sample-limited, or still unresolved.

## Dark Matter Halos & Structure Formation

**Deepened trust framing:** scoped coverage extension; halo mass and assembly are locally supported, while broad cosmological structure formation remains outside this rebuilt evidence pass.  
**Reader guard:** halo mass can frame regulation, but it cannot be rendered as the only quenching predictor.  
**What would change status:** matched causal work decomposing halo, central, black-hole, gas, and environment predictors would narrow the halo-versus-central debate.

Dark matter halos set the gravitational boundary conditions for galaxy formation. Their mass and assembly history affect gas accretion, virial heating, recycling, satellite infall, and whether feedback energy escapes or remains coupled to the circumgalactic medium. Halo assembly therefore belongs early in the page because it shapes what later baryonic mechanisms can do.

The deeper caution is that halo mass is not a universal explanation for visible galaxy state. Similar halo masses can host galaxies with different star-formation histories, central densities, gas reservoirs, and environments. The local debate map treats central black-hole, bulge, and velocity-dispersion predictors as real axes that can compete with or qualify halo-mass explanations. The page should therefore say that halo growth is a governing condition, not that halo mass alone fixes the outcome.

This section remains deliberately scoped. It supports a reader-facing discussion of halo regulation, assembly history, and the halo-versus-central-predictor tension. It does not claim to rebuild all of cosmological structure formation or all halo occupation evidence. That broader expansion would require a separate source pass.

## Gas Supply, Star Formation & Feedback

**Deepened trust framing:** reservoir response is actively debated; non-AGN alternatives and retained-gas qualifiers are required context.  
**Reader guard:** expulsion does not necessarily empty reservoirs; central depletion must be kept distinct from global gas loss.  
**What would change status:** global gas-reservoir studies linked to AGN stage, star-formation efficiency, and resolved depletion could separate retained reservoirs from central-local depletion.

The gas cycle links accretion, star formation, stellar feedback, recycling, and quenching. Star formation requires usable cold gas, but the presence of gas is not equivalent to efficient star formation. Some systems can retain reservoirs and still form stars slowly; others show central kiloparsec depletion; still others lose or heat gas through environmental and feedback channels. These outcomes imply different mechanisms and should not be collapsed into one phrase.

The status map's `reservoir_response` axis is the strongest guardrail for this section. It records two relevant kinds of evidence: retained gas and low star-formation efficiency can qualify simple gas-removal accounts, while some AGN-host central regions show lower molecular gas fractions in central-kiloparsec-scoped measurements. That combination makes the reservoir question active, not settled. A reproducible page must therefore avoid claims like "feedback empties the reservoirs" unless later product binding supplies direct, global, population-level support.

Stellar feedback remains a core channel, especially in lower-mass systems where star formation can drive outflows, enrich surrounding gas, and regulate future accretion. In the dominance debate, star-formation-driven outflows also serve as a countercase to over-attributing every outflow to AGN activity. The result is not anti-AGN wording; it is a cleaner separation of mechanisms.

## AGN Feedback & Quenching

**Deepened trust framing:** ejective/depletion mechanism is widely supported in selected systems; outflow prevalence is emerging and sample-limited; dominance is actively debated; maintenance heating is model-dependent in this corpus.  
**Reader guard:** use "can", "may", and "in selected systems"; do not say AGN generally quenches galaxies or empties reservoirs.  
**What would change status:** selection-controlled observational samples linking AGN-driven removal or heating to later star-formation suppression could sharpen mechanism status; systematic misattribution would downgrade it.

AGN feedback is real, but the reproducible version is conditional. The `mechanism_ejective_feedback` axis supports that AGN activity can drive outflows capable of removing or depleting star-forming fuel in selected massive or AGN-host galaxies. Representative local bibcodes include `2014A&A...562A..21C`, `2024MNRAS.528.4976D`, `2024NatAs...8.1443D`, and `2012MNRAS.420.2662D`. That is enough to describe an important mechanism, but not enough to claim universal quenching.

Outflow frequency needs even more care. The `outflow_prevalence_frequency` axis explicitly says fractions are tracer-, selection-, and epoch-specific. The ledger records a MOSDEF z = 1.4-3.8 AGN sample with 17 percent ionized outflows and one JWST massive z ~ 2 sample with 46 percent excess Na I D absorption, half of absorption profiles blueshifted. Those are not one combined prevalence range. They are two different scoped measurements. The page can use them as examples of sample-limited prevalence evidence only when the sample and tracer stay attached.

The dominance question remains open. The `dominance_debate` axis blocks a winner. AGN feedback is one important axis, but central predictors, halo and environment, satellite pathways, gas retention, strangulation, stripping, stellar-feedback-driven outflows, and recycling all remain in the map. The right prose says "AGN feedback can matter strongly in selected contexts" rather than "AGN is the dominant cause."

Maintenance or preventive heating is also separated from outflows. In this corpus, the `maintenance_heating_prevention` axis is model-dependent: simulations and models can show a possible preventive channel under assumptions, but they do not establish observed maintenance-heating prevalence. The page can mention this mode only with model-dependent wording unless a later P3 source pass binds observational evidence such as accepted X-ray cavity, hot-halo, or radio-mode duty-cycle rows.

## Environment, Morphology & Structural Growth

**Deepened trust framing:** environmental pathways are widely supported; morphology and structural growth remain scoped and correlational unless later evidence narrows causality.  
**Reader guard:** environmental strangulation is separate from internal AGN starvation; structural correlations are predictors, not automatically causes.  
**What would change status:** matched samples separating group, cluster, satellite, central, halo, bulge, and black-hole predictors would reduce ambiguity.

Environment changes galaxies by stripping, starving, perturbing, and merging them. After infall into groups or clusters, satellites can be affected by ram pressure, tidal processing, strangulation, and changes in gas supply. Those pathways are real alternatives to internal AGN feedback and need to appear in the explanation itself.

The same section should include structural growth, but with correlational guards. Bulge growth, central stellar density, velocity dispersion, size evolution, mergers, and black-hole mass often track quenching. Tracking is not proof of a single independent cause. The status map's central-predictor and halo-environment axes support these relations as important predictors, while the dominance axis prevents the page from presenting a single causal ranking.

## Chemical Enrichment & Cosmic Timing

**Deepened trust framing:** scoped coverage extension; strongest local basis is the mass-metallicity relation, the fundamental metallicity relation, and stellar-age or abundance timing.  
**Reader guard:** redshift scope travels with the statement; do not generalize z ~ 0-2.3 FMR language to all epochs.  
**What would change status:** broader product-bound chemical-evolution rows across more epochs would be needed for a universal enrichment narrative.

Chemical enrichment records how gas has been accreted, processed into stars, enriched, expelled, recycled, or diluted. Metallicity is therefore both a clock and a regulator: it marks integrated star formation and the gas flows that changed the interstellar medium.

The local basis supports a scoped account, not a complete chemical-evolution review. It supports mass-metallicity and fundamental-metallicity-relation language around the local universe through cosmic noon, with the strongest stability statement roughly scoped to z ~ 0-2.3. It also supports timing language from stellar ages and abundance gradients, especially the idea that galaxies can reach similar present-day classes through varied histories.

The page should preserve that distributional framing. Massive systems may have older central populations and younger outskirts; recently quenched galaxies can preserve different formation timescales; metallicity normalization can evolve with redshift. None of those observations permits a single universal timeline.

## High-Redshift & Reionization Frontier

**Deepened trust framing:** cosmic-noon shutdown remains sample- and model-dependent; reionization and z > 10 mass tension remain open frontier debates.  
**Reader guard:** do not conflate hydrogen reionization at z > 6 with helium II reionization near z ~ 3; present z > 10 tensions as constraints, not conclusions.  
**What would change status:** better constrained stellar masses, dust, IMF, burst histories, baryon-conversion efficiencies, halo abundances, and photon escape fractions would narrow these frontiers.

High-redshift galaxy evolution contains several different regimes. At cosmic noon, roughly z ~ 1.5-3, some massive galaxies appear to shut down rapidly, and AGN-linked explanations are discussed as possible fast channels. That is sample- and model-dependent evidence, not a universal high-redshift rule.

The reionization frontier remains more open. A page can describe competing ionizing-photon budgets, including star-forming galaxies and faint AGN, while keeping strong emission-line galaxies near z ~ 7 in their own accounting context. It should also keep helium II reionization near z ~ 3 distinct from hydrogen reionization at z > 6.

JWST-era z > 10 massive-galaxy tensions should be written as tensions with possible explanations. Top-heavy initial mass functions, enhanced baryon-conversion efficiency, bursty star formation, dust and modeling effects, and halo-abundance expectations can all be named as positions or constraints. This candidate avoids treating the older early-black-hole-seeding clause as product evidence because the local row for claim `2374` has garbled text.

## Observational Evidence & Surveys

**Deepened trust framing:** evidence-method framing; simulation claims remain model-dependent unless observation-linked validation is bound later.  
**Reader guard:** simulation-only rows cannot establish observed prevalence, observed maintenance heating, or observed reservoir depletion.  
**What would change status:** observation-linked validation of predicted outflow rates, heating signatures, and quenching outcomes in unbiased samples could raise individual simulation-backed claims.

The evidence base is multi-wavelength and redshift-dependent. Rest-frame optical and near-infrared data constrain stellar populations, nebular lines, metallicities, and dust. Submillimetre and radio data trace molecular or atomic gas and obscured star formation. X-ray and radio signatures can reveal AGN power, hot atmospheres, cavities, and jets. Integral-field spectroscopy maps resolved kinematics, outflows, turbulence, gradients, and central-versus-global quenching.

The deeper reason to include observing modes is ambiguity reduction. Different facilities and methods distinguish whether gas is absent, retained but inefficient, locally depleted, globally removed, heated, stripped, or prevented from accreting. They also distinguish single systems from population trends. This v2 candidate therefore frames observations as a method map rather than an unsourced facility list.

Simulations belong beside observations but not inside observational prevalence claims. The `simulation_model_scope` axis supports mechanisms under named model assumptions and blocks simple observed-frequency language. Simulation-only statements should be prefixed with "in simulations" or "in this model" until later product binding connects them to observational validation.
