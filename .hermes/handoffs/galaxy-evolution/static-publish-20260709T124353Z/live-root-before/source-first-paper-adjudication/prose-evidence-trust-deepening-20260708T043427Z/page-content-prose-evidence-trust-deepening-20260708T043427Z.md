## Claim 2942 — AGN feedback is scoped, not universal  
_Trust: qualified_

<!--claim:2942-->AGN feedback is best understood as a context-dependent regulator, not a universal switch that shuts down every galaxy. The support here is entirely accepted-limited and deliberately diverse in kind: a review-level synthesis stressing that AGN feedback works through many interacting modes rather than one; a group-scale study finding its influence is strongest in intermediate-mass systems, where the feedback energy is comparable to the binding energy of the gas; and two rows from the well-studied M51 galaxy, where models both require AGN feedback and reveal one specific low-power kinetic mode. None of these is a full-strength primary observation of universal quenching — which is exactly why the claim is written 'scoped', and why it must never be read as 'AGN quench all galaxies'.<!--/claim:2942--> <!--cite-unmatched:Method2 source-adjudication evidence 28087 (arXiv:2009.11175), 28151 (arXiv:2403.17145), 28074 (arXiv:2604.15438), 28155 (arXiv:2604.15438); unresolved to product cite ID--> [source positions & adjudication](../p1-source-position-ledger.html)

## Claim 2943 — AGN outflows can remove or suppress star-forming gas  
_Trust: observation-anchored_

<!--claim:2943-->This is the strongest-supported claim on the page, and the only one anchored by a full accepted position: direct observations of quasar-driven outflows sweeping star-forming gas out of distant host galaxies, confirmed with a human +1. ## Claim 2944 — Stellar feedback is a real but limited alternative  
_Trust: qualified_

<!--claim:2944-->Not every galaxy-scale outflow is driven by an AGN, and this claim keeps that alternative visible. Spectroscopic DESI/Mg II observations tie strong outflows and a measurable baryon deficiency in low-mass galaxies to their star-formation rate — a genuine, non-AGN channel with a clear scaling. But the same evidence base is honest about the ceiling of stellar feedback: a high-redshift simulation result finds it is generally insufficient, on its own, to fully quench high-mass galaxies. All three supporting rows are accepted-limited, so this reads as a real but bounded alternative, not a competing universal mechanism.<!--/claim:2944--> <!--cite-unmatched:Method2 source-adjudication evidence 28069 (arXiv:2512.05584), 28073 (arXiv:2512.05584), 28088 (arXiv:2605.03008); unresolved to product cite ID--> [source positions & adjudication](../p1-source-position-ledger.html)

Evidence & trust:
  - **LIMITED** supports `e:28069` (arXiv:2512.05584) — DESI/Mg II: stellar feedback drives strong outflows and a baryon deficiency in low-mass galaxies
  - **LIMITED** supports `e:28073` (arXiv:2512.05584) — outflow rate and mass-loading scale with the star-formation rate
  - **LIMITED** supports `e:28088` (arXiv:2605.03008) — stellar feedback alone is generally insufficient to fully quench high-mass galaxies

## Claim 2945 — Gas removal competes with recycling  
_Trust: caution_

<!--claim:2945-->Observing an outflow is not the same as permanently removing a galaxy's star-forming reservoir, and this claim exists to stop that over-reading. Two accepted-limited cautions travel with every outflow statement on the page: outflowing gas can fall back before it ever escapes the halo, and winds are less effective at expelling gas in low-redshift, low-mass systems in the first place. Both are cautions, not support — they qualify claim 2943 rather than adding to it.<!--/claim:2945--> <!--cite-unmatched:Method2 source-adjudication evidence 28066 (arXiv:2512.05584), 28075 (arXiv:0901.1880); unresolved to product cite ID--> [source positions & adjudication](../p1-source-position-ledger.html)

## Claim 2946 — Maintenance heating is real but model-dependent

Its support is chiefly two model-bounded rows — cosmological simulations that require AGN feedback to avoid over-producing massive galaxies, and the observation that different simulations implement that feedback differently — plus a single direct observational anchor: X-ray cavities inflated by AGN in hot atmospheres. ## Claim 2947 — A kinetic, radio-mode channel operates in massive systems  
_Trust: qualified_

<!--claim:2947-->AGN can also act through a kinetic, radio-mode channel, in which jets couple mechanically to the surrounding gas. The one full accepted position for this is a review synthesis — attributed as a review, explicitly not a primary detection — paired with a radio-mode observation in massive radio galaxies. Two accepted-limited cautions are kept alongside on purpose: understanding of jet feedback and its outflow power remains incomplete, and jet-gas coupling can be weak in high-redshift radio galaxies. A second support row from the same review paper (28111) is deliberately held out, so the claim does not lean twice on one source.<!--/claim:2947--> <!--cite-unmatched:Method2 source-adjudication evidence 28095 (arXiv:2009.11175), 28131 (arXiv:0901.1880), 28108 (arXiv:2009.11175), 28062 (arXiv:2508.06707); unresolved to product cite ID--> [source positions & adjudication](../p1-source-position-ledger.html)

## A caution with no target claim — 28060

<!--cite-unmatched:Method2 source-adjudication evidence 28060 (arXiv:2604.15438); unresolved to product cite ID--> One row does not belong to any claim at all. In the M51 galaxy, AGN feedback can be locally positive — compressing gas and encouraging star formation, the opposite of quenching. It was adjudicated accepted-limited but given no target claim, and it is preserved here purely as an anti-overclaim caution: a standing reminder that AGN feedback is not always negative. It is never used to support a quenching statement, which is exactly why it sits outside all six claim boxes.
