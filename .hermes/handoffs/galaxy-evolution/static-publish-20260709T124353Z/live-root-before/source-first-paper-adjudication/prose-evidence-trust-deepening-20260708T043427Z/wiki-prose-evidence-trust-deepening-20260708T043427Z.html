Galaxy Evolution: AGN feedback, outflows & quenching

A source-first synthesis — every highlighted claim is traced to individually-adjudicated source positions, with its trust level and its limits stated in plain English. Deepened second pass.

Galaxy evolution runs on a regulated cycle of gas: halos gather it, galaxies turn part of it into stars, and feedback — from supernovae and from active galactic nuclei (AGN) — returns energy and metals to the surrounding gas, sometimes shutting star formation down. This page does not narrate that whole story. It reports only what the Method2 source-first adjudication actually supports about the AGN-feedback part: six claims, each carried by specific source positions that were judged one at a time, and each written no more strongly than those positions allow.

How to read the trust labels — the Method2 source-first scale

These labels describe how strong each source position was judged to be. They are not product-database trust scores, and they are not the same scale Method 1 or Method 3 uses — do not compare a Method2 "accepted" to a label on another method's page.

ACCEPTED — full support
Accepted outright as support, with a human confirmation (+1). The strongest evidence this method carries; there are only two on the whole page (one for claim 2943, one for 2947).
ACCEPTED-LIMITED — qualified support, or a caution
Accepted but bounded: abstract-only, model-based, a single-galaxy (M51) case, or an explicit caution. It may support only a qualified statement, never a universal one.
EXCLUDED — held out by a rule
Kept out of support by an adjudication note (background-only, or single-source stacking). Preserved on the record, never cited.
REJECTED — held out outright
Off-topic, duplicate, or the wrong scale. Never cited.

How the citations work — what "cite-unmatched" means

Every evidence tag on this page (the e:28xxx IDs) is a Method2 source-adjudication ID — an internal identifier for a source position this method judged. None of them has yet been matched to a product citation ID in the wiki's live evidence database, so the honest label is cite-unmatched. Rather than invent a product citation, each ID links to the local source-position ledger where its adjudication is recorded (claim-status: p2 ledger). Matching these to product citations is a separate, future step; this page does not pretend it is done.

A note on the numbers — why 22 cited but the boxes sum to 21

The page draws on 22 accepted or accepted-limited source positions in total (2 full-accepted + 20 accepted-limited). But if you add up the evidence listed under the six claims below, you get 21, not 22. The one that is missing is 28060: it is a genuine accepted-limited position, so it counts toward the 22, but it carries no target claim (it is the positive-feedback caution described under "A caution with no target claim"), so it appears in none of the six claim boxes. In short — 22 counts every accepted position; 21 counts only the ones attached to a claim. Both are correct.

Claim 2942 — AGN feedback is scoped, not universal

qualified

AGN feedback is best understood as a context-dependent regulator, not a universal switch that shuts down every galaxy. The support here is entirely accepted-limited and deliberately diverse in kind: a review-level synthesis stressing that AGN feedback works through many interacting modes rather than one; a group-scale study finding its influence is strongest in intermediate-mass systems, where the feedback energy is comparable to the binding energy of the gas; and two rows from the well-studied M51 galaxy, where models both require AGN feedback and reveal one specific low-power kinetic mode. None of these is a full-strength primary observation of universal quenching — which is exactly why the claim is written 'scoped', and why it must never be read as 'AGN quench all galaxies'.

Claim 2943 — AGN outflows can remove or suppress star-forming gas

observation-anchored

This is the strongest-supported claim on the page, and the only one anchored by a full accepted position: direct observations of quasar-driven outflows sweeping star-forming gas out of distant host galaxies, confirmed with a human +1. Around that anchor sit four accepted-limited corroborations of deliberately different character — high-velocity outflow detections in lower-redshift AGN, an ultra-fast outflow detection framed only broadly, a simulation-based Seyfert-jet comparison carrying an explicit subgrid caveat, and the single-galaxy M51 case where outflow-driven turbulence locally halts star formation. Even with a full anchor, the claim reads 'can remove or suppress … in some systems', not 'always removes': the mechanism is demonstrated, its universality is not. One same-topic row (28133) is held out because it is background-only — it measures how to count outflows, not that outflows quench.

Claim 2944 — Stellar feedback is a real but limited alternative

qualified

Not every galaxy-scale outflow is driven by an AGN, and this claim keeps that alternative visible. Spectroscopic DESI/Mg II observations tie strong outflows and a measurable baryon deficiency in low-mass galaxies to their star-formation rate — a genuine, non-AGN channel with a clear scaling. But the same evidence base is honest about the ceiling of stellar feedback: a high-redshift simulation result finds it is generally insufficient, on its own, to fully quench high-mass galaxies. All three supporting rows are accepted-limited, so this reads as a real but bounded alternative, not a competing universal mechanism.

Claim 2945 — Gas removal competes with recycling

caution

Observing an outflow is not the same as permanently removing a galaxy's star-forming reservoir, and this claim exists to stop that over-reading. Two accepted-limited cautions travel with every outflow statement on the page: outflowing gas can fall back before it ever escapes the halo, and winds are less effective at expelling gas in low-redshift, low-mass systems in the first place. Both are cautions, not support — they qualify claim 2943 rather than adding to it.

Claim 2946 — Maintenance heating is real but model-dependent

model-dependent

Preventive, maintenance-mode heating that offsets cooling flows is the most model-dependent claim here, and the page keeps that label explicit rather than burying it. Its support is chiefly two model-bounded rows — cosmological simulations that require AGN feedback to avoid over-producing massive galaxies, and the observation that different simulations implement that feedback differently — plus a single direct observational anchor: X-ray cavities inflated by AGN in hot atmospheres. With only one observation against two model rows, 'model-dependent' is not optional hedging; it is the honest strength of the claim.

Claim 2947 — A kinetic, radio-mode channel operates in massive systems

qualified

AGN can also act through a kinetic, radio-mode channel, in which jets couple mechanically to the surrounding gas. The one full accepted position for this is a review synthesis — attributed as a review, explicitly not a primary detection — paired with a radio-mode observation in massive radio galaxies. Two accepted-limited cautions are kept alongside on purpose: understanding of jet feedback and its outflow power remains incomplete, and jet-gas coupling can be weak in high-redshift radio galaxies. A second support row from the same review paper (28111) is deliberately held out, so the claim does not lean twice on one source.

A caution with no target claim — 28060

caution-only

One row does not belong to any claim at all. In the M51 galaxy, AGN feedback can be locally positive — compressing gas and encouraging star formation, the opposite of quenching. It was adjudicated accepted-limited but given no target claim, and it is preserved here purely as an anti-overclaim caution: a standing reminder that AGN feedback is not always negative. It is never used to support a quenching statement, which is exactly why it sits outside all six claim boxes.

Conclusion & limitations (plain English)

What this page claims: AGN feedback is scoped, not universal; AGN outflows can remove star-forming gas, most convincingly in distant quasars (the one observation-anchored claim); stellar feedback is a real but limited alternative; gas removal competes with recycling; maintenance heating is real but largely model-dependent; and a kinetic radio-mode channel operates in massive systems.

What it does not claim: that every AGN quenches its host; that any of this is product-database trust (it is this method's own source-first status); or that the citations are product cites (all 7 groups are cite-unmatched). Most support is accepted-limited — abstract-only or model-bounded — so these claims are directionally supported, not quantitatively settled.
