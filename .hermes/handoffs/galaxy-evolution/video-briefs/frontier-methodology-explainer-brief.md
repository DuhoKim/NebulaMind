# Video Brief — "How We Mapped a Science Frontier from 12,000 Papers"
**For:** Yui (Flow) · **Requested by:** Duho (overnight 2026-07-16) · **Status:** ready to produce

## Concept
A ~75–90s informative explainer on how NebulaMind derived its galaxy-evolution *research frontiers* bottom-up from a large paper corpus (the Astro-Note-AI-inspired method), ending at the real studies it seeded. **Informative, not cosmic** (per Duho's standing note): on-screen infographics carry the message; any cosmic footage is a dim backdrop only.

## Narration script (onyx / bake-off-winner voice; ~90s)
1. "How do you find the most important *unanswered* questions in an entire field of science?"
2. "The usual way is top-down — a few experts write a review. But that can miss the gaps that are only *emerging*."
3. "So we did it bottom-up, starting from a plethora of papers."
4. "First, we pulled **twelve thousand** refereed galaxy-evolution papers from NASA ADS."
5. "We turned every abstract into a vector — a point in a space of meaning — so related ideas sit near each other."
6. "Clustering those points, the field self-organized into **32 research topics** — no human labeling."
7. "Then we overlaid **278 open debates** and **200 open questions**, extracted from 19 landmark reviews."
8. "Ranking each topic by how many open questions concentrate there — and how fast it's growing — the frontiers rose to the top."
9. "Number one: do our galaxy *simulations* get the physics right? Then the JWST high-redshift frontier, and cosmic chemical evolution."
10. "From this map, we ran real studies on public data — SDSS, JWST, and IllustrisTNG — and published them openly."
11. "That's how a machine reads a whole field, and points to where discovery is most likely next. NebulaMind."
