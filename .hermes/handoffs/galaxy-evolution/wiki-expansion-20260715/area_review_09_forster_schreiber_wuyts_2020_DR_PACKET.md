## 2. Established Findings

| ID | Epistemic type | Bounded finding | Boundary | Confidence | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| REV09-E01 | review_synthesis | Cosmic noon, centered near z about 2, is the peak era of cosmic star formation and a major assembly epoch for present-day massive galaxies. | The review emphasizes massive, observable star-forming populations mainly at z about 1-3, not every galaxy. | High as a population synthesis. | [REV09-R00] |
| REV09-E02 | observation | Massive star-forming galaxies occupy a relatively tight SFR-stellar-mass main sequence through cosmic noon. | SFR indicators, dust corrections, IMF, mass completeness, and treatment of high-mass curvature differ across surveys. | High for a sequence; moderate for exact slope and normalization. | [REV09-R00] [REV09-P025] [REV09-P026] [REV09-P028] [REV09-P029] |
| REV09-E03 | observation | Main-sequence star formation dominates the cosmic-noon SFR budget; strong starburst outliers contribute a minority. | IR-selected z about 2 samples and a chosen offset from the main sequence define starbursts. | High for the selected population. | [REV09-R00] [REV09-P027] |
| REV09-E04 | observation | Massive main-sequence galaxies at z about 1-3 contain large molecular-gas reservoirs with shorter depletion times than local disks. | CO or dust gas tracers require excitation and alpha_CO assumptions and are biased toward detectable massive galaxies. | High for elevated gas fractions, moderate for absolute masses. | [REV09-R00] [REV09-P013] [REV09-P014] [REV09-P015] [REV09-P030] |
| REV09-E05 | observation | A large fraction of resolved, massive star-forming galaxies at cosmic noon are rotation-dominated turbulent disks. | Emission-line selected/resolved IFU samples; inclination, beam smearing, sensitivity, and v/sigma criteria matter. | High for surveyed massive SFGs; not a claim that all z~2 galaxies are disks. | [REV09-R00] [REV09-P005] [REV09-P006] [REV09-P008] [REV09-P016] |
| REV09-E06 | observation | Ionized-gas velocity dispersions are elevated at high redshift and decline toward lower redshift. | Halpha-based disk samples with forward modeling; unresolved rotation and beam smearing must be removed. | High for the trend, moderate for the turbulence driver. | [REV09-R00] [REV09-P005] [REV09-P041] |

## 3. Open Debates and Tensions

| ID | Topic | Competing positions | Why unresolved / boundary | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| REV09-D01 | Main-sequence slope and normalization | Near-linear versus bent/high-mass-flattened relations and different redshift evolution. | SFR indicators, dust, IMF, selection, stacking, and bulge contamination shift fits. | [REV09-R00] [REV09-P025] [REV09-P026] [REV09-P028] [REV09-P029] |
| REV09-D02 | Disks versus mergers | Most massive main-sequence SFGs show ordered rotation, while disturbed systems and mergers remain important. | Resolution, depth, kinematic classification, pair timescales, and sample selection prevent a universal fraction. | [REV09-R00] [REV09-P005] [REV09-P006] [REV09-P008] |
| REV09-D03 | Turbulence drivers | Gravitational instability/accretion versus stellar-feedback energy and momentum injection. | Both scale with gas fraction and SFR; beam smearing and multiphase tracers complicate energy budgets. | [REV09-R00] [REV09-P041] [REV09-P048] |
| REV09-D04 | Clump longevity and migration | Long-lived bound clumps migrate inward versus short-lived clumps disrupted by feedback. | Ages, masses, and radial gradients are resolution/model dependent; simulations differ in feedback strength. | [REV09-R00] [REV09-P009] [REV09-P032] [REV09-P033] [REV09-P038] |
| REV09-D05 | Low inferred inner dark-matter fractions | Baryon-dominated inner disks or apparent low fractions caused by pressure support, IMF/gas masses, halo contraction/cores, and selection. | Rotation curves rarely extend far beyond the luminous disk and mass components are degenerate. | [REV09-R00] [REV09-P011] [REV09-P012] [REV09-P022] [REV09-P039] [REV09-P042] |
| REV09-D06 | IMF, SED, and dust systematics | Different stellar population histories, attenuation laws, and IMFs alter stellar masses and SFRs. | Integrated photometry cannot uniquely recover age-dust-metallicity mixtures. | [REV09-R00] [REV09-P001] [REV09-P050] [REV09-P051] |
| REV09-D07 | Compaction versus inside-out growth | Central compaction before quenching versus gradual inside-out disk growth with bulge buildup. | Cross-sectional populations do not uniquely reconstruct tracks; resolved SEDs are dust/model sensitive. | [REV09-R00] [REV09-P021] [REV09-P023] [REV09-P024] [REV09-P038] |
| REV09-D08 | Simulation-observation comparison | Cold-stream, unstable-disk, and feedback models reproduce subsets of observed scaling relations but not uniquely. | Forward modeling must reproduce selection, resolution, tracers, and correlated observables together. | [REV09-R00] [REV09-P034] [REV09-P035] [REV09-P033] |
