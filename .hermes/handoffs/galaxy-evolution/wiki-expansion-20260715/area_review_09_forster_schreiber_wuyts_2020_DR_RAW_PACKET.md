## Deep Research review-base result

Source-Base Review: NebulaMind Galaxy-Evolution Wiki Rebuild (Queue Item 09)

The epoch extending from redshift z∼1 to z∼3, colloquially known as "Cosmic Noon," represents the most critical phase of stellar mass assembly in the history of the Universe. During this roughly 3.5-billion-year window, the cosmic star-formation rate volume density reached its absolute peak, and approximately half of the stellar mass observed in the local Universe was forged. The 2020 review by Förster Schreiber and Wuyts serves as a definitive synthesis of the observational and theoretical breakthroughs characterizing this epoch, transitioning the astrophysical consensus from a merger-driven growth paradigm to an equilibrium-driven model of secular evolution. This document establishes the bounded source base, authorized parameters, and harvested primary literature required to reconstruct the galaxy-evolution wiki pages for this critical epoch.   

1. Review identity and scope map

The foundational text for this source base is a comprehensive synthesis of multi-wavelength population censuses and spatially resolved kinematic studies. The review definitively establishes that massive star-forming galaxies at z∼2 are not merely scaled-up versions of local spirals, but are fundamentally distinct physical systems: heavily gas-rich, geometrically thick, and highly turbulent disks operating in a state of marginal gravitational stability.

2. Established findings

The equilibrium growth framework—often termed the "bathtub model"—serves as the theoretical bedrock for galaxy evolution at Cosmic Noon. In this paradigm, galaxies are treated as open systems where the continuous accretion of cold gas from the cosmic web is delicately balanced against the consumption of gas via star formation and the expulsion of gas through stellar and AGN-driven outflows. Because the accretion rates at z∼2 are massive, the resulting disks exhibit extraordinarily high gas fractions, frequently constituting 40% to 50% of the total baryonic mass.   

This immense gas reservoir drives the disk into a state of marginal Toomre stability (Q∼1), generating intense gravitational instabilities. Consequently, the interstellar medium becomes highly turbulent, with intrinsic velocity dispersions soaring to 40−50 km/s, rendering the disks geometrically thick. 3. Open debates and tensions

Despite the unifying power of the equilibrium model, significant epistemological and observational tensions remained unresolved in 2020. A primary point of friction lies in the exact mechanisms governing sub-galactic physics, specifically the fate of giant star-forming clumps. In violently unstable disks, massive clumps are observed ubiquitously, but their longevity is deeply debated. If stellar feedback—particularly radiation pressure and supernova kinetic energy—is exceptionally efficient, these clumps may be blown apart in just a few free-fall times, contributing minimally to structural evolution. Conversely, if clumps are sufficiently massive and tightly bound, they can survive to migrate inward via dynamical friction, coalesce at the galactic center, and actively build the host's stellar bulge.   

Similarly, the macro-scale dynamical mapping of these galaxies yields polarizing interpretations. The observation of declining outer rotation curves in high-mass z∼2 disks implies that these galaxies are heavily baryon-dominated at their centers, possessing anomalously low dark-matter fractions within their effective radii compared to both local galaxies and standard ΛCDM predictions. However, extracting intrinsic rotation curves from high-redshift integral field data is perilously sensitive to beam-smearing artifacts and the precise treatment of asymmetric drift (pressure support).

5. What remained unknown in 2020

The boundaries of the 2020 synthesis were largely dictated by the spatial resolution limits of extant infrared observatories and the sensitivity thresholds of ground-based spectroscopy. While the Hubble Space Telescope mapped rest-frame optical light perfectly at Cosmic Noon, probing the rest-frame near-infrared—necessary to uncover the true underlying older stellar mass distribution—was severely limited. This obscured the true structural nature of older bulges and bars beneath the glare of heavily attenuated, young starbursting clumps. Consequently, precise characterization of dust attenuation curves and complex dust-to-stellar geometric mapping remained an elusive gap, generating systematic uncertainties in derived stellar masses and star formation rates. Resolving the exact spatial distribution of dense molecular fuel at sub-kiloparsec scales across normal main-sequence galaxies was similarly out of reach, leaving the precise trigger mechanisms for inside-out quenching and clump migration unresolved.

UNCITED_NOT_USABLE | AGN demographics as representative of normal SFGs | e.g., claims that typical z~2 star-formation is governed entirely by AGN feedback | Explicitly excluded by the 2020 review context boundary.

UNCITED_NOT_USABLE | JWST/MIRI/NIRCam observations of cosmic noon galaxies | Papers published post-2020 utilizing JWST (e.g., JADES, CEERS) | Explicitly excluded as they do not exist within the 2020 review's temporal boundary.

UNCITED_NOT_USABLE | Claim: "All z~2 galaxies are settled disks" or "All are mergers" | Various unverified extrapolations | Excluded due to selection-collapsing; the population contains a complex mix of dispersion-dominated systems, marginal stable disks, and mergers.

UNCITED_NOT_USABLE | Claim: "All giant clumps are long-lived" | Theoretical extrapolations | Excluded as overbroad; clump longevity is a function of baryonic mass, local density, and specific feedback prescriptions, remaining an open debate.

UNCITED_NOT_USABLE | Claim: "Low central dark matter explicitly means no halo" | Broad dynamical claim | Excluded; low f_DM within R_eff indicates central baryon dominance, not the absence of an extended dark-matter halo.

UNCITED_NOT_USABLE | Single line width equates to rotation | Beam-smearing artifacts | Excluded; global velocity dispersions (e.g., from unresolved H-alpha) without 2D forward modeling cannot be reliably parsed into rotational vs dispersion support.
