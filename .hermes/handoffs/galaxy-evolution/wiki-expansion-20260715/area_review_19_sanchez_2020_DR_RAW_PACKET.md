Spatially Resolved Spectroscopic Properties of Low-Redshift Star-Forming Galaxies: A Synthesis Review
1. Review Identity and Scope Map

The foundational understanding of galaxy evolution in the local universe has undergone a paradigm shift, transitioning from the analysis of aperture-limited, single-fiber integrated spectra to fully spatially resolved, two-dimensional spectroscopic mapping. This report provides a comprehensive, advisory-only synthesis of the integral-field spectroscopy (IFS) framework for low-redshift star-forming galaxies, operating strictly as a reference base for the NebulaMind galaxy-evolution wiki. The primary node of this synthesis is the core review authored by Sebastián F. Sánchez (2020), published in the Annual Review of Astronomy and Astrophysics (Volume 58, pages 99-155), titled "Spatially Resolved Spectroscopic Properties of Low-Redshift Star-Forming Galaxies". The verified identity of this core document is anchored by DOI 10.1146/annurev-astro-012120-013326, arXiv identifier 1911.06925, and ADS bibcode 2020ARA&A..58...99S.   

The scope of this synthesis captures the macroscopic and kiloparsec-scale characteristics of star-forming galaxies, their composite transitioning counterparts, and retired systems within the local universe (0.005<z<0.05). By leveraging data from the largest contemporary multiplexed optical IFS surveys—chiefly the Calar Alto Legacy Integral Field Area (CALIFA) survey, the Mapping Nearby Galaxies at Apache Point Observatory (MaNGA) survey, the Sydney-AAO Multi-object Integral field spectrograph (SAMI) survey, and the AMUSING++ compilation—the framework bridges the gap between global scaling relations and localized astrophysical processes. The synthesis maps the distribution of ionizing sources across galactic disks, distinctly separating regions dominated by young OB stars from those ionized by active galactic nuclei (AGN), shock fronts, or Hot Low-Mass Evolved Stars (HOLMES) and post-Asymptotic Giant Branch (post-AGB) populations.   

Crucially, this framework translates classical integrated relationships—such as the Star-Formation Main Sequence (SFMS), the Mass-Metallicity Relation (MZR), and the Schmidt-Kennicutt (SK) gas-depletion law—into their spatially resolved counterparts (the rSFMS, rMZR, and rSK). The methodology demands rigorous accounting for spatial resolution functions (Point Spread Functions smearing over ∼1 kiloparsec scales), signal-to-noise binning utilizing Voronoi tessellations, explicit Initial Mass Function (IMF) definitions, and specific stellar population synthesis (SPS) model libraries (e.g., Bruzual & Charlot or Vazquez & Leitherer) to derive accurate mass-weighted and luminosity-weighted histories. Furthermore, interpretations of radial gradients, inside-out mass assembly, and quenching mechanisms explicitly incorporate the necessary geometric deprojections and normalization to the galactic effective radius (R

2. Established Findings in Resolved Galaxy Evolution

The advent of optical integral-field spectroscopy has allowed astronomers to effectively dissect galaxies, transforming monolithic data points into heterogeneous, spatially resolved maps of chemical and star-formation histories. The 2020 review establishes a rigorous consensus on how local, kiloparsec-scale physics dictates the macroscopic properties of star-forming disks.

Inside-Out Mass Assembly and Structural Gradients

A cornerstone of the contemporary galaxy evolution framework is the empirical validation of the inside-out growth model. By performing full-spectrum fitting on individual spaxels across hundreds of disk-dominated galaxies, researchers have reconstructed spatially resolved star-formation histories (SFHs) and chemical enrichment timelines. The archaeological record preserved in the stellar continuum reveals pervasive negative radial gradients in both mass-weighted stellar age and stellar metallicity. The innermost regions and bulges of massive spirals contain the oldest, most metal-rich stellar populations, indicating rapid, early assembly and efficient chemical recycling deep within the central gravitational potential. Conversely, the extended outer disks are characterized by younger stellar ages and lower metallicities, pointing to prolonged, ongoing gas accretion and subsequent star formation at larger galactocentric radii. This structural regularity confirms that typical disk galaxies build their mass from the inside out, a process strongly modulated by the total integrated stellar mass of the system.   

Parallel to the stellar archaeological record, the present-day interstellar medium (ISM) exhibits a highly stable, nearly universal radial decline in gas-phase oxygen abundance. Resolved Scaling Relations: The rSFMS and rMZR

Consequently, the position of a galaxy on global scaling diagrams is fundamentally dictated by the localized, kiloparsec-scale processes occurring within its distinct internal sub-structures.   

Gas Depletion, Quenching, and Ionization Sources

Understanding why galaxies cease star formation—a process termed quenching—requires mapping the availability and efficiency of cold molecular gas. Spatially resolved studies integrating optical IFS with millimeter-wavelength interferometry (tracing CO to infer H$2$) have validated the localized Schmidt-Kennicutt (SK) law. On kiloparsec scales, $\Sigma{SFR}$ scales directly with the molecular gas surface density (Σ

When galaxies transition off the main sequence through the "green valley" toward retirement, this transition is predominantly driven by a structural depletion of the cold gas reservoir (a shrinking gas fraction) rather than a uniform, catastrophic drop in star-formation efficiency across the entire disk.   

Furthermore, IFS surveys have radically reshaped the classification of ionizing sources via spatially resolved BPT and WHAN diagnostic diagrams. A critical finding is the true nature of Low-Ionization Emission Regions (LIERs). Historically confused with weak, central Active Galactic Nuclei (LINERs), spatially resolved maps reveal that LIER emission is distributed widely across the retired disks of early-type galaxies and the older bulges of spirals. Based on equivalent width cuts (EW(H$\alpha$) <3 Å), this diffuse emission is conclusively attributed to a hard but dilute radiation field produced by ubiquitous, old post-AGB stars and HOLMES, effectively divorcing it from supermassive black hole accretion. When true optical AGN are isolated, they show a statistically significant correlation with central, inside-out quenching, suppressing kiloparsec-scale star formation relative to non-AGN control samples.

3. Open Debates and Theoretical Tensions

Despite the robust framework established by widespread IFS mapping, significant theoretical and methodological tensions remained actively debated in the literature up to 2020. The spatial resolution limit of ∼1 kpc for large surveys like CALIFA and MaNGA meant that physical processes operating on the scale of individual giant molecular clouds (GMCs) or distinct supernova remnants were inherently smoothed. This spatial blending, coupled with degeneracies in empirical calibrations, fueled several critical controversies.

A primary debate centers on the driving mechanism behind the resolved Star-Formation Main Sequence (rSFMS). While the correlation between Σ

Resolving this required massive, perfectly matched ALMA CO maps to perform partial correlation analyses controlling for gas mass, a dataset that was only beginning to mature in 2020.

Consequently, debates raged over whether the Fundamental Metallicity Relation (FMR), which posits that metallicity depends on both mass and SFR, holds locally at kiloparsec scales. Opponents argue that any secondary SFR dependence in the local rMZR is an artifact introduced by strong-line calibrators (like N2 or O3N2) that intrinsically correlate with the ionization parameter.

4. Key Measurements and Model Calibrations

The analytical backbone of the 2020 spatially resolved framework depends entirely on a series of rigid mathematical parameterizations, calibration slopes, and empirical thresholds. To derive physical meaning from raw spectral cubes, pipeline algorithms (such as Pipe3D or the MaNGA Data Analysis Pipeline) apply these specific values to deconvolved emission lines and continuum shapes.

The separation of active star formation from retired, older stellar populations relies explicitly on the WHAN diagram's equivalent width threshold. Spaxels exhibiting an H$\alpha$ equivalent width of less than 3 Å are flagged as retired, indicating that their sparse ionizing photon budget is supplied by hot, low-mass evolved stars rather than zero-age main-sequence OB stars. For regions that surpass this threshold and qualify as actively star-forming via the BPT Kewley demarcations, the internal dust extinction must be calculated prior to deriving star-formation rates. This is universally achieved utilizing the Balmer decrement, assuming an intrinsic Case B recombination ratio of H$\alpha$/H$\beta = 2.86$ at a standard electron temperature of 10,000 K.

5. Critical Unknowns and Future Frontiers

Despite the comprehensive mapping of kiloparsec-scale distributions, the 2020 review underscored several fundamental limits to the methodology that required next-generation instrumentation to overcome.

The highly structured, inside-out nature of galactic mass assembly and the maintenance of steady-state metallicity gradients over cosmic time strongly implies the continuous operation of galactic fountains and multiphase accretion. However, directly observing the gas inflow/outflow cycle at the disk-halo interface remained elusive. The spectral resolution of standard surveys was insufficient to seamlessly decouple the complex, low-surface-brightness kinematic signatures of vertical multiphase outflows from generic turbulent non-circular motions within the disk plane. Furthermore, the foundational assumption of a universal Initial Mass Function (IMF) across all spatial coordinates within a galaxy introduces profound, systematic uncertainties. If the IMF is heavily bottom-heavy in central bulges and top-heavy in active star-forming spiral arms, derivations of both the stellar mass surface density (Σ
∗
	​

) and star-formation rates (Σ
SFR
	​

) are significantly distorted, potentially altering the perceived slopes of the rSFMS and the rMZR.

8. Composite Identity Ledger: Methodological Covariance and Constraints

To accurately map spatial and physical quantities across distinct surveys (CALIFA, MaNGA, SAMI, AMUSING++), the 2020 review depends heavily on standardizing a highly complex methodological ledger. Direct comparison of derived quantities without carrying these underlying structural identities invariably induces false tensions in the literature.

Spatial and Resolution Mismatch: The characteristic physical resolution of IFS instruments varies significantly as a function of redshift and fiber-bundle design. For surveys like CALIFA and MaNGA, the instrumental Point Spread Function (PSF) smears data over ∼1−1.5 kiloparsec physical scales. Because typical individual HII regions range from 10 to 100 parsecs in diameter, these 1 kpc "spaxels" physically blend the intense emission of zero-age starbursts with the surrounding Diffuse Ionized Gas (DIG) and background continuum. Comparing these kpc-scale smoothed maps directly to sub-500 pc resolved maps introduces severe scatter due to the "stochastic" sampling of individual Giant Molecular Cloud (GMC) lifecycles. Therefore, every measurement must be rigidly tagged as either a kpc-scale or HII-region-scale extraction.
