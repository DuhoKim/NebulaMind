## Paper 1 — unlensed z≈9–10 metallicity deficit

Headline: Five unlensed z≈9.3–9.9 galaxies show gas metallicities about 0.7 dex, approximately five times, below local galaxies at the same stellar mass; the deficit survives the tested anchor swap.

Facts:

1. Pollock et al. sample: −0.69 ± 0.03 dex; leave-one-out spread 0.04 dex.
2. Curti+20 to Andrews & Martini 2013 anchor swap moves it 0.04 dex, to −0.65 dex.
3. Independent roughly 1,500-galaxy stacked-Te JADES analysis gives −0.5 to −0.6 dex.

Required caveat: descriptive, machine-generated draft; not a validated measurement and not a formal statistical detection; absolute Te scale has a roughly 0.1–0.2 dex systematic floor. Do not use `detection`, `discovery`, or `22σ` in public copy.

## Paper 2 — scaling relations from z≈0 to JWST

Headline: Anchored on about 490,000 SDSS galaxies, JWST galaxies form stars 6–100 times faster at fixed mass while their metal deficit stays near 0.4 dex from z≈4 to 7.

Facts:

1. Main-sequence elevation rises from +0.77 dex at z≈3.5 to +1.94 dex at z≳6.
2. Metallicity deficits are −0.43, −0.37, and −0.40 dex at z≈4.6, 5.3, and 7.2.
3. Tremonti anchor is about 0.24 dex high; recomputing SDSS on a Te-anchored PP04 O3N2 scale with N=200,000 prevents overstating the deficit by about 0.1 dex.

Required caveat: autonomous draft, no human clearance; emission-line selection favors high-sSFR systems; z>6 bin has 46 galaxies; local relations are extrapolated below log stellar mass about 8. Avoid `proves`, `resolves`, or claiming the debate is settled.

## Paper 3 — massive-galaxy abundance versus TNG

Headline: The z≈5–6 `too massive, too early` excess disappears when a 0.28 dex stellar-mass systematic is allowed, within the roughly 1 dex literature budget.

Facts:

1. Observed number density above 10^10.5 solar masses is about 3×10^-5 per cubic megaparsec versus TNG's 1.1×10^-5 at z=5, a factor about 2.7.
2. A downward mass shift around 0.28 dex erases the z≈5–6 excess; z≈7–9 candidates need about 0.44 dex in the paper's headline calculation.
3. A distinct residual remains: spectroscopically confirmed quiescent galaxies at z>6 sit about 2 dex above prediction and are not dissolved by these systematics.

Required caveat: descriptive automated confrontation, not validated; it says the data do not require new physics at z≈5–6, not that ΛCDM is confirmed or every tension is solved.

## Paper 4 — aperture/calibration framework

Headline: A practitioner's checklist for why metallicity surveys can disagree by up to a factor of five before real physics is involved.

Facts:

1. Calibration-scale offsets reach roughly 0.7 dex.
2. Aperture bias exceeds roughly 0.15 dex below a roughly 20% fiber covering fraction.
3. Diffuse ionized gas supplies 30–60% of H-alpha and can flatten or invert inferred metallicity gradients.

Required caveat: machine-generated methods synthesis; the contribution is the framework, not a new measurement. `REVIEW-CLEARED` is an automated workflow gate, not journal or human peer review. The thresholds are literature-sourced, not NebulaMind measurements.

## Paper 5 — calibration is not validation

Headline: Subtract TNG's own z≈0 tuning error and the story changes: star formation over-grows at high redshift, while the supposed factor-of-three-to-four chemical failure largely dissolves into an abundance-scale artifact.

Facts:

1. At z≈0 TNG is already 0.30 dex low on the main sequence and 0.12 dex high on the MZR.
2. Internal main-sequence growth is +1.30, +1.45, and +1.61 dex at z=4, 5, and 6 versus about +0.9–1.0 dex observed.
3. On one Te-anchored scale, metallicity evolution is about −0.23 to −0.25 dex versus approximately −0.50 observed, described by the paper's final abstract/conclusion as only about 1.5 times short and within the residual 0.1–0.15 dex systematic.

Required caveat: autonomous draft, no human clearance; SFR over-growth is the robust discrepancy; metallicity is suggestive and not significant. Avoid `TNG is broken`, `simulation fails`, or `validated` in titles.
