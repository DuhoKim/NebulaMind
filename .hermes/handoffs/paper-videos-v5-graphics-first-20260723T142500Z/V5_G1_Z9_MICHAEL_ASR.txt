A galaxy's oxygen abundance is how much oxygen its gas holds. Stars forge that oxygen, so it grows as galaxies build stars. This paper asks whether galaxies at Redshift 9 to 10 had already reached nearby levels. Answering is tricky because gravitational lensing can distort galaxy masses, and the nearby benchmark is extrapolated. So the paper uses five strictly un-lensed galaxies measured by the direct electron temperature method. The result, their oxygen sits near one-fifth of the nearby benchmark. In logarithmic units, that is 0.69 decks lower. The sample is small, and the absolute abundance scale stays uncertain. This is not a formal statistical detection.
