A galaxy's oxygen abundance is how much oxygen its gas holds.
Stars forge that oxygen, so it grows as galaxies build stars.
This paper asks whether galaxies at redshift nine to ten had already reached nearby levels.
Answering is tricky, because gravitational lensing can distort galaxy masses, and the nearby benchmark is extrapolated.
So the paper uses five strictly unlensed galaxies, measured by the direct electron-temperature method.
The result: their oxygen sits near one fifth of the nearby benchmark.
In logarithmic units, that is zero point six nine dex lower.
The sample is small, and the absolute abundance scale stays uncertain.
This is not a formal statistical detection.
