## The question, made answerable

"Is there new data?" only means something once we know **what would be evidence**. So the survey
runs in that order:

1. **What does BHU actually predict that is observable?** Not what it evokes — what it forecloses
   or requires. If nothing concrete survives contact with primary sources, that is the answer and
   the survey stops there honestly.
2. **What data now exists that did not when those predictions were framed?** Concretely, with
   coverage, scale, access and date.
3. **What kills each candidate?** Systematics, selection, instrument handedness, footprint.
4. **Is each candidate's provenance actually usable?** The gate we just built, applied before any
   design commitment.

## Why this is worth doing carefully rather than quickly

The GZ1 spin line died on human-classifier handedness bias, and specifically on a *bias control*
that could not be constructed from the available fields. That is a data-era problem, not a physics
problem. Modern morphology catalogues are produced by classifiers **we can control** — a CNN can be
evaluated on deliberately mirrored inputs because we own the pipeline — and modern surveys give
independent instruments and footprints. If BHU has a testable observable at all, the reason it was
untestable in 2008 may simply not hold now.

That is a hypothesis for the crew to confirm or kill, not a conclusion.
