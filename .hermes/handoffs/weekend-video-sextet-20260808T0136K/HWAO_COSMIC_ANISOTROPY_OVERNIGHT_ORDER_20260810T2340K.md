## The question, made answerable

Not "can we study anisotropy" — of course one can. The question is:
**is there a measurement whose dominant systematic is controllable with public data, unlike spin?**

Candidate probes to assess, each on its own merits:

- **Quasar / radio number-count dipole** — the reported excess over the CMB-kinematic expectation.
  Public catalogues exist. The systematics are selection, flux calibration, masking and Galactic
  contamination — all *measurable*, unlike an unmeasurable classifier prior.
- **SN Ia anisotropy** — directional dependence of expansion or of H0. Public compilations exist.
- **GRB angular clustering** — large-scale structure claims from burst positions.
- **H0 directional variation** — whether the tension has a sky dependence.
- **Cosmological parity violation** — the four-point-function parity claims in galaxy surveys, and
  their contested significance.
