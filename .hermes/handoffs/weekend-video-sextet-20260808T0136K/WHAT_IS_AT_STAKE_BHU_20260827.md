# What the black-hole-universe work is actually asking, and what is at stake

Tori, 27 August 2026, revised the same evening after two independent reviews ruled on the
sharpest open question. The revision is at the end; nothing above it has been rewritten. Written for someone intelligent who does not work on this.
No mechanism, no symbols — the receipts hold those.

## The question

Is the universe we live in the inside of a black hole?

That sounds like a slogan, and most versions of it are. But one version is written in
equations rather than words: a pair of mathematicians published an exact solution of Einstein's
equations in which the Big Bang was an explosion that happened *inside* a black hole, and a
shock wave — a real, sharp front, like the edge of a blast — surrounds everything we can see.
We would be sitting inside that blast, looking outward at a wall we cannot see through.

The work of the last week has not been asking whether that story is true. It has been asking
something narrower and more useful: **could we ever tell?**

## Why anyone should care

Ideas about our universe being contained in something larger are cheap. Nearly all of them are
unfalsifiable in the strict sense: nothing you could measure would come out differently. This
one looked different, because it is a genuine solution with a genuine boundary, and a boundary
is the kind of thing that might leave a mark on the sky.

So the prize was never "prove we live in a black hole". The prize was to take one member of
that family and either give it a real test or show precisely why it cannot have one. Both
outcomes convert an argument into knowledge.

## What we found, in the order we found it

**First: from the inside, the model looks exactly like an ordinary universe.** This was a
surprise and it cost us our starting idea. We had assumed that if we sat off to one side of
the wall, the universe would expand slightly faster in one direction than another, and that
this lopsidedness would be the giveaway. It is not. The interior of this model is *exactly* the
ordinary expanding universe of textbook cosmology — not approximately, exactly — so measuring
expansion in different directions can never detect the wall, no matter how good the
measurement gets or how far off-centre we sit. That test is dead, permanently, and we killed
it ourselves.

**Second: the only possible fingerprint is a patch of sky.** If the wall happens to be close
enough that our view of the oldest light reaches past it, we would see one round region of sky
— tens of degrees across, in one direction — that looks different from everywhere else. One
patch, one direction. Nothing else about the model is visible at all.

**Third, and this is where it got hard: nobody had ever worked out what crossing that wall
would look like.** The original papers construct the geometry and stop. So we could say *where*
the patch would be but not *whether you could see it* — which is the difference between a test
and a picture.

That question turned out to have a moving answer. My first attempt said the wall was
effectively see-through; that was wrong, and wrong in an embarrassing way — inside a black hole
the role of space and time swaps over, and I had used a time direction as though it were a
distance. Corrected, the answer became: it depends on what the material beyond the wall is made
of, and the equations do not tell us. We can bracket it, but not pin it.

**Fourth, and most important: there is a setting where the model produces no signal at all.**
Two effects fight each other. The wall's motion brightens the sky on one side, and the fact
that different directions look through the wall at different moments in its history dims it on
that same side. For one particular composition of the material beyond the wall, those two
effects cancel *exactly*. Not approximately — exactly, at a specific value we can now name.
In that case an observer could sit as far off-centre as you like and the sky would look
perfectly uniform.

I did not find this. An adversarial reviewer did, and it found it because my own check was
built in a way that could not have seen it: I took the size of the signal without its sign, and
I looked at six settings rather than searching between them. A cancellation lives exactly in
the gaps between samples.

## So what is actually at stake

**If the model survives**, we learn less than people expect. Surviving means "not ruled out",
and the price of survival is that we would have to be sitting very close to the centre of the
blast — within a fraction of a percent of the way to the wall, on current numbers. That is a
coincidence, not a confirmation. It would make the idea live but awkward.

**If the model is excluded**, one specific version of the black-hole-universe idea dies — the
shock-wave version, with its particular geometry. It would not kill the broader family. Other
versions place the boundary elsewhere, or hide it behind different physics, and they would need
their own work.

**And if the honest answer is that this cannot be tested by light at all — that is a real
result, and it may well be where we land.** It would not be a failure or a shrug. It would be
the statement that a specific published cosmology, for specific and demonstrable reasons, makes
no prediction any telescope can check: because the interior is exactly ordinary, because the
wall may be beyond our view, and because even when it is not, there is a setting where its
signature vanishes identically. That converts "an idea that sounds untestable" into "an idea
proved untestable, here, by this argument". Very few speculative cosmologies have been given
that dignity, and it is worth more than a weak bound would be.

## What is genuinely riskable, stated plainly

- The exclusion is **not established**. It holds across most of the range we can compute, and
  fails completely in a narrow band. Until that band is understood, we cannot say the model is
  ruled out, and I will not say it.
- Even outside that band, the strength of the constraint has moved **three times** as the
  calculation improved, and once by more than a factor of two after I swept properly something
  I had previously only sampled. A number that unstable is not ready to be quoted at anyone.
- Several of the biggest corrections came from my own errors, caught by review rather than by
  me: a published figure that more than doubled, a claim about what light does at the boundary
  that was backwards, and the cancellation above. The work is more trustworthy for having been
  attacked, but nobody should mistake the current state for a settled one.
- Two independent calculations now agree on the central physics — that nothing reaches us from
  beyond the wall, and that the wall's own glow is the only thing we could ever see. That part
  I would defend.

## The one thing to know before anyone calls this a test

**The model can produce no signal at all without being wrong.** Any claim that this is a test
of black-hole cosmology has to survive that sentence first. It is the single most important
finding of the phase, it was found by someone attacking my work rather than by me, and it is
the reason I am reporting the state of play instead of a result.

# Revision, 27 August, evening: the hole is deeper than the document above says

Everything above stands. One thing has got worse, and it is the thing the document turns on.

## What I asked two reviewers

The passage above says there is a setting where the two effects cancel exactly and the model
produces no signal at all. Naturally the next question is: **which** setting? Where, exactly,
does the model go silent? I had one answer from my own calculation and a second, different
answer from an independent one, and I asked two reviewers to decide between them.

## What they said

They disagreed with each other, and the disagreement resolved against **both** answers.

The first ruled that the rival construction was **geometrically forbidden**. Varying which
direction you look sweeps across a family of *different* pieces of matter on the boundary, not
one piece of matter evolving through time — and the rule that construction used only applies to
a single piece of matter following its own history.

The second ruled that **the published theory does not answer the question at all**. The papers
specify how the pressure of the material beyond the wall relates to its density. They never
specify its *temperature* — not its composition, not its heat content, none of it. And my own
answer, it pointed out, quietly assumed the material was pure radiation, which would fix a
property of that material to a value contradicted by the very settings where the silence
occurs. I had been caught making that same assumption once before, fixed it in one place, and
left it standing in another.

## So the honest position, stated plainly

**The model has a setting where it produces no signal at all — and the theory as published
cannot say which setting that is.**

Not "we have not calculated it yet". The information needed is absent from the source material.
To locate the silent setting you would have to *add* physics the original papers never wrote
down — what the material beyond the wall is made of, how hot it is, how its heat is distributed
— and then the answer would be a property of what you added, not of the model being tested.

## Why this is a result rather than a disappointment

It moves the finding from "here is a gap in the test" to something sharper:

**This cosmology contains a blind spot, and cannot tell you where its blind spot is.**

A test you cannot perform is a nuisance. A theory that cannot specify the circumstances under
which it becomes untestable is a different and more interesting object — and demonstrating that
takes more work than confirming or refuting would have.

## What survives, and what I have taken back

- **Survives, and is stronger than before:** the silence itself. Two calculations, resting on
  *different* and *incompatible* assumptions about the material beyond the wall, both produced
  the cancellation. That it appears under either set of assumptions is better evidence for the
  phenomenon than agreement on a single number would have been.
- **Survives:** that nothing reaches us from beyond the wall, and that the wall's own glow is
  the only thing we could ever see. Both calculations agree.
- **Taken back:** every claim about *where* the silence falls, mine included. They were not
  merely unreconciled; they are now unsupported.
- **Taken back:** part of my own treatment of how the wall's temperature varies across the sky,
  for assuming a composition the model excludes at the settings that matter.

## The line to hold if anyone quotes this work

Before: *the model can produce no signal at all without being wrong.*

Now: **the model can produce no signal at all without being wrong, and the theory cannot say
under what circumstances that happens.** Any claim that this is a test of black-hole cosmology
has to get past both halves.
