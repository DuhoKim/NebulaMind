## ATTACK 4 -- Postdiction vs Falsifiability
You are correct that evidential weight and falsifiability are different concepts. A rigid mathematical model that postdicts a known value is still falsifiable *in principle* (because the model's rigid structure could have output a value that contradicts the data). The fact that the data was known in advance lowers the *evidential weight* of the success (due to the risk of tuning the model to fit the answer), but not its falsifiability. You are not making the mirror error in theory. However, because this specific model is not rigid (see Attack 2) and the parameter was fit from the data (see Attack 5), it is not falsifiable in practice either.
