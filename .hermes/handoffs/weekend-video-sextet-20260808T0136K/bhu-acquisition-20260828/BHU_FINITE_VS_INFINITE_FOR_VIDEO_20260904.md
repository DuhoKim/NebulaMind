## 1. The objection

> *The universe is infinite in size and mass, but a black hole has finite mass and size.*

## 2. It is correct, and as stated it is decisive

An infinite universe containing infinite mass is not the interior of a finite black hole. Those are incompatible, and
no amount of arithmetic reconciles them. **If the universe is infinite, the black-hole-universe idea is simply false.**

The video should say that plainly rather than softening it. What follows is not a rescue — it is what the objection
settles once you notice that the papers already accept it.

## 3. Both founding papers are finite universes *by construction* — the objection is why

**Pathria (1972), entry 1.** His identity is `R_s = R_max`, and `R_max` — a maximum expansion radius — only exists if
the universe recollapses. He says so at **L395**: this holds "only for k = +1, which therefore seems the natural
choice," and concludes at **L406–407**: "accordingly, we must have **k = +1**." A closed universe: finite volume,
finite mass. Put an infinite universe into Pathria's paper and there is no argument left to make.

**Gaztañaga (2023), entry 56.** His opening sentence, **L24–L25**: "we argue instead that our Universe has a very
large but **finite** regular mass M". Then **L25–L27**: "A system with a finite mass M has a finite gravitational
radius r_S = 2GM. When M is contained within r_S, this is a black hole… In the limit where there is nothing else
outside, the inside corresponds then to a local isolated universe."

**And he states the objection himself, as the opposite limit of his own model — L262–263:** "In the other limit, if
r_S = ∞, we recover the **infinite ΛCDM model with M_T = ∞**."

That is the infinite universe, sitting explicitly at one end of a one-parameter family, with the finite black-hole
universe at the other. He is not claiming an infinite universe is a black hole. He is claiming ours is finite, and
that you can tell which by measuring Λ.

## 4. So the objection converts into the real question

**Is the universe finite or infinite?** That is an observational question, and it is open.

**A point the video should not get wrong:** "the universe is infinite" is not a measurement. Standard flat ΛCDM is
usually *taken* to be spatially infinite, but what is measured is that space is flat to within errors. Flatness data
bound the curvature radius from below — they cannot establish exact flatness, and exact flatness is what an infinite
extent requires. **Neither "finite" nor "infinite" is an established fact.** Presenting either as settled would be the
same error in opposite directions.

## 5. Why the famous coincidence cannot answer it

This is where the objection meets the arithmetic in the companion note, and the two turn out to be the same
observation from opposite sides.

The coincidence is computed over a sphere of radius `R_H = c/H₀` — **a finite sphere, whether or not the universe is
finite.** The mass inside it is finite either way. So `R_s = R_H` comes out exactly 1 in an infinite flat ΛCDM
universe just as it does in a finite black-hole universe.

**The coincidence cannot distinguish the two cases, which is precisely why it is not evidence for either.** The
companion note shows the same thing algebraically: the identity follows from flatness at critical density and holds at
every value of H₀.

## 6. What *would* distinguish them — and what our audit found about it

Gaztañaga names the observable himself, **L29–L30**: his BH universe "is observationally very similar to the ΛCDM,
except for the **lack of the largest scale perturbations, which are bounded by r_S**." A finite universe cannot hold
waves longer than itself, so the largest-angle structure in the cosmic microwave background should be missing.

That is a real, testable difference, and it is exactly what this lane spent the week auditing. Two findings, both
filed:

- **The size of the effect is not fixed by the theory.** `PROGRAM_A_FREEDOM_MAP_20260902.md`: the paper supplies no
  perturbation prescription, so every route to a number passes through choices external to the theory.
- **The derived boundary does not determine the modes.** `K4_RESULT_20260904.md`, class `K4_UNDETERMINED`: imposing the
  model's own boundary on the perturbations leaves the interior modes undetermined — a prediction needs a condition
  about radiation at infinity that the sources never state.

**So the one observable that could settle finite-versus-infinite is, in these constructions, not yet calibratable.**
That is the honest place the story currently stands. It is not a refutation of the idea; it is a statement that the
idea has not yet been brought to the point where the sky can judge it.

## 7. One complication, ours to state rather than hide

A finite universe is not automatically a comfortable option. `K2_RESULT_20260903.md` established that the closed
(`k = +1`) matching **recollapses** — Easson's Proposition 2 confirmed, not refuted. Our universe appears to be
accelerating, not heading for recollapse. That is a real problem for Pathria's closed model, and it is why Gaztañaga's
construction is a **flat (`k = 0`) finite top-hat that expands forever** (K2's table, entry 56's cell), with the
acceleration coming from the boundary rather than from a recollapse-free closed geometry.

**Finite does not mean closed, and the video should not blur them.**
