**The perturbation spectrum of the torsion universe is not computed, and the paper says so twice.** Abstract-level: "the reconstruction of an inflation potential is only a mathematical technique we use to calculate the scalar spectral index and tensor-to-scalar ratio" (L88–93). Section IV opening: "one needs to consider the quantized perturbations of the torsion field in a background FLRW universe ... For our model, we shall address this in a future publication. Here, we find a scalar field potential which gives the same dynamics of the scale factor" (L283–290, L301–306). So the acknowledgement is explicit, but it is framed as a technique rather than as a gap; the conclusions (L465–482) then report r, n_s, α_s as if they were properties of "a universe in a black hole with spin, torsion, and particle production".

What would be needed to justify the transfer: (i) the linear perturbation equations of the spin fluid + torsion + particle-production sector on this background in Riemann–Cartan spacetime, including the Cartan constraint (torsion is algebraic in ECSK, so tensors propagate as in GR but the *scalar* sector is fluid-like); (ii) proof that the comoving curvature perturbation of that fluid obeys the canonical-inflaton equation with c_s = 1 and no non-adiabatic pressure. Neither is plausible a priori: the matter is an ultrarelativistic thermal fluid with p = ρ/3 (L90), so its adiabatic sound speed is 1/3 not 1, the torsion term ∝ T⁶ has its own equation of state, and K ≠ 0 is a dissipative source, so fluctuations near T ≈ T_max/√2 are thermally sourced (warm-inflation-like), not Bunch–Davies vacuum. For a general-c_s fluid P_ζ ∝ H²/(ε c_s) and r = 16 ε c_s, and thermal sourcing changes the tilt formula altogether. Hence r (which needs the scalar amplitude) and n_s (which needs the scalar equation) are the *unwarranted* outputs; only the tensor amplitude ∝ H² is robust, and the paper never quotes it. The Ellis–Madsen equivalence buys the background only.

## 2. Inputs and freedom

- **β**: free, bounded only by β < β_cr = 1/929.0915 (L215–218). Fiducial β = 1/929.25 (L266) ⇒ β/β_cr = 929.0915/929.25 = 0.999829, i.e. 1 − β/β_cr = 1.71×10⁻⁴: the coefficient is tuned to 2 parts in 10⁴ of criticality. Table I (L228–238): a single bounce needs β/β_cr ≳ 0.996 (0.984 already gives 2 bounces); "if β is slightly less than β_cr, then the universe has only one bounce" (L225–244). Total e-folds "about 60-150, depending on the value of the production coefficient" (L455–456); "the total number of e-folds ... only depends on β" (L397–398). The text never prints which β-range gives Planck-consistent n_s; it is in Fig 9 (not in the text pin) and the conclusions say only "for a particular range of the particle production coefficient" (L474–476).
- **g_* / g_f / g_n1**: standard-model g_b = 28, g_f = 90 (L95), g_n1 = 9 (L118) — fixed by assumption of SM content.
- **a₀** (bounce scale factor): 10⁻²⁷ m, "order of the Cartan radius of an electron" (L262–266). Sensitivity claim: n_s, r, α_s and total e-folds "do not change significantly with a₀ and are only sensitive to β" (L397–402, L428–433). I accept this as the paper's numerical finding; it is not a strength — it just moves all the freedom onto β.
- **T₀** = 0.99 T_max, "not sensitive" (L267–271); not varied in Sec. V (L402).
- **Reference epoch N**: NOT derived. "For most inflationary models, N is about 50-60 ... the lower limit ... can be as low as N = 18 [47]. We shall calculate more precise bounds on this scale for our model in a forthcoming work" (L357–364). They scan N ∈ [18, 60] (L365–366) and then *select* N ≈ 20–25 because that is where n_s ≈ 0.96 lands (L369–373); Sec. V evaluates everything at N = 20 (L399–401). Adversarial point: the model has **no reheating** — inflation ends at t = 1.33×10⁻⁴² s and the universe "enters the radiation-dominated era (decelerating) without needing reheating" (L169–175, L282–286) at T ≈ T_max/√2 ~ 8×10³¹ K, i.e. near-Planck energy density (Eq 9, L249–251). The low-N escape of their ref. [47] (Liddle & Leach 2003) is obtained by pushing the reheating temperature down to the MeV scale; instantaneous radiation domination at near-Planck density puts the horizon-exit N at the *high* end of the standard bookkeeping (≈ 60 or above). So the in-model N is the one the paper says gives n_s ≈ 0.99, and the N ≈ 20 choice is imported against the model's own thermal history.

- **Is n_s a definite number?** No. Printed values: n_s ≈ 0.96 for N ≈ 20–25; n_s ≈ 0.99 for N = 50–60 (L369–377); "we always can observe a red tilt" (L369–370, L402–425). r "for all permissible values of N ... about an order of magnitude smaller than ... r_0.05 < 0.12" (L381–384) and, across a₀ and β at N = 20, "approximately constant ... between 0.01 and 0.03" (L425–426). Running: "about O(10⁻³)" (L389–390), "always less than 0 and its absolute value is O(10⁻³ − 10⁻⁴)" (L427–428). So: n_s is a function of N spanning ~0.96–0.99 (30 mσ of Planck 2018 width) and, at fixed N, a function of β over an unprinted band; r and α_s are narrow bands.
- **The "0.965" at L231 is not n_s.** It is the β/β_cr = 0.965 row of Table I ("3 bounces"), L228–233. Any reading of that line as a spectral index would be an extractor artefact; the spectral-index numbers are only the prose "≈ 0.96" / "≈ 0.99" and Figs 5, 9.
- Minor internal issues: α_s is defined as "d ln n_s/d ln k" (L348) — a typo for dn_s/dlnk; Eq (16) uses the Hubble η while Eq (18) uses the potential η_V (L343–354), harmless at this precision; and the paper's own tension arithmetic is off: (0.99 − 0.968)/0.006 = 3.7σ against the Planck 2015 value it quotes, not "about 6σ" (L373–377).

## 4. Warrant

The mechanism does **not** produce this falsifier. The chain from the torsion universe stops at a(t); everything from Eq (10) onward is the physics of a canonical, vacuum-fluctuating, single scalar field that the authors *construct* to reproduce that a(t). The paper says so (L88–93, L283–290). The prediction is therefore inflation's prediction, evaluated on a particular H(t) — which is why r and α_s come out "of the same order of magnitude as in Starobinsky's model" (L392–394).

Would a lane-owned threshold make it CALIBRATED? No. The missing item is not a threshold but a NUMBER the lane may not supply: the scalar (and tensor) power spectrum of the spin-fluid + torsion + particle-production system on this bounce background, plus the in-model horizon-exit N. Both are the authors' declared future work (L289–290, L363–364, L483) and, as far as the pinned corpus shows, remain undetermined in the published literature (RQ-B 2026-09-01 filing). Even the sign of the tilt is not guaranteed to transfer, because the fluid's c_s and thermal sourcing enter the scalar equation directly (§1). A lane threshold on n_s or r would be a threshold on the inflaton reconstruction, not on the torsion universe; scoring it either way (LIVE at N = 20, FIRED at N = 60) would credit or debit the mechanism for a computation that was never done for it.

Consequence for entry 52 (Unger & Popławski 2019): its sentence that the torsion bounce with particle production "predicts the CMB parameters consistent with Planck 2015, as was shown in" this paper over-reads it. What was shown is that a reconstructed inflaton with the same a(t) is Planck-consistent at a hand-picked N. Entry 52 should not be upgraded on this citation, and the Popławski branch's background-only filing stands.

## 5. Tier proposal, argued

**CANDIDATE_CONSISTENCY_ONLY.** Case:

- Not CANDIDATE_CALIBRATED_FALSIFIER: the numbers are (a) borrowed from canonical inflation via a background-only reconstruction the paper itself calls "only a mathematical technique" (L88–93); (b) a function of a free coefficient β tuned to 1.7×10⁻⁴ of criticality with the Planck-consistent β-range never printed (L266, L474–476); (c) a function of a free epoch N ∈ [18, 60] chosen post hoc at the value that matches, spanning n_s ≈ 0.96 → 0.99 (−1.2σ → +6.0σ against Planck 2018). A falsifier whose value the author selects after seeing the data is not calibrated.
- Not CANDIDATE_QUALITATIVE_DIRECTIONAL: the only direction-level statements ("always a red tilt", "running always < 0", r ≈ 0.01–0.03; L369–370, L425–428) are properties of any slowly-decreasing-H inflaton and are explicitly compared to Starobinsky (L392–394); they discriminate nothing about torsion, and the red-tilt sign is itself not warranted to transfer (§1, §4).
- Not CANDIDATE_PROSPECT: the paper does name the missing computation (L289–290, L363–364, L483), but its own claim is a present-tense consistency ("Our predictions ... are consistent with the Planck 2015 observations", L30–31, L480–482), and PROSPECT would under-describe what it asserts while CALIBRATED would over-credit it.
- Not UNDETERMINED_NEEDS_x: the paper's tier is determinable from its text; what remains undetermined is the *lane's* RQ-B question (torsion-universe perturbation transfer), and this paper does not move it — it confirms the gap in the authors' own words.

Standing arithmetic to file alongside, if Duho admits it: n_s LIVE at −1.2σ at the paper's chosen N ≈ 20–25; FIRED at +6.0σ at N = 50–60 (the epoch the model's no-reheating history implies); r LIVE (0.01–0.03 vs < 0.058); running LIVE-but-unscorable (0.5–0.7σ, below Planck resolving power). Neither the LIVE nor the FIRED transfers to the torsion mechanism.

## Plain language

This paper does not compute what light and gravitational-wave ripples a torsion bounce would leave in the microwave background. It computes the expansion history of the bounce, then builds an ordinary inflation field that would expand the universe the same way, and reads the ripple numbers off that stand-in field using inflation's own formulas — and it says so in its own text. The stand-in's answer depends on two dials the paper leaves free: the particle-production coefficient, tuned to within two parts in ten thousand of a critical value, and the moment during inflation at which today's sky left the horizon; the authors scan that moment from 18 to 60 e-folds and keep the value (about 20) where the tilt happens to match Planck. At the value their own no-reheating history points to (about 60), the same machinery gives a tilt Planck 2018 rules out at six sigma. The tensor ratio and the running come out small and are fine with the data, but they are the generic inflation numbers, not a torsion fingerprint. So this paper is a demonstration that the model can be dressed to fit, not a prediction the model makes; the number the lane would need — the actual perturbation spectrum of the torsion universe — is still missing, and the paper is the clearest statement in the corpus that it is missing.
