# Adversarial verdict

## Attack 1 — scope

**C1 survives only in a qualified form.** Poplawski does not restrict the claimed minimum mass to colliders, a formation epoch, or a formation mechanism. In the Discussion he first conjectures a maximum density for ordinary matter made of quarks and leptons, says gravitational collapse of any configuration of such matter cannot create a singularity even when a horizon forms, and then says: “The mass density of a black hole also cannot exceed” the electron Cartan density, from which he states the minimum mass. The LHC is introduced only in the following sentence as a consequence. Treating the LHC as the scope is therefore too narrow.

But “any black hole, however formed” is stronger than the derivation. The paper repeatedly labels the density step an expectation/conjecture, bases it on elementary Dirac particles and ordinary fermionic matter, and does not mention primordial black holes. Carr et al. list PBH formation routes involving radiation overdensities, scalar condensates, false-vacuum bubbles, domain walls, and other mechanisms. The pinned Poplawski source does not establish that its fermionic-density argument applies unchanged to every such source. It does support applying the bound to a PBH formed by collapse of matter with spin; it does not pin a mechanism-independent theorem for every possible PBH.

The enormous ambient density in radiation domination is not an exemption found in Poplawski. His argument is about the density of the collapsing matter/system and explicitly claims cosmological singularity avoidance for matter with spin. However, the paper supplies no calculation of PBH formation in a radiation background, so I cannot verify that horizon mass, pressure, torsion, and the mixed particle content leave the simple minimum-mass inference unchanged.

## Attack 2 — placement and the review's claim

**The quotation is current within the review, not historical.** Carr et al. first identify the decade-old asteroid, sublunar, and intermediate-mass windows, then say the “middle mass window has shifted to” \(10^{17}\)–\(10^{23}\,\mathrm g\). Thus the script correctly distinguishes the historical sentence from the review's then-current sentence.

**C2 nevertheless overstates the source.** The same sentence says the lowest and highest windows have narrowed and are “sometimes argued” to be excluded. The review also stresses that limits change, have caveats, and usually assume a quasi-monochromatic spectrum. Elsewhere it reports claims that PBHs below \(10^{18}\,\mathrm g\) contribute less than 1% of DM depending on mass function and astrophysical assumptions, and notes that rotating-hole evaporation constraints can close the lower window. Therefore \(10^{17}\)–\(10^{23}\,\mathrm g\) is a caveated summary window, not an unqualified continuous region in which PBHs can certainly be all DM.

Using Poplawski's printed \(10^{16}\,\mathrm{kg}=10^{19}\,\mathrm g\), the arithmetic placement is correct: it lies inside those quoted endpoints. Calling the entire \(10^{17}\)–\(10^{19}\,\mathrm g\) interval a presently open, forbidden band is not supported by the review's full discussion.

## Attack 3 — the unreproduced floor

**The pinned paper contains no actual route from \(\rho_{Ce}\) to \(10^{16}\,\mathrm{kg}\).** It gives the two estimates consecutively but supplies no black-hole radius, density convention, or intermediate equation. With Schwarzschild mean density

\[
\rho=\frac{3c^6}{32\pi G^3M^2},
\]

inverting \(10^{51}\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}\) gives \(2.70\times10^{14}\,\mathrm{kg}=2.70\times10^{17}\,\mathrm g\), as the brief says. Dropping geometrical factors or using \(r\sim GM/c^2\) moves the result only by order-unity factors, not to \(10^{16}\,\mathrm{kg}\).

It is right not to declare a demonstrated algebraic error: Poplawski says “expect,” “approximately,” “on the order,” and “\(\sim\),” and the input Cartan radius itself is only quoted by decade. Since density scales as \(M^{-2}\), a factor 37 in mass corresponds to about \(1.4\times10^3\) in density, which is plausible under stacked order-of-magnitude estimates but fatal to precise window placement. I could not verify the stated \(10^{16}\,\mathrm{kg}\) floor from the pinned source. At the directly inverted value, only roughly \(10^{17}\)–\(2.7\times10^{17}\,\mathrm g\) lies below the floor—about 0.43 decade, not two decades.

## Attack 4 — whether detection is a live firing route

**Logically yes, operationally not established as claimed.** A secure observation that independently establishes (i) a compact object is a black hole, (ii) it is primordial, and (iii) its mass is below the applicable ECKS floor would contradict Poplawski's minimum-mass claim. It need not constitute all dark matter; “PBH dark-matter detection” is unnecessarily restrictive.

But the pinned review does not demonstrate an available positive-identification experiment across \(10^{17}\)–\(10^{19}\,\mathrm g\). The old GRB femtolensing bound over \(5\times10^{16}\)–\(10^{19}\,\mathrm g\) is disputed because of finite-source and wave-optics effects and is omitted from the master constraint plot. Proposed GRB parallax and X-ray-pulsar microlensing probe asteroid masses, but the review describes the latter as existing data being close to excluding \(f\sim1\) and future observations potentially closing the window—constraint prospects, not a pinned ability to identify an individual PBH and establish primordial origin. Hawking-radiation and induced-GW signals are also model-dependent population inferences, not automatically a mass-tagged black-hole detection.

The band is therefore not “impossible anyway,” but neither is the claim “both bands are open right now; the test is available” verified. The review supplies prospective sensitivity and contested constraints. A genuinely identified sub-floor PBH would fire the claim, but this corpus does not pin a present, achievable detection protocol with the required identification.

## Bottom line

The bibliography should broaden the conceptual falsifier beyond the LHC: a securely identified PBH below a defensible ECKS minimum mass is a valid candidate route for the class of matter to which Poplawski's argument applies. The stronger B12 route is not confirmed. Its two-decade band depends on an unreproduced floor, its universality exceeds the fermionic derivation, and its claimed present detectability is not established by the pinned review. Record this as a **narrowed, conditional astrophysical route**, not as a confirmed open test 37 decades better than the collider route.
