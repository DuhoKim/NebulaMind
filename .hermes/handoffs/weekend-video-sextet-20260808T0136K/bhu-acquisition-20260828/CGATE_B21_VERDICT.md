The quoted objection is indeed in reference-note (11) under “REFERENCES AND NOTES” on p. 202, not in the article body. Word-for-word, it reads:

> Spatially closed universes have a single future singularity (Harrison, E., Nature, 204 (1964), 1179). Thus object A in a distant galaxy recedes while object B in our Galaxy falls into a nearby black hole. While B approaches asymptotically the event horizon of the black hole, but in cosmic time never crosses it, object A in the distant galaxy ceases to recede and starts to approach. When the rising density of the collapsing universe approaches the density of the black hole, the black hole loses its event horizon and A and B in company with the rest of the universe collapse together into a common singularity. See also Hawking, S.W. & Penrose, R., Proc. R. Soc. London, A314 (1969), 529. This argument suggests that the black hole population fails to affect the reproductive rate of universes, and each closed universe in Smolin’s theory produces at most one offspring universe.

The qualification is therefore only that the paper does contain body-text criticism of Smolin; “its objection” should mean this particular single-future-singularity objection, not every objection Harrison makes.

## Claim 2 — refuted

The proposed cross-entry implication does not follow. Harrison's premise is not merely the sign of spatial curvature. It is a globally closed universe that later recollapses to **one future singularity**, swallowing the black-hole region into the common crunch. Entry 54's `Omega_k < 0` denotes positive spatial curvature in its bounce cosmology. That sign alone does not entail a future global recollapse, much less Harrison's single-common-singularity causal history. Inflation, a bounce, and late-time accelerated expansion make the omitted dynamics decisive. Thus “closed” overlaps geometrically but is not an interchangeable dynamical premise in the two arguments.

Harrison's reasoning is also explicitly tentative—“This argument suggests”—and does not establish that a daughter universe has not already branched off through the conjectured quantum-gravity process before the parent black-hole horizon is erased in a later crunch. Its claim that the infaller never crosses the horizon “in cosmic time” also imports a coordinate-sensitive black-hole description into a global collapsing spacetime without supplying the needed causal or quantum-gravitational derivation.

If Harrison's stronger premise and conclusion were granted, the objection would be important to cosmological natural selection: reducing every closed parent to at most one offspring removes the black-hole-number variation that is supposed to furnish differential reproduction. But confirmation of entry 54's curvature-sign prediction would establish neither that premise nor that conclusion. Consequently it would not, by itself, show that entry 31's selection mechanism fails, and the claimed corpus-level tension should not be recorded as an implication.
