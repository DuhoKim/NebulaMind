## 1. Entry 37 — the existence/obstruction boundary

Entry 37 opens and closes as a construction paper:

> We construct a class of global exact solutions ...

Its operative result is a family of exact FRW/TOV shock-wave cosmologies inside a black hole. Theorem 1 proves existence and uniqueness within that family; Theorems 2 and 3 characterize the resulting shock speed. Theorem 2 says the constructed shock is everywhere subluminal iff `σ≤1/3`, and Theorem 3 gives its Big-Bang limit for the three σ regimes.

That is a real, source-contained parameter constraint. It is not a sufficient reason to reclassify the whole paper as an obstruction. The principled test separating entry 37 from entries 10 and 40 is:

> For a one-label-per-paper tier, classify the paper by the logical role of its operative contribution. A constructive paper remains constructive when a theorem delineates the physical domain of the constructed family. Use `THEORETICAL-OBSTRUCTION` when excluding a stated model class from a desired conjunction is itself the paper's operative result, rather than a bound internal to an existence construction.

This is not a distinction between formal theorem and informal derivation. It is the difference between:

- **constructing allowed solutions and describing their domain** (entries 10, 37, 40); and
- **showing that the target conjunction cannot be achieved in the stipulated class** (entries 5 and 22).

B30 crosses CGATE B29's warning by negating Theorem 37's characterization and promoting the negation over the paper's central existence result. The same method would convert uniqueness theorems, causal-horizon properties, energy conditions, and parameter bounds in many constructive papers into paper-level no-gos. That is incompatible with the primary-paper convention already used to reject entry 25 as a screen true positive.

The σ result should not disappear. The bibliography should say that within the paper's exact constant-equation-of-state shock family, everywhere-subluminal propagation requires `σ≤1/3`, with `σ>1/3` excluded. That is a claim-level obstruction/constraint inside a construction paper, not its paper-level tier.

**Entry 37 ruling: keep `CONSISTENCY-ONLY`; record the theorem explicitly.**

## 2. Entry 5 — class scope and shell interpretation

The source does say “the general problem,” but B30 overreads that phrase if it treats it as all FRW/Schwarzschild junctions. The actual derived domain is narrow and explicit:

- a pressureless, positively curved, expanding FRW interior;
- a Schwarzschild exterior modified by the same cosmological constant;
- matching at maximum expansion/event horizon;
- the separating hypersurface is null;
- Barrabès–Israel null-shell junction conditions apply.

Within that parameterized class, the paper computes the transverse extrinsic curvatures. The angular components agree, but the `uu` component jumps. Equation 17 therefore gives a nonzero surface pressure. The conclusion states that the expansion-to-contraction transition **“can only be done through a null shell”** characterized by that pressure.

This is more than a neutral description of a junction. It is both:

1. a constructive description of what the correct junction contains; and
2. an obstruction to the conjunction “Pathria-type interior/exterior identification + null horizon matching + smooth shell-free transition.”

The second is the note's operative contribution: it was written to test Pathria's identification through matching conditions, and its central result is that the presumed smooth match fails. A counterexample with continuous transverse extrinsic curvature in the stated domain would refute the result; no observation would.

The class is narrow, but the tier rule does not require breadth. Scope belongs in the label prose. The defensible entry is:

> `THEORETICAL-OBSTRUCTION` — for the stated pressureless closed-FRW/Schwarzschild-(Λ) null-horizon setup, smooth shell-free matching is excluded; the transition requires a pressure-bearing null shell. This is not a no-go against all universe-in-a-black-hole models or all FRW/Schwarzschild junctions.

**Entry 5 ruling: propose `THEORETICAL-OBSTRUCTION`, with the narrow domain printed.**

## 3. Entry 49 — adopt an ownership-of-proof convention

The factual reading is clear. Entry 49's discussion says:

- each exact inflationary-bubble solution studied begins with an initial singularity;
- under exact spherical symmetry, the initial singularity cannot be avoided;
- the proof uses the Penrose theorem and assumes the weak energy condition;
- quantum weak-energy-condition violation is the stated escape.

So B29's original characterization of all its impossibility language as “technical asides” was wrong. This is substantive obstruction content.

The full proof, however, is not printed in entry 49. The paper says the result “can be shown,” gives the theorem and intuition, and cites Farhi and Guth—entry 48—for the proof. It does not itself check all Penrose-theorem premises for the laboratory initial-data class or supply the full derivation. Entry 49 is a dynamics/taxonomy paper that **reports and uses** the companion no-go.

### Convention the corpus should adopt

Use an **ownership-of-proof convention** for `THEORETICAL-OBSTRUCTION`:

> A paper receives the proof-based tier when it presents the no-go derivation itself, or gives a self-contained application of an established theorem by establishing the relevant hypotheses and conclusion. Merely stating, summarizing, or citing a no-go proved in another identifiable paper does not transfer the tier. Record the cited obstruction in prose and tier the proof-owning paper after audit.

Why this convention is preferable:

- The tier explicitly says the paper **proves** an impossibility, not merely mentions one.
- It prevents one theorem from making every review, sequel, and neighboring construction paper an obstruction entry.
- It preserves citation provenance and directs readers to the actual warrant.
- It scales consistently: a paper can discuss an observational exclusion without becoming the measurement paper, and can discuss a theorem without becoming its proof source.

This is not a strength/quality judgment. Entry 49's reported claim may be correct; the issue is which document owns the derivation represented by the tier.

**Entry 49 ruling: keep its current paper-level tier, but add a note that it reports the spherical/WEC singularity obstruction proved in entry 48.**

## 5. `CONSISTENCY-ONLY` is not the logical negation of obstruction content

B30 sharpens the category beyond the bibliography's record. The bibliography introduces the classes as **testability classes** and elsewhere says tiers describe claim shape. `CONSISTENCY-ONLY` is used for papers whose operative contribution supplies compatibility/construction without a discriminating observational statistic. It is not a universal assertion that every sentence and subsidiary theorem in the paper is silent about impossibility.

The “silent about what cannot happen” language comes from B1's explanation of the boundary between a pure no-go and a paper that **merely** fails to predict. It is useful for distinguishing archetypes, but it cannot serve as the literal negation of “contains any impossibility result.” Existing construction papers routinely contain causal exclusions, uniqueness results, forbidden parameter regions, energy-condition bounds, and critiques of alternatives.

Therefore:

- filing entry 37 `CONSISTENCY-ONLY` does not deny Theorem 2; it identifies the paper's operative construction role;
- filing entry 49 there does not erase its cited obstruction; provenance prose must preserve it;
- entry 5 moves because its operative contribution is the matching obstruction itself.

If the corpus wants lossless representation of every claim, it needs claim-level secondary tags or a constraint/warrant field. Reassigning the sole paper-level tier whenever any subsidiary no-go appears is not a workable substitute.
