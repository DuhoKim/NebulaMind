## 1. Entry 51: the Papapetrou result is a derivation, not an expectation

The paper's strongest justified result is narrow, but real. Starting from spin-density conservation, the Papapetrou multipole definitions, and the total antisymmetry of the Dirac spin density, the paper derives the single-pole consequences

`N^{ijk}=0 -> Sigma^{ijk}=0 -> psi=0`.

For a nonzero Dirac field this is a contradiction. The paper therefore concludes that the single-pole Dirac distribution is not a solution of the ECKS gravitational field equations. The same reasoning excludes a system of singular points. This has exactly the preregistered form: within the specified ECKS/Dirac/Papapetrou domain, no nonzero field satisfies both the field/conservation equations and point support. A counterexample in that mathematical domain would refute it; no measurement is needed.

The ring result is also derived, though more narrowly than the abstract advertises. The author assumes the string has the symmetry of the Dirac–Kerr–Newman ring, evaluates the relevant moments for support on `delta(r-a) delta(z)`, and obtains a vanishing axial spin component, contradicting the intended fermion. That excludes the symmetric Dirac–Kerr–Newman singular ring. It does **not** prove the abstract's unrestricted wording for every conceivable one-dimensional string geometry; the move from “string” to “ring” is introduced by “from symmetry considerations we expect.” The obstruction record must preserve that limitation.

Several later claims are not proved:

- the Cartan radius follows from an order-of-magnitude balance, not from the Papapetrou contradiction alone;
- the toroidal replacement is suggested as a natural nonsingular structure, not constructed as a solution;
- the maximum density and `~10^16 kg` black-hole floor are estimates;
- avoidance of all collapse singularities sourced by quarks and leptons is explicitly a conjecture;
- the ultraviolet cutoff is explicitly an expectation.

Those qualifications narrow the theorem; they do not turn the point-support contradiction into plausibility prose.

### The proposed claim-level disposition is a dodge

The bibliography's adopted ownership rule says a paper earns `THEORETICAL-OBSTRUCTION` when it presents the no-go derivation itself. Entry 51 does. The no-go is also the title, the abstract's opening result, the stated purpose of the introduction, the whole Papapetrou analysis in Sections III–IV, and the first result restated in the discussion. It is the operative contribution.

Calling `CALIBRATED-FALSIFIER` the “higher-information label” has no support in the taxonomy. The classes distinguish claim shapes; they are not an ordinal scale. Moreover, the numerical floor is downstream, heuristic, and currently recorded as unreproduced from the stated inputs. It is especially perverse to let that weaker corollary suppress the paper's self-owned derivational result.

Entries 37 and 55 do not establish the proposed analogy. Their exclusions were restrictions internal to constructive results or cited/no-proof claims under the paper-level convention. Here the paper itself sets out to prove and does prove a no-member result over a stated class. If the schema permits only one paper-level label, the operative-contribution test selects `THEORETICAL-OBSTRUCTION`. Preserve the calibrated floor/LHC route as a secondary claim rather than erasing either half of this dual paper.

Defensible obstruction wording:

> In the ECKS theory, under the paper's Dirac-field and Papapetrou multipole assumptions, a nonzero Dirac field cannot be a single-pole point distribution or a system of such points; under the additional symmetry assumptions used for the Dirac–Kerr–Newman construction, it cannot be the singular ring considered there.

Do not inflate this to “ECKS proves that all fermionic collapse is nonsingular” or “all arbitrary string-supported Dirac fields are excluded.”

The submission is also wrong that the result is “recorded nowhere.” Entry 51 already says “Dirac fields in ECKS cannot be singular.” What is missing is the precise proven domain—point distributions, systems of points, and the symmetric Kerr–Newman ring—plus the separation of that result from the conjecture about all collapse singularities.

## 2. Entry 12 versus entry 40

Entry 12 is constructive at paper level. For a homogeneous relativistic spin fluid it derives a Friedmann-type equation whose right-hand side becomes negative before `a=0`, so the displayed solution has two turning points and a nonsingular bounce. It then treats shear explicitly:

- the Raychaudhuri inequality gives a necessary condition for avoiding a singularity;
- without shear, the paper says the condition is sufficient;
- if the initial shear dominates torsion, both scale as `a^-6` and a singularity would form;
- the proposed repair is fermion production, with a phenomenological `beta H^4` rate making `n_f^2` grow faster than shear.

That conditional failure mode is a real limitation, but not the paper's operative no-member theorem. The paper does not prove that no member of a specified model class can satisfy a target conjunction. It exhibits a torsion-plus-particle-production mechanism intended to produce a bounce and then develops the resulting oscillatory/inflationary universe. Its final claim that the scenario remains valid for realistic inhomogeneous rotating collapse is asserted, not proved.

Thus entry 12 is in the same relevant family as entry 40: torsion alone can lose to shear, followed by a particle-production repair. The newer Tolman-sphere treatment adds derivational detail and a necessary-condition statement, not a paper-level theoretical obstruction.

## 3. Entry 31 and footnote 6

Entry 31's early statements that stars and carbon chemistry occur only in narrow parameter ranges are review-level anthropic/sensitivity claims supported by citations. The paper does not define and exhaust a model class or prove the requested conjunction impossible outside it.

Section 4 is unambiguously measurement-refutable. Its proposed decisive observation is a sufficiently massive neutron star, certainly `2.5 M_sun`, which would put the strange-quark mass on the opposite side of the critical value and thereby refute hypothesis S under the stated independence assumptions.

Footnote 6 is not a hidden obstruction. It says a skeptic can rescue S by adding a coupling between the strange-quark mass and parameters affecting star formation, calls such unsourced rescue an ad hoc hypothesis, and allows that it could be warranted if an independently supported fundamental theory actually supplied the coupling. This is Duhem-style hypothesis protection and a qualification of the falsifier's scope. It proves neither that such a coupling is impossible nor that no coupled model can satisfy a conjunction. Entry 31 remains `CALIBRATED-FALSIFIER / LIVE`.
