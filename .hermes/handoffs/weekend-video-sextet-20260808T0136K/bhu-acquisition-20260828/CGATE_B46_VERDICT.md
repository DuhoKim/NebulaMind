# B46 adversarial verdict — entry 14

Entry 14 remains `CONSISTENCY-ONLY` at paper level. Its operative contribution is constructive: under an expressly conjectural limiting-curvature hypothesis, it builds Schwarzschild/de-Sitter and collapse junctions, explores evaporation endpoints, and constructs closed, semiclosed, and flat Friedmann worlds inside black holes. The paper also explicitly presents itself as an escape from Farhi–Guth's entry-48 theorem by violating null positivity and potentially global Cauchy-surface existence.

Two points require narrowing. First, Section IV's forbidden `sigma` intervals are a genuine claim-level exclusion: within the paper's exact Vaidya/de-Sitter matching family, no admissible junction exists in those intervals because the same hypersurface cannot have compatible intrinsic causal character on both sides. That is family-delimiting and does not organize the paper, but “nothing class-wide” is too dismissive unless qualified as “not paper-level.” Second, the scan does not contain eight figure sheets. It contains **13 text/front-matter sheets, seven figure sheets, and one colophon sheet**. The bibliography and B46 docstring should correct that custody/read-scope count.

I visually inspected all 21 rendered sheets at 170 dpi, including the complete prose and equations, both appendices, references, all 12 figure captions, all seven diagram sheets, and the final colophon. I used OCR only to navigate and independently checked its garbled equations against the page images.

## 1. Results in Sections 2–5

### Section 2: eternal black hole

The paper begins from a limiting-curvature hypothesis rather than deriving a UV theory. It assumes all curvature invariants, including the Weyl contribution, are bounded at a Planckian scale and then makes what it calls its “second main hypothesis”: at limiting curvature the Ricci tensor takes de-Sitter form, `R_mu_nu = Lambda g_mu_nu`. The introduction equivalently describes the effective stress tensor as vacuum-like. The authors repeatedly call the underlying theory unknown and their assumptions guesses about it.

Within those assumptions, Section 2:

- locates the Schwarzschild radius `r0` where the curvature reaches its bound;
- replaces the deeper interior by de Sitter;
- joins the two across a short spacelike transition layer using Israel's massive-thin-shell formalism; and
- derives the shell stresses and conformal diagram.

This is a conditional construction, not a no-member result. The special values of `alpha` that simplify or remove a shell choose subcases of the constructed matching rather than exclude the overall model class.

### Section 3: collapse geometry

Section 3 replaces the eternal black hole with a homogeneous spherical dust collapse. It joins Friedmann, Schwarzschild–Kruskal, and de-Sitter regions, gives the matching conditions, and illustrates the result in Figure 5. Again, this is constructive. Statements that certain special `alpha` values give a smooth join or that a transition layer is needed otherwise are internal matching facts, not the operative endpoint.

### Section 4: evaporation and the `sigma` exclusions

Section 4 is openly speculative about the late evaporation regime. It models decreasing mass with a Vaidya metric, joins it to de Sitter on the limiting-curvature hypersurface, and assumes evaporation stops at

`m_min = sigma l`.

With `beta = (alpha/12)^(1/4)`, the paper states:

- if `beta > 1`, `1/(2 beta) < sigma < beta^2/2` is forbidden;
- if `beta < 1`, `1/2 < sigma < 1/(2 beta)` is forbidden; and
- if `beta = 1`, no such interval exists.

The stated reason is exact compatibility: the Vaidya and de-Sitter metrics must induce the same intrinsic geometry on the junction, so the junction must be spacelike or timelike for both simultaneously. That fails in the listed parameter intervals.

This satisfies the b28 logical shape at **claim level**:

> Within the stipulated Vaidya/de-Sitter junction model, no member whose `sigma` lies in the forbidden interval admits the required common junction hypersurface.

A compatible counterexample in one of those intervals would refute it. The result is nevertheless family-delimiting, like entry 37's parameter partition. Section IV uses the allowed regions to enumerate stable remnants, closed pinched-off worlds, and two limiting conformal diagrams; the paper's title, abstract, Section V, and conclusion remain organized around constructed nonsingular interiors and child worlds. Thus it is not an operative paper-level obstruction and does not change the tier.

The record should preserve the claim-level exclusion rather than summarize Section IV only as containing endpoints.

### Section 5: future interiors

Section 5 considers de-Sitter decay and constructs or sketches several outcomes:

- an eternal black-hole continuation retaining the collapsing matter's quantum numbers;
- an asymptotically flat new universe/white-hole development if the information is lost;
- a closed Friedmann universe attached after de-Sitter deflation;
- a flat Friedmann universe; and
- an open case that is compatible at the conformal-diagram level but described as questionable.

The authors call the Figure-11 case an example of creating a closed or semiclosed world in a laboratory. The uncertainty and speculative language weaken the physical warrant but do not turn the contribution into an exclusion.

**Ruling on attack 1:** no Section 2–5 result is the paper's operative class exclusion. Section IV does contain genuine claim-level no-match intervals and should be recorded at that strength.

## 2. Entry-48 escape and the shell-stress chain

The escape reading is faithful.

For the spacelike Schwarzschild/de-Sitter shell, equations (2.15)–(2.18) give the surface stress components. In the paper's notation,

`S_t^t = lambda/(4 pi)`,

while the two angular components are `(H + lambda)/(8 pi)` and the radial component vanishes. With

`beta = (alpha/12)^(1/4)`,

equation (2.18) gives approximately

`H = (beta/2 + 1)/l`,

`lambda = -(beta - 1)/l`.

For the explicitly discussed `alpha = 24`, `beta = 2^(1/4) > 1`, so `lambda < 0` and therefore `S_t^t < 0`; the angular components remain positive. The authors then expressly infer that for a particular null vector, `T_mu_nu l^mu l^nu` may be negative in the transition region. They say the Penrose-singularity conditions are therefore not fulfilled and add that, since the radial shell stress vanishes, the energy density must change sign through the transition layer.

The record's chain from equation (2.18) to `S_t^t < 0` and then to possible null-energy violation is correct as the paper's own argument. It should remain scoped to the effective transition layer and to the conditional shell construction; it is not a general derivation that every limiting-curvature theory violates NEC.

Section 5 supplies an independent explicit cross-link. It says the model does not contradict reference [7] because the existence of a global Cauchy surface and `T_mu_nu l^mu l^nu >= 0`, the conditions used there, may be violated. Reference [7] is visually confirmed as E. Farhi and A. H. Guth, *Phys. Lett.* 183B (1987) 149—entry 48.

The two records therefore do corroborate the hypothesis boundary from opposite sides:

- entry 48 proves its classical singularity result under noncompact global Cauchy development and NEC;
- entry 14 constructs an escape by allowing those assumptions to fail at limiting curvature.

**Ruling on attack 2:** confirmed, with the transition-layer/conditional-model scope retained.
