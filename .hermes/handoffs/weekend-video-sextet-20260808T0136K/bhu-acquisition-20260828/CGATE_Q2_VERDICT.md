Its title is `[0910.1181] ...`. The *Physics Letters B* volume/pages/Elsevier line occurs **inside that ar5iv rendering of the arXiv submission**. Journal metadata in an arXiv manuscript does not turn the artifact into Elsevier's version of record and does not prove textual identity with it. The official records distinguish the [arXiv item](https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.1181) from the [publisher article](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0370269310005691).

The publisher's abstract does independently confirm that the published article makes the same maximum-density-to-minimum-mass assertion. That is useful corroboration, but an abstract does not establish that the publisher full text has no intervening derivation elsewhere. A later [publisher erratum](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S037026931300899X) also exists; its advertised corrections concern equations 21, 26 and 29 rather than this mass estimate. This makes the target claim unlikely to have been repaired by that erratum, but it still does not make the local ar5iv artifact the version of record.

Therefore these statements in `OPEN_QUESTIONS_FOR_DUHO.md`, the bibliography, and the `b13` docstring are unsupported or false as written:

## 2. The transition is real; the exact 86-character claim is not stable

The relevant source sentence is indeed consecutive and supplies no displayed or verbal intermediate calculation:

> The mass density of a black hole also cannot exceed ρCe, from which its minimum mass in the ECKS theory is ∼10¹⁶ kg ...

The entire asserted inference is carried by “from which.” The claimed exact count, however, depends on extraction conventions:

- normalized typography, from `cannot` through `kg` inclusive: **78** characters (`ρ_Ce`, `∼10¹⁶`);
- plain `10^16` with a space after `∼`: **80** characters;
- ar5iv's spaced math rendering, beginning at `also` (`ρ C e`, `∼ 10 16`): **86** characters.

Thus 86 is reproducible only under one undocumented normalization and with the span beginning at **“also,” not “cannot.”** It is not a source-invariant fact and the current wording should be replaced by the sentence itself or by “one sentence, with no intermediate step.”

## 3. “Unreproduced” versus “error”

The ruling reaches the right cautious label, but its argument is too binary. “Error” is not merely an unverifiable psychological claim about what the author did. It carries checkable mathematical content: that the printed inference is inconsistent under the intended physical definitions or that no admissible completion can yield the stated value. “Unreproduced” deliberately withholds that stronger conclusion. The two labels therefore do **not** record identical checkable content.

On the available record, withholding the stronger conclusion is still correct. Six conventional reconstructions miss the target, but the script itself admits untested Kerr-Newman/local-proper-density/full-interior possibilities and suppressed coefficients in order-of-magnitude relations. That prevents a proof of error. The strongest defensible formulation is:

> The arXiv-derived text states the density and minimum mass consecutively without a connecting calculation. None of six tested conventional reconstructions reaches the printed floor; the nearest is a factor 13.1 low and the mean-density reconstructions require a density 3.14–4.09 decades below the stated/normalized values. The enumeration is non-exhaustive, so this establishes an unreproduced and quantitatively severe gap, not an arithmetic error.

That wording avoids understatement while preserving the epistemic boundary.

The supposed published-version fact would not weaken AGATE's error argument. If a verified version of record omitted the step too, that would strengthen evidence that the omission survived publication. It still would not prove that no physically admitted route or suppressed coefficient reaches the estimate. Since the fact is not established here, it cannot bear either conclusion.
