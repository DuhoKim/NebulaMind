# Question-6 implementation verdict

The implementation accurately records the selected outcome—entry 51 remains `CALIBRATED-FALSIFIER / LIVE`—but its stated factual basis is not supported by the question-3 record. Q3 established a standing/scope treatment for one calibrated claim in entry 44. It did **not** establish the generic paper-level priority rule now attributed to it: “when a paper carries a calibrated-falsifier claim, that tier leads over a competing primary class.” That rule is retrospective extrapolation, and it is the closure's “whole basis.” The implementation is therefore refuted on its load-bearing premise, without reopening or changing Duho's Q6 outcome.

## 1. Attack 1 — what Q3 actually established

Q3's closed record says that the agent interpreted Duho's instruction “answer question 3” as returning the decision to the agent, selected “Reviewer B's option,” and made four edits. It did these relevant things:

1. classified entry 44 as `CALIBRATED-FALSIFIER / FIRED`;
2. scoped the firing to the Section 4 thermal free-5D prediction `n_s = 1`, not the whole holographic framework;
3. added entry 44 to the existing tier/standing table; and
4. propagated already-adjudicated FIRED/LIVE standings to entries 7 and 31.

The two Q3 gate verdicts confirm precisely that implementation. `CGATE_Q3_VERDICT.md` says the rejected `n_s=1` spectrum is a model component inside the BHU construction and approves the component-level scoping. `AGATE_Q3_VERDICT.md` likewise says the row honestly scopes what fired. Neither verdict states or tests a priority rule for a paper that independently owns two different tier-shaped contributions.

Entry 44's alleged duality was **standing duality inside one model**: a computed spectrum fired while a broader framework and an uncomputed repair survived. Q3's question was how to record what observation rejected without overstating that rejection. It was not a contest between a central self-owned theoretical-obstruction theorem and a downstream calibrated corollary.

Entry 51 presents that different contest. As `CGATE_B34_VERDICT.md` established, its point-support contradiction is the title result, the abstract's opening result, the purpose of the analysis, and the result actually derived in the body. The calibrated floor is downstream, heuristic, and unreproduced from the paper's stated inputs. `AGATE_B34_VERDICT.md` preferred the calibrated tier, but did so using the “higher-information label” ranking that the Q6 closure expressly disclaims. Thus B34 produced a substantive split; Q3 did not pre-resolve it.

There is a principled distinction between entry 44 and entries 37/55, but it is narrower than Q6 claims. The presence of a genuine calibrated falsifier distinguishes entry 44 from constructive papers containing only internal bounds. It supports giving the calibrated claim its own row and standing. It does **not**, without an additional adopted rule, prove that the calibrated class must displace every other candidate paper-level class regardless of operative contribution. Q3 supplies claim scoping, not label precedence.

There is a second attribution problem. The Q3 closure itself says, “I read that as returning the decision to me rather than answering it, and I acted on it.” Calling the resulting implementation “Duho's Q3 ruling” or saying “you already decided this shape” overstates the record. Duho authorized/returned the question; the recorded agent chose the option. Q6 may adopt that implementation as an analogy, but it cannot honestly present a generic Duho-decided convention as an antecedent fact.

Required correction: retain the Q6 outcome, but replace the precedent claim with an explicit new Q6 convention, for example:

> Q6 selects the calibrated-falsifier claim as entry 51's paper-level label and records the independently proved theorem as a scoped secondary claim. Q3 demonstrated that the standing table can preserve claim-level scope, but did not itself decide priority between calibrated and obstruction classes.

That wording implements the ruling without inventing its premise.

## 2. Attack 2 — coherence of entry 51's table row

The row is internally intelligible and unusually candid. Its columns say:

- tier: `CALIBRATED-FALSIFIER`;
- standing: `LIVE`;
- warrant: the floor is unreproduced from the stated inputs;
- contrast: the Papapetrou theorem is the paper's best-warranted content;

That is not a formal contradiction because the bibliography explicitly separates **shape**, **standing**, and **warrant**. A claim can have calibrated shape while its derivational warrant is disputed or unreproduced. The contrast is honest evidence rather than hidden tension.

However, “the **tier-bearing** floor” is not a neutral warrant statement. It embeds the very paper-level priority decision whose supposed precedent fails attack 1. The row should say “the floor claim retained as the paper-level tier under the Q6 ruling” rather than imply the table's schema mechanically makes it tier-bearing. The table can faithfully hold the chosen outcome after that attribution is corrected.
