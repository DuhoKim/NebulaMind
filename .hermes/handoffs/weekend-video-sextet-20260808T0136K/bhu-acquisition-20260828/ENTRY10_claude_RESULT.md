## 1. The bounce mechanism here vs entries 9/11

**Effective term.** The torsion piece of the combined Dirac stress tensor is (3/4)κ s_l s^l g_ik (eq. 9, L105). In the comoving frame s^0 = 0 (L111-112), so s_l s^l = −s² and the term is −(3/4)κ s² g_ik. Read against a perfect fluid T_ik = (ε+p)u_iu_k − p g_ik, that is ε̃ = −p̃ = −(3/4)κ⟨s²⟩ = −α n², α = 9κ/16 (eq. 10, L117). Checked: (3/4)·(3/4) = 9/16 ✓. So the term has the w = −1 *form* (ε̃ = −p̃) but a density ∝ n² ∝ T⁶ (via n = h_n T³). It is NOT the stiff (w = +1) spin-fluid term ε̃ = p̃ = −κs²/4 of Hehl–von der Heyde–Kerlick (L119-121). This is the structural difference from entries 9/11, and it is the origin of the cusp (see §2): a w = −1 term that nevertheless scales as a^-6 cannot conserve itself, so the total conservation law (eq. 13, L150) pushes the extra term −2α a³ n dn into the *matter* T(a) relation.

## 2. The cusp: derived or artefact?

**Within the paper's first-order system it is derived; against the full field equations it is an artefact of the closure, and the paper supplies nothing that regularises it.**

(i) *Why it is forced.* The conservation law (13) (with closure e) makes a(T) have a minimum at T_cr (eq. 15). The Friedmann constraint (11) (k dropped) gives ȧ² = (κ/3)(h_*T⁴ − αh_n²T⁶)a², which at T_cr is (κ/9)h_*T_cr⁴a_cr² = v² > 0 — because the Friedmann zero is at T_st = √(3/2) T_cr ≠ T_cr (eq. 23, L273-277). So at the point where da/dT = 0, ȧ ≠ 0, which forces Ṫ → ∞ (eq. 19 diverges at β_cr) and any continuation that respects a ≥ a_cr is a velocity reversal −v → +v. That much is a genuine consequence of (11)+(13)+(e); the paper's arithmetic here is right. I also checked the continuation the paper excludes: (19) is real for T_cr < T ≤ T_st too (its reality condition is β² ≥ (2/3)β_cr², NOT β ≥ β_cr — L231's "which yields β ≥ β_cr and T ≤ T_cr" is not what (19) yields; that exclusion comes from L185). On that branch a rises to a(T_st) = e^{1/4}/√1.5 · a_cr = 1.048 a_cr at a smooth turning point (Ṫ = 0 at η = 0), then recontracts to a_cr and cusps again — a universe trapped oscillating in [a_cr, 1.048 a_cr] that never reaches a radiation era. So both continuations cusp; the paper's branch choice (L185) is by hand but does not create the cusp.

(ii) *Why it is an artefact.* The paper solves the first-order equations (11) and (13) but the cusp solution VIOLATES the second Friedmann equation (12), which it claims to be using (L137-142): (12) reads ȧ² + 2aä + k = −κ(p + αn²)a². With ȧ = −v for t < 0 and +v for t > 0, ä = 2v δ(t), so the LHS contains 4a_cr v δ(t), while the RHS is bounded (p, n are finite functions of T and T = T_cr is finite at the cusp). No term in the matter model is impulsive. Equivalently, in the Raychaudhuri form the paper invokes (L326-330): with the strong energy condition holding — which the paper concedes, ε̃ + 3p̃ = 2αn² > 0 (L323-325) — dθ/dt ≤ −θ²/3 < 0, so θ cannot jump UP by 6v/a_cr (L332-336) unless R_ik u^iu^k contains −(6v/a_cr) δ(t): an unsourced, SEC-violating impulse at t = 0. The "nonsingular (with respect to curvature)" claim (L264) holds only for the finite part; R ∝ ä/a carries a δ(t), i.e. a distributional (thin-layer) curvature singularity at the bounce hypersurface, with [K] = 2v/a_cr ≠ 0 in the extrinsic curvature. The paper's own comparison to a geodesic-completing discontinuity in an Einstein–Rosen bridge (L339-340, [21]) is an analogy, not a source term.

(iii) *Which approximation produces it.* The cusp is produced by closure (d)+(e): tying n to T by kinetic equilibrium and squaring it makes the torsion term a function of T alone, so its non-conservation is absorbed into T(a), which becomes double-valued. Had the fermion number been tracked by its own conservation (n a³ = const), the Friedmann function would have a genuine zero and the bounce would be smooth (as in the spin-fluid models the paper cites, L123-124). The sharpness is also self-undermining: Ṫ → ∞ at T_cr (eq. 19) is the maximal violation of the quasi-static assumption behind (e); and the regime is T_cr ≈ 0.78 m_P, torsion-era timescale t_0 = β_cr²√(3/κh_*) ≈ 0.47/m_P ≈ 1.3 × 10⁻⁴³ s (my number, not printed), where SM interaction rates Γ ~ 10⁻³ T give Γ/H ~ 10⁻³ ≪ 1 — kinetic equilibrium fails by the paper's own textbook criterion [18] before the cusp is reached.

(iv) *Regularisation.* None offered. The paper presents the discontinuity as the physical feature that "prevents θ from decreasing to −∞" (L337-339). Temperature-dependent g's are assumed away (L167); k is dropped (L195-196) and would only shift v² by 1 part in 10⁶².

(v) *Perturbations.* Not addressed anywhere: no perturbation action, no mode equation, no matching condition (confirms RQ-B). My assessment: because [K] ≠ 0 across the bounce surface, the standard continuity-of-(induced-metric, extrinsic-curvature) junction conditions are inapplicable without a surface layer, so the model as published does not define how a perturbation crosses the bounce at all. The "horizon problem solved" statement (L371-375) is a volume count N ~ v_ant³, not a spectrum.

## Plain language

This paper asks what happens if the spin of ordinary fermions talks directly to the twist of spacetime that Einstein–Cartan gravity allows. The author works the equations honestly as far as they go, and the two numbers that matter most — the bounce temperature (about 0.8 Planck masses) and the minimum size of the universe (about half a millimetre) — come out exactly as printed when I redo them from his own inputs. But the bounce itself is not a gentle turn-around: the universe hits a floor at full speed and reverses instantly. That instant reversal is real inside the simplified equations he keeps, yet it breaks the one equation he drops — the acceleration equation, which would need an infinite kick at that instant that nothing in his matter model provides — and it appears exactly where his thermal assumptions (an ideal gas in equilibrium at 10⁻⁴³ s) have already stopped being valid. So the cusp is a product of the averaging shortcut, not a discovery. Two of the four headline numbers (the expansion speed and how flat the universe was at the bounce) do not reproduce from the paper's stated inputs and are off by factors of ~3000 and ~10⁸ respectively, and the "explains flatness" claim uses today's flatness as an input. Nothing in the paper points at anything a telescope could check, and the sign of curvature is assumed rather than derived. The consistency-only label is the right one; it is neither too harsh nor too generous.
