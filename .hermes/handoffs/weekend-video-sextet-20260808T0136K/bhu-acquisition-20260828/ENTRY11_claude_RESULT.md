## 2. "nonsingular, closed, nearly flat, homogeneous, and isotropic" (L10) — derived vs assumed
- nonsingular: DERIVED within the model (ε̃ = 0 at finite a_min > 0, eq (22), L155; a_min = a_i T_i/T_max = 1.19×10⁻¹⁶ m for a_i = 10⁴ m, verified: T_i = (3/(κh⋆a_i²))^{1/4} = 1.375×10¹² K, a_min = 10⁴·1.375×10¹²/1.152×10³² = 1.19×10⁻¹⁶ m; paper L200 CORRECT).
- closed: ASSUMED. k = 1 is written into the metric at L93 ("k=1 Lor; LL"). The only argument for closure is verbal: L28 "the interior of a black hole becomes a new, closed universe, which can be thought of as a three-dimensional analogue of the two-dimensional surface of a sphere torsion1" and L121 "the interior of a black hole is causally connected and becomes a new, closed universe torsion1". No equation in this paper produces k=+1; it is imported from torsion1 (Popławski 2010, PLB 687, 110) as an analogy. The Friedmann equation (10) is written with general k and k is then "neglected" at the bounce (L148, L169, L302); nothing downstream selects the sign of k. Per Duho ruling A(a) this closure does NOT count as a derived direction.
- homogeneous, isotropic: ASSUMED as the FLRW ansatz (L93-94). L122 concedes the interior "is initially inhomogeneous and anisotropic but it becomes more homogeneous and isotropic near a bounce" — one sentence, no anisotropy (Bianchi/Kasner) or perturbation equation anywhere; L256 repeats the claim ("more isotropic") for later cycles, again without an equation. The paper's own to-do list (L364): "we need to derive primordial density fluctuations". No perturbation action exists in the text (consistent with RQ-B).
- nearly flat: DERIVED as a bounce-epoch statement, NOT as a present-day Ω_k. What is computed: Ω − 1 = kc²/ȧ² (L183, eq 28), minimum at T = T_max/√2, Ω_min − 1 = 4cτ/a_i (L186). I re-derived this: ȧ² is maximal where g(T) = h⋆T² − αh_nf²T⁴ is maximal, T² = T_max²/2, g_max = h⋆²/(4αh_nf²), giving Ω−1 = 12αh_nf²/(κh⋆²(a_iT_i)²) = 4cτ/a_i using (a_iT_i)² = a_i(3/κh⋆)^{1/2}. CORRECT as a formula.
  NUMBER CHECK, eq (29) L194: 4cτ = 4 × 2.998×10⁸ × 4.751×10⁻⁴⁵ = 5.70×10⁻³⁶ m. The paper prints Ω_min − 1 = 5.7×10⁻³⁶ with NO a_i stated, i.e. it silently used a_i = 1 m. For the stellar a_i = 10⁴ m adopted four lines later (L198), the value is 5.7×10⁻⁴⁰. The number in (29) is therefore mislabelled by 10⁴ (an unstated input, not a concealed observable; the error runs in the paper's favour, flatter). N_max ≈ 10⁵² (L194) matches neither (a_i/l_P)³ (2.4×10¹⁰⁴ for a_i=1 m, 2.4×10¹¹⁶ for 10⁴ m) nor (Ω−1)⁻³ (5×10¹⁰⁵) as written at L187-188; it matches (Ω_min−1)^{−3/2} = 7.3×10⁵² and (a_i/l_P)^{3/2} = 1.5×10⁵² with a_i = 1 m. So the exponent "−3" at L187 is a typo for −3/2 (correct: N ≈ (ȧ/c)³ with ȧ/c = (Ω−1)^{−1/2} gives (Ω−1)^{−3/2}), and again a_i = 1 m was used. Neither slip changes any conclusion, but both numbers in (29) are internally inconsistent with the paper's own a_i.
  With particle production, (39) L250: Ω̃_min − 1 = (Ω_min−1)(a_i/ã_i)² < 10⁻⁵⁵ (5.7×10⁻³⁶ × 10⁻²⁰ = 5.7×10⁻⁵⁶; arithmetic CORRECT given the paper's inputs). ## 3. The exponential-expansion phase — fixed inputs or free β / parent mass?
- Mechanism: particle production rate K (41) is "a scalar … constructed from the curvature, torsion, and metric tensors" (L272); the actual form is "the simplest form of K which vanishes at a bounce" K = β(κε̃)² (46), L298-301, with "β > 0 is a nondimensional constant" — FREE. L297: "Ultimately, K should be derived from quantum field theory in the Riemann-Cartan spacetime" — not done.
- The only computed bound on β is β_cr (50): I recomputed both forms, β_cr = 1.076×10⁻³ = 1/929.1 (paper 1/929, L319, CORRECT; cf. 1/(288π) = 1.105×10⁻³). β_cr is fixed by g⋆, g_f, g_b1 = 9 only. But β_cr is a theoretical cutoff (β ≥ β_cr ⇒ eternal inflation, L354; L308-311 "we must have … to avoid an indefinitely long exponential increase"), i.e. the requirement that our universe exited inflation is used as an INPUT to bound β; β itself is never fixed.
- The e-fold number: L347 "H t_infl ≳ 23". Source: (38) ã_i/a_i > 10¹⁰, ln(10¹⁰) = 23.03 (CORRECT). But (38) is derived from (37) with ALL of the following inputs: T_eq = 8820 K and Λ/κ = 5.24×10⁻¹⁰ N m⁻² taken from OUR universe (L246, "In our Universe"), and a_i T_i for a_i = 10⁴ m. I recomputed: Λ = 1.088×10⁻⁵² m⁻², (a_iT_i)³ = 2.60×10⁴⁸, bound = (2/(κh⋆T_eq(a_iT_i)³√Λ))^{1/3} = 9.99×10⁹ ≈ 10¹⁰ CORRECT. It is NOT a fixed-input derivation: the bound scales as a_i^{−1/2} (a_iT_i ∝ a_i^{1/2}), so for a 10¹⁰ m (supermassive) parent it is 10⁷ (16 e-folds), for 10¹³ m it is 3×10⁵ (12.7 e-folds). And it is a lower bound (the minimum expansion needed for the closed universe to escape recollapse, eq (34)), not a prediction of the actual e-fold count.
- Duration: L348 "This interval depends on τ(β_cr − β)⁻¹" — explicitly a function of the free β. No matter yield is computed; "an enormous amount of matter" (L11, L29) is never quantified (M_univ appears only symbolically, L224).
Verdict on Q3: e-folds = lower bound set by observed T_eq, Λ and the assumed parent mass; actual number depends on free β. Nothing here is a fixed-input prediction.

## Plain language
This paper says that inside every black hole, the spin of the particles being crushed pushes back hard enough to stop the collapse and start a new expanding universe, and that a burst of particle creation can then blow that universe up to the size of ours. I traced every equation. The "push-back" part is real arithmetic once you accept two borrowed ingredients — a textbook recipe for turning a crowd of spinning particles into a smooth fluid, and a quoted formula for how much spin-pressure that fluid has — and the numbers the author prints for the peak temperature, the characteristic time, the expansion rate, the minimum size and the critical particle-production constant all check out on my recomputation (two of his flatness/horizon numbers were computed with a 1-metre black hole rather than the 10-km one he names, and one exponent is a typo, but nothing hangs on that). The "closed universe" is written into the equations at the start, not deduced from them; the smoothness of the new universe is assumed, not shown; and the amount of inflation depends on a constant the author admits is free and on values (matter-radiation temperature, cosmological constant) taken from our own universe. The author himself says the density-fluctuation spectrum is still to be derived and that his scenario predicts phenomena similar to other scenarios. So there is nothing in the paper that a telescope in our universe could prove wrong; the tier stays CONSISTENCY-ONLY.
