## 1. The collapse

The paper starts with a general spherically symmetric Tolman metric, with three functions \(\nu(\tau,R),\lambda(\tau,R),\mu(\tau,R)\) (lines 80–86), but reaches its tractable collapse model only after imposing homogeneous pressure: \(\tilde p'=0\), hence \(\nu'=0\), and choosing synchronous time (lines 119–124). It then integrates the interior equations to \(e^\lambda=r'^2/(1+f)\) (lines 140–148) and an interior radial equation (8)/(9) (lines 148–162). These steps are explicitly sourced to older work: the tensor equations and conservation equations are tagged [6] (lines 80–86, 109–119, 126–139), the integration to (8) is [7] (lines 147–155), and the mass/Schwarzschild-radius identification is [6] (lines 163–170).

At the surface \(R=R_0\), the paper defines \(r_g=2GM/c^2\), identifies it with an interior integral, and writes \(\dot r^2=f(R_0)+r_g/r\) (lines 163–171). Initial rest at radius \(r_0\) gives \(f(R_0)=-r_g/r_0\) (lines 173–177). This is Oppenheimer–Snyder-like interior kinematics, not a demonstrated matching. No Schwarzschild exterior line element is written; no induced-metric or extrinsic-curvature continuity conditions (Darmois–Israel conditions) are stated; no boundary condition such as vanishing physical/effective pressure at \(R_0\) is imposed; and no surface stress tensor is calculated. Therefore the text neither proves that a surface layer is absent nor determines whether one is required. Calling \(r_g\) the Schwarzschild radius and using the surface equation does not close that junction gap.

For the spin fluid, the new equations remain an interior construction. Kinetic equilibrium and ultrarelativistic equations of state are assumed (lines 184–193); homogeneity then produces (12)/(13) (lines 193–204). The author notes that the general solution still contains three arbitrary functions \(f,g,\tau_0\) (lines 204–206), then says “We seek a solution” with \(f=-\sin^2R\) and \(r=a\sin R\) (lines 207–214). Thus the particular collapse geometry is selected by ansatz, not by junction data. The resulting relations (18) are again attributed to [6,7] (lines 234–238), and singularity avoidance is attributed to [7] (lines 252–255).

## 2. The bounce and the “universe”

Within the selected ansatz, the bounce is mathematically consistent. Equation (17) contains a negative torsion term proportional to \(-a^{-4}\) (lines 228–233); as \(a\) decreases, its right-hand side becomes negative before \(a=0\), which cannot equal \(\dot a^2\), so the paper concludes that \(a\) remains positive and densities remain finite (lines 252–256). This is a background-level nonsingular turning point for the idealized homogeneous model.

Closedness is not dynamically selected. The positive-curvature choice is already encoded in \(f(R)=-\sin^2R\), \(r=a\sin R\) (lines 207–214). Substitution yields \(\dot a^2+1=\kappa\tilde\epsilon a^2/3\), which has the form of the closed Friedmann equation (lines 222–227), and produces the closed-FLRW interior metric (lines 245–248). Accordingly, “the matter expands ... as a new, closed universe” (lines 257–261) is a consequence of the chosen solution, not a proof that generic collapse or matching forces \(k=+1\). Indeed, the paper expressly says the metric describes only “a part of a closed universe with \(0\le R\le R_0\)” (lines 247–248).

There is no treatment of the PRD-85 cusp: the paper neither names nor regularizes a cusp and supplies no smoothing prescription or local analysis of one. There is also no perturbation action, quadratic action, mode equation, spectrum, or stability calculation. Shear is appended phenomenologically as a positive \(\sim a^{-4}\) term in the background equation (lines 296–300), while its avoidance argument uses scaling and an assumed particle-production law (lines 262–292). The closest explicit deferral beyond the idealized model is the unsupported future-facing assertion that the scenario “would still be valid for a more realistic gravitational collapse of an inhomogeneous and rotating fluid” (lines 320–325); no derivation follows. Thus neither missing closure is supplied in 2025.

- The only printed inflation threshold is dimensionless: the maximum of \(\beta H^3/(3c^3h_{nf}T^3)\) must be below 1 to avoid eternal inflation; a value slightly below 1 yields approximately constant \(T,H\) and inflation (lines 305–313). There is therefore no calculated dimensional numerical prediction for a black hole or for our Universe. The paper’s numbers depend on particle-content choices and otherwise unstated object/initial-condition inputs; the inflation result additionally depends on free \(\beta\).

## 4. Observation-facing content

No signed observational direction, relic abundance, spectrum, mass, length, redshift, or other number-plus-threshold is derived for our Universe. The paper says our Universe “may be closed” and born inside a parent black hole (lines 320–323), and says finite inflation is “consistent with the astronomical data” by citation [10] (lines 323–324), but gives no observable calculation or discriminator here. The internal inequalities at lines 243–244 and 305–313 govern existence of model turning points and avoidance of eternal inflation; they are not calibrated observational thresholds.

The paper does mention a parent black hole becoming an Einstein–Rosen bridge to a baby universe (lines 320–322). It does not derive a surviving black-hole remnant, a remnant mass, a black-hole mass function, or any population signature. It also contains no preferred-axis claim, axis direction, anisotropic sky observable, or use of “axis.” Rotation appears only in the unproved generalization claim at line 325. The record note that this is not an axis-line paper is confirmed.

## 5. Tier consequence

**Hold CONSISTENCY-ONLY; A(a).** The paper demonstrates background consistency for one homogeneous, ultrarelativistic spin-fluid solution: with the chosen closed ansatz, torsion creates a finite-\(a\) bounce, and an assumed particle-production rule can outgrow shear. It does not close the earlier gaps. The exterior junction and possible surface layer are not derived; \(k=+1\) is selected by ansatz; the general interior retains arbitrary functions before that selection; \(\beta\) is free; the PRD-85 cusp is untreated; and there is no perturbation action. Its observation-facing statements are possibilities or citations without a derived observable number. That is insufficient for QUALITATIVE-DIRECTIONAL because no robust signed direction for our Universe is derived, insufficient for PROSPECT because no concrete observation-facing relic/measurement mapping is supplied, and far short of a CALIBRATED-FALSIFIER. A lane may add a missing observational threshold to an already derived number, but it cannot supply the missing number, matching, or perturbation dynamics here.

In plain language: this paper shows that a carefully chosen, perfectly symmetric ball of hot spinning matter can bounce instead of hitting a singularity, and that the same chosen geometry can be read as part of a closed universe. It does not show that a real collapsing star must choose that geometry, connect it rigorously to the outside black-hole spacetime, or predict something measurable in our sky. The inflation and shear claims also depend on an unfixed particle-production rate. So the work remains a consistency example, not an observational forecast or falsifiable calibration.
