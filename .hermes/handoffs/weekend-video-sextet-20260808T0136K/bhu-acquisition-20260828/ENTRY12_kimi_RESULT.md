1. The collapse — metric/matching, junction, derived vs cited.

The metric is the Tolman metric, ds² = e^ν c²dτ² − e^λ dR² − e^μ(dθ²+sin²θ dϕ²), quoted with its Einstein-tensor components from ref [6] (Lemaître, Tolman, Oppenheimer–Snyder, Bondi, Landau–Lifshitz) at lines 80–109 — cited, not derived here. The reduction to synchronous comoving form (eqs. 4–9, lines 110–162) follows Tolman/Oppenheimer–Snyder closely: pressure homogeneity ⇒ ν=0 (lines 120–124), integration of G⁰₁ gives e^λ = r′²/(1+f(R)) with 1+f>0 (lines 140–144), and the radial equation of motion ṙ² = f(R) + (κ/r)∫ϵ̃r²r′dR (line 158–162) is tagged "[7]" — i.e., the integration is credited to Popławski 2021, not done in this paper.

On the junction the paper is silent. There is no matching section at all: no Darmois–Israel conditions, no statement about the interior–exterior surface at R = R₀, no surface layer, no extrinsic-curvature jump. The exterior appears only implicitly through the definition rg = κ∫₀^{R₀} ϵ̃r²r′dR = 2GM/c² (lines 165–167) and the surface equation of motion ṙ²(τ,R₀) = f(R₀) + rg/r(τ,R₀) (lines 169–171). Oppenheimer–Snyder is only a bibliographic entry inside ref [6] (line 351). So the answer to "which steps are equations here and which are cited": the Tolman metric and its G_ik components are cited from [6] (lines 80–109); the specific spin-fluid solution ansatz and its consequences (eqs. 12–18) are presented in-line but repeatedly tagged to [6,7] (lines 198, 222, 234, 246); the junction conditions are neither derived nor stated — that gap from the 2010–2016 lineage is not closed here.

2. The bounce and the "universe" — is k=+1 derived?

No — it is assumed via the ansatz, exactly the pattern flagged in entries 9/10/11. The decisive step is eq. (14) at line 209: "We seek a solution of equations (12) and (13) as f(R) = −sin²R, r(τ,R) = a(τ) sin R." Choosing f = −sin²R is precisely the closed-universe (k=+1) embedding; it is an ansatz ("we seek"), not a dynamical selection and not forced by any matching. Only then does line 226 note that the resulting equation "has the form of the Friedmann equation … in a closed, homogeneous, and isotropic universe," and lines 245–248 conclude the interior metric "has a form of the closed Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker metric." The bounce itself is established from eq. (17) at lines 252–256: a never reaches zero because the RHS goes negative as a decreases. The shear threat is handled by requiring particle production so that n_f² ∼ a^{−6−2δ} outgrows σ² ∼ a^{−6} (lines 268–271, 286–292).

Neither RQ-B gap is closed. (a) The PRD-85 cusp: no perturbation treatment, no cusp discussion; the Dirac-field version is invoked by citation only — "This scenario also occurs if the fermionic matter is described by the Dirac fields instead of the spin fluid [11]" (line 324), where [11] includes PRD 85, 107502 (line 358). (b) The perturbation action RQ-B found missing is still missing: the paper contains background field equations only; particle production enters as the semiclassical rate eq. (19) (lines 272–276) with a free dimensionless β, and the inflation bound is the condition that the maximum of (βH³)/(3c³h_nfT³) stay below 1, cited to [4] (lines 305–308). Deferred-by-citation sentences: "Equation (17) can be solved analytically in terms of an elliptic integral of the second kind [5]" (line 252); "R₀ → π (completely closed universe) [7]" (lines 302–304); the inflation mechanism "generating inflation [4]" (lines 309–311); "It would still be valid for a more realistic gravitational collapse of an inhomogeneous and rotating fluid" (line 325) — an unsupported one-sentence extrapolation with no equation.

Recomputation (done, exact agreement): set ȧ=0 in eq. (17) (lines 230–233), giving a⁴ − Aa² + B = 0 with A = (κ/3)h⋆T₀⁴a₀⁴, B = (κ/3)αh_nf²T₀⁶a₀⁶. Two positive turning points require A² > 4B ⇒ a₀² > 12αh_nf²/(κh⋆²T₀²). Initial Friedmann (line 240, torsion term negligible at ȧ(0)=0) gives a₀² = 3/(κh⋆T₀⁴), so the condition becomes T₀⁴ < h⋆²/(16α²h_nf⁴). Mass-energy (line 241: Mc² = (4π/3)r₀³h⋆T₀⁴) plus rg = 2GM/c² (line 165) give T₀⁴ = 3c⁴rg/(8πGr₀³h⋆). Substituting and using α = κ(ℏc)²/32, κ = 8πG/c⁴ yields r₀³/r_g > 3πGℏ⁴h_nf⁴/(8h⋆³) — symbolically identical to line 243 (verified by exact symbolic simplification). The ∼ l_P² claim also checks dimensionally: h_nf⁴/h⋆³ ∝ (ℏc)^{-3}, so Gℏ⁴h_nf⁴/h⋆³ ~ Gℏ/c³ = l_P². Unstated-input pattern: the inequality is never instantiated — no M, r₀, or T₀ is ever chosen, so "valid for systems forming black holes [5]" (line 243) is an order-of-magnitude remark pinned to a citation, not an evaluated condition.

Plain-language paragraph: This 2025 conference paper is Popławski retelling his own black-hole-to-universe story in the cleanest classical setup: a sphere of spinning fluid collapses, torsion pushes back hard enough to stop the crunch, and the inside keeps going as a little closed oscillating universe that can inflate and eventually becomes a big bang. As physics narrative it is tidy, and the algebra I could check (the condition for the bounce to exist) is exactly right. But as new evidence it adds nothing: the closed geometry is put in by hand on line 209, the junction across the surface of the collapsing star is never written down, the hard parts (shear, particle production, inflation) are all outsourced to his earlier papers with a free knob β, and there is not a single evaluated number or testable prediction for our universe — the only nod to data is "consistent with the astronomical data" via a 2016 citation. So it stays exactly where the earlier entries are: an internally consistent mechanism, not yet an observable claim. CONSISTENCY-ONLY.
