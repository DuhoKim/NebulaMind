## 1. The junction construction
The paper matches an interior FLRW metric to an exterior Schwarzschild metric on a timelike hypersurface Sigma of constant comoving radius chi_§, imposing Israel conditions h_- = h_+ and K_+ = K_- (lines 119-130). Crucially, the reduction is cited, not derived here: "The matching conditions h−=h+ and K+=K− reduce to Gaztanaga 2021" (line 130), yielding Eq. (9) R = a(tau) chi_§, Rdot^2 = R^2 H^2 = R*/R with R* = 2GM (lines 132-136). The surface stress is stated only by its absence: "the joint BHU metric is also a solution ... with no surface terms in the junction" (line 139) — no thin-shell stress tensor is computed or displayed. The junction horizon condition is HR = 1 giving R* = 2GM = 1/H_Lambda (line 138). "There could be a different universe outside" rests on a heuristic embedding argument, not on the junction math: the SW metric "is a perturbation of a larger FLRW metric ... outside r*=1/H_Lambda, we have another FLRW metric, like in a Matryoshka doll ... could be better described by a fractal" (lines 143-144). The exterior is not solved for; its content is unspecified.

## 2. "Incoming photons can enter and leave an imprint on our CMB sky"
The imprint is NOT derived from the junction. Every sentence that specifies it reduces to the same causal-horizon cutoff as the series. The only concrete observable statement: at last scattering, where d_A ≃ chi_O, "chi_§ corresponds to an angle theta = chi_§/d_A ≲ 1 rad ≃ 60 deg. Therefore, we can actually observe scales larger than chi_§, scales that are not causally connected!" (line 184). The follow-up is explicitly tentative: "This could be related to the so-called CMB anomalies ... or the parameter tensions" (line 185) — "could be," with citations, no computed pattern. No temperature pattern, amplitude, sign, multipole, or C_ell is derived anywhere from the matching conditions. The null-geodesic argument (lines 109-115: outgoing r_out < 1/H_Lambda = r*, incoming can exceed r* looking back far enough) establishes only that outside information can be visible, not what it looks like. The imprint claim is the 60-degree causal boundary restated, plus a pointer to anomalies.

## 3. "Fossil record from the analysis of CMB maps"
The analysis is Fosalba & Gaztañaga 2021 (named at line 189; Camacho-Quevedo is not cited here). Content: best-fit Omega_Lambda from partial-sky discs of Planck shows "three distinct regions across the CMB sky ... radii ranging from 40 to 70 degrees" (lines 189-190); "the size of each of these regions is correlated with the mean value of Omega_Lambda over that portion of the sky, in good agreement with the BHU prediction" (line 191); the anisotropy has "a tiny probability (of order 10^-9) of being a Gaussian statistical fluctuation" (line 194). Presentation is comparison-after-the-fact, not a pre-stated test: the paper lists agreements — "(a) regions with a given value of Omega_Lambda have a corresponding angular size theta that agrees with the BHU prediction" (line 197) — with no pre-registered threshold, null, or rejection criterion stated in this paper. "But challenges" (abstract, line 17) is spelled out at lines 201-202: the mechanism sourcing the anisotropy "encompasses scales beyond our causal universe. This is in apparent tension with simple models of inflation ... and opens the door to revisiting our basic understanding of the origin of the primordial universe." It challenges the primordial-origin picture (inflation as source of the largest scales), not a quantified LambdaCDM prediction with a stated falsifier.

## Plain-language paragraph
This paper says our universe sits inside its own event horizon and matches smoothly onto a black-hole exterior, so light from outside can fall in and leave marks on our CMB. But when you ask what the mark actually is, the paper only gives one concrete thing: a boundary about 60 degrees across on the sky — the same number the rest of the series already had. The matching math is imported from the author's earlier paper rather than worked out here, no surface layer is computed, and no brightness, pattern, or strength of the imprint is ever calculated. The "fossil record" is a 2021 map of dark-energy density across the sky showing three big patches of 40-70 degrees whose sizes line up with that boundary; the paper reports this as agreement after the fact, not as a test with a pass/fail line set in advance. The one exterior quantity it pins down — the black hole's mass — is just our own cosmological constant written differently, so nothing new is measured or fixed. Bottom line: interesting framing, but it restates the series' qualitative claim and compares it to existing data; it adds no calibratable prediction, so the QUALITATIVE-DIRECTIONAL tier stands.
