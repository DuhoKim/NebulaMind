## 1. Is the cutoff derived or asserted?

The background geometry is derived, but the perturbation cutoff is only a verbally inferred boundary condition. Equation (3) gives the FLRW evolution and shows that, for ordinary matter, the Hubble radius grows faster than a comoving physical scale; the text then says modes become super-horizon during collapse and re-enter during expansion (source lines 77–83). Equation (11) derives the finite cloud boundary,
`R=[r_H^2 r_S]^(1/3)=r_S(a/a_BH)^(1+w)` (lines 210–216). The paper uses that equation to establish `R>r_H` inside the black hole (lines 217–226), and Figures 3–4 label the region `R>r>r_H` as matter containing frozen/super-horizon perturbations (lines 231–234, 250–253). Appendix A independently recovers the same junction from metric and extrinsic-curvature matching (lines 568–584, 594–598, 667–671).

No perturbation equation connects those results to spectral support. The next steps are prose: the frozen region is called the source of super-horizon perturbations (lines 257–260); the bounce irregularities are said to re-enter and seed structure (lines 292–294); and the paper then states that incoming fluctuations have no scales `lambda>2R`, equivalently `k<pi/R` (line 295). It supplies neither a formula for `P(k)` on either side nor a transition/window. “Cutoff” most naturally says absent below the stated `k`, while the surviving amplitude is described only as scale invariant (line 260). Indeed, the paper says estimating the perturbations requires further work (lines 329–334) and calls the observed `delta T~10^-5` amplitude a remaining mystery (line 339). Thus the direction is a physically motivated assertion from finite support, not a perturbative derivation.

## 2. What is R, and is it fixed non-circularly?

`R(tau)` is the physical radius of the finite FLRW cloud/junction, not the Schwarzschild radius. Equation (11) makes it time-dependent (lines 210–216); for dust its comoving radius `R/a` is constant (line 221, and Appendix A lines 598–601). By contrast, the asymptotic event horizon is `r_S=r_Lambda`, inferred from `Omega_Lambda` and `H_0`, with `M~5x10^22 M_sun` and quoted `r_S~6x10^22 km` (lines 177–192). During the relevant evolution `R<r_S`, becoming equal only for asymptotically constant `H` (lines 218–225). The bounce does not fix a special cutoff radius: its detailed conditions and perturbations are explicitly unfinished (lines 329–334).

For the CMB claim the relevant object is the cloud radius at CMB time, expressed comovingly as `R_CMB/a_CMB`. The paper says at `a~10^-3`, `r_H~5x10^-5 (c/H_0)` and `R` is about 30 times larger (line 224), then plots the BHU prediction as `2R/a` and separately plots both `chi_section` and `chi_Lambda=r_S/a` (line 308). That separation shows that `R` is neither `r_S` nor literally entry 23's `chi_section`.

The arithmetic is consistent only at the paper's rough precision. With `c/H_0~4.28 Gpc`, line 224 gives physical `R_CMB~0.0015(c/H_0)~6.4 Mpc`, hence `R_CMB/a_CMB~6.4 Gpc`. Taking `d_CMB~14 Gpc`, `2(R/a)/d_CMB~0.92 rad~53 degrees`, reasonably near the claimed “about 60” given both inputs are rounded. Exactly 60 degrees with `d_CMB=14.0 Gpc` requires `R/a=(pi/6)d_CMB~7.33 Gpc`. This is roughly half, not all, of `chi_section=14.015 Gpc` (Program A lines 31–35), consistent with Figure 7 comparing `2R/a` to `chi_section` (source line 308). The scale can therefore be obtained non-circularly from the background solution and measured `H_0, Omega_m/Omega_Lambda`, but the paper's displayed 60-degree comparison is coarse; the `66+/-9 degrees` value is observational input, not what fixes Equation (11) (source lines 301–308).

## 3. Does entry 27 supply the Fourier convention left free?

(a) Yes, at the level of a sharp-support assertion: line 295 explicitly identifies `lambda>2R` with `k<pi/R`. That selects Fourier Reading A and fixes the factor of `pi`, unlike entry 23's ambiguous prose. It still does not derive a stochastic perturbation prescription or say whether the edge is mathematically sharp.

(b) No. It calls the gravitational-instability amplitude scale invariant (lines 257–260), but gives no normalization, no `P(k)` above the edge, and no `C_l` prediction. Nor does it explicitly adopt LambdaCDM's fitted amplitude and transfer functions; doing so would be an external completion. The admission that the `delta T~10^-5` amplitude remains unexplained is decisive (line 339).

(c) No rejection or decision threshold is stated. The Phase B result reports 0.4–0.8% for the externally completed Reading A model at `pi/chi_section`, versus 0.15–0.20% under LambdaCDM (Phase B reconciliation lines 12–20, 25–37), but those are computed percentiles, not a threshold owned by this paper.

Moreover, entry 27's `R` does not equal `chi_section=14,015 Mpc`. Using the exact-60-degree value above gives a comoving `R/a~7.33 Gpc` and therefore `k_cut=pi/(R/a)~4.29x10^-4 Mpc^-1`; using line 224's rough numbers gives about `4.9x10^-4 Mpc^-1`. If `2R/a~chi_section`, entry 27's prescription is `k_cut~2pi/chi_section`, not `pi/chi_section`. Phase B's matching externally completed row is therefore the `2pi/chi_section` row (2.2–2.8%), while the specifically cited `pi/chi_section` row (0.4–0.8%) uses a cutoff wavenumber smaller by about two (Program A lines 209–215; Phase B reconciliation lines 16–20). Neither percentile belongs to entry 27 without the external LambdaCDM normalization and transfer choices.

## 4. Is 66 +/- 9 degrees a prediction or a comparison measurement?

It is a measurement compared with the model. The paper explicitly says Camacho-Quevedo and Gaztanaga (2021) “finds a cutoff scale” `Theta_H=66+/-9 degrees` and plots it as a black data symbol (lines 301–303); Figure 7 compares measured horizons with the BHU curves (line 308). The nearby theoretical statement is only `theta~2R/d_CMB~60 degrees` (line 306), backed by the rounded background calculation above. Thus the paper does forecast an approximate scale near 60 degrees that a sufficiently discrepant 2021 measurement could have missed, but it gives no forecast uncertainty, likelihood, statistic amplitude, or predeclared miss criterion. The author's participation in the 2021 measurement does not turn `66+/-9` into a prediction.

## 5. Tier consequence

Entry 27 should remain **QUALITATIVE-DIRECTIONAL**. It materially improves on entry 23 by selecting a Fourier direction and constant: suppress/omit modes below `pi/(R/a)` at a background-predicted scale of order 60 degrees. That is enough to preserve a directional, scale-bearing prediction rather than fall to CONSISTENCY-ONLY, even though the finite-boundary-to-zero-power step is asserted rather than obtained from perturbation equations. But it does not reach CALIBRATED-FALSIFIER: the paper supplies no normalized spectrum above the cutoff, no `C_l` or large-angle statistic, no uncertainty on its approximate 60-degree forecast, and no threshold. A lane may own a missing threshold, but it would also have to invent the missing normalization/transfer prescription to obtain the test number. Program A expressly identifies those external choices (lines 82–97), and entry 27 itself concedes the amplitude problem (source lines 334, 339).

In plain language: this paper does say something sharper than “large-angle power should be low.” It says which Fourier modes should be missing and ties their scale, approximately, to the finite collapsing cloud. But it never calculates how much CMB correlation should remain, and its quoted 66-degree figure comes from an earlier measurement rather than a blinded forecast. So the paper earns a directional prediction at a characteristic scale, not a calibrated pass/fail test.
