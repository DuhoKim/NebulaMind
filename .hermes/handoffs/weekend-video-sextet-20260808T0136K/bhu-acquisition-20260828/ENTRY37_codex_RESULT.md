## 1. The construction

The paper proves a conditional exact-GR construction, not a fit to our universe. It matches a critically expanding, spatially flat FRW solution to a TOV-form perfect-fluid solution inside a black hole across a shock beyond one Hubble length (lines 23–26, 58–62, 115–142). For the constant equation of state (p=\sigma\rho), (0<\sigma<1), Theorem 1 proves a unique solution of the reduced shock ODE satisfying the entropy/physical inequalities throughout (0<S<1), with the stated endpoint limits (lines 165–205, 207–221). The imposed entropy conditions are (0<\bar p<p) and (0<\bar\rho<\rho); they make the outgoing shock compressive and exclude unstable rarefaction shocks, while the additional bound is (0<\bar p<\bar\rho) (lines 150–161, 193–205). Theorem 2 proves that this solution's shock is everywhere subluminal iff (\sigma\leq1/3) (lines 223–229). Theorem 3 proves the Big-Bang shock-speed limits: zero for (\sigma<1/3), infinite for (\sigma>1/3), and light speed for (\sigma=1/3) (lines 231–247). Thus radiation's (\sigma=1/3) is uniquely selected only by the conjunction “finite, nonzero Big-Bang speed,” not as the sole existing or sole subluminal member of the family (lines 39–42, 207–247).

The paper also proves conditional position/time bounds. If (t_0) is defined as the first time the shock becomes visible at the FRW center, it gives the relation between (H_0^{-1}) and the free initial comoving shock position, bounds the shock's distance at (t_0), and bounds the later horizon-exit time (lines 249–291). It further states that the solutions are global and exact and that their distance at a given Hubble constant is set by one free parameter (lines 293–297).

What is deferred is especially important: immediately after the thermalization claim the authors say, “Details will appear in our forthcoming paper,” and characterize this paper as a summary and physical interpretation (lines 43–45). The paper itself also limits its status: these are “only rough qualitative models,” because the TOV equation of state is output rather than imposed and the FRW equation of state is restricted to constant (\sigma); more realistic transitions could require other waves that are not modeled here (lines 324–341).

## 2. Observation-facing candidates

**(a) Thermalization beyond the light horizon.** The potentially observation-facing sentence is that a shock beyond the Hubble length “would thermalize the radiation in a region well beyond the light cone” of a central observer without inflation (lines 43–44). No CMB angular scale, correlation function, temperature profile, anisotropy sign, or shock-edge signature is derived. The immediate deferral of details (lines 44–45) makes this a qualitative physical remark, not an earned signed observable.

**(b) The (\sigma=1/3) selection.** The mathematical sign/selection is derived within the assumed constant-(\sigma), flat-FRW/TOV shock family: only (\sigma=1/3) has finite nonzero limiting speed, equal to light speed (lines 165–189, 231–247). But (\sigma) is an input equation-of-state parameter, and the authors describe (p=\rho/3) as already relevant to early-Big-Bang physics (lines 39–42, 324–336). No measurement is predicted that could distinguish this construction from ordinary radiation-era physics. This is internal consistency/consonance, not a new observation-facing prediction.

**(c) Present shock location or strength.** The paper gives no present-day value. It proves conditional bounds at the specially defined first-visibility time (t_0): then the shock remains beyond one Hubble length, is within 4.5 Hubble lengths for (\sigma=1/3), and exits the white-hole horizon by at most (4.5t_0) (lines 249–291). But (t_0) is not identified with the present, and the shock distance at a given Hubble constant depends on a free initial-position parameter (lines 293–297). The paper gives no current shock strength, redshift, sky location, or determination that the shock is in our past light cone. Its qualitative statement that the shock weakens and approaches an OS interface (lines 37–39) supplies no observable value or signed sky effect.

**(d) White-hole exit/event horizon.** The derived geometry says the shock crosses the ambient white-hole event horizon when it is one Hubble length from the FRW center and then proceeds to infinity along a Schwarzschild geodesic (lines 313–317); the earlier bounds condition its exit time on (t_0) (lines 266–291). The paper states no observable consequence of that crossing—no signal, spectrum, angular feature, or timing tied to our epoch. It is a causal/geometric feature of the constructed spacetime.

## 3. The blind directional flag

The exact sentences capable of sounding directional are: (i) the shock “would thermalize the radiation in a region well beyond the light cone” without inflation (lines 43–44); (ii) the shock “continues to weaken” until the Hubble length catches it (lines 37–39); (iii) the entropy condition “selects the explosion over the implosion” (lines 305–307, also 15–18); and (iv) the universe/shock eventually emerges through the white-hole horizon (lines 288–291, 313–317).

Items (ii)–(iv) are derived directions of internal dynamical or causal variables, but the paper never maps them to the sign of an observable. Item (i) is the only sentence plausibly aimed at an observable cosmological problem, yet it asserts thermalization without deriving any measurable uniformity scale or the sign/magnitude of a CMB statistic, and its details are expressly deferred (lines 43–45). Under ruling A(a), none earns QUALITATIVE-DIRECTIONAL: a physical narrative direction is not a derived signed observable.

## 4. What a strict model can compute from this paper alone

From the paper alone one can solve the specified constant-(\sigma) construction: the FRW density and scale factor are explicit (lines 165–184), the matching ODEs and conservation constraint determine the TOV-side solution (lines 115–150), the shock speed is explicit (lines 144–148), and theorem-grade bounds relate shock distance and horizon-exit time to (t_0), (H_0), (\sigma), and the free initial position (lines 249–291). These are genuine derived properties of the idealized solution.

One cannot compute the CMB temperature anisotropy of an off-center observer from the paper alone. No observer displacement, radiative-transfer prescription, recombination/last-scattering treatment, photon propagation calculation, perturbation spectrum, or mapping from the shock to a temperature field is supplied; the only radiation-thermalization statement is followed by the forthcoming-paper deferral (lines 43–45). Nor does the paper fix an absolute age/size for our universe: (t_0) is a normalization/first-visibility epoch (lines 178–184, 251–269), and shock distance retains one free initial-position parameter (lines 293–297). A lane could choose parameters and extend the model to generate observables, but that would supply missing physics and numbers rather than merely a missing decision threshold. The paper does not own such a calibratable prediction.

## 5. Tier consequence

**CONSISTENCY-ONLY holds.** The exact solution, uniqueness result, entropy selection, speed classification, and conditional distance/time bounds are substantial and fully derived within stated hypotheses. They show that this shock cosmology is mathematically possible and that the radiation equation of state has a distinguished internal role. They do not derive a signed observation-facing effect, fix an observation-reachable present shock, or state a calibrated quantity that data could falsify. QUALITATIVE-DIRECTIONAL fails because every actual direction belongs to model-internal dynamics, while the sole observation-adjacent thermalization sentence is asserted and deferred. CALIBRATED-FALSIFIER fails because the quantitative bounds are conditional on a free epoch/initial position and are not mapped to data. PROSPECT also overstates what the paper owns: reaching observations requires missing radiative/observer physics and model numbers, not merely a lane-supplied threshold.

In plain language: the paper really does prove an impressive exact family of universes with an outward shock, and it proves how that shock behaves under its assumptions. But it stops before telling us what a telescope should see. Its remark about smoothing radiation beyond the light horizon is not turned into a CMB pattern or any other signed measurement, and the numerical bounds are tied to a freely placed shock and a conditionally defined visibility time rather than to today. So the work supports the model's mathematical consistency, not an observational direction or falsifier.
