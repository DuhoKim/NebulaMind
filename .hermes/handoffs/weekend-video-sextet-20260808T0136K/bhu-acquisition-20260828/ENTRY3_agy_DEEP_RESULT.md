Stuckey's 1994 paper is a theoretical teaching exercise demonstrating how physicists can mathematically stitch a dust-filled Friedmann expanding universe together with a Schwarzschild black hole spacetime. By dropping the standard assumption that the universe looks the same everywhere (the cosmological principle), the author shows that a region of expanding space can be seamlessly surrounded by a black hole vacuum. The paper meticulously works through the coordinate transformations and boundary matching for closed, open, and flat universe models, but it never plugs in real-world astronomical numbers like the expansion rate or the universe's mass. Therefore, it solely proves that an "observable universe inside a black hole" is a mathematically consistent idea in general relativity, rather than arguing that astronomers have observed it or are testing it against physical data.
