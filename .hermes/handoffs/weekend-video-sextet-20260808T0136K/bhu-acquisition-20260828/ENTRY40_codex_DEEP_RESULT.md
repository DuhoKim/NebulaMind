## 1. The collapse

The paper begins with the general spherically symmetric Tolman metric and a comoving ideal spin-fluid stress tensor (lines 72–105). It then imposes homogeneous pressure, \(p'=0\), which makes \(\nu'=0\) and permits synchronous coordinates (lines 117–123). The resulting Tolman equations integrate to \(e^\lambda=r'^2/(1+f(R))\) and a radial evolution equation (lines 127–164), but at that stage the solution still contains three arbitrary radial functions, \(f(R),g(R),\tau_0(R)\) (lines 248–256).

The homogeneous, shear-free collapse is therefore a selected solution, not the general solution: “We seek a solution” with
\[
f(R)=-\sin^2R,\qquad r(\tau,R)=a(\tau)\sin R
\]
(lines 258–265). Substitution gives \(e^\lambda=a^2\) and exactly the closed FLRW interior metric (lines 285–298; metric displayed at lines 220–227). Because a single scale factor multiplies every spatial direction, this ansatz has zero shear by construction. The paper itself states the result conditionally—after horizon formation, “if there is no shear” torsion replaces the singularity with a bounce (lines 57–62)—and later confirms that the torsion condition is sufficient only without shear (lines 326–338).

The matter closure is also assumed. Macroscopic random-spin averaging supplies the effective spin-fluid correction
\(\tilde\epsilon=\epsilon-\alpha n_f^2\), \(\tilde p=p-\alpha n_f^2\) (lines 39–49). The collapse calculation then assumes ultrarelativistic matter in kinetic equilibrium, \(\epsilon=h_*T^4\), \(p=\epsilon/3\), and \(n_f=h_{nf}T^3\), with Standard Model degree counts quoted as \(g_b=29,g_f=90\) (lines 237–240). Conservation yields \(aT=\mathrm{const}\) (lines 266–284).

Under those closures the Friedmann equation is
\[
\dot a^2=-1+\frac{A}{a^2}-\frac{B}{a^4},
\quad A=\frac{\kappa h_*T_0^4a_0^4}{3},\quad
B=\frac{\kappa\alpha h_{nf}^2T_0^6a_0^6}{3}
\]
(lines 285–295). Arithmetic: setting \(x=a^2\), a turning point satisfies \(x^2-Ax+B=0\), hence
\(x_\pm=(A\pm\sqrt{A^2-4B})/2\). Two positive turning points require \(A^2>4B\), represented by the paper's condition (29), which it says astrophysical black-hole-forming systems satisfy (lines 300–309). The negative \(-B/a^4\) term then forbids \(a=0\); the lower root is reached only after the horizon forms, so the model bounces at finite density (lines 311–317).

This does not establish the result for generic collapse. With shear, \(\sigma^2\sim a^{-6}\), the same scaling as \(n_f^2\); if shear initially dominates, it continues to dominate and a singularity forms (lines 340–344). The proposed rescue introduces a phenomenological particle-production law with free dimensionless rate \(\beta\) (lines 346–356). It changes \(n_f\sim a^{-(3+\delta)}\), so \(n_f^2\sim a^{-6-2\delta}\); during contraction \(\delta>0\), making torsion grow faster than shear (lines 360–375). Arithmetic: relative to shear, the torsion scaling is \(n_f^2/\sigma^2\sim a^{-2\delta}\), increasing as \(a\) shrinks only when the assumed production gives \(\delta>0\).

The expressly open domain is realistic inhomogeneous and rotating collapse: the author says it “should” be included, explains that pressure inhomogeneity destroys the comoving-synchronous simplification, and says rotation adds Kerr angular momentum as a parameter (lines 413–420). The final claim that the “general character” remains valid is an extrapolation there, not a demonstrated solution (line 420).

## 2. “Closed universe on the other side”

Closure is chosen, then inherited—not derived uniquely from generic collapse or from an exterior/interior matching condition. In the dust warm-up the paper explicitly says “Choosing” \(f(R)=-\sin^2R\), together with special \(F(R)\) and constant \(\tau_0\), yields the closed-FLRW form (lines 181–227). In the spin-fluid calculation, after acknowledging the three arbitrary functions (lines 254–256), it again says “We seek a solution” with the same \(f(R)=-\sin^2R\) and \(r=a\sin R\) (lines 258–265). Equations (8), (22), and that ansatz then consistently produce \(e^\lambda=a^2\) and the closed metric (lines 285–298). Thus the calculation derives positive curvature *conditional on the ansatz*; it does not show that the Tolman equations or Oppenheimer–Snyder boundary matching force \(k=+1\).

This is the same logical step identified for entry 12 in the audit brief: \(f(R)=-\sin^2R\) is the selected ansatz that supplies the closed spatial geometry. The horizon condition fixes when the surface crosses \(r_g\) (lines 300–302), but no displayed matching calculation selects \(f\) from that condition.

## 3. “Several bounces” and the \(R_0\to\pi\) cycles

There are two distinct regimes. Without particle production, equation (28) has two turning points and therefore produces an infinite sequence of identical bounces and crunches within the selected closed model (lines 303–324). With production, the paper says the next maximum is larger and the cycle longer (lines 382–383), while a cosmological constant eventually prevents recollapse after a finite series (lines 394–411). That latter “several bounces” history is a parameter-dependent scenario: production is controlled by free \(\beta\); too large a value gives eternal inflation, while inflation requires the dimensionless combination \(\beta H^3/(3c^3h_{nf}T^3)\) to approach but remain below 1 (lines 394–404). Escape from cycling additionally requires reaching an unspecified “critical size” where the cosmological constant matters (lines 407–409).

No fixed bounce count, cycle-duration ratio, scale-factor growth factor, produced-matter amount, or numerical \(\beta\) window is calculated. The only explicit inflation inequality is “maximum ... lesser than 1” (lines 396–400), a condition on model evolution rather than a calibrated value for our universe.

The claimed \(R_0\to\pi\) limit is not secured by the displayed arithmetic. Equation (35) gives
\(\sin^3R_0=r_g/a_{\max}\) (lines 384–390). If successive \(a_{\max}\) grows, the right side tends to zero, so only \(\sin R_0\to0\) follows; this permits \(R_0\to0\) **or** \(R_0\to\pi\). Selecting the \(\pi\) branch (lines 391–410) needs an additional branch/geometry argument not supplied here. There is also an unresolved tension between “\(R_0\) does not change” in the identical-cycle solution (line 322) and the later cycle-by-cycle changing \(R_0\) construction (lines 384–392). Accordingly, increasing cycles are qualitative consequences of the phenomenological production scenario, not a fixed quantitative prediction.

## 4. Observation-facing content

“Recent observations suggest that our universe may be closed” is introductory motivation (lines 22–24), supported only by citations listed at lines 424–430. It is not inserted into the collapse equations, fitted, or converted into a predicted curvature value or threshold. The Planck-consistency statement about finite inflation likewise cites earlier work (lines 50–54); this paper supplies no derived observable spectrum, relic abundance, signed curvature magnitude, uncertainty, or numerical comparison for our universe.

The paper uses possibility language—“Our universe might” have arisen this way (lines 18–20 and 413–415)—and expressly states that formation and subsequent dynamics could not be observed from outside the parent black hole because of infinite horizon redshift (lines 24–27). That is a direct concession that the proposed parent-side collapse has no exterior observational test in this treatment. No alternative descendant-universe test is derived.

## 5. Tier consequence

**CONSISTENCY-ONLY holds under A(a).** The paper demonstrates mathematical consistency of a nonsingular bounce for a chosen homogeneous, isotropic/zero-shear, closed-FLRW spin-fluid interior and gives conditional scaling arguments when phenomenological particle production is added. It does not derive closure from generic collapse or matching; does not solve the admitted inhomogeneous/rotating problem; and does not produce an observation-facing signed prediction, relic, numerical value, or calibrated threshold for our universe. The inequality involving the free production history cannot substitute for the missing predicted number: a lane may own a missing threshold, never a missing number.

The 2026-08-28 **QUALITATIVE-DIRECTIONAL flag does not survive** this adjudication. The directional statements—torsion can reverse collapse, production can outrun shear, cycles can grow, and positive curvature results—are conditional on selected geometry, matter closure, free production dynamics, and/or extrapolation beyond the solved case. They do not constitute a derived directional observable for our universe. PROSPECT is also too high because no viable observation channel is developed, and CALIBRATED-FALSIFIER is excluded a fortiori.

In plain language: the paper shows that a carefully chosen, perfectly uniform and nonrotating ball of spin-carrying matter can bounce instead of hitting a singularity, and that an added particle-production model can make later cycles bigger. But the closed-universe geometry is put into the chosen solution, realistic lumpy or rotating collapse is left unsolved, the number and growth of cycles are not predicted, and nothing measurable for our universe is calculated. It is therefore a consistency demonstration, not an observationally directional result.
