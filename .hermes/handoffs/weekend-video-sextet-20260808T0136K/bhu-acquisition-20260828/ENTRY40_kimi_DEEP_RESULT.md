2. "CLOSED UNIVERSE ON THE OTHER SIDE" — chosen, not derived

Closure enters exactly at the ansatz. For the dust warm-up: "Choosing f(R) = −sin²R, F(R) = 2a₀ sin³R" (eq (15), line 191) — an explicit choice among all f satisfying only 1 + f > 0 (line 145). With r = a sin R the metric becomes ds² = c²dτ² − a²[dR² + sin²R dΩ²] (eq (19), line 222) and the paper states "This metric has a form of the closed FLRW metric and describes a part of a closed universe" (line 227). The spin-fluid section repeats the identical step: "We seek a solution … f(R) = −sin²R, r(τ,R) = a(τ) sin R" (eq (23), line 260). Tolman's f(R) is the LTB generalization of the FLRW curvature constant k; f = −sin²R with r = a sin R is precisely k = +1. f = +sin²R (k = −1) or f = 0 (k = 0) satisfy 1 + f > 0 equally well and are not excluded by anything in the paper. The summary line "the resulting universe … is closed" (line 62) and "has a closed geometry (constant positive curvature)" (line 319) are therefore restatements of the ansatz, not outputs of the collapse dynamics. Line 64's "Thanks to torsion, the universe in a black hole becomes closed with topology S³" overreaches: what torsion supplies is the bounce (turning point at small a); the S³ spatial geometry is supplied by the chosen f(R). The contrast case — without torsion, the Kantowski–Sachs interior with topology R×S² (line 63) — is singular; it does not show closure is forced.

3. "SEVERAL BOUNCES" AND THE R₀ → π ASYMPTOTICS — parameter-dependent scenario, no fixed numbers

Without particle production: "an infinite number of bounces and cycles … each cycle is alike" (lines 62, 324) — a oscillates between the two turning points of eq (28). With production: cycles grow ("each cycle is larger and longer then the previous cycle," lines 69, 383, 408) because production raises the maximum a at each crunch (line 382); the count becomes finite only because Λ eventually dominates (lines 70, 380, 407–409). R₀ obeys sin³R₀ = r_g/a(0) (eq (35), line 386); as the per-cycle maximum a grows, sin R₀ decreases and "R₀ approaches π" (lines 391–392, 410). "The last bounce is the big bang" (line 411).

No bounce count is derived — the number of cycles depends on the free phenomenological β (eq (31), line 349), on initial conditions a(0) = (r₀³/r_g)^{1/2} (line 377), and on Λ (line 380). No growth factor per cycle is given; "larger and longer" is qualitative. The production dynamics itself is schematic: eq (34), line 369, δ ∼ −a^δ ȧ³, is an order-of-magnitude relation for a time-varying exponent, not an integrated law. The only bound on β is the internal no-eternal-inflation condition, max[βH³/(3c³h_nfT³)] < 1 (lines 396–398). This is a parameter-dependent scenario, not derived dynamics with fixed numbers.

Plain-language paragraph: This paper shows, correctly and by explicit calculation, that if you assume a perfectly uniform, non-rotating ball of hot spin-carrying fluid and choose the closed-universe form of the collapse solution, then torsion's repulsion stops the collapse at a finite minimum size and the interior bounces into an oscillating closed baby universe. That is the profile of a consistency result: the mechanism works on its own stated terms, and nothing here can be tested.
