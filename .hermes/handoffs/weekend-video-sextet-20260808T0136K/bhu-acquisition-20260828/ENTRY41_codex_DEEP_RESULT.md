## 1. Torsion versus shear

This part is derived in the paper, not merely cited. The spin-fluid correction is
\(\tilde\epsilon=\epsilon-\alpha n_f^2\), \(\tilde p=p-\alpha n_f^2\), with
\(\alpha=\kappa(\hbar c)^2/32\) (lines 39–45). For the KS metric, the paper derives
\(a^3=XY^2\) and \(H=(\dot X/X+2\dot Y/Y)/3\) (lines 186–205), and
\(\sigma^2=\frac13(\dot X/X-\dot Y/Y)^2\) (lines 282–294). Combining the KS evolution
equation with that shear definition gives

\[
\dot\sigma^2+6H\sigma^2
=\frac{2}{3Y^2}\left(\frac{\dot X}{X}-\frac{\dot Y}{Y}\right),
\]

whose right-hand side the paper states is positive; hence during contraction
\(\sigma^2\) grows faster than \(a^{-6}\) (lines 305–329). With conserved fermion
number, \(n_f\propto a^{-3}\), so the torsion term \(n_f^2\propto a^{-6}\) and shear
eventually wins (lines 348–353).

The patch is explicitly phenomenological, not a QFT calculation in Riemann–Cartan
spacetime:

\[
\frac1{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{d(\sqrt{-g}n_f)}{dt}=\beta H^4,
\qquad \sqrt{-g}=a^3,
\]

so \(\dot n_f+3Hn_f=\beta H^4\) and
\(\dot n_f^2+6Hn_f^2=2\beta n_fH^4\) (lines 357–373). The source itself says the
rate “can be phenomenologically given” this way (line 362); \(\beta\) is a free
production-rate parameter, supported only by citation rather than derived here.

## 2. Bounce condition

There is a derived local inequality. Raychaudhuri gives the necessary-and-sufficient
condition
\(-\kappa(\tilde\epsilon+3\tilde p)/2>2\sigma^2\) (lines 331–342). For radiation,
\(p=\epsilon/3\), the arithmetic is
\(\tilde\epsilon+3\tilde p=2\epsilon-4\alpha n_f^2\), hence

\[
2\kappa\alpha n_f^2>2\sigma^2+\kappa\epsilon,                         \tag{32}
\]

as printed at lines 342–346. To keep the torsion side outgrowing shear, comparison
of the two rate equations yields

\[
\frac{2\beta n_f}{81}
\left(\frac{\dot X}{X}+2\frac{\dot Y}{Y}\right)^4
>
\frac{2}{3Y^2}\left(\frac{\dot X}{X}-\frac{\dot Y}{Y}\right),       \tag{35}
\]

where the factor \(1/81\) is simply \(H^4=[(\dot X/X+2\dot Y/Y)/3]^4\)
(lines 371–382). Thus “particle production dominates over shear” is more than a
qualitative slogan. But it is not reduced to a numerical or parameter-only bound:
it depends on the evolving \(X,Y,n_f\), and no solution or threshold value of
\(\beta\) is supplied. The model parameters are parent-hole mass \(M\) and free
\(\beta\); the remaining arbitrary initial datum is \(\dot X_0\), related to \(B\)
(lines 400–405). “Big enough,” “too small,” and “too big” delimit a viable range
only verbally (lines 408–409 and 425–429).

## 3. “Closed”

The Schwarzschild-to-KS coordinate transformation is derived: \(r=\tau/b\), and
the spatial metric is \(X^2dr^2+Y^2d\Omega_2^2\) (lines 91–109). That is the KS
product geometry with spatial topology locally \(\mathbb R\times S^2\) (absent an
extra compact identification of \(r\)); it is not the compact constant-curvature
\(S^3\) geometry of a closed FLRW model. The paper then labels this metric “closed”
(line 113), but does not derive compactness or an FLRW \(k=+1\) curvature parameter.
Even when inflation makes \(X\) approximately proportional to \(Y\), the stated
operation is only a rescaling of \(r\) to obtain \(X\approx Y\) (lines 438–443);
that does not change \(dr^2+d\Omega_2^2\) into the \(S^3\) spatial metric. The paper's
observation that one KS equation is identical to a closed-Friedmann pressure equation
(lines 155–168) likewise is not a derivation that the full three-geometry becomes
closed FLRW.

Line 21's closure evidence is reference 1, E. Di Valentino, A. Melchiorri, and
J. Silk, *Nature Astronomy* 4, 196 (2020) (lines 458–459). It is used as motivation:
“may be closed” precedes the scenario (lines 21–22), and the summary again says our
universe “may be closed” (lines 445–447). No predicted curvature sign is confronted
with that evidence. Under A(a), this is assumed/borrowed consistency, not a derived
directional output.

## 4. Observation-facing content

The paper supplies no calculated present curvature, residual-shear amplitude,
relic abundance, uncertainty, or observational threshold. Its Planck statement is
that earlier particle-production inflation is “consistent with” Planck 2015 by
citation (line 50), not a fit or calculation in this paper. Likewise, matching the
observed FLRW state is posed as something the scenario “must” accomplish (lines
55–60). It derives the conditional isotropization relation
\(Y\ddot Y\gg1\Rightarrow X\propto Y\) during inflation, but then only states—partly
by citation—that anisotropy grows in radiation/matter domination and decreases in
the cosmological-constant era (lines 438–443). The summary's “may be closed and
slightly anisotropic” is tied to observational references rather than a computed
model output (lines 445–447; the cited anisotropy papers are listed at lines
596–597). These are consistency statements, not a derived signed discriminator for
our universe.

## 5. Tier consequence

**CONSISTENCY-ONLY holds.** The work contains genuine dynamical derivations—most
notably equations (30), (32), and (35)—but the decisive new mechanism is an ansatz
with free \(\beta\), its allowed range is not calculated, and no present observable
number or derived observational sign follows. The lane may own a missing threshold,
but here it would first need a model-produced observable number (or at minimum a
genuinely derived observational direction); neither is present. A(a) does not lift
the entry because “closed” is a label/motivation rather than a derived FLRW
\(k=+1\) result. Therefore the 2026-08-28 QUALITATIVE-DIRECTIONAL flag does **not**
survive this proper adjudication.

In plain language: the paper does show mathematically why ordinary torsion loses to
anisotropic shear and what inequality an added particle source would have to satisfy.
But it chooses that source phenomenologically, leaves its strength free, and never
turns the construction into a numerical or even uniquely signed observational test.
Its use of “closed” for the black-hole interior also does not establish that our
late-time universe has positive FLRW curvature. The scenario can be made compatible
with observations, but this paper does not extract a discriminating prediction from
it.
