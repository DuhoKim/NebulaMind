## 1. The equivalence

The black-hole metric introduced as eq. (1) is the Euclidean Schwarzschild metric

\[
ds^2_{\rm bh}=\left(1-{2m\over r}\right)d\tau^2+
\left(1-{2m\over r}\right)^{-1}dr^2+r^2d\Omega_2^2,
\]

with \(\tau=-it\); the paper notes that this displayed Euclidean form applies for \(r>2m\) (lines 88–100). The wormhole family in eq. (2) is

\[
ds^2_{\rm wh}=\left(1-{M^n\over r^n}\right)^{-1}dr^2+r^2d\Omega_3^2,
\]

with \(d\Omega_3^2=d\psi_3^2+\sin^2\psi_3\,d\Omega_2^2\), and its FRW scale factor is \(a=M\cosh^{2/n}(n\eta/2)\) (lines 101–125; some superscripts and fractions are damaged in the OCR). Here \(n\) labels the exponent—and hence the known solution class—in that family: the paper assigns \(n=4\), \(2\), and \(1\) to three sets of cited wormhole solutions (lines 107–121). Thus “\(n=1\)” is a particular wormhole solution/scale-factor law, not a perturbative order or a limit.

For \(n=1\), the paper rewrites the metric as eq. (6), with a Schwarzschild-like \((1-M/a)\) radial factor and an overall \(\sin^2\psi_3\) factor (lines 157–168). It then changes the time coordinate, integrates the transformation in eqs. (8)–(11), promotes the integration constants to independent Kruskal-like coordinates, and obtains \(U,V\) of the standard exterior/interior form: factors \(|r/M-1|^{1/2}e^{r/(2M)}e^{\mp t'/(2M)}\) (lines 202–270). Those coordinates give the maximally extended metric (12) as a conformal multiple of the Schwarzschild–Kruskal metric; in the interior the conformal factor is explicitly written as eq. (13) and is real, continuous, finite, and nonzero throughout \(0<r<M\) (lines 276–310). The OCR badly drops several \(r/M\) fractions in eqs. (7)–(13), but the prose and the surviving coordinate factors fix the claimed map and domain.

The factor relating scales is derived in the construction, not merely asserted: the resulting causal structure is Schwarzschild–Kruskal for \(r<M\) with \(M=2m\) (lines 224–244), and the final result again states black-hole mass \(m=M/2\) (lines 329–337). In geometrized units the Schwarzschild gravitational/event-horizon radius is \(r_g=2m=M\). The abstract's phrase “gravitational radius is half the wormhole neck radius” (lines 8–11) is therefore inconsistent with the body if \(M\) itself is the neck radius; it likely uses a different radius convention or reflects OCR/source wording. The equation-level body unambiguously supplies \(m=M/2\), hence \(r_g=M\).

This is an exact conformal equivalence on the open interior submanifold \(0<r<M\), not an asymptotic or approximation-limit equality. It preserves classical causal structure, not all metric or quantum-field observables. It is also not a global identity: the conformal factor has the wrong sign outside \(r=M\), while it is genuine only inside (lines 302–310), and the two spaces differ at \(r=0\) (lines 358–371). The claimed uniqueness versus \(n=2,4\) is argued by their different extended causal structures (lines 311–328).

## 2. Physical meaning of the baby-universe sector

The mathematically careful claim is that the maximally extended \(n=1\) wormhole has a baby-universe submanifold \(M_{bu}\) conformally mapped to the black-hole interior submanifold \(M_{ibh}\) (lines 329–342). The paper then gives this a stronger physical interpretation: a black-hole interior is the manifestation, in one of the two connected universes, of an \(n=1\) wormhole topology change (lines 377–387). So the derivation establishes equivalence of sectors; the statement that the interior “is” the physical manifestation of branching is interpretation layered on it.

The paper does **not** claim that our whole universe is a black-hole interior or an interior cosmology. “Our universe” appears as the region containing a real observer and one end of the branching process: that observer sees a spacetime behaving as a Schwarzschild black hole (lines 399–408, 439–455). The baby universe is a closed universe branching from our region in the motivating scenario (lines 22–35), not an identification of our universe with somebody else's black-hole interior.

## 3. Flux result

The OCR reads \((T_{00})_{bh}=(129\pi M^2)^{-1}\) at lines 399–408. That “129” is almost certainly an OCR transposition of **192**, because the standard 1+1-dimensional conformal-scalar Hawking flux is

\[
F={\kappa^2\over48\pi}={1\over768\pi m^2}.
\]

Using the paper's derived \(m=M/2\),

\[
F={1\over768\pi(M/2)^2}={1\over192\pi M^2}.
\]

This also matches the paper's standard Schwarzschild temperature: \(T=1/(4\pi M)=1/(8\pi m)\) (lines 450–455). By contrast, literal \(1/(129\pi M^2)\) would be larger than the standard value by \(192/129\simeq1.488\), with no derivation or normalization change in the source explaining that factor. The internally consistent reading is therefore 192, an OCR error, and standard Hawking flux. This is the reduced two-dimensional stress-tensor/moving-mirror normalization, not the full four-dimensional greybody-corrected luminosity.

The ideal-observer zero is partly derived and partly interpretive. For the specific \(n=1\) wormhole trajectory (14), the moving-mirror calculation is said to yield \((T_{00})_{wh}=0\) (lines 411–427): that is the derived part. The preceding claim that an ideal observer spanning both universes “should be expected” to see nothing because the baby universe must remain closed is interpretive (lines 388–411). The subsequent assignment of equal and opposite flux to the negative-time copy follows from an imposed energy-conservation argument (lines 428–445), rather than a separately displayed stress-tensor calculation. Moreover, the conformal deletion that turns the ideal geometry into the real observer's black-hole geometry is explicitly acknowledged as not rigorously proved (footnote, lines 418–424).

Nothing here produces an observational departure from standard Hawking radiation. A real observer sees the customary Schwarzschild flux, entropy, temperature, evaporation, and singularity; only a hypothetical observer with access to both universes sees the wormhole-wide cancellation (lines 399–455).

## 4. Observation-facing content

There is no signed prediction for our universe, no measured quantity, and no numerical threshold separating this model from ordinary Schwarzschild evaporation. The observation-facing formulas are \(S=\pi M^2\), \(T=1/(4\pi M)\), and the standard reduced flux \(F=1/(192\pi M^2)\), with the free scale \(M\) (lines 399–408 and 450–455). The paper also says semiclassical Hawking reasoning applies when the hole is much heavier than the Planck mass and becomes unclear near the Planck scale (lines 12–18), but gives no calibrated cutoff. Its remarks about macroscopic-wormhole nucleation are qualitative—“overwhelmingly close to zero” for generic macroscopic wormholes and increasing probability with \(M\) for the \(n=1\) case—without an evaluated number or test threshold (lines 63–80, 343–357).

## 5. Tier consequence

**CONSISTENCY-ONLY; A(a).** The paper derives a nontrivial exact conformal/casual equivalence for a particular wormhole sector and checks that a one-universe observer recovers ordinary Schwarzschild thermodynamics and Hawking radiation. Its only apparent numerical novelty, OCR “129,” resolves to the standard coefficient 192 when the paper's own \(m=M/2\) relation is inserted. The ideal/real observer distinction concerns global access to two branches, is partly interpretive, and offers neither a distinct observable direction nor a number-plus-threshold test in our universe. A lane can supply a missing threshold, but here it would first have to supply a missing nonstandard observable number; that cannot elevate the entry.

In plain language: the paper finds that the inside-causal-structure of a specific, maximally extended baby-universe wormhole can be redrawn as the inside of an ordinary Schwarzschild black hole, up to a smooth conformal rescaling. It then interprets ordinary black-hole evaporation as what one side of a two-universe branching event looks like. The garbled flux coefficient is standard Hawking radiation once corrected from OCR and expressed using the paper's mass convention. Because an observer in our universe gets no different signal, magnitude, or decision threshold, the entry remains a consistency result rather than a directional prediction or falsifier.
