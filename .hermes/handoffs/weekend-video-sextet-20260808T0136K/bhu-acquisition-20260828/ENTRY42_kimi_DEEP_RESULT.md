1. The equivalence.

Metrics. The Schwarzschild (euclidean instanton) metric is eq. (1): ds² = (1 − 2m/r) dτ² + (1 − 2m/r)⁻¹ dr² + r² dΩ₂² (lines 91–96). The wormhole family is eq. (2): ds² = (1 − M/r)⁻¹ dr² + r² dΩ₃² (OCR-garbled prefactor, lines 107–111), with the 3-sphere metric written dΩ₃² = dχ₃² + sin²χ₃ dΩ₂² (line 111) and the FRW transformation a = M cosh^(2/n)(½nη), η = ∫dτ/a (eq. 3, lines 119–121). The n = 1 member is recast as eq. (6): ds² = sin²χ₃ [(1 − M/a)⁻¹ da² − dτ² + a² dΩ₂²] (line 162), with sin²χ₃ = 4(M/a)(1 − M/a) (eq. 7, lines 194–195) — the overall factor is exactly the conformal factor that turns the bracket into a Schwarzschild-like metric with mass M/2.

The map. The author defines new time variables t′, τ′ via eqs. (8)–(9) (lines 202–209), integrates to the tortoise-type relations eqs. (10)–(11) (lines 213–222), promotes the integration constants K± to Kruskal-type null coordinates U, V = (r/M − 1)^{1/2} exp(r/2M) exp(∓t′/2M) (lines 258–268), and arrives at the maximally extended n = 1 metric eq. (12) (lines 294–300) with conformal factor Ω² = 4UV exp(−r/M)[1 − UV exp(−r/M)] (eq. 13, lines 307–310), which is "real, continuous, finite and non-vanishing" for 0 < r < M (lines 309–310).

Derived or asserted? Derived, in the sense that the coordinate construction is shown step by step and the conformal factor is exhibited explicitly; the factor relating gravitational radius to neck radius, m = M/2 (stated line 134, used lines 232, 337), falls out of eq. (7), sin²χ₃ = 4(M/a)(1 − M/a), which matches the Schwarzschild g-factor 1 − 2m/r precisely when 2m = M. However, one step is openly NOT rigorous: the restriction to a real single-universe observer is done by "conformally deleting" the z ≤ 0 events, and the footnote admits "we have not succeeded in proving this rigorously" (lines 418–424, proposing conformal factor sin⁻¹χ₃). Exactness: the equivalence is claimed exact (not a limit) for the baby-universe submanifold 0 < r < M, and unique to n = 1 — the n = 2 case fails condition (5) except as a formal 3-dim section (lines 145–156), and the n = 2, 4 Kruskal-type extensions have the wrong causal structure (lines 311–328).

What is n = 1? The exponent labelling the three known wormhole-instanton families (eq. 2): n = 4 Giddings–Strominger/Myers, n = 2 Hawking/Halliwell–Laflamme ("Tolman-Hawking"), n = 1 González-Díaz/Coleman–Lee (lines 112–115). It enters through the scale factor a = M cosh^(2/n)(½nη) (line 119).

2. What "baby universe sector" means physically.

The paper's claim is the second option in the brief: the baby-universe submanifold M_bu of the n = 1 wormhole "can be conformally mapped into the internal black hole submanifold M_ibh" of a Schwarzschild hole of mass m = M/2 (lines 329–337). It then goes one interpretive step further: the black-hole interior "is not but what is physically manifested out from the occurrence of an n = 1 wormhole topology change, in only one of the two universes" connected by it (lines 381–387) — i.e., the interior IS the manifestation of a branching baby universe, seen from one side. The two-copy structure (each point of M_bu is two classically indistinguishable events at ±z, lines 365–371) is used to recover Hawking's antimatter mirror-hole picture (lines 438–445). No claim anywhere that OUR universe is such an interior; "our universe" appears only as the region hosting the real observer and the first black hole (lines 402, 450).

3. The flux result.

Paper: a real observer detects (T₀₀)_bh = (129πM²)⁻¹ (line 404); an ideal observer (both universes) detects (T₀₀)_wh = 0 (line 427), derived via the moving-mirror approach for the n = 1 trajectory eq. (14) (lines 411–427, citing Fulling–Davies), with energy conservation then forcing a −(T₀₀)_bh flux for the mirror observer (lines 428–438).

Check against standard Hawking. For a Schwarzschild hole of mass m = M/2, the canonical massless-scalar luminosity (Page/Birrell–Davies) is L = 1/(15360π m²) = 1/(3840π M²). The paper's number 1/(129π M²) is ~29.8× larger. So "129π" is NOT the standard scalar coefficient. Possibilities: (a) OCR garble — but the printed context "129riM²" (line 404, ri = π) is a plausible clean read of "129πM²"; (b) a different normalisation — González-Díaz may be quoting the Birrell–Davies (ref. 17) RSET for a specific quantum state/vacuum choice or an all-species sum, which can shift the coefficient by an order-unity-to-30 factor; (c) a genuinely different number. From the pinned source alone I cannot resolve (a)/(b)/(c); what I CAN say is that the paper presents the value as the standard Hawking flux for the effective hole ("would radiate according to the Hawking process", lines 450–455), not as a new prediction, and the entropy/temperature it assigns — S = πM², T = 1/(4πM) (line 452) — check out EXACTLY against the standard formulas for m = M/2: S = 4πm² = 4π(M/2)² = πM² ✓; T = 1/(8πm) = 1/(4πM) ✓. So the thermodynamics is standard Hawking restated in the M variable, and the flux is at most a coefficient-level deviation, not a new functional form (still ∝ 1/M²).

"Ideal observer sees 0": derived within the paper's formalism (moving-mirror calculation on trajectory (14) gives (T₀₀)_wh = 0, lines 411–427), but the premise — an observer who "observes events taking place in the two universes simultaneously" (lines 388–389) — is itself interpretive; no such observer exists operationally. The cancellation is by construction of the two-copy ±z structure plus energy conservation (lines 428–438), and the footnoted non-rigorous conformal deletion (lines 418–424) sits exactly at the step connecting the two statements.

Observationally distinguishable from standard Hawking? No. The real observer's phenomenology is: thermal flux ∝ 1/M², S = πM², T = 1/(4πM), shrinkage to the Planck scale (lines 450–455) — identical in form to a Schwarzschild hole of mass M/2. The only deviation is the coefficient 129 vs 3840π, which (even if taken at face value) is a normalisation of an unobservable-in-practice flux, and the zero-flux statement refers to a non-physical observer.

4. Observation-facing content.

None for our universe. Every number in the paper carries the free parameter M (neck radius): S = πM², T = 1/(4πM) (line 452), (T₀₀) = (129πM²)⁻¹ (line 404). There is no signed direction, no threshold, no value of M fixed by anything observable. The one qualitative directional statement — for n = 1, larger M gives larger nucleation probability P, so the baby-universe area "could well play the role of entropy" and macroscopic branching is not suppressed (lines 350–357) — is a consistency remark about the second law, not a prediction with a threshold.

Plain-language paragraph.

This 1991 paper shows, with the coordinate work shown explicitly, that one specific wormhole solution (the "n = 1" kind) has an inside region that is mathematically the conformal double of the inside of a Schwarzschild black hole whose mass is half the wormhole's neck size — and it uses that to dress Hawking's "evaporating black holes branch off baby universes" idea in equations. Everything a real observer would see — temperature 1/(4πM), entropy πM², thermal flux falling off as 1/M² — is exactly standard Hawking radiation for a hole of mass M/2, just re-labelled; the one odd digit string (129π instead of the textbook 3840π in the flux) is either an OCR artefact or a different normalisation, and the "ideal observer sees nothing" line is a formal cancellation for an observer who could watch two universes at once, which nobody can. There is no prediction about our universe, no threshold, and no number that an observation could check, so the entry stays CONSISTENCY-ONLY.
