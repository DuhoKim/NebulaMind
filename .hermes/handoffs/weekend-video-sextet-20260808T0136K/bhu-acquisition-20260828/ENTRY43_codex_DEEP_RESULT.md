## 1. The construction

The paper numerically evolves the collapse of a precomputed, spherically symmetric equilibrium boson star. The equilibrium initial data themselves come from the cited earlier construction [42], whereas this paper evolves one unstable configuration with the BSSN Einstein–Klein–Gordon system in the Einstein frame and then uses the algebraic conformal relation to reconstruct the Palatini or \(f(\mathcal R)\) frame. The authors state: “The initial data describing spherically-symmetric BS in Palatini gravity were obtained in [42]” and specify an unstable model, \(\Phi_0=0.1\), \(\xi=0.1\), perturbed upward by 3% to trigger collapse (line 56). Their numerical strategy is derived from the frame map: solve the Einstein-frame action and recover the original-frame metric algebraically (lines 28–46), with the BSSN implementation and convergence tests supplied in the appendix (lines 112–320). Some ingredients are explicitly cited rather than newly derived: initial equilibria [42], coordinate conversion [43], numerical grid/code methods [44,45], apparent-horizon finder [46], event-horizon reconstruction [47], and embedding procedure [50,51] (lines 56, 62, 84–87).

In the Einstein frame, the computed endpoint is an ordinary nonrotating black hole with a long-lived scalar cloud: the event horizon forms at \(t\approx80\), the apparent horizon at \(t\approx87\), its mass is \(M_{\rm AH}=0.61831\,M_{\rm Pl}/\mu\), and the initial Misner–Sharp mass is \(0.61918\,M_{\rm Pl}/\mu\) (lines 58, 62–64). “Birth of a baby universe” refers instead to the reconstructed \(f(\mathcal R)\)-frame two-sphere area \(A\sim g_{\theta\theta}\): as \(f_{\mathcal R}\) approaches zero near the center, \(g_{\theta\theta}\) develops a local minimum followed inward by a local maximum whose height grows exponentially (lines 68–73). The minimum is the throat/cosmic-bounce surface; the inner patch between it and the Einstein-frame center is the expanding baby universe. Thus it is not merely area growing in one frame while shrinking in the other: operationally it is a finite inner region joined to the parent region by a minimal-area two-sphere, represented by embedding diagrams as an inter-universe throat (lines 73, 84–88). The reported expansion is exponential and superluminal over the resolved interval \(t\in[84.6,91.8]\), with unchanged \(\mathbb R^4\) topology (lines 75–76). The causal characterization is incomplete at the innermost end because the slicing makes the region inside the maximum unreachable after \(t=91.8\), so the authors cannot confirm null-geodesic convergence at the center (line 75).

## 2. The frame question

The baby universe is not present in the Einstein-frame geometry. The abstract is explicit: “in that frame, the endpoint is a nonrotating black hole surrounded by a quasi-stationary cloud,” whereas “the dynamics in the \(f(\mathcal R)\) frame is dramatically different” and contains the expanding baby universe and throat (line 9). The detailed results likewise call the Einstein-frame evolution “qualitatively identical” to boson-star collapse in GR (lines 62–64), while the minimum and growing inner area arise only after applying \(q_{\mu\nu}=f_{\mathcal R}g_{\mu\nu}\) (lines 34–46, 68–73).

The paper does not dismiss this as a frame artefact. In its formulation, \(g_{\mu\nu}\) is the metric in the original action to which the complex scalar is minimally coupled (lines 21–34), while \(q_{\mu\nu}\) is explicitly an auxiliary Einstein-frame metric used to solve the equations (lines 22, 34). The authors therefore interpret the \(g\)-frame minimum as a “cosmic bounce” and the inner region as the “newborn universe” (lines 68–73). That physical interpretation is an inference tied to the stipulated matter coupling, not a frame-invariant feature: the two geometries give radically different area structures, and the effect occurs as the conformal factor tends toward zero. The paper says the mapping is well posed only while the frame relation is “nondegenerate and sufficiently smooth” (line 50), but it neither reports an actual degeneracy nor analyzes whether closeness to zero undermines the interpretation. Within its own model the claimed physical frame is the matter/Jordan \(f(\mathcal R)\) frame; across representations, the baby-universe geometry is plainly frame-dependent.

## 3. Parameters and regime

The displayed collapse uses \(f(\mathcal R)=\mathcal R+\xi\mathcal R^2\) with positive \(\xi=0.1\), central field amplitude \(\Phi_0=0.1\), and a 3% perturbation of the radial scalar profile (line 56). The scalar is a free complex massive field with \(V=-\mu^2|\Phi|^2/2\); no independent scalar self-coupling is introduced (lines 24–30). The boson mass \(\mu\) sets the scale: reported horizon masses are in \(M_{\rm Pl}/\mu\) units (line 62). The paper does not translate \(\xi\), \(\mu\), time, throat area, or density into a phenomenologically fixed physical scale.

The numerical evidence shown in detail is therefore a selected unstable-branch case, not a mapped parameter survey. The authors nevertheless assert that the result “persist[s] for all values” of \(\xi\) and for other \(\Phi_0\), provided the equilibrium lies on the unstable branch and the perturbation is large enough to cause collapse (line 95). That sentence gives no tested range, sign split, sample count, or collapse threshold. Stable solutions are deferred (line 56), and the appendix’s stable example—\(\xi=0.1\), \(\Phi_0=0.02\), \(\omega/\mu=0.9545\), \(M=0.471\)—is used for code convergence, not baby-universe production (line 320). Accordingly, the outcome is claimed generic conditional on unstable collapse, but the paper documents it as a narrow numerical existence demonstration and supplies no quantified basin of attraction.

## 4. Observation-facing content

The throat is always inside the event horizon. The authors conclude that light from the baby universe cannot escape and that “distant external observers will not be able to tell if the outcome of the collapse is an ordinary black hole or a black hole with an inner expanding universe” (lines 88–89). No gravitational-wave waveform, echo, remnant discriminator, exterior threshold, or other observation-facing signature is calculated. The numerical numbers—\(t_{\rm EH}\approx80\), \(t_{\rm throat}\approx84.6\), \(t_{\rm AH}\approx87\), \(M_{\rm AH}=0.61831\), \(M_{\rm MS}=0.61918\), and an apparent late throat pseudocoordinate \(g_{\theta\theta}\approx0.89\)—characterize this simulation rather than a calibrated observational test (lines 58, 62, 87–91).

The paper does not claim that our universe is such a baby universe. It only says that further work is needed to determine whether the inflating phase could be compatible with the mechanism associated with early-universe homogeneity, and calls the central-density buildup merely suggestive of homogenizing conditions (line 104). Late-time persistence is also unsettled: the throat area shrinks as the scalar cloud is absorbed and is expected eventually to close, but numerical limitations prevent resolving that behavior (lines 88, 101–102). Hence the result is numerical existence/consistency evidence, not a forecast or falsifiable observation program.

## 5. Tier consequence

**CONSISTENCY-ONLY holds.** The paper constructs a collapse solution in a specified modified-gravity model and demonstrates that its matter-frame geometry can contain a temporarily inflating inner region behind a horizon. This is constructive evidence that baby-universe formation is dynamically possible under the model assumptions; it neither identifies our universe with that region nor tests such an identification. Its sole discussion of our universe is explicitly prospective and compatibility-level (line 104), while it states that external observers cannot distinguish the result from an ordinary black hole (line 89). There is therefore no observable sign, corpus-facing directional claim, forecast, calibrated number, or threshold supporting elevation to QUALITATIVE-DIRECTIONAL, PROSPECT, or CALIBRATED-FALSIFIER. AUDIT_FLAG_MEMBERSHIP is unwarranted because, at consistency tier, the work directly bears on dynamical possibility rather than needing to make or test an observational identification. The absent exterior signature and unquantified collapse/parameter thresholds cannot be repaired by promoting the tier: the lane may own a missing threshold, never a missing number.

In plain language: the authors successfully simulate one collapsing unstable boson star by evolving an equivalent GR problem and translating it back to the metric that matter feels. In that physical frame, the innermost area bounces and grows into a finite inflating pocket joined through a throat, but the whole structure remains behind the black-hole horizon and may disappear when the leftover scalar cloud is consumed. The paper therefore shows that a baby universe can occur in this model; it does not show that ours began this way, give outsiders a way to detect one, or establish a measured parameter threshold. The existing CONSISTENCY-ONLY classification is the right one.
