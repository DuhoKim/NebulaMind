AUDIT_HOLDS_PROSPECT

## 1. Construction and the (10^{77}) count

The paper does not derive multi-production from an unrestricted first-order transition. It derives it inside a simplified construction and then extrapolates the construction to realistic domains.

The chain is:

1. True-vacuum bubbles nucleate in an exponentially expanding false vacuum; late overlap leaves trapped false-vacuum domains (lines 25–45). The background is specialized further by neglecting radiation relative to constant false-vacuum density, setting the true-vacuum energy density to zero, and using de Sitter expansion with (l=(8\pi G\rho_v/3)^{-1/2}) (lines 47–66).
2. The tractable trapped domain is an explicitly assumed spherical model: infinitely many bubbles nucleate simultaneously on one sphere and their walls move at light speed (lines 67–69, 93–100). Birkhoff's theorem supplies a Schwarzschild true-vacuum shell, with (M=(4\pi/3)\rho_v[R(\tau_0)X_0]^3) and (r_g=2GM=(aX_0)^3l) (lines 99–111). Equations (7)–(8) evolve its walls (lines 113–123). For (X_0<a^{-1}), the inner domain vanishes and leaves a black hole; for (X_0>a^{-1}), it expands indefinitely as a de Sitter-like sub-universe joined by an Einstein–Rosen bridge (lines 124–134).
3. Extension beyond spherical symmetry is not proved. The authors say the model's essential feature is “expected” to apply if nucleation is sufficiently small, argue heuristically that small domains make black holes and large ones make wormholes, and explicitly say general wormhole formation is “not exactly proved yet” (lines 135–160).
4. The statistical counting assumes a constant nucleation rate per four-volume, (\nu l^{-4}), valid when thermal nucleation is negligible and quantum nucleation dominates (lines 163–170). Equation (9) gives the false-vacuum fraction; equation (10) gives bubble density (lines 171–187). Flat-space percolation is then imported with (u_c\simeq0.3), producing (\tau_c-\tau_*\simeq \nu^{-1}l), a mean bubble spacing, and the assumed same-order trapped-domain diameter (r_-(\tau_c)=0.5(10/\nu)^{1/3}l) (lines 187–201). The paper itself defends, but does not derive, applicability of flat-space percolation and says the scenario does not depend on the precise (u_c) (lines 218–226).
5. Comparing that assumed typical size with the spherical solution makes most trapped domains wormholes when ((10/\nu)^{1/3}>1) (lines 202–206). Separately, solving the horizon/homogeneity problem requires a transition lasting over (60l); hence (\nu<1/60), which also satisfies their wormhole condition (lines 207–214).
6. A typical wormhole mass and evaporation time are equations (13)–(15), but contain an order-factor (\nu/10<k<1). With the stated GUT input (\rho_v=(10^{15}\,\mathrm{GeV})^4) and (\nu=1/60), (t_{ev}\sim3\times10^{-13}k^3\) s (lines 227–249). Junction relation (16) and equation (17) give completion at Schwarzschild time (t_c\sim10^{-10}) s, so evaporation/disconnection precedes completion (lines 250–270). Survival is conditional: the note says (t_{ev}\lesssim t_c) may be indispensable (lines 306–314).
7. At percolation, (n_f\simeq \nu/(30l^3)); multiplying by a disconnected universe's volume and assuming **all** false-vacuum domains become wormholes gives (p\simeq e^{3/\nu}/3) children per universe (lines 326–336). At (\nu=1/60), this is (e^{180}/3\). Numerically, \(\log_{10}p=180/\ln 10-\log_{10}3\simeq77.70\), or (p\simeq5.0\times10^{77}), consistent only at order-of-magnitude level with the paper's “(10^{77})” (lines 337–338).

Thus the huge count is parameterized and arithmetically reproducible, but exponentially dependent on the assumed constant (\nu), inherits percolation and typical-size estimates, and additionally assumes every trapped domain becomes a wormhole. The GUT scale enters the size, mass, and lifetime estimates, while the dimensionless count in equation (19) depends directly on (\nu), not explicitly on (\rho_v) or a separately inserted horizon volume.

## 2. Present child radius and identification of our universe

This is a derived consistency statement within stated inputs, not an identification. Equation (18) estimates the immediate post-transition radius as (L\lesssim(10/\nu)^{1/3}e^{1/\nu}l); for (\rho_v=(10^{15}\,\mathrm{GeV})^4) and (\nu=1/60), the authors report (L\sim10\) cm (lines 283–287). They then assume adiabatic temperature scaling from (10^{15}\) GeV to (10^{-4}) eV, a factor (10^{28}), giving a present scale of order (10\times10^{28}=10^{29}) cm, larger than the quoted (H_0^{-1}\sim10^{28}) cm (lines 287–295). This supports “large enough” and “no observational conflicts,” i.e. allowance. The later wording is explicitly modal: our universe “might have been born” as a child (lines 322–325). It neither uniquely identifies our universe as one nor supplies a discriminant between child and non-child origins.

## 3. The baryon-to-entropy flag

The exact logical claim is that black-hole and wormhole evaporation generates “a large amount of entropy,” which “reduces” the baryon-number-to-entropy ratio “considerably”; even if initially order unity, evaporation “may dilute the baryon excess too much to agree with observations” (lines 347–353). The proposed escape is that a new baryon-generation mechanism “might be necessary,” and validity awaits more precise particle-physics investigation (lines 353–358).

There is no entropy yield, final (n_B/s), dilution factor, numerical inequality against an observational value, or parameter region. The paper supplies a verbal sign—adding entropy at fixed/unmentioned baryon number lowers (n_B/s)—but not a derived amplitude. Even “large,” “considerably,” and “too much” are not attached to equations or bounds. Therefore this is a route to a possible conflict, not a number or calibrated directional prediction. Under the stated scheme, the lane may own a missing observational threshold but not this missing model output; a named observable with no amplitude remains **PROSPECT**. Calling the bare sign QUALITATIVE-DIRECTIONAL would over-credit a bookkeeping consequence whose required evaporation entropy was never calculated.

## 4. Other observation-facing content

The paper has conditional compact-object/topology statements but no present observable forecast. Small trapped domains are argued to leave black holes; large ones to form wormholes (lines 135–160). A typical wormhole mass is given parametrically by equation (13), (M_w\sim(k/8)(10/\nu)(4\pi\rho_vl^3/3)), with (\nu/10<k<1), and its GUT-scale evaporation time is equation (15) (lines 227–246). These objects are expected to evaporate/disconnect extremely early, before the electroweak transition (lines 247–269), not to form a calculated present relic population. The survival caveat in lines 306–314 further conditions the scenario.

No abundance or mass function of surviving black holes/wormholes is calculated, no present relic constraint is compared, and no gravitational-wave background, spectrum, peak frequency, or amplitude is mentioned. The opening references generic inflationary benefits—galaxy seeds, homogeneity, monopole suppression, and possible matter–antimatter domains (lines 14–24)—but the paper does not compute those observables for this multi-production construction. Its only explicit observation-facing checks are the child-radius consistency argument and the unquantified (n_B/s) concern.

## 5. Tier consequence

**PROSPECT holds.** The internal construction is substantially equation-backed once its idealizations are accepted, and the (10^{77}) count is a real parameterized estimate rather than a free assertion. But neither that count nor the radius check yields a distinctive observable: the radius only establishes compatibility. The sole named possible conflict, (n_B/s), has a sign in prose but lacks the entropy production needed to compute even a factor or inequality. There is consequently no calibrated falsifier, and the paper does not clear the missing-number requirement for QUALITATIVE-DIRECTIONAL. It is more than pure consistency discussion because it identifies a concrete observable route and mechanism, so CONSISTENCY-ONLY would discard relevant prospective content. A(a): retain at **PROSPECT**; no tier change.

In plain language: the paper really does build a mathematical toy model in which a slow first-order GUT transition can make an enormous number of child universes, and its quoted count follows from its chosen nucleation rate. But several bridges from the toy geometry to a realistic transition are assumptions, and the argument that our universe could be a child only says the size is not obviously wrong. Most importantly for the audit, the paper notices that evaporation entropy could wash out the baryon excess but never calculates how much. That is a promising route to an observational test, not yet an observational prediction, so Entry 47 should stay PROSPECT.
