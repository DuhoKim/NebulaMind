## 1. Threshold result

The EC Friedmann and conservation equations then use that effective source (lines 99–109). For the early universe the paper additionally assumes an ultrarelativistic fluid in kinetic equilibrium, \(\epsilon=h_*T^4\), \(p=\epsilon/3\), and \(n_f=h_{nf}T^3\), with fixed Standard Model state counts \(g_b=29\), \(g_f=90\) (lines 111–116). Thus the spin-density-squared closure is embedded in the effective \(-\alpha n_f^2\) source and the equilibrium \(n_f\propto T^3\) relation; these are model/microphysical closures, not results derived in this paper.
2. **Equations within those closures.** The conservation equation produces \(\dot a/a+\dot T/T=0\) (lines 120–132), hence \(xy=C\) (lines 170–176). The paper explicitly begins the relevant section with “Let us consider a closed relativistic universe” (lines 134–136), so it has already selected \(k=+1\). Substitution into that closed Friedmann equation gives \(\dot y^2+1=3C^4/y^2-2C^6/y^4\) (lines 164–181).
3. **Discriminant threshold.** Imposing the bounce/crunch turning-point condition \(\dot y=0\) gives \(y^4-3C^4y^2+2C^6=0\), whose roots contain \(\sqrt{9C^8-8C^6}\) (lines 186–203). Reality of those roots gives \(C\ge\sqrt{8/9}\), equivalently \(aT\ge\sqrt{8/9}\,a_{cr}T_{cr}\) (lines 205–220). Equality is stationary; expansion requires the strict inequality \(C>\sqrt{8/9}\) (lines 222–228, 337–343).

Therefore “closed” is **not derived** as a dynamical selection of \(k=+1\). The paper analyzes all three preselected curvature cases (lines 65–68), and the threshold is an existence/turning-point condition attached to the already assumed closed ansatz. Flat and open cases are separately obtained by putting the corresponding curvature term into the equations and have no analogous restriction (lines 346–393).
