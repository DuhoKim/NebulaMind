# Entry 55 deep audit

## 1. Construction

The paper studies the homogeneous Schwarzschild interior, with metric variables \(R,\Lambda\) and their conjugate momenta, but does not begin by quantizing that minisuperspace. Its QRLG route first partially gauge-fixes the full LQG kinematical Hilbert space, reduces spin networks and holonomy-flux operators, and evaluates the Hamiltonian constraint on coherent states peaked on spherically symmetric data. The authors say that this framework is equivalent to the action of full-theory operators only in the large-spin limit and that the present paper adds first subleading inverse-volume and coherent-state-spread terms to the earlier construction (lines 100–108). The actual base Hamiltonian derivation is imported from Alesci et al. 2018c: the paper explicitly says the first effective spherically symmetric Hamiltonian was derived there and that it will review, not reproduce, the reduced Hilbert-space construction (lines 319–341). The prior regularization choices are non-graph-changing Thiemann regularization, a fixed cuboidal graph with no sum over plaquette number, spin-1/2 holonomies, and a flux/Ricci-scalar Lorentzian term (lines 321–338).

The states are Gaussian coherent states with dimensionless spreads \(\delta_\ell\), and the expansion assumes semiclassical spins \(\tilde j_\ell\gg1\) (lines 403–439). The new calculation retains inverse-volume and coherent-state corrections, while neglecting coherent-state corrections to the Lorentzian term because that term is already subleading at the adopted order (line 443). Angular plaquette sums are replaced by integrals in the small-coordinate-cell limit (lines 465–477). Only after this full-theory-motivated construction do the authors impose the homogeneous interior foliation (lines 522–525; also 104–108).

State/graph dependence remains material. The authors explicitly call the predictions state-dependent and say the state choice is justified by geometrical demands (lines 527–531). A fixed cuboidal lattice supplies angular and longitudinal cell sizes and average spins (lines 535–575); an exterior/interior simplicity argument imposes \(j=\gamma j_x\) (lines 577–588); and three rescaled spread parameters \(\delta,\delta_x,\nu\) remain free subject to the peakedness condition (lines 592–621). Thus this is a conditional semiclassical, large-spin, fixed-graph/coherent-state effective construction, not an ambiguity-free solution of full LQG.

## 2. De Sitter result

The result is derived as a conditional asymptotic solution, not obtained from an unconstrained numerical scan. The authors posit Laurent-series asymptotics that match the de Sitter form, require the lapse corrections \(N_1=N_2=0\), and obtain \(\lambda=3/(N_0^2\ell^2)\) (lines 813–863). They then expand the constraint and evolution equations order by order. Two algebraic equations couple \(\xi\) and \(\gamma\) and “admit two sets of solutions” (lines 903–919); subleading equations force \(L_1=R_1=0\) (lines 921–927) and tune the remaining spread parameters (lines 929–944). Table 1 labels the model parameters as “tuned to bring about” de Sitter and gives the two branches \((\xi,\gamma)=(0.957,0.227)\) and \((0.974,0.274)\) (lines 946–969). The converse appendix is still conditional on the assumed Laurent form, and says more complicated solutions are possible and the leading form is motivated by prior numerics (lines 1227–1246); it concludes that the tuned solution is necessarily de Sitter under that ansatz and calls attractor behavior only “most likely” (lines 1352–1358).

The asymptotic metric is checked against the conformal-boundary and stress-tensor falloff criteria, rather than inferred merely from a curvature resemblance: the conformal metric is smooth and complete (lines 1034–1055), the effective stress tensor vanishes with the required powers (lines 1057–1069), and the paper therefore classifies it as asymptotically Schwarzschild–de Sitter.

The cosmological constant is not fixed. Equation (59) gives \(\lambda\propto1/(\ell_P^2j)\) (lines 994–1006), and on the black-hole-relevant branch it is
\[
\lambda=\frac{0.06}{\ell_P^2j}
\]
(lines 1087–1096). The remaining collective spin \(j\) is free within semiclassical bounds; the paper explicitly says this leaves \(\lambda\) “largely unconstrained” (lines 1098–1118). It is not tied to the black-hole mass by the derived effective dynamics. A mass dependence, \(\bar\lambda\sim c^4/(G^2m^2)\), appears only after a conjectured spin-renormalization rule (lines 1120–1138).

## 3. The \(\gamma\) coincidence

The de Sitter equations yield two rounded values, \(\gamma^{dS}_1\simeq0.227\) and \(\gamma^{dS}_2\simeq0.274\) (lines 1008–1012). The comparison values, attributed to Agullo et al. 2010, are \(\gamma^E_1\simeq0.237\) for the U(1) entropy calculation and \(\gamma^E_2\simeq0.274\) for SU(2) (lines 1014–1022); the cited SU(2) sources also include Engle et al. 2010 (lines 1551–1558). Arithmetic at the paper's reported precision is: SU(2) difference \(0.274-0.274=0.000\); U(1)-like difference \(0.237-0.227=0.010\), about \(4.2\%\) of 0.237. “Exact” therefore means equality of the displayed three-decimal numerical approximations, not an exhibited exact analytic identity.

Nor is 0.274 mathematically unique: the asymptotic equations admit the 0.227 branch too. The paper's numerical integrations near Schwarzschild horizon data select the positive-sine/0.274 branch as relevant to black-hole physics, while stating that 0.227 is not excluded by the dynamics (lines 1023–1029). The authors present the result as an independent derivation and a “striking coincidence”/confirmation of special physical meaning (lines 1025–1028), but also explicitly describe \(\gamma\) as precisely fine-tuning the trajectory to de Sitter (lines 134–135). It is therefore a conditional selection/consistency coincidence, not a parameter-free prediction: de Sitter is imposed as the target asymptotic character, several coherent-state parameters are tuned, two \(\gamma\) roots result, and near-horizon numerical branch relevance chooses 0.274.

## 4. Observation-facing content

The derived statement is about an expanding Kantowski–Sachs universe inside a black-hole event horizon: the singularity is replaced by a bounce and the interior approaches de Sitter (lines 1069–1071, 1163–1166). The paper does discuss the possibility that “our observable universe could have emerged from within the interior of a black hole” (lines 1148–1155), but does not derive that identification, an expansion history, or an observational relic.

Its only numerical bridge to our universe depends on the conjectured spin renormalization. Substituting the observed baryonic mass \(m\simeq1.46\times10^{53}\,\mathrm{kg}\) gives \(\bar\lambda\simeq0.85\times10^{-52}\,\mathrm{m}^{-2}\), compared with \(\lambda_{obs}\simeq1.1\times10^{-52}\,\mathrm{m}^{-2}\) (lines 1140–1144). Arithmetic: \(0.85/1.1=0.773\), so the estimate is about 23% low, appropriately only order-of-magnitude. But the authors say the microscopic ambiguity cannot currently be resolved (lines 1120–1122), introduce the rescaling as something one “could contemplate” (lines 1124–1138), and leave the open-system mechanism for future work (line 1146). This is not a calibrated comparison produced by the derived Hamiltonian.

## 5. Tier consequence

**CONSISTENCY-ONLY holds.** The paper derives a nontrivial conditional internal result: within a particular QRLG effective Hamiltonian, coherent-state/lattice prescription, large-spin expansion, asymptotic ansatz, and tuned spread parameters, one black-hole-relevant branch has \(\gamma\simeq0.274\), matching the rounded SU(2) entropy value. That is a genuine cross-calculation consistency check, but it neither predicts a fixed \(\Lambda\) nor tests an identification with our universe. The derived \(\Lambda=0.06/(\ell_P^2j)\) contains a free number, and the observed-\(\Lambda\) estimate requires an un-derived renormalization law plus the hypothesis that our universe lies behind a horizon. Under A(a), this cannot be promoted to QUALITATIVE-DIRECTIONAL, PROSPECT, or CALIBRATED-FALSIFIER: the lane is missing not merely a threshold but the dynamical value of \(j\), a derived mapping to our universe, and an observable prediction. It does make a relevant theoretical identification rather than wholly missing corpus membership, so AUDIT_FLAG_MEMBERSHIP is not warranted.

In plain language: the paper really does calculate a de Sitter-like future for a specially selected quantum-corrected black-hole interior, and the black-hole-relevant solution lands on the same displayed value, 0.274, used in SU(2) black-hole entropy. But that match comes after demanding de Sitter behavior and tuning state parameters, another mathematical branch exists, and the size of the resulting cosmological constant still depends on a free spin. Connecting it to the measured cosmological constant of our universe requires an additional speculative renormalization mechanism and the assumption that our universe is such an interior. The result is therefore an interesting internal consistency coincidence, not an observational prediction.
