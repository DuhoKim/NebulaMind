**Summary of what is original here:** Theorems 1–3 are recalled from ARMA 1994 [7] and Israel [1]. Theorems 4–13 (Thm 10 as a sketch) and Lemmas 1–3 are proved here. The genuine content is (a) the closed-form pressure p₊ (3.9), (b) the autonomous ODE system (3.22)–(3.23) for the A>0 outgoing shock, (c) the shock-speed and sound-speed formulas, (d) the Lax/subluminal admissibility region h₋<h<h₊, (e) the exclusions of p₋ and of implosions, and (f) the local freedom of equations of state (Thm 11). The stated purpose is numerical: "The resulting formulas are important for the numerical simulation of these solutions" (l.18–19, repeated l.43–44).

**Scope restriction that matters for the branch:** "In this paper we restrict our attention to the case when the shock wave lies outside of the Schwarzschild radius (A>0; cf. (2.21) below)" (l.41–42). The entire admissibility analysis is conditioned on A>0 (Thm 8 remark l.1512–1518: "the only physically interesting case left when A>0 is when p=p₊ and z=ρ̄/ρ<1, the case of an explosion when the FRW metric is on the inside"). The interior-of-the-Schwarzschild-radius regime (A<0), which is where a "universe inside a black hole" reading would have to live, is out of scope of every theorem in this paper.

## 2. Physical interpretation offered — does the 1997 paper make a cosmological claim of its own?

**No cosmological claim is asserted as a result. Cosmology appears only as motivational framing in the abstract and introduction, and the one explicit cosmogonic sentence is attributed to the earlier paper [8].** Quotes:

- Abstract, l.11–12: FRW is parenthetically "(a cosmological model for the expanding universe)" and OT "(a model for the interior of a star)". The paper's own description of what it does: "demonstrate that our theory generates a large class of physically meaningful (Lax-admissible) outgoing shock waves that model blast waves in a general relativistic setting" (l.15–16).
- Intro, l.45–47: "The FRW metric … is generally accepted as a cosmological model for the expanding universe. The OT solution is a time-independent solution which models the interior of a star."
- Intro, l.49–53 (the strongest interpretive passage): "In our shock-wave solution, the FRW metric is an exploding inner core (of a star or the universe as a whole), and the boundary of this inner core is a shock surface that is driven by the expansion behind the shock into the outer, static, OT solution, which we imagine as the outer layers of a star, or the outer regions of the universe." — This is a dual reading ("of a star or the universe"), offered with "we imagine", not a claim that the actual universe is such an object.
- Intro, l.53–55, in parentheses and attributed to [8]: "(In the exact solution constructed in [8], the shock wave emerges from r̄=0 at the initial (big-bang) singularity in the FRW metric, and thus the model provides a scenario by which the big-bang begins with a shock-wave explosion.)" — "a scenario", credited to PRD 1995, not derived or defended here.
- Intro, l.23–25: [8] "models an explosion into a static, singular, isothermal sphere. This solution provides a general relativistic model that parallels a Newtonian model for stellar formation." — the astrophysical, not cosmological, framing of the companion paper.
- The paper's own interpretive conclusions are about the MODEL's admissible solution set, not about the universe: "our solutions model explosions, but not gravitational collapse (except in the Oppenheimer-Snyder limit p=0)" (l.107); "This demonstrates that system (3.22), (3.23) generates a large set of physically meaningful shock-wave solutions of the Einstein equations that model explosions" (l.2313–2315); "the equation of state can be freely assigned at a point for sufficiently strong shocks" (l.2482–2483).

Searched for and absent: "Hubble" (zero hits), "observ" (zero hits), any statement that the Hubble flow is the after-effect of an explosion, any statement that the universe is inside a black hole, any redshift/CMB/age statement. The words "universe" occur only in l.11, 46, 50, 53, and 439 ("not outside the FRW universe" — a coordinate-patch remark). Adversarial reading in the other direction: even the "universe as a whole" framing at l.50 is undermined by the paper's own A>0 restriction (l.41–42), which places the shock outside the Schwarzschild radius of the enclosed mass; a "universe inside a black hole" is the opposite regime and is not treated. ## 3. Observation-facing content — any number an observation could contradict?

**None.** There is no numerical value anywhere in the paper except the normalization c=1 (l.129), the curvature sign k∈{1,−1,0} (l.171–173), and the check-point z=1/3 used to compare with [8] (l.3785–3797). No H₀, no T₀, no density, no shock radius, no age, no redshift. The theory is shown to be maximally flexible rather than predictive:
- Free data: "we are free to choose two initial conditions r̄ and R … we can determine local shock wave solutions by arbitrarily assigning the OT equation of state, as well as r̄, ρ̄, M and one of r or R (because Rr=r̄), thus allowing four initial conditions in all" (l.1755–1763).
- Theorem 11 and its remarks (l.2341–2346, l.2482–2486): for strong shocks the equations of state on both sides can be prescribed independently at a point; the sign of k is then fixed by the relative size of A₀ and h₀ (l.2484–2485) — i.e. even the curvature sign is an output of freely chosen initial data, not a prediction.
- The only sharp, in-principle testable statements are internal consistency results: outgoing shocks must have ρ>ρ̄ and p>p̄ (Thms 5, 9, 13), shocks must be subluminal (Thm 8), and Lax-admissible (Thms 9, 10). Nothing here confronts a measurement; these are constraints on which mathematical solutions count as "physical".

**Plain language.** This 1997 paper is a mathematics paper about how to glue two well-known solutions of Einstein's equations — an expanding Friedmann universe on the inside and a static star-like fluid on the outside — along a moving shock front, and about when that glued object counts as a genuine, physically sensible explosion (shock moves slower than light, pressure and density higher behind it, the usual entropy-type conditions hold). It writes down the differential equations that govern the front, works out formulas for the pressure, shock speed and sound speed, and proves that explosions outward are allowed while implosions inward are not. It does not say the universe is inside a black hole; it deliberately confines itself to shocks outside the Schwarzschild radius, which is the opposite situation. It does not say the Hubble expansion came from an explosion; it mentions that idea in one bracketed sentence as a "scenario" from an earlier paper. It contains no number that a telescope could check, and it actually proves that the model is flexible enough to absorb almost any equation of state you feed it. So it is the toolbox the later cosmology papers reach for, nothing more, and the CONSISTENCY-ONLY tier is exactly right — with the caveat that the toolbox was built for the outside-the-horizon case, so the later papers own the step of taking it inside.
