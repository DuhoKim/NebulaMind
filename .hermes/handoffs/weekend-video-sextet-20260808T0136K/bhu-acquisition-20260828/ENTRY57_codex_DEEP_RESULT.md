## 1. The theorems: hypotheses, conclusions, and proof provenance

The paper contains thirteen numbered theorems. It does **not** prove all of the foundational matching results anew: Theorems 1–3 are explicitly recalled from earlier work, while Theorems 5–13 are derived or proved here (Theorem 4 is an immediate synthesis of the recalled results and the constraint calculation).

1. **Theorem 1 (recalled, not proved here in full).** Given any FRW and OT metrics for which the mass-matching shock surface \(M(\bar r)=4\pi\rho(t)\bar r^3/3\) is locally defined, assuming positive OT sound-speed derivative \(d\bar p/d\bar\rho>0\), exterior location \(A(\bar r_0)>0\), and a valid FRW coordinate position \(1-kr_0^2>0\), there is a locally regular one-to-one coordinate map under which the metrics match Lipschitz-continuously across the shock; sphere areas agree and the shock position is \(r(t)=\bar r(t)/R(t)\) (lines 395–454). The text says this “summarizes the result in Section 4 of [7]” (lines 393–395).

2. **Theorem 2 (recalled, not proved here in full).** For a smooth three-dimensional shock surface with spacelike normal and a metric smooth on each side and Lipschitz across it, the following are equivalent: continuity of extrinsic curvature; absence of delta-function curvature sources; existence of local \(C^{1,1}\) coordinates making the metric \(C^{1,1}\); and existence of a locally Lorentzian frame obtained by such a transformation. Any of them implies the Rankine–Hugoniot conditions (lines 461–497). The paper explicitly says this general theorem “is proved in [7, Theorem 4]” (lines 458–461).

3. **Theorem 3 (recalled, not proved here in full).** For two spherically symmetric Lorentz metrics matched Lipschitz-continuously across a non-null shock, with matching symmetry-sphere area function and nonzero normal derivative of that function, \([G^{ij}]n_i=0\), \([G^{ij}]n_i n_j=0\), and \([K]=0\) are equivalent to \(C^{1,1}\) regularity in Gaussian normal coordinates (lines 498–553). It is expressly attributed to “[7, Proposition 9]” (lines 497–498).

4. **Theorem 4 (established here as a synthesis).** If FRW and OT metrics match Lipschitz-continuously on the mass-matching surface and conditions (2.20)–(2.22) hold, then the four regularity/conservation statements in Theorem 2 hold iff algebraic constraint (2.30) holds on the shock (lines 710–718). The constraint is derived in this paper at lines 650–704, although its equivalent form had already been obtained in [8] (lines 603–616).

5. **Theorems 5 and 6 (proved here).** Under (2.20)–(2.22), exterior-to-interior density ratio \(z=\bar\rho/\rho<1\), and the positive-pressure branch \(p_+\): (i) \(p_+>0\) iff \(p_+>\bar p\) iff \(h_1<h<1\); and (ii), for fixed positive \(\bar\rho,\bar p,\rho\), \(p_+\) ranges monotonically from \(\bar p\) to infinity as \(h\) ranges from 1 to \(h_1\) (lines 958–984). Their joint proof is given at lines 985–1059.

6. **Theorem 7 (proved here).** Fix an OT solution with positive equation-of-state derivative. Any Lipschitz FRW–OT matching satisfying (2.21), (2.22), (2.36), and Rankine–Hugoniot must obey one of the two displayed autonomous shock-ODE systems; conversely, any smooth solution of either system satisfying those inequalities determines an FRW solution with the stated density and pressure, Lipschitz matching, and Rankine–Hugoniot conservation (lines 1288–1314). The reverse derivation immediately preceding the statement supplies the proof (lines 1248–1288).

7. **Theorem 8 (proved here).** For the \(p_+\) branch, inequalities (3.32) and (3.33) are each equivalent to subluminal shock speed (lines 1452–1480). This follows directly from the invariant speed formula (3.30) and its algebraic reduction (lines 1410–1479).

8. **Theorem 9 and Corollary 1 (proved here).** Given a smooth positive OT solution on an interval, with \(0<\bar p/\bar\rho<1\), \(0<d\bar p/d\bar\rho<1\), and mean enclosed density \(3M/(4\pi\bar r^3)>\bar\rho\), initial data in the interval generate an outgoing shock satisfying subluminal speed, \(p>\bar p\), \(\rho>\bar\rho\), \(0<p/\rho<1\), and the OT-Lax condition on the maximal subinterval where \(h_-<h<h_+\) (lines 2106–2188). The corollary gives the simpler sufficient bound (3.82) (lines 2189–2203). The proof invokes Theorem 5 and the earlier inequalities and is supplied at lines 2204–2220.

9. **Theorem 10 (proved/derived here).** Assuming (2.20)–(2.22), \(p=p_+\), fixed dimensionless \(\bar\lambda,\bar\sigma,A,h\) in the stated geometric range and satisfying (3.79), sufficiently small \(z=\bar\rho/\rho\) implies \(0<\sigma<1\), \(p>\bar p\), and both FRW- and OT-Lax conditions (lines 2272–2284). The strong-shock asymptotics motivating and establishing this statement are at lines 2245–2275, and after the sound-speed derivation the authors call it a “direct consequence” of formulas (3.86)–(4.43) (lines 3835–3849).

10. **Theorem 11 (proved here).** For arbitrary local target values \(0<\bar\sigma_0,\bar\lambda_0,\sigma_0,\lambda_0<1\) and \(z_0,h_0\in(0,1)\) obeying relation (3.90) and \(h_0<1-\bar\sigma\), there exists \(A_0\in(0,1)\) making the sound-speed relation (3.92) hold (lines 2364–2412). The paper interprets this as freedom to prescribe local equations of state for sufficiently strong shocks (lines 2413–2473); the proof, via Lemma 3 and continuity as \(A\) varies, appears at lines 2474–2524, with Lemma 3 proved at lines 2525–2624.

11. **Theorem 12 (proved here in full).** The FRW sound speed \(\sigma=dp/d\rho\) determined by the shock equations equals formula (3.83)/(4.41) (lines 2625–2640). The detailed differentiation and reduction occupy lines 2641–3825.

12. **Theorem 13 (proved here in full).** Under (2.20)–(2.22), a \(p_+\) shock with \(\bar\rho>\rho\) cannot have positive FRW sound speed; indeed \(\sigma<0\) (lines 3850–3860). The sign argument is completed at lines 4151–4274. Together with the superluminal \(p_-\) branch, this leaves the physically admissible exterior-Schwarzschild regime as an outgoing explosion with denser FRW material inside (lines 1481–1494).

The Oppenheimer–Snyder limit is discussed but not separately numbered as a theorem here. For \(k>0\), choosing vacuum Schwarzschild outside makes the general ODEs reproduce the OS equation of state \(p\equiv0\); hence these shocks generalize OS to nonzero pressure, though OS has a reversible contact discontinuity rather than a true irreversible shock (lines 94–116).

## 2. Physical interpretation

The paper is not purely mathematical and does not defer every cosmological interpretation to later work. It calls FRW the generally accepted model of the expanding universe and says that in the matched solution the FRW region is “an exploding inner core (of a star or the universe as a whole),” with the shock driven into a static OT exterior imagined as either stellar layers or “the outer regions of the universe” (lines 65–76). It then reports that the exact solution of [8] has the shock emerge at the big-bang singularity and “provides a scenario by which the big-bang begins with a shock-wave explosion” (lines 76–80). Thus the cosmological claim offered here is an explosion-origin scenario for the expansion, albeit expressly via the prior exact solution [8].

There is **no universe-inside-a-black-hole claim** in this paper. In fact, its analysis is expressly restricted to shocks outside the Schwarzschild radius, \(A>0\) (lines 51–52), and the theorem assumptions repeatedly enforce that restriction (for example lines 417–424 and 3850–3859).

The paper's own new physical conclusion is structural: outside the Schwarzschild radius, positive sound speed forces the FRW density to exceed the OT density, so the models describe explosions rather than gravitational collapse except in the zero-pressure OS limit (lines 122–128; theorem proved at lines 3850–4274). It also claims a “large set” of physically meaningful, Lax-admissible explosion solutions for sufficiently strong shocks (lines 2264–2284).

## 3. Observation-facing content

There is no observation-facing numerical prediction here. The displayed numbers are mathematical bounds and dimensionless admissibility inequalities—positive/sub-light sound and shock speeds, density and pressure ordering, Lax inequalities, and ranges of free initial data—not a fixed redshift, distance, Hubble constant, age, angular scale, luminosity, or other measured quantity. The paper emphasizes that one may choose the OT equation of state and four local initial conditions to obtain local shock solutions (lines 1755–1774), and Theorem 11 further demonstrates local equation-of-state freedom for strong shocks (lines 2285–2305 and 2413–2473). Consequently, no definite number is supplied that an astronomical observation could contradict. What could fail are internal physical-consistency conditions for a chosen member of the solution family, not a corpus-level empirical prediction.

## 4. Tier consequence

**CONSISTENCY-ONLY holds.** The paper proves a substantial mathematical existence/admissibility framework: FRW–OT shock matching, conservation, autonomous shock dynamics, subluminal and Lax regimes, equation-of-state constraints, and an exclusion theorem. It also attaches a qualitative explosion/big-bang interpretation. But it neither calibrates a member of the free-initial-data family to observations nor derives a unique observation-facing sign, scale, or number. The qualitative interpretation therefore does not promote the entry to QUALITATIVE-DIRECTIONAL in the audit taxonomy: its tested content remains conditional—*if* the stated equations of state, initial data, density contrast, and inequalities are chosen, *then* a physically admissible outgoing shock exists. It is appropriately retained as the theorem base/supporting consistency result for the branch, so **AUDIT_FLAG_MEMBERSHIP is not warranted**. The branch may later acquire an empirical threshold, but this paper supplies no missing observational number to which such a threshold could be applied.

In plain language: this is rigorous work showing that a broad family of relativistic explosion shocks can be made mathematically and physically self-consistent, and it explicitly connects that picture to a possible big-bang shock explosion. What it does not do is tell us which member of that family is our universe or predict a measurable value that the sky must exhibit. It therefore supports the possibility of the model, not an observational test of it.
