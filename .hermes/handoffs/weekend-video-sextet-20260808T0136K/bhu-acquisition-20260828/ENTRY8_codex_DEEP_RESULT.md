## 1. Physical inequivalence and the status of the interior

The paper does not establish that the Einstein–Rosen construction is merely another chart on the maximally extended Schwarzschild manifold. It first notes that the isotropic metric is invariant under the inversion (r\mapsto r_g^2/(16r)), is asymptotically flat at both (r\to\infty) and (r\to0), and treats the two Schwarzschild exterior sheets as joined at the degenerate isotropic-coordinate surface (r=r_g/4) (lines 39–47). For (r>r_g/4), the areal-radius transformation explicitly gives the standard Schwarzschild exterior (line 57); for (r<r_g/4), equivalence to the other exterior sheet follows from the inversion symmetry, not from an extension into Schwarzschild region II (lines 58–60). The geodesic equations transform accordingly: decreasing (r) on the second sheet becomes increasing (r'), interpreted as outgoing motion from a white hole (lines 104–117).

The global distinction is imposed through a different identification/gluing. In ordinary maximal Schwarzschild, an infaller goes from region I to region II and reaches the singularity, and cannot causally pass from region I to region III (lines 174–177, 224). The proposed bridge instead identifies the two future-horizon events (B) and (C), after which the curve proceeds into region III (lines 226–244). Thus the paper's physical-inequivalence claim is conditional on treating Rindler's elliptic horizon identification as physical (line 244), not a consequence of a coordinate transformation alone.

“Regular” requires qualification. Away from the throat, the displayed Kretschmann scalar is finite and tends to zero at both asymptotic ends (lines 48–56), and region III has no curvature singularity (lines 243–246). But the paper later explicitly says the bridge is vacuum only away from (r=r_g/4), requires a divergent, energy-condition-violating stress tensor there, and has a delta-function curvature contribution at the throat (lines 258–269). It therefore does not prove a smooth, everywhere-regular vacuum interior. It proves two locally Schwarzschild exterior regions plus a non-vacuum distributional throat and a stipulated global identification.

## 2. Raychaudhuri, discontinuity, and the junction

The expansion scalar is explicitly calculated from the radial congruence. Equation (15) contains \(\operatorname{sgn}(1-r_g/(4r))\), so its one-sided values jump from \(-3/(2r_g)\) to \(+3/(2r_g)\) at the throat (lines 162–170). In that limited sense the discontinuity is derived from the chosen piecewise geometry, rather than simply asserted. But it is inseparable from the inversion/gluing at the degenerate isotropic surface: the paper's regular Kruskal description still requires identifying the two antipodal future horizons (lines 229–244), and its own field-equation discussion assigns a singular lightlike-brane source and delta-function curvature to that throat (lines 258–269). It is consequently not evidence that a smooth Schwarzschild horizon intrinsically produces a discontinuity in a congruence scalar.

The paper states that the jump prevents focusing to \(-\infty\) and “guarantees” timelike geodesic completeness (lines 168–172). The calculation shows absence of the particular Schwarzschild caustic along the studied infalling radial congruence, while the global continuation and infinite proper time additionally rely on the elliptic identification and continuation into nonsingular region III (lines 229–246). The paper does not present Israel junction conditions. It does, however, directly acknowledge and write a distributional field-equation source at the junction, including divergent stress, energy-condition violation, and a delta-function curvature term (lines 258–269). Those statements materially limit any reading of the jump as a benign coordinate-patching artefact or as a general completeness proof independent of the assumed global construction.

## 3. Distant versus interior observers

Exterior indistinguishability is derived at the metric level: the (r>r_g/4) sheet transforms to the standard Schwarzschild exterior (line 57), and the discussion calls the two solutions equivalent on the static exterior sheet while different in their proposed interiors (lines 248–251). The infaller asymptotically approaches the horizon in exterior coordinate time, so exterior observers never see the post-horizon reversal (lines 89–97, 232–235).

The paper expressly concedes the observational consequence: distant observers see only the exterior sheet, so they cannot satisfactorily determine the interior; an observer would have to enter or reside there (lines 271–273). It supplies theoretical statements about such an infaller—finite proper time to and across the throat (lines 98–105), continuation toward the white-hole exterior (lines 117–120), and ultimately unbounded proper time in region III (lines 243–246)—but no operationally specified measurement, signal returned to the exterior, or exterior discriminant. Hence the paper contains no presently calibrated test of the proposed interior.

## 4. “New universe” and torsion

The new-universe claim is a suggestion, not a result derived by the calculations in this Letter. The abstract labels it as suggested (lines 24–25). The discussion offers only a time-reversal analogy between gravitational collapse and an exploding white hole resembling an expanding universe (lines 274–275), cites possible cosmological advantages (line 276), and explicitly leaves collapse models, Schwarzschild–de Sitter generalization, and rotating cases for future study (line 277). No scale factor, expansion history, matter content, cosmological curvature evolution, matching to observations, or numerical prediction for a daughter universe is calculated.

The paper works within general relativity: it discusses the Einstein equations, vacuum Schwarzschild sheets, and a distributional lightlike-brane throat source (lines 258–269). No torsion mechanism is invoked or derived. The “our Universe” proposal is therefore speculative extrapolation from the white-hole-sheet interpretation, not a torsion-supported cosmology in this paper.

## 5. Tier consequence

The tier remains **CONSISTENCY-ONLY**. The paper gives a concrete mathematical construction and radial-geodesic analysis, so it supports logical/mathematical possibility under stated assumptions. But the crucial global outcome is conditional on accepting an elliptic identification (lines 229–244), while the throat requires singular exotic stress rather than being an everywhere-regular vacuum bridge (lines 258–269). Most decisively for tiering, the exterior is exactly Schwarzschild and the paper says distant observations cannot determine the interior (lines 250–252, 271–273). It supplies neither an externally observable directional signature nor a measurable threshold, number, or falsification criterion. The cosmological extension is expressly suggestive and deferred (lines 274–277). This does not reach QUALITATIVE-DIRECTIONAL, PROSPECT, or CALIBRATED-FALSIFIER.

In plain language: the paper shows how an infalling particle can avoid the Schwarzschild singularity if one replaces the usual black-hole interior with a second exterior sheet and identifies selected horizon points. That is a genuinely different global construction, not just a change of coordinates, and the calculation is internally informative. But the join carries a singular exotic source, the completeness argument depends on that join and identification, and an outside observer sees exactly the usual Schwarzschild exterior. The “new universe” idea is therefore an interesting possibility raised by the construction, not an observational result or a worked cosmology, so the consistency-only classification holds.
