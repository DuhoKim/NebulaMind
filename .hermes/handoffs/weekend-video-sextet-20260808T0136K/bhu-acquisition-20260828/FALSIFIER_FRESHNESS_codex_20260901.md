# Falsifier freshness check — 2026-09-01

Scope: bounded search for late-2025/2026 measurements or constraints that cross either live calibrated bar. “Secure” in Q1 means an identified neutron star with a well-constrained radio/optical dynamical mass, not a compact-object classification inferred from gravitational waves or an equation-of-state model. Null means no qualifying result found in the searched 2026 literature/data.

## Q1 — entry 31, Smolin cosmological natural selection

**Bar:** a securely identified neutron star at or above **2.50 M_sun**.

**Current best secure value:** **PSR J0952−0607, 2.35 ± 0.11 M_sun**. Romani et al. used new 2025 Keck/LRIS photometry plus revised companion radial-velocity/light-curve modelling; arXiv submission **4 December 2025** (arXiv:2512.05099). This is a radio-identified pulsar with an optical dynamical mass, although—as a black-widow solution—it remains modelled rather than a clean Shapiro-delay mass. Source: [Romani et al., “PSR J0952−0607: Tightening a Record-High Neutron Star Mass”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.05099).

**New late-2025/2026 qualifying data:** **null**. The bounded search found no newer secure radio/optical dynamical measurement whose central value is ≥2.50 M_sun, much less one securely bounded above that value. A useful negative check is the 2026 report on the putative very massive pulsar PSR J1748−2021B: its project page quotes a model-derived **2.74 ± 0.20 M_sun** candidate estimate but states that the companion was not detected and therefore the mass could not be constrained or the claim demonstrated. It is not a secure bar crossing. Source (page current in 2026; no publication date displayed): [“Confirming the most massive neutron star with observations of its companion”](https://oa.tib.eu/renate/items/11253e5e-fed3-4969-ad0c-2c615ae3b954).

GW190814 remains excluded from the secure column: its **2.50–2.67 M_sun** secondary is a compact object whose neutron-star versus black-hole identity is unresolved, so its mass interval does not securely identify a neutron star. No 2026 result located in this check resolves that classification.

**Margin to bar:** central-value gap **0.15 M_sun**, or **1.36σ** using the reported ±0.11 uncertainty. Thus the secure central estimate remains below 2.50 M_sun; no fired-falsifier event.

## Q2 — entry 51, Popławski density/mass floor

**Bar/current floor:** Popławski’s stated minimum black-hole mass is approximately **10^16 kg** (approximately **5.6 × 10^39 TeV/c²**). A qualifying crossing would be an actual black hole securely detected below that mass, not merely a model-specific non-observation.

**New 2026 microscopic-BH constraints:** yes, but both are **null searches and do not move/cross the floor**.

- CMS, submitted **12 April 2026** and published as JHEP 08 (2026) 098: no excess in 138 fb⁻¹ at 13 TeV. In large-extra-dimension benchmark models it excludes semiclassical black holes below **8.4–11.4 TeV** at 95% CL (depending on model), extending the prior reach by 1–1.6 TeV; model-independent cross-section limits improve by about a factor of four. Source: [CMS EXO-24-028 official result](https://cms-results.web.cern.ch/cms-results/public-results/publications/EXO-24-028/).

- ATLAS, submitted **21 April 2026**: no significant excess in 164 fb⁻¹ of 13.6-TeV Run-3 data; benchmark quantum-black-hole production limits reach **9.4 TeV** at 95% CL, described as the strongest such exclusions to date. Source: [ATLAS Collaboration, arXiv:2604.19495](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19495).

These collider limits are theory-dependent production exclusions (extra-dimensional/quantum-BH benchmarks), not detections or universal lower bounds on four-dimensional black-hole mass. Even the largest quoted threshold, 11.4 TeV, is about **4.9 × 10^38** below the stated 10^16-kg floor in mass-energy, so it supplies no bar crossing.

**New 2026 PBH-window constraint bearing directly on ~10^16 kg:** **null** in this bounded search. The 2026 review submitted **9 January 2026**, revised **1 April 2026**, summarizes the asteroid-mass window as roughly **10^−17–10^−10 M_sun** (about 2 × 10^13–2 × 10^20 kg), which contains 10^16 kg, and says it remains the only unconstrained monochromatic PBH region. That is a current synthesis, not a new detection or a floor-crossing constraint. Source: [“Primordial black holes: constraints, potential evidence and prospects,” arXiv:2601.06024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06024); [published review](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40766-026-00080-z).

**Margin/status:** no measured black-hole mass exists on the below-floor side, so there is no empirical crossing margin to quote. The nearest new collider *search scale* cited here is not commensurate with the floor and lies ~38.7 decades lower in mass-energy; the PBH window still spans the floor without a detection. Entry 51 therefore remains live and unmoved.

## Determination

No late-2025/2026 secure neutron-star measurement reaches 2.50 M_sun, and no 2026 microscopic/PBH result securely detects a black hole below Popławski’s floor. The newer CMS and ATLAS results strengthen model-dependent null constraints only. **BARS_UNMOVED; no tier change.**
