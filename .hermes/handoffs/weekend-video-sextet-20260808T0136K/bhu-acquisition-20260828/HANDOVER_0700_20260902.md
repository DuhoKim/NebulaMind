## What actually got established

from Ω_Λ = 0.69 — measured from supernovae and BAO, *not* from the CMB correlation being tested — and
gets ~57°. Non-circular. A gate charged that the paper's own numbers give 22°; **I checked and that
charge is wrong** (it substituted a different quantity for the causal scale). The "one real a-priori
prediction" label holds.

**2. The cutoff cannot be calibrated, in the author's own words.** There is no model of the initial
conditions in the paper — he writes that quantifying it is "impossible without a model for the
initial conditions," and the paper he cites for one resolves to "In preparation." Three prior
attempts didn't fail for lack of effort; there is nothing there to complete.

**3. THE SURVIVING RESULT — and it is now gated.** The paper claims CMB temperature "should not be
correlated above 60°." Its own most favourable implementation leaves **six times more correlation
than is actually observed**. So the causal cut gives a *partial* suppression, not the disappearance
it asserts. The gate calls this **conservative**: including the late ISW and lensing effects the
paper omits would *add* large-angle power and **widen** the gap, not close it.

**What I had to withdraw.** I also claimed the cutoff moves the anomaly's odds "from ~0.1% to at most
~3%." The **"at most" is retracted** — the gate broke it using my own printed table, where a smoothed
cut suppresses the statistic *more* (6,113) than the hard cut (6,897). I had checked one column and
stated a conclusion the column beside it contradicted.

**What is still open against point 3:** the observed number comes from a masked sky while my
simulation is full-sky; ΛCDM transfer functions may be inconsistent with a strict causal boundary;
and deepest — a sharp cut in Fourier space mathematically *guarantees* correlation above 60°, so
testing this real-space claim in Fourier space is partly an artifact. Fixing that last one means
building the real-space version, which is exactly what I did not build.
