## What actually got established

**1. The corpus's 60° prediction is sound as a scale prediction.** The paper derives its causal
scale from Ω_Λ = 0.69, which is measured from supernovae and BAO — *not* from the CMB correlation
being tested — and gets ~57°. Non-circular, and it survives the check. A gate seat charged that the
paper's own numbers give 22°; **I checked and that charge is wrong** (it substituted a different
quantity for the causal scale). The corpus's "one real a-priori prediction" label holds.

**2. The cutoff cannot be calibrated, and now we know why in the author's own words.** The paper
contains no model of the initial conditions — the author writes that quantifying it is "impossible
without a model for the initial conditions," and the paper he cites for one resolves to "In
preparation." So three prior attempts didn't fail from lack of effort; there is nothing there to
complete.

**3. But the paper makes a second claim that needs no completion — and it doesn't hold up.** It says
CMB temperature "should not be correlated above 60°." Its own most favourable implementation leaves
**six times more correlation than is actually observed**. So the causal cut gives a *partial*
suppression, not the disappearance it asserts. In probability terms it improves the odds of the
observed sky from ~0.1% to at most ~3% — a real effect, roughly 25×, that stops well short of making
the observation ordinary.

**That third point is the closest thing to a publishable result this program has produced.** It is
also completely ungated.
