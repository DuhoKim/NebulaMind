## 1. Does phase (b) change the evidential status of the 60° cutoff? In what direction?

Yes, in one specific way, and the packet under-states it. The 08-31 filing reads "**NOT yet calibrated**: the papers supply no cutoff amplitude / C_l threshold (the model owns that missing proof)" (BIB L888, L900). The word "yet" implies a calibration that is pending. Program (A) turned "pending" into "not obtainable from the papers as written": FM L14–15 ("the paper supplies no perturbation prescription at all, so every route to a number passes through choices external to the theory"), FM L48–49 (three seats `READING_C`, one `CLASS_REFUTED`). So the *calibration prospect* has moved DOWN — from "candidate awaiting a number" to "candidate that cannot be completed without a perturbation model the author still owes" (FM L41–42: the initial-conditions reference resolves to "In preparation").

What phase (b) did NOT change: the directional consistency. The sky's large-angle deficit was already known and already listed as consistent on 08-31 (BIB L888 "consistent with Planck's low quadrupole + the >60 deg S_1/2 large-angle deficit"). The new numbers (REC L16–20) show that lane-built completions move the sky from 0.15–0.20% to at most 2.2–2.8% — an improvement attributable to *our* completion, not the theory (FM L122–123). That is a note on the record, not new evidence for or against the papers. Net: status changes slightly against the "calibrated falsifier" prospect and is neutral on the directional claim. Any annotation must therefore replace "NOT yet calibrated" with "NOT CALIBRATABLE from the papers (Program A, 2026-09-02)".

## 2. Is QUALITATIVE-DIRECTIONAL the right label? Does the scheme need a new one?

QUALITATIVE-DIRECTIONAL fits by the letter of the scheme: "a predicted direction/sign or scale without a calibrated amplitude" (brief L19). This claim is exactly "direction + scale, no amplitude". I checked whether the a-priori 57.4° scale could on its own carry CALIBRATED-FALSIFIER ("a definite number with a threshold the observation can hit or miss", brief L18): it cannot, because with no amplitude there is no miss condition — any residual correlation beyond 60° can be called "small enough", and L435 of the source (quoted FM L54–56) explicitly allows uncorrelated temperature differences beyond θ§ without saying how uncorrelated. No re-tier; no new label. The scheme already separates the CLAIM's shape (tier) from its STANDING and WARRANT (BIB L66–71), and that is where the "amplitude provably free" fact belongs — in the note, not in a fifth tier.

## 3. Under option (b), whose falsifier would it be, and what would FIRED/LIVE mean?

The lane's, entirely — both the number and the threshold. The amplitude would come from Reading A (FM §4 title: "a number, set by an unlicensed convention", FM L80) or the Reading B construction, which are mutually exclusive lane choices (FM §3 title, L62); the papers supply neither (FM L48–49). The lane's threshold rule (brief L23–24) lets the lane supply a missing *threshold* on an author-supplied *number*; here the number itself is missing, and stretching the rule to supply the prediction would be the lane writing the paper's physics for it.

FIRED would mean: "our completion, under standard ΛCDM transfer physics plus an infrared window (FM L172–175), gives a 2–3% sky, and we chose a cut it fails." That is a verdict on our construction. Worse, it would reinstate a decision rule Program (A) already refuted: FM L85–87 records that the original rule "would have refuted ΛCDM itself", and by this same statistic ΛCDM sits at 0.15–0.20% (REC L16) — so whatever cut fires the completion fires ΛCDM harder. LIVE would mean crediting four papers (23/25/26/27) with a calibrated prediction none of them makes; their warrant cells would have to read "NOT AUTHOR-SUPPLIED", which the bibliography's own warrant reasoning (BIB L82–84, "a theory cannot keep a falsifier its own logic does not generate") treats as the case where the tier is in doubt. It would also raise the calibrated tally at BIB L107 from 5 to 9 with four lane-made rows. (b) is correctly rejected.

## Plain-language justification

Gaztañaga's papers say the sky's temperature pattern should stop being correlated beyond about 60 degrees, and they get that angle from things measured elsewhere, so the angle is an honest, non-circular prediction. But the papers never say *how much* weaker the correlation should be, and our own months of work established that no reading of the papers can produce that number — the author himself says the needed model is still unwritten. The new phase (b) result shows that even when we fill in the missing number ourselves in the most natural ways, the real sky stays unusual (a 1-in-40 sky at best, versus 1-in-500 with no cutoff). That is worth writing down, but it is a statement about our fill-ins, not about the papers. The bibliography already has a shelf for exactly this kind of claim — "right direction and scale, no amplitude" — and the papers belong on it. Promoting them to "calibrated" would put our number in the author's mouth and revive a scoring rule we already threw out because it would also condemn standard cosmology; demoting them would pretend a real, non-circular angle prediction is nothing. So: keep the tier, but fix the write-up so it stops saying the prediction came "before the data" (it did not; the anomaly was known — what is true is that the angle was derived without using it) and stops crediting the theory with an improvement our completions produced.
