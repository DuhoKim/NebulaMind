## 2. The finding

The critic note's objection was well founded: an `n²` term **does** return, and it returns from exactly the place the
note named — the Fock (exchange) contraction of the coincident-point four-fermion operator. It is not the term either
printed closure asserts.

With the medium-normal-ordered one-body density matrix written, after the angular integral, as `ρ_med = A·1 + B·β`:

- **direct (Hartree) contraction = 0** for the unpolarized state — K3 step 1's result, reproduced as a control, not assumed;
- **exchange (Fock) contraction = −3(A² + B²)**, with `A = n/(4N_f)` fixed by `Tr ρ_med = n`, and
  `B = ∫d³p/(2π)³ (m/2E)(f₋ + f₊)`;
- summed over `N_f` degenerate species:

| regime | exchange contribution |
|---|---|
| non-relativistic, `p_F ≪ m` | `−(3/8) n² / N_f` |
| ultrarelativistic, `p_F ≫ m` | `−(3/16) n² / N_f` |

The bounce chain's own regime is extreme density, hence ultrarelativistic, hence `−(3/16) n²/N_f`.

**Three things follow, and the third is the one that matters.**

1. **The density power `n²` is real** — the printed closures were not wrong to write `n²`; they were wrong about where
   it comes from. It is a Fermi-statistics exchange term, not the average of a randomly oriented classical spin sum.
2. **The coefficient is not a number.** It runs with `m/p_F` between `−3/8` and `−3/16`, and it carries `1/N_f`. No
   single constant multiplies `n²` for the audited object across the regimes these papers span.
3. **The sign is negative.** Both printed closures are positive. The bounce in the torsion chain is driven by the sign
   of this term; a negative `⟨s²⟩` contribution does not merely rescale the bounce, it points the other way.
   *This result establishes the sign of the exchange contribution to the audited scalar. What that does to the bounce
   is a downstream question this step did not preregister and does not answer.*

## 3. The split, and why it was not a contradiction

codex filed `EXCHANGE_NEGLIGIBLE`. It computed a **different object**:

- **Object L** — `⟨Σ_a s_a(x) s_a(x)⟩` at coincident `x`: square, then coarse-grain. The exchange line is unconstrained
  by a spatial delta function, so it is a product of two momentum integrals and scales as `n²`.
- **Object C** — `⟨S_a S_a⟩/V²` with `S_a = ∫_V s_a`: coarse-grain, then square. The spatial integrals force equal
  momentum modes, collapsing the double integral to a single one, so everything scales as `n/V` and vanishes in the
  thermodynamic limit. This is K3 step 1's object.

Route 2 computed **both**, blind, by a different method, and reproduced both: Object L `→ −3N_f(c₁²+c₂²)`, Object C
`→ 0` as `V → ∞`. The two route-1 seats were never in contradiction about one quantity.

**Which object the field equations require.** `U^ik` (entry 10 Eq. (6), L84–86) is a local algebraic function of the
spin tensor, carried into Eq. (9) (L104–106); the Einstein–Cartan equations are local, so the source term is the
expectation of the local product — **Object L**. Route 2 adds the decisive check: on Object C the unpolarized mean spin
is zero, so squaring after averaging would annihilate the term entirely, and the paper plainly does not intend that.
**But the source text does not say.** L108–110 says only that the first term of Eq. (9) "can be macroscopically averaged
at cosmological scales as a perfect fluid" and L113 then asserts `⟨s²⟩ = ¾ n²` without defining the average, the
ordering, or the contractions. The physics fixes the object; the paper does not.

## 4. codex's error, located and independently confirmed

The third seat found that codex evaluated the per-momentum spinor trace in the rest frame and pulled it out of the
momentum integral as a constant. Tori re-derived that claim rather than accepting it (`K3S2_tori_verify.py`,
output `K3S2_tori_verify.out`):

`Σ_a Tr(Σ_a P₊(p) Σ_a P₊(p)) = 2 + 4m²/E²`  — equal to **6** at `p = 0` and **2** at `m = 0`.

It is not a constant and may not leave the integral. codex's coefficient for Object C is therefore wrong. Its
qualitative conclusion for Object C — that everything scales as `n/V` and vanishes in the thermodynamic limit — is
independently reproduced by route 2 and stands. **codex was right about its object and wrong about its arithmetic;
it was answering a question the field equations do not ask.**

The same script verified Tori's own step without the shortcut it used: keeping the full `α·p` term and doing the solid-angle
integral explicitly returns `(1 + βm/E)/2`, so `ρ_med = A·1 + B·β` is exact for an isotropic medium and
`−¼Σ_a Tr(Σ_aρΣ_aρ) = −3(A²+B²)` follows with no angular-averaging assumption. Both verifications print `PASS`.

## 8. What survives against a critic

The residual freedom, stated exactly rather than papered over:

1. **The object is fixed by the physics, not by the text.** A critic who insists that "macroscopically averaged" in
   entry 10 L109 means Object C gets `EXCHANGE_NEGLIGIBLE` and no `n²` at all — which contradicts the paper's own
   assertion of a nonzero `⟨s²⟩`. Either reading refutes the printed closures; they disagree only on how.
2. **The coefficient is regime- and species-dependent**, so this result does not install a replacement number. It
   removes the possibility that any single number is derivable for the audited object.
3. **The subtraction is a declared choice.** Medium normal ordering is used throughout. A different renormalisation of
   the coincident-point product shifts the state-independent part; it does not change the `n²` coefficient, which is
   built from `ρ_med` alone.
4. **Not addressed:** interactions beyond the free field, finite-temperature corrections between the two limits,
   and what the negative sign does to the bounce. None was preregistered.
