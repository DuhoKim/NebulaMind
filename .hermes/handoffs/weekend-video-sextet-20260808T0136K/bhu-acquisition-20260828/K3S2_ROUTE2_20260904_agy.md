## Method & State
The method uses a position-space N-fermion Slater determinant of plane-wave spin-orbitals in a box of volume V, with an unpolarized grand-canonical Fermi gas state. Medium normal ordering is employed (subtracting the T=mu=0 vacuum).

## Evaluation
- **Object L (local, square-then-average)**: The direct contraction evaluates exactly to 0. The exchange contraction evaluates to `-3 * N_f * (c_1^2 + c_2^2)` where `c_1 = n/(4 N_f)`. In the thermodynamic limit, this yields an n^2 scaling.
  - UR limit (p_F >> m): leading term is -3/(16 N_f) n^2
  - NR limit (p_F << m): leading term is -3/(8 N_f) n^2
  - Classical limit: scales linearly with n at fixed V, then vanishes as n/V in the thermodynamic limit.
  - Reproduces neither printed coefficient.

- **Object C (cell-averaged, average-then-square)**: The exchange correlation decays over a distance 1/p_F. Its macroscopic average evaluates as ~1/V, which identically vanishes in the thermodynamic limit (V -> infinity at fixed n). It does not yield an n^2 term.

## Printed Relations
Neither object reproduces the spin-fluid relation s^2 = (1/8) n^2 or the Dirac relation <s^2> = (3/4) n^2. The coefficient recovered for Object L has the wrong sign, depends on the number of fermion species N_f, and matches neither fraction.

## Which Object does entry 10 require?
Entry 10 definitively requires **OBJECT L**. 
The source text leaves no ambiguity: it defines the spin-spin interaction as a local term in the gravitational field equations. At L109-110, it states: "The first term on the right of (9) is the GR part of the energy-momentum tensor for a Dirac field and can be macroscopically averaged at cosmological scales as a perfect fluid... the second term on the right of (9) is equal to -3/4 \kappa s^2 g_{ik}, where s is the spatial spin pseudovector which measures the spatial density of spin. The average value of its square is <s^2> = 3/4 n^2."
Averaging the local energy-momentum tensor over a fluid cell corresponds to taking the expectation value of the local field operator and then integrating, which is explicitly the square-then-average operation of Object L. If it were Object C, the macroscopic average of s (which is 0 for an unpolarized fluid) would be squared, eliminating the term entirely. Thus the equations strictly require Object L.
