## 1. The strongest objection: this result is a statement about entry 10's model, not about nature

`R = 2/3` at the bounce is computed from entry 10's own `ε(T)`, `n(T)` and `a(T)`. A critic can say: of course the
spin term is order one at the bounce — the bounce is *caused* by the spin term in this model, so any consistent
accounting must make it comparable to the energy density there. The result is therefore internal to the model, and
tells us nothing about whether such a bounce occurs.

**The lane's answer:** that is exactly what it claims, and no more. The finding is that **within the model, at the
point the model's own claim lives, the calculation is uncontrolled**. That is a statement about the derivability of the
closure, which is what this whole K3 cluster audits. It is not a statement about nature and the record does not make
one.

The sharper version of the objection is worth stating: `2/3` is not far from `1`, and the difference between
"comparable" and "cancelling" is not scientifically dramatic. Route 2's point stands that `2/3` is *derived* rather
than assumed — but a critic is entitled to say the derivation's interest is provenance, not magnitude.

## 2. The threshold is ours, not the sources'

Limb A's exit condition `|R| ≥ 0.1` was declared by this lane in the preregistration. It is not from entry 10 and not
from the literature. A critic who prefers `0.5` or `0.05` gets the same class here, since `2/3` and `10⁻⁵⁶` are far
from both — but the threshold is a convention and the record should not pretend otherwise.

**What would settle it:** nothing; it is a declared convention. The defence is that it was declared *before* computing
and gated, not chosen after seeing `2/3`.

## 3. The ultrarelativistic kinetic-equilibrium assumption is entry 10's, and it is doing work

`ε ∝ T⁴` and `n ∝ T³` hold for ultrarelativistic matter in kinetic equilibrium (entry 10 **L152**). `R ∝ T²` follows
from those forms alone. If the matter at the bounce is not in kinetic equilibrium, or not ultrarelativistic, the scaling
changes and so does the number.

**What would settle it:** recomputing `R` under a stated non-equilibrium or massive-particle distribution. That would be
a different study and is **not proposed**.
