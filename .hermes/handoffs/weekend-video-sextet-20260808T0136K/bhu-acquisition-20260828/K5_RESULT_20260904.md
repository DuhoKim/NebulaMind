## 2. The finding

Entry 21 supplies, and this study does not dispute: the static equilibrium (Eqs. 4–7, **L245**), the axial
perturbation equation and its scattering potential (**L250, L269**), and the quasi-normal-mode **frequencies**
(Table 1, **L365**; band statement **L395**).

What it does not supply is a **normalisation**. Route 2's construction makes the split exact: writing the driven
problem with a source `S` and solving with the retarded Green's function `G = φ₁φ₂/W`, the late-time signal is a sum
over the poles `W(ω_n) = 0` with coefficients

`C_n = (dW/dω|_{ω_n})⁻¹ ∫ φ₁ Ŝ dr*`

The first factor is a property of the potential, hence of `(M, α)`. **The integral is a functional of the source** —
and for a ringdown "following a binary merger" (entry 21's own phrase at **L400**) the source is the merger, which a
**static equilibrium** does not contain.

**So the pole structure is fixed up to scale, and the scale is free.** The derivation halts at exactly one new
independent variable: the merger initial data, equivalently the excitation coefficient `C_n`, equivalently the
radiated-energy fraction.

## 3. The non-conflation, which is the point of the study

Entry 21 says at **L400** that the excitation factors "have to be calculated. This is an involved task, that this work
urges the community to perform." **The lane's conclusion does not rest on that sentence**, and all three seats say so
explicitly. Resting on it would be an argument from ignorance — "the authors did not compute it" is a fact about the
authors, not about the model.

The conclusion rests instead on the structure: a static equilibrium contains no merger source. Kimi's independent
logic check confirms the dependency chain (item 4, `VALID`): premise 6 — the paper's omission — "appears nowhere in
the derivation … deleting it leaves conclusion 3 intact."

## 4. A correction from the logic check, which sharpens the finding

Kimi ruled **one inference INVALID** (item 1): the claim that "the excitation *factor* is determined by the model" is
valid only under the narrow definition in which the excitation factor is the Wronskian residue alone — in which case
it is trivial, restating the premise. And Kimi points out that **entry 21's own usage at L400 probably means something
wider**, because "the Wronskian factor is directly calculable from known functions" and would not be called "an
involved task".

**This strengthens the result rather than weakening it.** If the paper's "excitation factors" include the source
overlap, then what L400 defers to the community is precisely the part that a static construction can never supply —
not a hard calculation, but a quantity the model does not contain. The record therefore does **not** claim the
excitation factors are derivable without qualifying which definition is meant.

## 5. The standard escape, refused

In general relativity a ringdown amplitude is routinely set by calibrating the radiated-energy fraction against
numerical-relativity merger simulations. All three seats and the logic check agree this would be an **added
assumption, not a derivation**: no such calibration exists for de Sitter-core objects in this theory, and importing the
ordinary black-hole value assumes the merger dynamics and surface structure are unchanged — which is exactly what the
model alters. Kimi: "any amplitude obtained via it inherits the status 'assumed, not derived.'"

The prereg forbids manufacturing one (§9). None was manufactured.
