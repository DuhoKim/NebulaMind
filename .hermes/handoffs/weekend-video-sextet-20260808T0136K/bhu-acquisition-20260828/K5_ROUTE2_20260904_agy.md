## Construction

The perturbation problem for the ringdown of a compact object sourced by a binary merger is described by an inhomogeneous wave equation. In the time domain, a perturbation $\psi(r^*, t)$ is driven by a dynamical source term $S(r^*, t)$ arising from the merging matter:
$$-\frac{\partial^2 \psi}{\partial t^2} + \frac{\partial^2 \psi}{\partial r^{*2}} - V(r^*)\psi = S(r^*, t)$$
where $r^*$ is the tortoise coordinate and $V(r^*)$ is the effective scattering potential.

Taking the Laplace transform $\hat{\psi}(r^*, s) = \int_0^\infty \psi(r^*, t) e^{-st} dt$, with $s = -i\omega$, the equation becomes:
$$\frac{d^2 \hat{\psi}}{dr^{*2}} + (\omega^2 - V(r^*))\hat{\psi} = \hat{S}(r^*, \omega) + \text{initial data}$$

The solution is constructed using the retarded Green's function $G(r^*, r^{*'}, \omega)$:
$$\hat{\psi}(r^*, \omega) = \int G(r^*, r^{*'}, \omega) \hat{S}(r^{*'}, \omega) dr^{*'}$$
The Green's function itself is built from two linearly independent homogeneous solutions, $\phi_1(r^*, \omega)$ (purely ingoing at the horizon) and $\phi_2(r^*, \omega)$ (purely outgoing at spatial infinity):
$$G(r^*, r^{*'}, \omega) = \frac{\phi_1(r^*_<, \omega) \phi_2(r^*_>, \omega)}{W(\omega)}$$
where $W(\omega) = \phi_1 \frac{d\phi_2}{dr^*} - \phi_2 \frac{d\phi_1}{dr^*}$ is the Wronskian. 

The quasi-normal modes $\omega_n$ are the poles of $G$ in the complex plane where $W(\omega_n) = 0$. Using contour integration to perform the inverse transform, the late-time signal is a sum over these poles:
$$\psi(r^*, t) = \sum_n \text{Re} \left[ C_n e^{-i\omega_n t} \phi_2(r^*, \omega_n) \right]$$
The residue $C_n$ gives the overall amplitude of each mode:
$$C_n = \left( \left. \frac{dW}{d\omega} \right|_{\omega_n} \right)^{-1} \int \phi_1(r^{*'}, \omega_n) \hat{S}(r^{*'}, \omega_n) dr^{*'}$$

This explicit derivation separates two distinct components:
1. **The excitation factor**: $\left( \left. \frac{dW}{d\omega} \right|_{\omega_n} \right)^{-1} \phi_1(r^{*'}, \omega_n) \phi_2(r^*, \omega_n)$. This is a property of the background potential $V(r^*)$ entirely.
2. **The excitation coefficient**: The integral of the source $\hat{S}(r^{*'}, \omega_n)$ against the inner mode function. This is a functional of the dynamical source.

The observable strain amplitude depends on the product of both.
