## 1. The strongest objection: "free" may be too strong a word for "not yet supplied"

A critic can say: the amplitude is not *irreducibly* free — it is fixed by physics the model has not yet been developed
enough to describe. Simulate a merger of two of these objects and the excitation coefficients follow. The lane has
recorded a statement about the model's current state and dressed it as a structural limitation.

**The lane's answer, and its limit.** The result says the amplitude is not fixed *by the construction entry 21
supplies*, which is exactly what the prereg asked and what the seats showed. It does not say no theory could ever fix
it. The distinction matters for what the record may claim, and §6 of the result was written to keep it: entry 21 keeps
`W_ROUTE_CONNECTED`, not a demotion.

**Where the critic lands a hit:** the phrase "amplitude irreducibly free", inherited from the freedom map, is stronger
than what K5 establishes. K5 establishes "not fixed by this construction". The result uses the weaker phrasing in its
own findings; the stronger phrase survives only where it quotes the earlier freedom-map language, and a reader could
carry it across. **That is a real risk in the record and is flagged here rather than defended.**

## 2. The merger-source argument assumes the ringing event is a merger

Entry 21 names a binary merger (**L400**) and the seats followed it. But a de Sitter-core object could ring from
another perturbation — accretion, an instability, formation itself. For some of those the "source" might be fixed by
the construction in a way a merger is not.

**What would settle it:** identifying a ringing channel whose source *is* determined by the static construction, and
computing `∫ φ₁ Ŝ` for it. That is a different study and **not proposed**. Note that entry 21's own detectability
claim (L399, L401) is framed around merger ringdowns, so the objection does not rescue the paper's own argument.
