## WHAT THE PAPER CLAIMS

Abstract: "We present a simple prediction to explain the observed value of M ~ 6e22 Msun or
equivalently Omega_Lambda ... and the coincidence problem Omega_m ~ Omega_Lambda."

Section 4: "the maximum probability corresponds to observers that appear in BHs with
Lambda_O/9 < Lambda < Lambda_O, where Lambda_O = 4/(3 tau_O^2) is the value corresponding to
r_S ~ 3 tau_O/2 in Equation (8) for the minimum time tau_O needed for observers to exit. If we
assume that this time tau agrees with the age of our galaxy, we find good agreement between this
prediction and the observed Lambda measurements (Omega_Lambda ~ 0.75)."

Also stated: "M ~ 6e22 Msun in Equation (3) has a typical collapse time of tau ~ 11 Gyr in
Equation (5) (i.e., Equation (8))", and "tau = 4GM/3".

## WHAT I CONCLUDED

  F1  The paper's own numbers disagree. tau = 4GM/3 at M = 6e22 Msun gives 12.49 Gyr, not the

  F2  Inverting the band gives 3.90 < tau_O < 11.70 Gyr (Planck). So: the stated tau = 11 Gyr is

## ATTACK 3 -- is tau really 4GM/3?

I took "tau = 4GM/3" from the text near Equation (5)/(8) and used it in geometric units,
tau[s] = (4/3) G M / c^3. Verify against Equations (5) and (8) in the source. If the collapse
time carries another factor (a pi, a 3/2, a free-fall coefficient), F1 evaporates and part of F2
moves. My positive control only validates the UNIT conversion (G*Msun/c^3 = 4.9265 us vs textbook
4.9255 us), not the formula I fed it.
