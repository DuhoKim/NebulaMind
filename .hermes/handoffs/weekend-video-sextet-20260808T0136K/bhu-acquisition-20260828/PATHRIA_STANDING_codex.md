## Refutation condition (stated before evaluation)

Pathria's identification requires the conjunction he states in his equation (18): a positively curved universe, \(K=+1\), and a cosmological constant no larger than the critical value for which a finite maximum radius exists, \(\Lambda\leq\Lambda_c\). In his dust model the present-day observables obey

\[
\frac{c^2}{H_0^2R_0^2}=2q_0-1+\lambda,
\qquad \lambda\equiv\frac{\Lambda c^2}{H_0^2},
\]

while \(\Lambda\leq\Lambda_c\) is the condition that the turnaround cubic has a physical maximum.  Eliminating Pathria's mass constant at the critical point gives

\[
\lambda(3q_0+\lambda)^2=(2q_0-1+\lambda)^3,
\]

whose positive upper-bound branch is

\[
\lambda_c(q_0)=\frac{q_0^2}{2}-2q_0+\frac12+
\frac{\sqrt3}{6}\sqrt{(q_0+1)^3(3q_0-1)}.
\]

For a closed model the lower inequality is \(\lambda>1-2q_0\).  The upper and lower inequalities have a nonempty physical interval only for \(q_0>1/2\) (at \(q_0=1/2\) they meet degenerately at \(\lambda=0\)).  Therefore the predeclared firing condition is: **fire if the measured configuration establishes \(q_0<1/2\), because then no \(\Lambda\)—including a positive acceleration-producing one—can satisfy both Pathria's \(K=+1\) and \(\Lambda\leq\Lambda_c\) requirements.** A separately measured \(\Omega_K>0\) would also directly violate \(K=+1\), while \(\Lambda>\Lambda_c\) eliminates both \(R_s\) and \(R_{\max}\) in Pathria's own words.

This is an **author-supplied requirement**, not a threshold supplied by this adjudication: the threshold follows from Pathria's equations and his explicit conjunction \(K=+1,\ \Lambda\leq\Lambda_c\). The use of Planck's quoted 68% errors to report a Gaussian-equivalent number of standard deviations is only the evaluation convention.

## Evaluation against the pinned Planck values

I use the internally matched Planck TT,TE,EE+lowE+lensing values \(H_0=67.36\pm0.54\) km s\(^{-1}\) Mpc\(^{-1}\), \(\Omega_m=0.3158\pm0.0073\), and the flat-base result \(\Omega_\Lambda=0.6847\pm0.0073\). Since the latter two are complements in this fit, their errors must not be treated as independent: \(q_0=\tfrac32\Omega_m-1\) and \(\sigma_q=\tfrac32\sigma_{\Omega_m}\).

\[
q_0=\frac{\Omega_m}{2}-\Omega_\Lambda
=\frac{0.3158}{2}-0.6847=-0.5268,
\]

\[
\sigma(q_0)=\frac32(0.0073)=0.01095,
\qquad
\frac{0.5-q_0}{\sigma(q_0)}
=\frac{0.5-(-0.5268)}{0.01095}=93.8\sigma.
\]

Thus the necessary \(q_0>1/2\) condition is decisively violated. The separate curvature result \(\Omega_K=0.001\pm0.002\) has an open central value; relative to the closed/open boundary it is only \(0.001/0.002=0.5\sigma\), so curvature alone would not justify firing.

For the density calculation (cgs units),

\[
H_0=\frac{67.36\times10^5}{3.0856776\times10^{24}}
=2.18299\times10^{-18}\ {\rm s^{-1}},
\]

\[
\rho_{\rm crit}=\frac{3H_0^2}{8\pi G}
=\frac{3(2.18299\times10^{-18})^2}{8\pi(6.67430\times10^{-8})}
=8.5227\times10^{-30}\ {\rm g\,cm^{-3}},
\]

\[
\rho_{\rm matter}=\Omega_m\rho_{\rm crit}
=0.3158(8.5227\times10^{-30})
=2.6915\times10^{-30}\ {\rm g\,cm^{-3}}.
\]

Propagating the quoted marginal errors without a published covariance for this derived display,

\[
\sigma_\rho=\rho_{\rm matter}
\sqrt{(0.0073/0.3158)^2+[2(0.54/67.36)]^2}
=7.57\times10^{-32}\ {\rm g\,cm^{-3}}.
\]

## Step 3: the strongest \(\Lambda\) defence

The measured cosmological constant is

\[
\Lambda=\frac{3\Omega_\Lambda H_0^2}{c^2}
=3(0.6847)\frac{(2.18299\times10^{-18})^2}{(2.99792458\times10^{10})^2}
=1.089\times10^{-56}\ {\rm cm^{-2}}.
\]

The same central value follows from Planck's directly quoted \(\Omega_\Lambda h^2=0.3107\pm0.0082\):

\[
\Lambda=3(0.3107)\frac{(100\times10^5/3.0856776\times10^{24})^2}{c^2}
=(1.0892\pm0.0287)\times10^{-56}\ {\rm cm^{-2}}.
\]

Pathria's representative calibration at \(H_0=75\), \(q_0=1\) gives \(\Lambda_c=1.0\times10^{-57}\ {\rm cm^{-2}}\). The measured value is therefore

\[
\frac{\Lambda_{\rm measured}}{\Lambda_{c,\rm representative}}
=\frac{1.0892\times10^{-56}}{1.0\times10^{-57}}=10.89.
\]

So even the printed representative upper bound does **not** accommodate the measured \(\Lambda\) (the excess is \(34.4\sigma\) if only Planck's measurement error is assigned; Pathria gives no error for that differently calibrated bound). More importantly, it would be incorrect to stop at that cross-calibration: inserting the measured \(q_0=-0.5268\) into Pathria's own general bound makes \((q_0+1)^3(3q_0-1)<0\), so \(\lambda_c(q_0)\) has no real physical branch. Equivalently, closed curvature demands

\[
\lambda>1-2q_0=2.0536,
\quad\hbox{while}\quad
\lambda_{\rm measured}=\frac{\Lambda c^2}{H_0^2}=3\Omega_\Lambda=2.0541,
\]

but at that \(q_0\) there is no \(\Lambda\leq\Lambda_c\) turnaround solution. Positive \(\Lambda\) therefore does not rescue the identification; it is precisely too large for a finite \(R_{\max}=R_s\) compatible with the measured acceleration.
