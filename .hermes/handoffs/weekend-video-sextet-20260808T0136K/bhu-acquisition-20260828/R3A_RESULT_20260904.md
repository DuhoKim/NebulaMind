## 2. What the paper actually says

Entry 59 (Desai & Popławski 2016, *Phys. Lett. B* 755, 183):

- **L122–124:** "For a rigorous treatment, it should be derived from quantum field theory in the Riemann–Cartan
  spacetime. **Following [32], we assume that**"
- **L126:** `K = β(κ ε̃)²`  — **L128:** "where β is a dimensionless particle production coefficient."
- **L197–199:** `β < β_cr`, and "For standard-model particles, `β_cr = 1/929.0915`."
- **L228–229:** "We need a value of β which is slightly smaller than `β_cr`. **Thus, we choose β = 1/929.25**."
- **L373–374:** the reported `n_s`, `r`, `α_s` "do not change significantly with `a₀` and are **only sensitive to β**."

**Two different objects, two different answers.** `β_cr` **is derived** — it follows from the expansion condition and
standard-model particle content. **`β` is not.** It is chosen to sit just below `β_cr`.

## 4. The split, and why `FREE` rather than `FITTED`

codex's argument was real and is recorded: the paper's conclusion (**L459–463**) says the spectral index agrees with
Planck 2015 "when evaluated about 20 e-folds before the end of inflation **for a particular range of the particle
production coefficient**". On its face that is a fit.

The third seat set it aside on the prereg's own definitions, and its reasoning is the load-bearing part of this result:

> "If the parameter had been 'chosen to reproduce an observable' (FITTED), the authors would have tuned it to
> eliminate the tension at the standard evaluation scale (N = 50–60). The fact that they openly report a 6σ tension
> and then separately note that agreement can only be found at a non-standard N demonstrates that β was carried freely
> through the model… Codex's quote simply describes the subset of parameter space that happens to match the data,
> which is standard reporting for a free parameter, not evidence of it being fixed to a target."

The paper's own reported tension is the discriminator: **at standard e-fold counts it gets `n_s ≈ 0.99`, about 6σ from
Planck 2015 (L368–369)**, and recovers agreement only at `N ≈ 20–25` or by assuming an extra relativistic degree of
freedom. A fitted parameter would not leave that gap.

derivation; the authors say so themselves. What the record gains is that **the inflationary observables of this branch
rest on a coefficient neither paper derives**, and the paper's agreement with Planck holds only for a selected range of
it at a non-standard evaluation scale.

**Relation to the pattern.** This is a **fourth instance** of the recorded shape/magnitude pattern, and the sharpest so
far: the construction fixes the *form* of the production law and the *critical value* `β_cr`, and leaves free the one
number the observables are sensitive to. Unlike K5's and K6's instances, here the free magnitude has already been
