## 1. The strongest objection: an isolated black hole's mass IS conserved

A critic can say the lane is being pedantic. The construction posits a black hole with nothing outside it; an isolated
hole's mass is conserved by Birkhoff and by energy conservation; therefore `M_T` constant is not a "choice" but a
consequence of the physical setup, and `w = −1` really is forced.

**This is the serious objection and it may be right.** The result rests on the paper's own conditional phrasing
("**If we want** `M_T` to be constant…") and on L143–144 permitting `M(τ)`. It does not prove that a time-dependent
`M_T` is *physically* admissible in a genuinely isolated configuration — only that the printed construction does not
exclude it.

**What would settle it:** deriving `M_T` constancy from the junction conditions and an exterior vacuum, rather than
adopting it. If that derivation goes through, the class becomes `RIGIDITY_HOLDS` and limb B follows. **That is a real
follow-up and it is NOT proposed here.**

## 2. The distinction may be too fine to matter

Even if constancy is assumed rather than derived, every working cosmologist would take an isolated hole's mass as
fixed. A critic can say the lane has found a wording difference, not a physics difference.

**Partly fair.** The lane's answer: the difference decides what a measurement would mean. If rigidity were derived, an
evolving-`w` detection would refute the model. Because it is assumed, the same detection selects a permitted branch
instead. **That is a difference in falsifiability, which is the whole subject of this audit.**
