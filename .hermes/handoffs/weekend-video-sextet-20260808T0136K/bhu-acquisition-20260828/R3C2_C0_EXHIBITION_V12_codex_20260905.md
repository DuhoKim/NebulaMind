## Direct answer on `CENSUS_COMPLETE`

`CENSUS_COMPLETE` is **REACHABLE**, including for a real corpus containing many papers, under the input condition that every included quantitative claim receives exactly one arithmetic-group outcome (`REPRO_EXACT` or `REPRO_FAILED`) and no earlier study-level stop class applies. For example, extend C1/C2 to any finite number of included claims, each with a stated recipe and sufficient `PRINTED`/`STANDARD` inputs, with every calculation either matching or failing to match the printed number; if controls, enumeration, origin agreement, audit, and receipts also clear, the §4 precedence falls through to `CENSUS_COMPLETE`.

A single included claim filed as `REPRO_BLOCKED`, `REPRO_INPUT_ABSENT`, `REPRO_NO_DERIVATION_STATED`, or `REPRO_NOT_EVALUABLE` makes `CENSUS_PARTIAL` true after two attempts. Its explicit precedence then prevents `CENSUS_COMPLETE` for that corpus/run. Thus one such claim forces partial, but the document does not require that every real corpus contain one; it does not make complete unreachable in practice. Whether the pinned corpus actually contains such a claim is not decided by C0 and cannot be known until enumeration and attempts occur.

The reachability of both tally classes depends on §1’s marked human boundary: the two seats must agree which numerals are “the paper’s own result.” That dependence can change the included denominator. It does not eliminate either path: agreement on a denominator whose claims are all arithmetic reaches complete; agreement on one containing a non-arithmetic claim reaches partial; unresolved disagreement instead reaches `CENSUS_DENOMINATOR_DISPUTED`.
