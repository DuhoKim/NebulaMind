## C. Direct answer on `CENSUS_COMPLETE`

`CENSUS_COMPLETE` is **REACHABLE**. Its concrete many-paper realization is any nonempty corpus in which every included claim receives either `REPRO_EXACT` or `REPRO_FAILED`, with no earlier §4 stop condition. For example, replicate Claim E and Claim F across 100 papers, varying their printed operands while keeping every recipe and every needed input stated; after successful controls, agreement, audit, and receipt verification, all included claims are in the two-member arithmetic group and §4’s final limb files `CENSUS_COMPLETE`.

A single included `REPRO_BLOCKED`, `REPRO_INPUT_ABSENT`, `REPRO_NO_DERIVATION_STATED`, or unresolved `REPRO_NOT_EVALUABLE` claim does force `CENSUS_PARTIAL` for that realized corpus, provided no earlier stop class applies: §4.2 requires only “at least one included claim” with a non-arithmetic outcome and expressly gives partial precedence over complete. That makes completeness demanding, not unreachable. Whether the actual pinned corpus contains such a claim cannot be known before enumeration and the §2 attempts; reachability does not assume that empirical result.

## D. Dependence on the §1 boundary

The routes above necessarily begin only after §1 includes a passage. That inclusion boundary is operationally enumerated but not fully mechanical: the document’s own marked note says whether a numeral is “the paper’s own result” remains a judgement assigned independently to two seats. Thus the per-claim outcome reachability shown here is conditional on a concrete passage being included under §1; persistent disagreement instead diverts the study to `CENSUS_DENOMINATOR_DISPUTED`. This dependence does not make any §3 verdict unreachable because each exhibited passage can straightforwardly satisfy the stated own-result rule.
