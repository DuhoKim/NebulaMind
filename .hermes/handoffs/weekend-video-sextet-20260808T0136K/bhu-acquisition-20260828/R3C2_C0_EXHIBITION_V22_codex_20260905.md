## Direct answer: can `CENSUS_COMPLETE` actually be filed?

**REACHABLE.** A real corpus can file `CENSUS_COMPLETE` whenever its agreed, nonzero set of included claims all receive exactly one of `REPRO_WITHIN_STATED_PRECISION` or `REPRO_FAILED`, C6 passes, and no earlier-precedence stop class applies. The two-claim W/F corpus above is a concrete witness. Chosen, fitted, imported, or undeclared provenance does not by itself prevent completion under the settled option (c): arithmetic consumes by `status` (`PRINTED` or `STANDARD`), while provenance is separately represented by `rests_on`.

**A single included claim with `REPRO_BLOCKED`, `REPRO_INPUT_ABSENT`, `REPRO_NO_DERIVATION_STATED`, or `REPRO_NOT_EVALUABLE` does force `CENSUS_PARTIAL` for that realized corpus**, assuming no earlier-precedence §4 class applies. The route is §3 non-arithmetic outcome → §4's condition “at least one included claim carries a non-arithmetic outcome” → `CENSUS_PARTIAL`, which precedes `CENSUS_COMPLETE`. That makes completeness demanding, not unreachable: the document does not require every possible or real corpus to contain such a claim.

The actual pinned corpus's filing cannot be inferred at C0. Whether it contains any such claim is an empirical output of the not-yet-run census.

## Marked §1 dependence

Every witness begins only after §1 inclusion: the numeral must be judged a result of the paper's own. The document acknowledges that this boundary “remains a judgement” and assigns it independently to two seats. That does not make a §3 verdict unreachable; a surviving inclusion disagreement routes to `CENSUS_DENOMINATOR_DISPUTED`, while an agreed inclusion permits the per-claim paths above. Thus the exhibition depends on the marked operational definition rather than silently assuming a broader meaning of “quantitative claim.”
