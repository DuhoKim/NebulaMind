================================================================================
(C) THE SUSPICION, ANSWERED DIRECTLY — is CENSUS_COMPLETE reachable?
================================================================================

Answer: REACHABLE.

What the suspicion gets right: `CENSUS_COMPLETE` is fragile. §4.2 makes `CENSUS_PARTIAL` fire
whenever "at least one included claim carries a non-arithmetic outcome (`REPRO_NO_DERIVATION_STATED`,
`REPRO_INPUT_ABSENT`, `REPRO_BLOCKED`, `REPRO_NOT_EVALUABLE`)" and gives it precedence over
`CENSUS_COMPLETE` (§4 precedence order: …, `CENSUS_AUDIT_FAILED`, `CENSUS_PARTIAL`, `CENSUS_COMPLETE`).
So a single blocked, absent-input, no-derivation-stated or not-evaluable claim anywhere in the corpus
does force `CENSUS_PARTIAL`, and the document itself says so in C0's rationale ("a single blocked or
absent input in the whole corpus is enough to prevent" it). `CENSUS_COMPLETE` also requires
`C6_AUDIT_SAMPLE=PASS` and a non-zero denominator, and §4.1 bars completeness "over nothing".

Why it is nonetheless reachable: no clause in the text REQUIRES any included claim to be blocked,
absent, silent or unevaluable, and no clause requires an audit failure. The condition that defeats
`CENSUS_COMPLETE` is a property of a corpus, not a necessity imposed by the text. The exhibit:

- Corpus: denominator N ≥ 1; every included claim states an equation or computational procedure;
  every input each equation needs is either printed in the claiming paper (`PRINTED`) or on C3's
  closed list printed in the claiming paper (`STANDARD`); every attempt finishes inside the
  120-second cap with available machinery.
- Routing: §2 steps 1–5 send every claim to the arithmetic group — §3 defines that group as exactly
  `REPRO_WITHIN_STATED_PRECISION` and `REPRO_FAILED`, so even a claim whose arithmetic FAILS still
  counts toward completeness. Both seats' enumerations, input lists, outcomes and origin
  classifications agree (origin-dispute rate ≤ 10%); the seed is supplied and receipted; the
  auditor's enumeration matches; the audit reconstructs every sampled claim clean
  (`C6_AUDIT_SAMPLE=PASS`).
- §4 precedence walked in order: `R3C2_NO_CLASS` — no (controls pass); `CENSUS_CONTROL_SPLIT` — no;
  `CENSUS_DENOMINATOR_DISPUTED` — no; `CENSUS_OUTCOME_DISPUTED` — no; `CENSUS_ORIGIN_DISPUTED` — no
  (≤ 10%); `CENSUS_AUDIT_FAILED` — no (audit PASS, receipts verify); `CENSUS_PARTIAL` — no (no
  non-arithmetic outcome; denominator ≥ 1); therefore `CENSUS_COMPLETE` is filed, the full tally
  reported with its denominator and the `rests_on` tally beside it.

The gap between "preventable by one bad claim" and "unreachable" is the whole of the C0 question:
C0 asks whether the verdict CAN OCCUR under the text, and it can. A verdict that is merely unlikely
on a real corpus of many papers is a frequency statement about corpora; a verdict is UNREACHABLE only
when the text blocks every path to it. No clause blocks every path to `CENSUS_COMPLETE`.
