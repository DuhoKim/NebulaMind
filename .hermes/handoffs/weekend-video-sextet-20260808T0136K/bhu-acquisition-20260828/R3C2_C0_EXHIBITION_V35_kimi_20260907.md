## (C) The CENSUS_COMPLETE suspicion, answered directly

**Answer: reachable.**

Routing, shown end to end on the concrete input of (B) row 1: every candidate passage is either excluded
with its kind recorded or included; every included claim states a recipe; every input each recipe needs is
`PRINTED` in the paper or `STANDARD` on C3's closed list; every arithmetic completes inside the 120-second
cap; each claim therefore files exactly one outcome, and each outcome is in the arithmetic group ("exactly
`REPRO_WITHIN_STATED_PRECISION` and `REPRO_FAILED`", §3); the denominator is 2 (not zero, so §4.1's vacuous
clause does not fire); `C6_AUDIT_SAMPLE=PASS`; and in §4's precedence ("`R3C2_NO_CLASS`,
`CENSUS_CONTROL_SPLIT`, `CENSUS_DENOMINATOR_DISPUTED`, `CENSUS_OUTCOME_DISPUTED`, `CENSUS_ORIGIN_DISPUTED`,
`CENSUS_AUDIT_FAILED`, `CENSUS_PARTIAL`, `CENSUS_COMPLETE`") no earlier class's condition holds, so
`CENSUS_COMPLETE` files, with the reproduction tally and the `rests_on` tally reported beside each other.

Engaging the suspicion on its own terms: the blocking fact it names is real, and it is declared, not hidden.
`CENSUS_PARTIAL` files when "at least one included claim carries a non-arithmetic outcome" and it "takes
precedence over `CENSUS_COMPLETE`". A single `REPRO_BLOCKED`, `REPRO_INPUT_ABSENT`,
`REPRO_NO_DERIVATION_STATED` or `REPRO_NOT_EVALUABLE` claim anywhere in the corpus does force
`CENSUS_PARTIAL`. But that makes `CENSUS_COMPLETE` contingent on the corpus, not unreachable: C0's test is
existential — "exhibit a concrete input that produces it" (§5, C0) — and the corpus above is such an input.
The class's condition is a property of the input corpus; no clause confines the census to corpora in which
some claim must block.

The option-(c) point that decides the practical half of the suspicion: under the settled §3, a claim
resting on chosen, fitted, imported or undeclared inputs does NOT force `CENSUS_PARTIAL`. The arithmetic
consumes "every ledger record with status `PRINTED` or `STANDARD`" whatever its `origin` (§2 step 4, §3);
provenance moved into the script-computed `rests_on` field. So a corpus whose papers print their chosen
constants still completes — the `rests_on` tally records `USES_CHOSEN` beside the verdict. Only the four
non-arithmetic outcomes block: an input absent from the paper with no named source, an input blocked at a
non-enumerable or non-matching named source, a missing derivation, or an infeasible computation. Whether
the real 89-text corpus contains any such claim is precisely the empirical question the census exists to
answer. If it contains even one, the filed class will be `CENSUS_PARTIAL` — that is §4's precedence working
as written, not a class that cannot occur.
