L00019	Abstract: We outline the basic ideas and analyze the possibilities of the quantum birth of universes
L00020	inside regular black holes with the de Sitter interior replacing a singularity. We compare different
L00021	cases and show that the most plausible case is the birth of a flat universe from an initial quantum
L00022	fluctuation with a small admixture of radiation and strings with the negative deficit angle, which
L00023	provides the existence of a potential barrier needed for quantum tunneling.
L00024	Keywords: baby universes; regular black holes; de Sitter vacuum

L00122	In 1988, Poisson and Israel assumed that transition to the de Sitter vacuum deeply inside the
L00123	Schwarzschild black hole can result from the self-regulation of geometry by the vacuum polarization
L00124	effects [3]. They analyzed such a transition in detail and found that in the resulting geometry there
L00125	exists the Cauchy horizon (HC in Figure 1).
L00126	The idea of arising a baby universe inside a black hole has been investigated by Farhi and Guth
L00127	in 1987 [4] as the idea of creation of a universe in the laboratory from a false vacuum bubble in
L00128	the Minkowski space. An expanding bubble of the de Sitter vacuum generates the Schwarzschild
L00129	geometry outside, separated by a thin wall from the de Sitter interior. The global structure of spacetime
L00130	in this case implies that the arising of a baby universe must be associated with an initial spacelike
L00131	singularity, which evidently represents a singular initial value configuration. Therefore, Farhi and
L00132	Guth concluded that the initial singularity would be an unavoidable obstacle to the creation of a
L00133	universe in the laboratory [4].
L00134	In 1990 Farhi, Guth, and Guven proposed the model with the little initial bubble arising without a
L00135	singularity [5]. Quantum effects allow it to tunnel into the larger bubble which disappears beyond the
L00136	black hole horizon of an outside observer, whereas on the inside region the bubble would classically
L00137	evolve towards a new universe [5]. Independently, the question of a new universe inside a black hole
L00138	has been addressed by Frolov, Markov, and Mukhanov in 1989 [6] in the framework of the hypothesis
L00139	of the existence of the limiting curvature of the Planckian scale with the equation p = −ρ, and taking
L00140	into account that the existence of the Cauchy horizon implies the absence of a global Cauchy surface.
L00141	In 1990, they presented the analysis of a smooth de Sitter–Schwarzschild transition, including the case
L00142	of the arising of a closed world in a new universe in the absolute future of the original universe, and
L00143	the possibility of a white hole arising [7].
L00144	Later, the appearance of a new universe inside a black hole has been considered by Poplawski [8]
L00145	in the Einstein–Cartan–Sciama–Kibble theory of gravity with torsion [9], where the spin induces
L00146	gravitational repulsion at extremely high densities, which prevents the collapse to a singularity and
L00147	provides formation of a de Sitter core and the appearance of a closed universe.
L00148	Oshita and Yokoyama [10] studied appearance of an inflationary universe beyond a wormhole
L00149	throat, as initiated by a phase transition with symmetry restoration inside a thin-wall bubble around
L00150	an evaporating black hole [11]. In this model, the thin-wall boundary of a false vacuum bubble induces
L00151	quantum tunneling to a wormhole-like configuration, with exponentially expanding space beyond the
L00152	wormhole throat filled with the false vacuum, interpreted as creation of a new inflationary universe in
L00153	the final stage of the black hole evaporation.
L00154	The comprehensive analysis of a universe production involving wormholes has been done by
L00155	Ansoldi, Merali, and Guendelman [12] on the basis of the causal structure of spacetime and connection
L00156	between vacuum decay and inflation, and including the question of creation of a baby universe within
L00157	our universe on the other side of a wormhole throat in future particle accelerators [13] or spontaneously.
L00158	The above models have also been based on the direct matching of the Schwarzschild and the de
L00159	Sitter metrics using the thin shell approach (the global structure of spacetime is shown in Figure 1),
L00160	which suggests that the whole dynamical evolution from the equation of state p = ρ = 0 (the
L00161	Schwarzschild sector) to p = −ρ (the de Sitter sector) occurs within or nearby a very thin junction
L00162	layer. The matched metrics typically have a jump at the junction surface [3].
L00163	The case of a distributed density profile ρ(r ) smoothly evolving from a large internal density of
L00164	the de Sitter vacuum, arising due to symmetry restoration, has been studied in a general setting in [14],
L00165	where it was found that solutions describing de Sitter–Schwarzschild transition belong to the class of
L00166	solutions to the Einstein equations with the source term such that

L00198	plausible case is the birth of a flat (Ω = 1) universe. In Section 4 we summarize the results.

L00577	It is known that a white hole can be described as a cosmological model (see, e.g., [27]). In the case
L00578	of a Schwarzschild white hole it includes the spacelike singularity r = 0, entirely open for outside
L00579	observers (Figure 2, left down); for this reason the existence of white holes (in a singular version) has
L00580	been forbidden by the cosmic censorship [28].
L00581	Replacing a Schwarzschild singularity with the de Sitter center transforms the spacelike singular
L00582	surface r = 0 in the past of a (singular) white hole, into the timelike regular surface r = 0 (Figure 2,
L00583	right), and rehabilitates a white hole in its regular version [26].
L00584	A cosmological model related to a regular white hole (segment RC , W H, U in Figure 2, right)
L00585	belongs to the cosmological models of the Lemaitre class with the de Sitter origin (for a review [21]).
L00586	The regular white hole (more precisely its RC region) models the initial stages of a regular cosmology
L00587	with the initial inflationary stage [26].
L00588	It is evident that a new universe inside a regular black hole can emerge in any of an infinite
L00589	number of BH regions with the de Sitter vacuum in their interiors. Geodesic structure of the de
L00590	Sitter–Schwarzschild spacetime shows [29] that the possibility of traveling to other universes through
L00591	a black hole, reported for the Reissner–Nordström and the Kerr geometry ([30] and references therein),
L00592	does exist in the case of a regular black hole with the de Sitter interior [29].
L00593	In the next Section, we estimate the probability of the arising of a closed world in the future of a
L00594	black hole (as proposed in [7]) in the spacetime with the global structure shown in Figure 2 [26]. We also
L00595	consider the additional option of a multiple quantum birth of open and flat causally disconnected
L00596	universes from the initial quantum fluctuations arising in the RC regions.
L00597	3. Birth of Universes Inside a Black Hole
L00598	In the general case of a Λ black hole with a distributed density profile and global structure
L00599	of spacetime shown in Figure 2, the region near the timelike de Sitter surface r = 0 is part of the
L00600	regular core RC , and can be considered as a small spherical bubble with the de Sitter vacuum trapped
L00601	inside [17], which can be a seed for the quantum birth of a closed universe. The global structure of the
L00602	de Sitter–Schwarzschild spacetime enables the arising of a closed world in any of an infinite number of
L00603	BH structures in the future of a black hole in the original universe. This case is illustrated by Figure 3,
L00604	left [26].
L00605	Global structure of spacetime for the regular black hole provides additional possibilities related
L00606	to instabilities of the de Sitter vacuum with respect to the quantum birth of a universe from
L00607	nonperturbative quantum fluctuations of the metric [31–38].
L00608	The regular cosmological model for a universe born from a quantum fluctuation in de Sitter space
L00609	and evolving from the de Sitter stage to the radiation-dominated stage has been proposed in 1975
L00610	in [39], where we also noticed the possibility of the multiple birth of causally disconnected universes
L00611	from the de Sitter background. In 1982, a similar possibility has been studied in detail by Gott III as
L00612	the creation of open FLRW universes in the quantum barrier penetration in de Sitter space, taking
L00613	into account the Gibbons–Hawking radiation from the de Sitter horizon [40]. The case of the arising
L00614	of open universes from the de Sitter vacuum is illustrated by Figure 3, right: The events E and E0 are
L00615	creations of the causally disconnected universes. The curved lines are the world lines of co-moving
L00616	observers. At the spacelike surface AB, the phase transition occurs from the inflationary to the radiation
L00617	dominated stage [40].

L00619	RC 1 in Figure 2, right; and the region BFD corresponds to a part of the region RC 2 . The regions RC 1

L00621	by spacelike curves only). Birth of an open (and flat) baby universe inside a regular black hole RC

L01162	4. Conclusions
L01163	The models with the direct matching of the Schwarzschild metric outside with the de Sitter
L01164	metric inside correspond to the direct birth of a closed universe inside a black hole (Figure 1). In the
L01165	general case of the de Sitter–Schwarzschild geometry with a smooth transition to interior de Sitter
L01166	vacuum inside a black hole, the global structure of spacetime contains an infinite sequence of black
L01167	and white holes with their future and past regular cores RC asymptotically de Sitter as r → 0, and of
L01168	asymptotically flat universes U in the future of a regular black hole (Figure 2, right). In this spacetime
L01169	a quantum birth of a closed universe means closing this sequence in some of BH regions in the future
L01170	of the original black hole (Figure 3, left).
L01171	In the case of a regular black hole with the de Sitter interior, the global structure of spacetime
L01172	provides also the possibility of the multiple birth of flat and open causally disconnected universes
L01173	in the RC regions (Figure 3, right), arising from initial fluctuations which contain strings with the
L01174	negative deficit angle leading to the appearance of a potential barrier needed for quantum tunneling.
L01175	To evaluate the contribution of strings in calculating the probability of tunneling, we take into
L01176	account decay of the inflationary vacuum into radiation within a nucleating bubble. Presence of
L01177	radiation in the initial fluctuation corresponds to the quantum birth of a universe with a non-zero
L01178	quantized energy, which is related to the strings content within an initial fluctuation. The observational
L01179	constraint on the temperature anisotropy (known for our Universe) provides the restriction on the
L01180	contribution of strings.
L01181	The probability of quantum birth of a universe is much bigger in this case than the probability
L01182	of the birth of a closed universe from "nothing", for all three cases including an open, flat, and
L01183	closed universe.
L01184	We compare different possibilities and show that the most plausible case is the birth of a flat
L01185	universe (Ω = 1) from an initial quantum fluctuation with a very small (of the order of 10−6 ) admixture
L01186	of strings with the negative deficit angle.
L01187	The probability of the quantum birth of baby universes inside a Λ black hole is not negligible, due
L01188	to the existence of an infinite number of the appropriate RC regions inside a particular ΛBH. All this
L01189	makes it possible to speculate (at least not exclude) that our Universe could be located inside some
L01190	regular black hole with the de Sitter interior.
