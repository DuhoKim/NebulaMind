L00019	 We give a comparative description of different types of regular static, spherically symmetric black holes (BHs) and discuss in more detail their particular type, which we
L00020	suggest to call black universes. The latter have a Schwarzschild-like
L00021	causal structure, but inside the horizon there is an expanding Kantowski-Sachs universe and a de Sitter infinity instead of a singularity. Thus a
L00022	hypothetic BH explorer gets a chance to survive. Solutions of this kind
L00023	are naturally obtained if one considers static, spherically symmetric distributions of various
L00024	(but not all) kinds of phantom matter whose existence is favoured by
L00025	cosmological observations. It also looks possible that our Universe has
L00026	originated from phantom-dominated collapse in another universe and
L00027	underwent isotropization after crossing the horizon. An explicit example
L00028	of a black-universe solution with positive Schwarzschild mass is
L00029	discussed. 
L00030	 1. Introduction 
L00031	 One of the long-standing problems in black hole (BH) physics is the existence of
L00032	curvature singularities beyond the event horizon in the BH solutions
L00033	obtained under the simplest and the most natural physical conditions
L00034	(the Schwarzschild, Reissner-Nordström, Kerr and Kerr-Newman solutions of general relativity and their
L00035	counterparts in many alternative theories of gravity). Singularities are
L00036	places where general relativity (or another classical theory of gravity)
L00037	does not work. Therefore, a full understanding of BH physics requires
L00038	avoidance of singularities or/and modification of the corresponding
L00039	classical theory and addressing quantum effects. There have been numerous
L00040	attempts on this trend, many of them suggesting that a singularity
L00041	inside the event horizon should be replaced with a kind of regular core. 
L00042	 In this paper, we discuss the possible geometry of classical nonsingular
L00043	BHs, restricting ourselves to asymptotically flat static, spherically symmetric configurations. In Sec.  2. we enumerate and briefly describe different known types of
L00044	regular BHs. The rest of the paper is devoted to their particular type
L00045	which we suggest to call black universes, namely, those in which a
L00046	possible BH explorer, after crossing the event horizon, gets into an
L00047	expanding universe [ 1 ] . Such objects are naturally obtained if one
L00048	considers local concentrations of dark energy in the form of phantom
L00049	matter, favoured by modern cosmological observations [ 2 ] .
L00050	Among theoretical reasons for considering phantom matter one may mention
L00051	natural appearance of phantom fields in some models of string theory
L00052	 [ 3 ] , supergravities [ 4 ] and theories in more than 11
L00053	dimensions like F-theory [ 5 ] . To avoid the obvious quantum
L00054	instability, a phantom scalar may perhaps be regarded as an effective
L00055	field description following from an underlying theory with positive
L00056	energies [ 6 ] . Curiously, a classical massless phantom field
L00057	even shows a more stable behaviour than its usual counterpart
L00058	 [ 8 , 7 ] . 
L00059	 In Sec.  3. we demonstrate the existence of black-universe
L00060	solutions in general relativity with minimally coupled phantom fields, formulate the
L00061	requirements to the field potential needed for obtaining them and present
L00062	a specific example. In Sec.  4. we obtain similar requirements in
L00063	a more general framework, general k-essence, and show that many (though
L00064	not all) forms of phantom matter discussed in the recent cosmological
L00065	literature can also lead to black universes. Sec.  5. is a
L00066	conclusion.

L00083	 To discuss BHs in the comparatively simple case of static spherical
L00084	symmetry, we may leave aside more general and more rigorous definitions
L00085	of horizons and black holes (see, e.g., [ 9 ] ) and rely on the following
L00086	working definition. A black hole is a space-time containing (i) a static
L00087	region which may be regarded external (e.g., contains a flat
L00088	asymptotic), (ii) another region invisible to an observer at rest
L00089	residing in the static region, and (iii) a Killing horizon of nonzero
L00090	area that separates the two regions and admits an analytical extension
L00091	of the metric from one region to another.

L00100	homogeneous (T) regions whose geometry is that of a Kantowski-Sachs anisotropic

L00378	 4. Black universes with other forms of phantom matter 
L00379	 Besided the minimally coupled scalar field ( 3 ), there are a
L00380	number of other suggestions for modelling the possible phantom
L00381	behaviour of dark energy, see, e.g., the review [ 24 ] and
L00382	references therein. 
L00383	 Let us show that type 4 regular BHs, or black universes, are, under
L00384	appropriate additional conditions, solutions to the field equations of

L00386	(STT) of gravity, or theories with nonminimally coupled scalar fields,
L00387	and the class of models called k-essence. We will show that
L00388	black-universe solutions may be found in both these classes.

L00577	 5. Conclusion 
L00578	 In this paper, we have classified the possible geometries of static, spherically symmetric,
L00579	asymptotically flat BHs and discussed in more detail their particular type, the
L00580	black universes. Such hypothetical configurations combine the properties
L00581	of a wormhole (absence of a centre, a regular minimum of the area
L00582	function) and a black hole (a Killing horizon separating R and T regions). 
L00583	 Quite evidently, such unusual objects require unusual matter for their
L00584	existence. It turns out that they are naturally obtained if one
L00585	considers static, spherically symmetric distributions of phantom matter, which is a subject
L00586	of vast discussion in modern cosmology. We have considered a
L00587	sufficiently general frameworks for the description of phantom matter,
L00588	namely, STT of gravity and the so-called k-essence, and found necessary
L00589	conditions for the existence of black-universe solutions. A conclusion
L00590	is that a great number of reasonable phantom matter models produce such
L00591	solutions. 
L00592	 The latter lead to the idea that our Universe could appear from
L00593	phantom-dominated collapse in another, “mother” universe and undergo
L00594	isotropization (e.g., due to particle creation) soon after crossing the
L00595	horizon. It is known that a Kantowski-Sachs nature of our Universe is not
L00596	excluded observationally [ 34 ] if its isotropization had happened
L00597	early enough, before the last scattering epoch (at redshifts z ≳ 1000 z\gtrsim 1000 ). One can notice that we are thus facing one more mechanism of
L00598	universes multiplication, in addition to the well-known mechanism
L00599	existing in the chaotic inflation scenario. 
L00600	 Somewhat similar ideas on possible appearance of baby universes inside
L00601	BHs have already been discussed from different standpoints
L00602	 [ 35 , 36 ] (see also references therein), and some
L00603	examples of two-dimensional regular BH metrics with properties
L00604	more or less similar to ours are known [ 35 , 37 , 38 ] ,
L00605	It can be remarked, however, that in two dimensions, where space is
L00606	simply a line, the notion of a centre,
L00607	which plays an essential role in the four-dimensional picture, cannot
L00608	be properly introduced. So it seems that the black-universe solutions
L00609	considered here and in Ref.  [ 1 ] are qualitatively new and (if
L00610	certainly amended by adding realistic matter ingredients) may even
L00611	lead to viable alternatives to the existing cosmological scenarios.
