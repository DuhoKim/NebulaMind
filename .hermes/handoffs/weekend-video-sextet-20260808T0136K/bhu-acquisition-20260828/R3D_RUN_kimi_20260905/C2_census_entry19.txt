   19| Abstract: We outline the basic ideas and analyze the possibilities of the quantum birth of universes
   20| inside regular black holes with the de Sitter interior replacing a singularity. We compare different
   21| cases and show that the most plausible case is the birth of a flat universe from an initial quantum
   22| fluctuation with a small admixture of radiation and strings with the negative deficit angle, which
   23| provides the existence of a potential barrier needed for quantum tunneling.
   24| Keywords: baby universes; regular black holes; de Sitter vacuum

  121| In 1988, Poisson and Israel assumed that transition to the de Sitter vacuum deeply inside the
  122| Schwarzschild black hole can result from the self-regulation of geometry by the vacuum polarization
  123| effects [3]. They analyzed such a transition in detail and found that in the resulting geometry there
  124| exists the Cauchy horizon (HC in Figure 1).
  125| The idea of arising a baby universe inside a black hole has been investigated by Farhi and Guth
  126| in 1987 [4] as the idea of creation of a universe in the laboratory from a false vacuum bubble in
  127| the Minkowski space. An expanding bubble of the de Sitter vacuum generates the Schwarzschild
  128| geometry outside, separated by a thin wall from the de Sitter interior. The global structure of spacetime
  129| in this case implies that the arising of a baby universe must be associated with an initial spacelike
  130| singularity, which evidently represents a singular initial value configuration. Therefore, Farhi and
  131| Guth concluded that the initial singularity would be an unavoidable obstacle to the creation of a
  132| universe in the laboratory [4].
  133| In 1990 Farhi, Guth, and Guven proposed the model with the little initial bubble arising without a
  134| singularity [5]. Quantum effects allow it to tunnel into the larger bubble which disappears beyond the
  135| black hole horizon of an outside observer, whereas on the inside region the bubble would classically
  136| evolve towards a new universe [5]. Independently, the question of a new universe inside a black hole
  137| has been addressed by Frolov, Markov, and Mukhanov in 1989 [6] in the framework of the hypothesis
  138| of the existence of the limiting curvature of the Planckian scale with the equation p = −ρ, and taking
  139| into account that the existence of the Cauchy horizon implies the absence of a global Cauchy surface.
  140| In 1990, they presented the analysis of a smooth de Sitter–Schwarzschild transition, including the case
  141| of the arising of a closed world in a new universe in the absolute future of the original universe, and
  142| the possibility of a white hole arising [7].
  143| Later, the appearance of a new universe inside a black hole has been considered by Poplawski [8]
  144| in the Einstein–Cartan–Sciama–Kibble theory of gravity with torsion [9], where the spin induces
  145| gravitational repulsion at extremely high densities, which prevents the collapse to a singularity and
  146| provides formation of a de Sitter core and the appearance of a closed universe.
  147| Oshita and Yokoyama [10] studied appearance of an inflationary universe beyond a wormhole
  148| throat, as initiated by a phase transition with symmetry restoration inside a thin-wall bubble around
  149| an evaporating black hole [11]. In this model, the thin-wall boundary of a false vacuum bubble induces
  150| quantum tunneling to a wormhole-like configuration, with exponentially expanding space beyond the
  151| wormhole throat filled with the false vacuum, interpreted as creation of a new inflationary universe in
  152| the final stage of the black hole evaporation.
  153| The comprehensive analysis of a universe production involving wormholes has been done by
  154| Ansoldi, Merali, and Guendelman [12] on the basis of the causal structure of spacetime and connection
  155| between vacuum decay and inflation, and including the question of creation of a baby universe within
  156| our universe on the other side of a wormhole throat in future particle accelerators [13] or spontaneously.
  157| The above models have also been based on the direct matching of the Schwarzschild and the de
  158| Sitter metrics using the thin shell approach (the global structure of spacetime is shown in Figure 1),
  159| which suggests that the whole dynamical evolution from the equation of state p = ρ = 0 (the
  160| Schwarzschild sector) to p = −ρ (the de Sitter sector) occurs within or nearby a very thin junction
  161| layer. The matched metrics typically have a jump at the junction surface [3].
  162| The case of a distributed density profile ρ(r ) smoothly evolving from a large internal density of
  163| the de Sitter vacuum, arising due to symmetry restoration, has been studied in a general setting in [14],
  164| where it was found that solutions describing de Sitter–Schwarzschild transition belong to the class of
  165| solutions to the Einstein equations with the source term such that

  573| It is known that a white hole can be described as a cosmological model (see, e.g., [27]). In the case
  574| of a Schwarzschild white hole it includes the spacelike singularity r = 0, entirely open for outside
  575| observers (Figure 2, left down); for this reason the existence of white holes (in a singular version) has
  576| been forbidden by the cosmic censorship [28].
  577| Replacing a Schwarzschild singularity with the de Sitter center transforms the spacelike singular
  578| surface r = 0 in the past of a (singular) white hole, into the timelike regular surface r = 0 (Figure 2,
  579| right), and rehabilitates a white hole in its regular version [26].
  580| A cosmological model related to a regular white hole (segment RC , W H, U in Figure 2, right)
  581| belongs to the cosmological models of the Lemaitre class with the de Sitter origin (for a review [21]).
  582| The regular white hole (more precisely its RC region) models the initial stages of a regular cosmology
  583| with the initial inflationary stage [26].
  584| It is evident that a new universe inside a regular black hole can emerge in any of an infinite
  585| number of BH regions with the de Sitter vacuum in their interiors. Geodesic structure of the de
  586| Sitter–Schwarzschild spacetime shows [29] that the possibility of traveling to other universes through
  587| a black hole, reported for the Reissner–Nordström and the Kerr geometry ([30] and references therein),
  588| does exist in the case of a regular black hole with the de Sitter interior [29].
  589| In the next Section, we estimate the probability of the arising of a closed world in the future of a
  590| black hole (as proposed in [7]) in the spacetime with the global structure shown in Figure 2 [26]. We also
  591| consider the additional option of a multiple quantum birth of open and flat causally disconnected
  592| universes from the initial quantum fluctuations arising in the RC regions.
  593| 3. Birth of Universes Inside a Black Hole
  594| In the general case of a Λ black hole with a distributed density profile and global structure
  595| of spacetime shown in Figure 2, the region near the timelike de Sitter surface r = 0 is part of the
  596| regular core RC , and can be considered as a small spherical bubble with the de Sitter vacuum trapped
  597| inside [17], which can be a seed for the quantum birth of a closed universe. The global structure of the
  598| de Sitter–Schwarzschild spacetime enables the arising of a closed world in any of an infinite number of
  599| BH structures in the future of a black hole in the original universe. This case is illustrated by Figure 3,
  600| left [26].
  601| Global structure of spacetime for the regular black hole provides additional possibilities related
  602| to instabilities of the de Sitter vacuum with respect to the quantum birth of a universe from
  603| nonperturbative quantum fluctuations of the metric [31–38].
  604| The regular cosmological model for a universe born from a quantum fluctuation in de Sitter space
  605| and evolving from the de Sitter stage to the radiation-dominated stage has been proposed in 1975
  606| in [39], where we also noticed the possibility of the multiple birth of causally disconnected universes
  607| from the de Sitter background. In 1982, a similar possibility has been studied in detail by Gott III as
  608| the creation of open FLRW universes in the quantum barrier penetration in de Sitter space, taking
  609| into account the Gibbons–Hawking radiation from the de Sitter horizon [40]. The case of the arising

 1153| 4. Conclusions
 1154| The models with the direct matching of the Schwarzschild metric outside with the de Sitter
 1155| metric inside correspond to the direct birth of a closed universe inside a black hole (Figure 1). In the
 1156| general case of the de Sitter–Schwarzschild geometry with a smooth transition to interior de Sitter
 1157| vacuum inside a black hole, the global structure of spacetime contains an infinite sequence of black
 1158| and white holes with their future and past regular cores RC asymptotically de Sitter as r → 0, and of
 1159| asymptotically flat universes U in the future of a regular black hole (Figure 2, right). In this spacetime
 1160| a quantum birth of a closed universe means closing this sequence in some of BH regions in the future
 1161| of the original black hole (Figure 3, left).
 1162| In the case of a regular black hole with the de Sitter interior, the global structure of spacetime
 1163| provides also the possibility of the multiple birth of flat and open causally disconnected universes
 1164| in the RC regions (Figure 3, right), arising from initial fluctuations which contain strings with the
 1165| negative deficit angle leading to the appearance of a potential barrier needed for quantum tunneling.
 1166| To evaluate the contribution of strings in calculating the probability of tunneling, we take into
 1167| account decay of the inflationary vacuum into radiation within a nucleating bubble. Presence of
 1168| radiation in the initial fluctuation corresponds to the quantum birth of a universe with a non-zero
 1169| quantized energy, which is related to the strings content within an initial fluctuation. The observational
 1170| constraint on the temperature anisotropy (known for our Universe) provides the restriction on the
 1171| contribution of strings.
 1172| The probability of quantum birth of a universe is much bigger in this case than the probability
 1173| of the birth of a closed universe from "nothing", for all three cases including an open, flat, and
 1174| closed universe.
 1175| We compare different possibilities and show that the most plausible case is the birth of a flat
 1176| universe (Ω = 1) from an initial quantum fluctuation with a very small (of the order of 10−6 ) admixture
 1177| of strings with the negative deficit angle.
 1178| The probability of the quantum birth of baby universes inside a Λ black hole is not negligible, due
 1179| to the existence of an infinite number of the appropriate RC regions inside a particular ΛBH. All this
 1180| makes it possible to speculate (at least not exclude) that our Universe could be located inside some
 1181| regular black hole with the de Sitter interior.
