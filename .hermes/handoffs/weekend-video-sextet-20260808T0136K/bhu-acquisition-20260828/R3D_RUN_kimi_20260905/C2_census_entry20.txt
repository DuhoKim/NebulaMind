   19|  We give a comparative description of different types of regular static, spherically symmetric black holes (BHs) and discuss in more detail their particular type, which we
   20| suggest to call black universes. The latter have a Schwarzschild-like
   21| causal structure, but inside the horizon there is an expanding Kantowski-Sachs universe and a de Sitter infinity instead of a singularity. Thus a
   22| hypothetic BH explorer gets a chance to survive. Solutions of this kind
   23| are naturally obtained if one considers static, spherically symmetric distributions of various
   24| (but not all) kinds of phantom matter whose existence is favoured by
   25| cosmological observations. It also looks possible that our Universe has
   26| originated from phantom-dominated collapse in another universe and
   27| underwent isotropization after crossing the horizon. An explicit example
   28| of a black-universe solution with positive Schwarzschild mass is
   29| discussed. 
   30|  1. Introduction 
   31|  One of the long-standing problems in black hole (BH) physics is the existence of
   32| curvature singularities beyond the event horizon in the BH solutions
   33| obtained under the simplest and the most natural physical conditions
   34| (the Schwarzschild, Reissner-Nordström, Kerr and Kerr-Newman solutions of general relativity and their
   35| counterparts in many alternative theories of gravity). Singularities are
   36| places where general relativity (or another classical theory of gravity)
   37| does not work. Therefore, a full understanding of BH physics requires
   38| avoidance of singularities or/and modification of the corresponding
   39| classical theory and addressing quantum effects. There have been numerous
   40| attempts on this trend, many of them suggesting that a singularity
   41| inside the event horizon should be replaced with a kind of regular core. 
   42|  In this paper, we discuss the possible geometry of classical nonsingular
   43| BHs, restricting ourselves to asymptotically flat static, spherically symmetric configurations. In Sec.  2. we enumerate and briefly describe different known types of
   44| regular BHs. The rest of the paper is devoted to their particular type
   45| which we suggest to call black universes, namely, those in which a
   46| possible BH explorer, after crossing the event horizon, gets into an
   47| expanding universe [ 1 ] . Such objects are naturally obtained if one
   48| considers local concentrations of dark energy in the form of phantom
   49| matter, favoured by modern cosmological observations [ 2 ] .
   50| Among theoretical reasons for considering phantom matter one may mention
   51| natural appearance of phantom fields in some models of string theory
   52|  [ 3 ] , supergravities [ 4 ] and theories in more than 11
   53| dimensions like F-theory [ 5 ] . To avoid the obvious quantum
   54| instability, a phantom scalar may perhaps be regarded as an effective
   55| field description following from an underlying theory with positive
   56| energies [ 6 ] . Curiously, a classical massless phantom field
   57| even shows a more stable behaviour than its usual counterpart
   58|  [ 8 , 7 ] . 
   59|  In Sec.  3. we demonstrate the existence of black-universe
   60| solutions in general relativity with minimally coupled phantom fields, formulate the
   61| requirements to the field potential needed for obtaining them and present
   62| a specific example. In Sec.  4. we obtain similar requirements in
   63| a more general framework, general k-essence, and show that many (though
   64| not all) forms of phantom matter discussed in the recent cosmological
   65| literature can also lead to black universes. Sec.  5. is a
   66| conclusion.

   85| of horizons and black holes (see, e.g., [ 9 ] ) and rely on the following

   88| asymptotic), (ii) another region invisible to an observer at rest
   89| residing in the static region, and (iii) a Killing horizon of nonzero

   91| of the metric from one region to another.

  378|  4. Black universes with other forms of phantom matter 
  379|  Besided the minimally coupled scalar field ( 3 ), there are a
  380| number of other suggestions for modelling the possible phantom
  381| behaviour of dark energy, see, e.g., the review [ 24 ] and
  382| references therein. 
  383|  Let us show that type 4 regular BHs, or black universes, are, under
  384| appropriate additional conditions, solutions to the field equations of
  385| at least two large classes of such theories: scalar-tensor theories
  386| (STT) of gravity, or theories with nonminimally coupled scalar fields,
  387| and the class of models called k-essence. We will show that
  388| black-universe solutions may be found in both these classes.

  577|  5. Conclusion 
  578|  In this paper, we have classified the possible geometries of static, spherically symmetric,
  579| asymptotically flat BHs and discussed in more detail their particular type, the
  580| black universes. Such hypothetical configurations combine the properties
  581| of a wormhole (absence of a centre, a regular minimum of the area
  582| function) and a black hole (a Killing horizon separating R and T regions). 
  583|  Quite evidently, such unusual objects require unusual matter for their
  584| existence. It turns out that they are naturally obtained if one
  585| considers static, spherically symmetric distributions of phantom matter, which is a subject
  586| of vast discussion in modern cosmology. We have considered a
  587| sufficiently general frameworks for the description of phantom matter,
  588| namely, STT of gravity and the so-called k-essence, and found necessary
  589| conditions for the existence of black-universe solutions. A conclusion
  590| is that a great number of reasonable phantom matter models produce such
  591| solutions. 
  592|  The latter lead to the idea that our Universe could appear from
  593| phantom-dominated collapse in another, “mother” universe and undergo
  594| isotropization (e.g., due to particle creation) soon after crossing the
  595| horizon. It is known that a Kantowski-Sachs nature of our Universe is not
  596| excluded observationally [ 34 ] if its isotropization had happened
  597| early enough, before the last scattering epoch (at redshifts z ≳ 1000 z\gtrsim 1000 ). One can notice that we are thus facing one more mechanism of
  598| universes multiplication, in addition to the well-known mechanism
  599| existing in the chaotic inflation scenario. 
  600|  Somewhat similar ideas on possible appearance of baby universes inside
  601| BHs have already been discussed from different standpoints
  602|  [ 35 , 36 ] (see also references therein), and some
  603| examples of two-dimensional regular BH metrics with properties
  604| more or less similar to ours are known [ 35 , 37 , 38 ] ,
  605| It can be remarked, however, that in two dimensions, where space is
  606| simply a line, the notion of a centre,
  607| which plays an essential role in the four-dimensional picture, cannot
  608| be properly introduced. So it seems that the black-universe solutions
  609| considered here and in Ref.  [ 1 ] are qualitatively new and (if
  610| certainly amended by adding realistic matter ingredients) may even
  611| lead to viable alternatives to the existing cosmological scenarios.
