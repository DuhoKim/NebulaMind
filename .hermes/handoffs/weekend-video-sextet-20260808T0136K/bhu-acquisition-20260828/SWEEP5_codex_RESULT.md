## Entry 1 — Pathria (1972)

**Token:** `CHALLENGE_CALIBRATED-FALSIFIER`

The body goes beyond a consistency construction: it derives bounded numerical intervals for the cosmological constant and present density, plus a lower bound on the present curvature radius, from observable values of the Hubble and deceleration parameters. These are explicit measurement-refutable thresholds (even though Pathria flags then-current 20–40% observational errors), so the full text meets the calibrated-falsifier criterion.

**Decisive quotes:**

> “these inequalities provide lower and upper bounds for the parameters Λ and ρ₀, and a lower bound for the parameter R₀, in terms of the observable quantities H₀ and q₀; so these parameters can be estimated from the kinematics of the universe alone.” (p. 299; clean-text lines 431–438)

> “using H₀ = 75 km s⁻¹ (Mpc⁻¹) and q₀ = 1 as ‘representative values’ / −6.7 × 10⁻⁵⁷ cm⁻² < Λ ≤ Λc ≤ 1.0 × 10⁻⁵⁷ cm⁻² / 1.5 × 10⁻²⁹ g cm⁻³ < ρ₀ ≤ 2.3 × 10⁻²⁹ g cm⁻³ / R₀ ≥ 1.1 × 10²⁸ cm.” (p. 299; clean-text lines 439–453; typography normalized from extraction)

## Entry 2 — Good (1972)

**Token:** `CONFIRM_CONSISTENCY_ONLY`

The one-page letter proposes a speculative, infinitely nested sequence of black/white-hole universes under continual creation. Its “physical probability one” follows from the stipulated infinite-time setup rather than a measured statistic or calibrated observational threshold; the author instead presents the idea as a consistent interpretation and an explanatory reconciliation of steady-state and big-bang pictures.

**Decisive quotes:**

> “The speculative theory that I shall put forward is of this form.” (p. 15; clean-text lines 75–82)

> “on the assumption of continual creation, a galaxy eventually becomes so heavy that it collapses into a black hole, so, in infinite time, we are certain to be in a hole (with physical probability one).” (p. 15; clean-text lines 93–100)

> “the present theory interprets collapsed galaxies, not elementary particles, as subuniverses, and it is intended to resolve the conflict between the big-bang and steady-state theories of the origin of the universe. Although the theory seems grandiose, as far as I can see it is the only possible consistent interpretation of the steady-state concept” (p. 15; clean-text lines 122–138)

## Entry 4 — Knutsen (2009)

**Token:** `CHALLENGE_THEORETICAL-OBSTRUCTION`

Knutsen himself derives the necessary and sufficient smooth-junction conditions for an FLRW perfect-fluid interior and false-vacuum exterior. Applying them to Pathria’s required boundary choice, he obtains a static boundary coincident with the event horizon, and therefore proves that this construction cannot represent an expanding universe. This is an owned junction no-go, not merely criticism or citation of another theorem.

**Decisive quotes:**

> “We want to obtain the sufficient and necessary conditions for smooth matching across the surface Σ of the interior region V− with a perfect fluid and the vacuum exterior V+.” (p. 275; clean-text lines 516–523)

> “The interior region with an FLRW metric and a perfect fluid is matched smoothly with the exterior false vacuum only if the pressure vanishes at the surface.” (p. 276; clean-text lines 1009–1016)

> “Pathria’s choice rb = 1 yields [Eq. (44)] … Hence, this choice of boundary value for the comoving radial coordinate just means that the fluid sphere is static, and the surface of the fluid sphere merges with its event horizon. Thus this particular model cannot describe our expanding universe.” (p. 277; clean-text lines 1063–1088)

## Entry 13 — Frolov–Markov–Mukhanov (1989)

**Token:** `CONFIRM_CONSISTENCY_ONLY`

The paper constructs a Schwarzschild-to-de Sitter black-hole interior conditional on assumed limiting curvature and vacuum-like high-curvature behavior. Its Planck-scale quantities set internal model scales, not an observable statistic of the daughter universe or a calibrated empirical discriminator; indeed, the authors say the governing modified equations are unknown and the core assumption cannot be verified. The daughter universe’s fate is presented as alternative possibilities.
