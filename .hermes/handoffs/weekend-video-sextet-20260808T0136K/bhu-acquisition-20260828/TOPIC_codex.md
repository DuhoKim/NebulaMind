## Topic 1

1. **TITLE — Does raising the neutron-star maximum mass reduce black-hole production? A population-synthesis test of cosmological natural selection (entries 6, 7, 31).**
2. **THE CLAIM —** Holding the empirically constrained stellar population fixed, increasing the maximum neutron-star mass through Smolin's live threshold decreases the expected number of astrophysical black holes, so the threshold is connected to the quantity cosmological natural selection says is optimized.
3. **FALSIFIER —** Run a pre-registered stellar/binary population synthesis while varying only the maximum neutron-star mass and marginalizing over the observationally allowed nuisance parameters: the claim is wrong if the total black-hole yield is non-decreasing across the published 2.5 M☉ “certain refutation” bar (`BHU_CORPUS_SYNTHESIS_20260902.md`, lines 60–71, 158–159), or if the sign reverses anywhere in the posterior-supported region. This directly meets the published Rothman–Ellis requirement that every parameter change reduce black-hole production, the corpus's stated unresolved challenge (`BHU_CORPUS_SYNTHESIS_20260902.md`, lines 67–70), rather than merely rechecking the present mass measurement, 2.35 ± 0.11 M☉ and 1.36σ below the bar (`BHU_CORPUS_SYNTHESIS_20260902.md`, lines 63–66).
4. **COST —** Estimated 20 seat-days; COMPAS (or an equivalently open population-synthesis code), public pulsar-mass likelihoods, LVK compact-object population posteriors, and empirical stellar initial-mass-function, metallicity, and binary-fraction constraints; moderate cluster compute for the nuisance-parameter ensemble. (The seat-day figure is a prospective planning estimate, not a corpus result.)
5. **GAPS CLOSED —** Entry 31 `W_BORROWED`/DISPUTED and entry 6 `W_DIRECTION_ASSUMED`: it derives or falsifies the missing selection-function link between a neutron-star mass cap and total black-hole yield; these are the recorded tokens and premises (`WARRANT_TABLE_20260903.md`, lines 8, 47). It also sharpens the only live direct observational falsifier in the corpus (`BHU_CORPUS_SYNTHESIS_20260902.md`, lines 156–164).
6. **SCORES — CONTESTED 5/5:** the record contains multiple published challenges to the selection inference and calls the warrant DISPUTED (`BHU_CORPUS_SYNTHESIS_20260902.md`, lines 67–70). **TRACTABLE 4/5:** established population-synthesis machinery and public population constraints make the sign test finite, although astrophysical nuisance degeneracies prevent a perfect score. **FRONTIER = 20.**
7. **RISKS —** Supernova-engine, metallicity, and binary-evolution uncertainties may permit both derivative signs, leaving the selection claim unidentified rather than supported or refuted.
