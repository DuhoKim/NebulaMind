TOPIC 1 (STRONGEST) — Closing the amplitude freedom of the causal-horizon cutoff: an amplitude-bounded S_1/2 or a no-go theorem

(1) Question: The causal condition fixes where the primordial spectrum cuts off (theta~60 deg, ell~3) but not the covariance of the surviving perturbations. Can the class of physically admissible stochastic completions — initial states for perturbations selected by the parent-interior/bounce geometry — be bounded tightly enough to turn a scale-fixed prediction into an amplitude prediction? Specifically: can ANY admissible completion reach the observed S_1/2 ~1150 uK^4 while keeping the ell~3 cutoff sharp, given that principled completions currently land ~6000-22000?

(2) Why frontier: This is the exact open problem the audit flagged. Nothing in the BHU literature derives the initial state — camps assume BD-like, de Sitter-invariant, or alpha-vacua ad hoc. The model currently explains the low quadrupole only in the weakest sense (low power "allowed"), and the existing band sits a factor ~5-20 ABOVE the observed value, so the honest status is "unfalsified but uncalibrated." Whoever bounds the admissible class either kills or arms the one real a-priori BHU prediction.

(3) Tractability: theory + public data only, no telescope time. Step a: enumerate the admissible two-point functions subject to minimal axioms — causal support at the parent's trapped surface, Hadamard/positive-energy behavior at the bounce or inner horizon, normalization to A_s at ell>~30 (outside the affected range). Step b: compute S_1/2, S_1/4, and full C(theta) for the boundary members of the class, i.e. an allowed band, not a point. Step c: confront with Planck NPIPE (SMICA/Commander) low-ell maps. Held-out predictions: the C(theta) shape past 60 deg (zero-crossing, anti-correlation knee), octupole planarity, and the low-ell E-mode spectrum the same completion implies — LiteBIRD will be cosmic-variance-limited there.

(4) Result-shape: a calibrated falsifier with two publishable endpoints. Endpoint A (no-go): a theorem that no Hadamard positive-energy completion gets S_1/2 below some bound B > 1150 uK^4 with a sharp ell~3 cutoff — the causal-cutoff explanation of the low quadrupole is then dead in its current form. Endpoint B (calibration): a specific state-selection principle (e.g. regularity across the bounce surface) yields a unique covariance with S_1/2 in a stated interval plus a C(theta) template testable now. Either endpoint is a result; the program cannot return nothing.

(5) Non-circularity + risks: the covariance is derived from the state axioms plus the high-ell normalization — never fit to the low-ell statistics it is then tested against; the low-ell outputs are pure prediction, and the C(theta) tail and E-mode spectrum are explicitly held out. Secondary risk: referees contest what counts as "physically admissible" — mitigate by stating axioms minimally and showing the band is stable when each axiom is relaxed one at a time.

TOPIC 2 — Spin-parity dipole pinned to a-priori CMB axes (the rotating-parent test)

(1) Question: A rotating parent imprints exactly one preferred axis. If that BHU variant is right, the same axis must appear in CMB hemispherical power asymmetry / low-ell alignment AND as a dipole in galaxy spin handedness. Does the handedness of ~10^6-10^7 spirals show a dipole along any of a small set of CMB-fixed axes, with redshift-independent amplitude?

(2) Why frontier: existing spin-dipole claims (Shamir-McAdam, Longo) are actively contested — null reanalyses blame PSF/depth/photo-z gradients and annotation asymmetry, and every claimed axis was fit a posteriori (look-elsewhere kills the evidence). The missing, decisive design is: axes fixed from the CMB BEFORE touching spin data, plus z-tomography as a systematics veto. That cross-probe coincidence requirement is the new content; a single-probe dipole is not.

(3) Tractability: all public. Spin catalogs (Shamir's DESI-LS/Galaxy Zoo compilations, VizieR), DECaLS imaging for algorithmic re-classification, NPIPE for the axes (power-asymmetry dipole, ell=2-3 alignment axis, mirror-parity axis — cap at 3 to bound trials). Pipeline: healpix dipole estimator along fixed axes, dipole amplitude in z and magnitude slices, null suite (spin randomization, mirrored images, catalog jackknife).

(4) Result-shape: measured asymmetry with threshold: D_axis +/- sigma on each pre-registered axis. BHU-consistent detection requires significance on a CMB axis AND z/magnitude independence (primordial imprint vs. systematics, which grow with z and depth) AND catalog independence. Otherwise a bound D < few x 10^-3 (95%) on the BHU-preferred axes — a real contribution given the contested +/-1-2% claims. A real dipole NOT on any CMB axis is itself a no-go against the rotating-parent variant.

(5) Non-circularity + risks: instrumental and annotation systematics cannot know the CMB axis; only a primordial imprint aligns the probes — that is the non-circular core. Methodological risk: human-annotation handedness bias — mitigate with algorithmic classifiers cross-checked against human labels on subsamples. Against the "z~0 anchor" bar: the novelty is the a-priori-axis design and the z-independence veto, not catalog assembly, and the contested claims are precisely at these depths.
