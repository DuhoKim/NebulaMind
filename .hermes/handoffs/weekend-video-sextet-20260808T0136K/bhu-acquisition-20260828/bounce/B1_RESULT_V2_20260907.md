## AMENDMENT B (2026-09-07 14:02 KST) — after the independent challenge (`KIMI_B2_CHALLENGE_20260907.md`, B2_REVIEW=AMENDMENT_STANDS)
The amendment survived, but three things in it were wrong or weak and are corrected here. The referee re-derived every step and
attacked the gluing point hardest; the attempt strengthened the result rather than breaking it.

**1. My justification was a non-sequitur, and the missing lemma is now stated.** I wrote that the branch carries no bounce
because "$H$ is constant and non-zero on that branch, so it never crosses zero". Read strictly that does not answer the real
objection: a branch with no turning point can still be *visited* by a bouncing trajectory that departs it. The correct argument,
supplied by the referee and re-derived from the filed assumptions (smooth functions on a connected open interval, $T>0$,
$\Psi=\beta H^4$ with fixed $\beta>0$):

> Where the first-law factor is non-zero, $E=0$ gives $\dot T=-HT$, and (37) then forces $\beta H^4=0$, so $H\equiv0$ there.
> On the interior of $\{T=T_\star\}$, (37) gives $\beta H^3=3n_\star$, so $H\equiv H_\star>0$. Hence $\{H\neq0\}$ is open and
> $H\equiv H_\star$ on each component; a component with an interior endpoint would need $H\to H_\star\neq0$ and $H=0$ at the
> endpoint at once. So $\{H\neq0\}$ is empty or everything. And $H\equiv0$ is impossible in both geometries — in Kantowski–Sachs
> it forces $v=0$ and then $\dot v=-K/3\neq0$; in flat Bianchi I it forces $\epsilon=0$, i.e. $T=0$, excluded.

**Therefore no solution of the printed system ever crosses $H=0$, and there is nothing to glue a bouncing solution to.** That
is the argument the conclusion actually rests on, and it was absent from both documents until now.

**2. My narrowing to "the bouncing sector" was an UNDERCLAIM, and inconsistent with my own preceding sentence.** The algebra
excludes **every evolving interval solution** of the printed thermal specialisation at fixed $\beta>0$ — bouncing or not, the
static case included — and at any $\beta$ off the single tuned value the printed Kantowski–Sachs system has **no smooth interval
solutions at all**. The three loopholes I asked the referee to try are closed: production cannot switch on and off, because
under $\Psi=\beta H^4$ it vanishes exactly where $H$ does; a non-zero-production set of measure zero must be empty, because the
set is open; and any evolving-temperature interval forces $T=T_\star$ wherever production is non-zero. So `B1_ILL_POSED` is not
confined to bouncing solutions: it is the printed specialisation admitting **only** the tuned steady state.

**3. "Exactly one compatibility condition" is right only at function level.** After that condition is imposed, the steady state
additionally requires a parameter-level tuning of $\beta$ — $\beta=81n_\star/\Lambda_\star^{3/2}$ in Kantowski–Sachs and
$9\sqrt3\,n_\star/\Lambda_\star^{3/2}$ in flat Bianchi I, a ratio of $3\sqrt3$. Both were disclosed in the tuning relations
already filed, but "exactly ONE compatibility condition" would mislead if read alone. There are two: one functional, one on the
constants.

**4. Scope, restated here rather than incorporated by reference.** The exclusion rests on smoothness AND on the fixed
$\Psi=\beta H^4$ prescription. **With a free $\Psi(t)$, or a time-dependent $\beta$, the evolving branch $\dot T=-HT$ exists and
a continuous gluing at a tuned instant is not excluded by the matter equations.** A reader of this file alone could otherwise
over-read "every bouncing solution is excluded" as prescription-independent. It is not.

**5. Terminology.** "de Sitter-like" is hedged but loose: the Kantowski–Sachs realisation carries constant non-zero shear and a
constant transverse scale factor, so it is not constant-curvature de Sitter. Read it as "constant-$H$ steady state".
