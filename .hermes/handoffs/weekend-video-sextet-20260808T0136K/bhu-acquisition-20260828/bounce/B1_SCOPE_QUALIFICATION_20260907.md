# B1 — scope qualification: what "fine-tuned" may and may not mean here, and to which version the inconsistency is bound
**Tori, 2026-09-07 13:17 KST. Attaches to `B1_RESULT_V2_20260907.md`. Written in response to Codex's 13:15 interpretation checks, which are correct.**

## 1. The label, corrected to what the algebra supports
The pre-registered class name is `B1_DEGENERATE_FINE_TUNED`, and a class name declared before computing cannot be renamed
afterwards without defeating the point of declaring it. But **the class name is not the physical claim**, and the physical claim
must be stated as what was actually shown:

**What is shown.** For the Weyssenhoff fluid in flat Bianchi I without production, a bounce occurs iff
$\kappa\alpha_F n_0^2 > \Sigma^2$; equivalently, with the dimensionless $\rho \equiv \Sigma^2/(\kappa\alpha_F n_0^2)$, iff
$\rho < 1$. **Both terms scale as $a^{-6}$, so $\rho$ is CONSTANT along the evolution.** That is the substantive result, and
it is a statement about the dynamics, not about probability: **the bounce condition cannot be reached dynamically. A collapsing
region that starts on the wrong side of the threshold stays there, however far it collapses.** That is what distinguishes this
from a condition the collapse itself could drive you into, and it is why the threshold matters.

**What is NOT shown, and is now explicitly disclaimed.** A strict inequality between two initial-data constants is a conditional
threshold. It is not, by itself, a measure of fine-tuning, improbability, or non-genericity in any technical sense. Establishing
that would require a measure on the space of initial data $(n_0,\Sigma^2,\epsilon_0,\alpha_F)$, and **no such measure is
defined here, nor is one obviously canonical** — the usual difficulty of measures on cosmological initial data applies in full.
So: **wherever V2 or its summary suggests improbability, read it as the threshold statement above.** The words "not generic" are
used in the restricted sense "not attained dynamically from data on the wrong side", and nowhere in the sense "of small measure".
Any future claim of small measure must define the measure first and justify it, and would be a separate result.

## 3. Incompatibility under declared assumptions is not failure of a family of models
The inconsistency filed is between printed equations of one paper under one declared thermal specialisation. It is not a claim
that Einstein–Cartan bounce models generally fail, that particle production cannot rescue a bounce, or that no consistent
production sector exists. Whether a nonzero-production branch survives the conflict is being computed now (B2) rather than
assumed; if such a branch exists, the ill-posedness is confined to the generic branch and the filing narrows accordingly.
