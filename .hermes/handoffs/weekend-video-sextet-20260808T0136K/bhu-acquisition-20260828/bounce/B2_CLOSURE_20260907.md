Full field-equation compatibility restricts that branch differently in curved Kantowski–Sachs (KS) and flat Bianchi I (BI). With a specified production prescription, closure cases **(b) and (c) are determined**; case (d) needs a pressure equation of state.

## 1. Simultaneous equations: FLUID matter sector

For curved KS use \(V=XY^2\), \(3H=\dot V/V\). For the separate flat BI reduction use \(V=a_1a_2a_3\), \(3H=\sum_i\dot a_i/a_i\). In each geometry separately, expansion of A (14) with the effective FLUID source, and A (34)'s production prescription, gives

\[
E:=\dot\epsilon-2\alpha n\dot n+3H(\epsilon+p-2\alpha n^2)=0,
\qquad N:=\dot n+3Hn-\Psi=0.
\]

Our identity is \(E=\dot\epsilon+3H(\epsilon+p)-2\alpha n\Psi-2\alpha nN\). Thus imposing number balance gives \(\dot\epsilon+3H(\epsilon+p)=2\alpha n\Psi\); it does not supply a pressure equation of state.

With all three thermal relations in A §9, our substitution into A (14), followed by A (37), gives

\[
E=(4h_\star T^3-6\alpha h_n^2T^5)(\dot T+HT),
\qquad E\big|_{(37)}=\frac{2\Psi T}{3h_n}(2h_\star-3\alpha h_n^2T^2).
\]

Hence on an interval with \(\Psi\ne0\), in the curved KS FLUID system, or separately in the flat BI FLUID reduction,

\[
T=T_*:=\sqrt{\frac{2h_\star}{3\alpha h_n^2}},\quad
n=n_*:=h_nT_*^3,\quad \dot T=\dot n=0,\quad
\Psi=3Hn_*=\beta H^4.
\]

Therefore \(H=H_*=(3n_*/\beta)^{1/3}>0\) is constant, \(V=V_0e^{3H_*(t-t_0)}\), and \(n_*V\) grows even though local number density is constant. These equations describe an expanding production interval, not a contracting interval or a bounce. With a free production function instead, the two matter equations require only \(\Psi=3H(t)n_*\); constant \(H\) follows specifically from the fixed-\(\beta\) prescription.

Our effective-source values are

\[
\alpha n_*^2=\frac23\epsilon_*,\quad
\rho_*=\frac13\epsilon_*>0,\quad P_*=-\rho_*,\quad
\Lambda_*:=\kappa\rho_*=\frac{4\kappa h_\star^3}{27\alpha^2h_n^4}.
\]

The first law is satisfied exactly. A (17), treated as its printed entropy evolution relation, also admits \(s(t)=2\alpha n_*^2/T_*+C_s/V(t)\). Its residual \(T_*\,d(sV)/dt-2\alpha n_*\Psi V\) vanishes. No additional entropy closure is needed to exhibit this solution.

### Curved KS FLUID: compatibility with the remaining equations of A

Set \(u=\dot X/X\), \(v=\dot Y/Y\), \(K=Y^{-2}>0\), and \(S=\sigma^2=(u-v)^2/3\). Rewriting A (10) gives the constraint and two spatial evolution equations:

\[
C_K=v^2+2uv+K-\kappa\rho=0,
\quad \dot v=-\tfrac12(3v^2+K+\kappa P),
\quad \dot u=-u^2-uv+\tfrac12(v^2+K-\kappa P).
\]

Our constant-\(H\), constant-source consequences are
\(S=(\Lambda_*-3H_*^2)/2\) and \(K=3(\Lambda_*-3H_*^2)/2\).
Both are constant. Since \(\dot K=-2vK\) and \(K>0\), \(v=0\). The spatial equations then force \(K=u^2=\Lambda_*\). Thus the exact curved KS branch is

\[
\boxed{Y=\Lambda_*^{-1/2},\quad
X=X_0e^{\sqrt{\Lambda_*}(t-t_0)},\quad
H_*=\sqrt{\Lambda_*}/3,\quad
\beta=81n_*/\Lambda_*^{3/2}.}
\]

Its shear is \(S=\Lambda_*/3\), not zero. The script verifies A (10)'s constraint and both spatial equations, A (11), first law (14), entropy relation (17), number law (34), and temperature law (37); A (36) is the same verified constraint after thermal substitution and A (16) is the definition of \(H\). All residuals vanish. Thus a full curved KS FLUID solution exists at this parameter tuning. For fixed positive parameters away from this tuning there is no such nonzero-production interval under these assumptions. This is not a trajectory matching A §9's suggested collapsing initial radial velocity; those suggested initial data are not additional field equations.

### Flat BI FLUID: separate compatibility calculation

For our flat BI reduction define \(H_i=\dot a_i/a_i\), \(3H=\sum H_i\), \(S=\tfrac12\sum(H_i-H)^2\). The isotropic effective FLUID Einstein equations give

\[
C_B=H_1H_2+H_1H_3+H_2H_3-\kappa\rho=0,
\qquad \dot H_i=\frac\kappa2(\rho-P)-3HH_i.
\]

Our shear identity is \(\dot S=-6HS\). The constant-source, constant-\(H\) Raychaudhuri equation makes \(S\) constant, so \(H>0\) forces \(S=0\). Hence this separate branch is

\[
\boxed{a_i=a_{i0}e^{\sqrt{\Lambda_*/3}(t-t_0)},\quad
H_* = \sqrt{\Lambda_*/3},\quad
\beta=9\sqrt3\,n_*/\Lambda_*^{3/2}.}
\]

It lies in the isotropic subset of flat BI; there is no strictly nonzero-shear flat BI branch on this production interval with fixed \(\beta\). Its constraint and three directional equations have zero residuals. Its parameter tuning is different from the curved KS tuning; the two geometries cannot be interchanged.

`SPECIAL_BRANCH=EXISTS` means the FLUID simultaneous equations have a branch, including full curved KS and separate flat BI realisations at their respective tunings. It does not mean one exists for every prescribed \(\beta\), or that either solution undergoes a bounce.

## 2. Independent equations and closure count

1. \(\dot X=uX\), \(\dot Y=vY\).
2. The two spatial evolution equations written above from A (10).
3. First law \(E=0\) and production \(N=0\), with specified \(\Psi=\beta H^4\).

This is six evolution equations. \(K=1/Y^2\), \(H=(u+2v)/3\), and \(\Psi\) are definitions, not extra unknown functions. Impose \(C_K=0\) on initial data. Our symbolic identity \(\dot C_K=-3HC_K\) proves propagation under this evolution system. Do not count first law (14) again as an independent equation alongside the full field system and the time derivative of its constraint.

For the **separate flat BI FLUID reduction**, geometry unknowns are \(a_1,a_2,a_3,H_1,H_2,H_3\). Our identity for this chosen off-constraint evolution is \(\dot C_B=-6HC_B\). The differing off-constraint factors do not change either constraint's propagation. This is a flat derivation using A's FLUID source, not a substitution of zero curvature into a claimed printed BI equation.

## 3. Precisely what extra physical input is needed

For **curved KS FLUID case (d)** the missing input is **one specified pressure equation of state \(p=\mathcal P(\epsilon,n)\)** (smooth on the chosen state domain). For the **separate flat BI FLUID case (d)** the same type of input closes that geometry's equations independently. Choosing \(p=\epsilon/3\) is one concrete possible added assumption: then the first law evolves \(\epsilon\), the prescribed production law evolves \(n\), and geometry evolves from its own spatial equations. Energy conservation alone does not choose this EOS.

If temperature is also required in **curved KS FLUID (d)**, supply a caloric relation \(T=\Theta(\epsilon,n)\) in addition to the pressure EOS. The **flat BI FLUID (d)** temperature extension independently needs the same type of caloric input. An entropy-based thermodynamic constitutive relation may instead supply these relations, but simply appending A (17) introduces \(s\) while giving one entropy evolution equation; it does not determine an otherwise free temperature or pressure.

For **curved KS FLUID (c)**, if \(F\) has not actually been supplied, the missing input is the function \(\epsilon=F(T)\), including its parameters. The **flat BI FLUID (c)** count has the same qualification independently. Stating that such a function exists is insufficient to predict \(T\).

All table rows already include a specified production law. If **curved KS FLUID** instead uses only the balance definition \(\Psi:=\dot n+3Hn\), it needs a prescribed source function or a matter-model production equation that determines \(\Psi\). The **flat BI FLUID** balance-only system independently needs that source input. A derivation of \(\Psi\) from a matter model would improve physical grounding, but is not an additional equation required by the table when A's phenomenological fixed-\(\beta\) prescription is accepted. Conversely, deriving only \(\Psi\) does not supply case (d)'s missing pressure EOS.
