**3. Explicit turning condition in the source's variables.** A regular volume turnaround has $X,Y>0$, finite matter and geometry, and

\[
\frac{\dot X}{X}+2\frac{\dot Y}{Y}=0.
\]

It is a contracting-to-expanding crossing precisely when

\[
\boxed{\kappa\alpha n_f^2>
\frac13\left(\frac{\dot X}{X}-\frac{\dot Y}{Y}\right)^2
+\frac1{Y^2}
=\frac{\Delta^2}{3}+K.}
\tag{B7.7}
\]

Then $\dot H=(\kappa\alpha n_f^2-S-K)/3>0$, and $\ddot a=a\dot H>0$ at $H=0$. This is a minimum of $a=(XY^2)^{1/3}$, not necessarily simultaneous minima of both directional scale factors. In particular, the positive-$\Delta$ example below has $\dot X>0$ and $\dot Y<0$ at the volume minimum.

The source's $\beta$ is absent from the **instantaneous** inequality because $\Psi=\beta H^4=0$ at the crossing for every finite $\beta$. Its accumulated effect is explicit through number balance:

\[
n_f(t)=\frac{n_{f0}X_0Y_0^2+
\dfrac\beta{81}\int_{t_0}^{t}XY^2
\left(\dfrac{\dot X}{X}+2\dfrac{\dot Y}{Y}\right)^4dt'}{XY^2}.
\]

Consequently (B7.7), at the zero of the volume expansion, is equivalently the following inequality carrying all requested source variables and $\beta$:

\[
\boxed{\kappa\alpha\left[n_{f0}X_0Y_0^2+
\frac\beta{81}\int_{t_0}^{t_b}XY^2
\left(\frac{\dot X}{X}+2\frac{\dot Y}{Y}\right)^4dt'\right]^2
>(X_bY_b^2)^2\left(\frac{\Delta_b^2}{3}+K_b\right).}
\tag{B7.8}
\]

This is a trajectory-dependent criterion, not a numerical lower bound on $\beta$ obtainable without solving the geometry. Away from $H=0$, the stronger condition for $\dot H>0$ is

\[
\kappa\alpha n_f^2>S+K+6H^2
=\frac{\Delta^2}{3}+K+
\frac23\left(\frac{\dot X}{X}+2\frac{\dot Y}{Y}\right)^2.
\tag{B7.9}
\]

Equality in (B7.7) is not silently counted as a turn. At $H=0$, $C=0$ and $\kappa\alpha n^2=S+K$, differentiating the same smooth system gives $\ddot H=-4K\Delta/9$. For $\Delta\ne0$, the leading term in $H(t_b+\tau)$ is quadratic and there is no sign-changing crossing. For $\Delta=0$, $\ddot H=0$ and $H^{(3)}=-4K^2/9<0$: the cubic crossing is from expansion to contraction. These symbolic boundary checks are in the script. Thus a degenerate equality does not hide a regular contracting-to-expanding crossing in these closures.

**5. Shear versus spin, and comparison with publisher (35).** The established KS identity is retained exactly. With $N=Vn_f$ and $D=V\Delta$,

\[
\dot N=\beta VH^4,\qquad \dot D=VK=X,\qquad
\frac{d(V^2S)}{dt}=\frac23V^2K\Delta.
\]

For $n_f>0$, $\Delta\ne0$, the actual dimensionless spin/shear ratio obeys

\[
R=\frac{\kappa\alpha n_f^2}{S}
=\frac{3\kappa\alpha N^2}{D^2},\qquad
\boxed{\frac{\dot R}{R}
=2\left(\frac{\beta H^4}{n_f}-\frac K\Delta\right).}
\tag{B7.11}
\]

For the positive-$\Delta$ branch, shear outpaces spin fractionally if $\beta H^4/n_f<K/\Delta$; production makes spin gain fractionally if the inequality reverses. In source variables the latter condition is

\[
\frac{\beta}{81n_f}\left(\frac{\dot X}{X}
+2\frac{\dot Y}{Y}\right)^4>\frac{1}{Y^2\Delta},
\qquad \Delta>0.
\tag{B7.12}
\]

For $\Delta<0$, both terms in (B7.11) increase $R$ when $\beta\ge0$; this alone does not establish a future turn. At $\Delta=0$, $R$ is undefined, while the signed equation $\dot\Delta+3H\Delta=K$ stays regular. During contraction on $\Delta>0$, both shear and spin can grow; the sign in (B7.11) compares their fractional growth, not whether either decreases absolutely.

**7. Regular events, actual singular behaviour, and failures are different.** Every reported positive-production turn has finite positive scale factors and ordinary matter variables, a small constraint residual, a strictly positive turn margin, and a successful continuation to $H>0$. The KS Kretschmann scalar used for diagnostics is

\[
\mathcal K=4\left[(\dot u+u^2)^2+2(\dot v+v^2)^2
+2(uv)^2+(v^2+K)^2\right].
\tag{B7.15}
\]

It is finite on these integrated intervals. Its large finite value in the representative example (of order $10^8$ in the chosen dimensionless units) is not labelled a singularity. The short post-turn continuation proves a regular local crossing, not completeness of the future solution.

For a separate **zero-production diagnostic control**, take the main positive-$\Delta$ initial datum and set $B=0$. This is the limit with production switched off, not a claim about B2's positive-production assumption. There is an analytic finite-time curvature obstruction, rather than merely a solver failure:

1. $\Delta_0>0$ stays positive because $d(V\Delta)/dt=X>0$. With $\beta=0$, $R$ decreases and $R_0=1/30<1$ implies $w=\kappa\alpha n^2<S$ throughout a regular contracting interval.
2. Raychaudhuri then gives $\dot H=-H^2-2(S-w)/3-\kappa\epsilon/3<-H^2$. Starting with $H_0<0$, contraction cannot turn. A regular solution cannot persist past $t_0+1/\lvert H_0\rvert $ without an unbounded contraction rate or an earlier breakdown.
3. This cannot be only a removable coordinate degeneration with all curvature bounded. Since $v=H-\Delta/3<0$, $Y$ decreases, while $V$ also decreases. A bound on (B7.15) bounds $K$ and $v$. The squared Ricci scalar is $4\kappa^2\alpha^2n^4=4w^2$, independent of the Ricci-sign convention; bounded curvature therefore bounds $n$. Since $Vn=V_0n_0>0$, $V$ is bounded away from zero. Then $X=VK$ is bounded, and $\Delta=(V_0\Delta_0+\int Xdt)/V$ is bounded on a finite interval. So $u,v,H,\epsilon=\epsilon_0(V_0/V)^{4/3}$ are bounded and the smooth system continues, contradicting step 2. Curvature must instead become unbounded in finite proper time.

This proves genuine singular behaviour for the zero-production control's KS geometry/matter evolution. In closure (b) its positive-temperature branch remains regular until this limit. In closure (c) it is a full temperature-domain statement only if the specified inverse branch continues over the required energies; if that branch ends earlier, report a closure-domain boundary rather than claim that boundary itself is a spacetime singularity.
