# The bounce route — what one day of bounded work established
**Tori, 2026-09-07 16:23 KST. For Duho. Selected route: can a collapsing black hole become an expanding universe under varied conditions, or only
under carefully chosen assumptions? Every claim below is filed, adversarially reviewed on an engine that did not produce it, and
carries its own scope.**

## The short answer
**Under the published mechanism, collapse does turn around — but only after you replace the paper's own thermal bookkeeping, and
every physical assumption the mechanism needs is unverified exactly where it does its work.**

## The four results, in the order they were established
1. **Without particle production, the bounce needs a condition that collapse cannot reach.** In the flat anisotropic case the
   shear term and the spin-torsion term both fall as the inverse sixth power of the scale factor, so a bounce happens exactly
   when one constant exceeds another, and their ratio never changes as the region collapses. A region starting on the wrong
   side stays there forever. This is a corroboration, not a discovery: the same structure is in published work from the 1970s
   that the source itself cites. What we added is that the conclusion holds for **every** value of the spin coupling, so it
   survives our own earlier finding that the coupling's value is uncontrolled at the bounce.
2. **The paper's own printed thermal closure admits no bouncing solution at all.** Its first law and its temperature law can
   both hold only on a single tuned branch with constant expansion, which never crosses zero — so nothing smooth bounces there.
   The obstruction is one compatibility condition plus a coefficient tuning, not a flat contradiction, and it is confined to
   that closure.
3. **With a consistent closure, particle production rescues the bounce, and provably.** Because production stops the particle
   number diluting as fast, the torsion term outruns shear during contraction. There is an analytic finite-bounce proof for
   every coupling and every fixed positive production coefficient, in both determined closures — not a grid observation. So the
   mechanism's core claim survives; the part that failed was the paper's own bookkeeping.
4. **But every assumption it rests on is unestablished in the regime where it operates.** The macroscopic spin-fluid averaging,
   the ideal thermal description, the unpolarised spin average that the whole shear argument depends on, and the extrapolation
   of the production law through an entire collapse: an adversarial search on a different engine tried to break all four by
   finding published work that establishes any of them, and returned **absences hold**. It also found, uncited by the source,
   a published result showing that shear does induce spin structure in a relativistic fluid — the very effect that would spoil
   the unpolarised average — while its integrated response is insignificant in that setting. Whether it transfers to this
   regime is not established.

## What this is and is not
It is a **conditional mathematical result plus a precise feasibility obstruction**. It does not prove or disprove
black-hole-universe cosmology, does not establish that the source law or the effective fluid is valid at these densities, and
constructs no global black-hole-to-universe spacetime. It concerns the Weyssenhoff spin-fluid row in flat anisotropic geometry;
the Dirac row and the curved geometry the source actually solves are carried separately and never spoken for.

## The one sentence that matters for deciding what comes next
**The mechanism works in exactly the regime where nobody has shown its ingredients are valid** — the bounce occurs where the
spin correction is at least the entire ordinary energy density, and that is precisely where the fluid averaging, the thermal
description and the unpolarised-spin assumption all lack an established validity window.

## What would move it, if you want the route continued
- **Spin polarisation under shear** is the load-bearing gap and the most tractable: if shear polarises spins, an anisotropic
  stress appears and result 1's shear argument changes. There is now a published handle on it.
- A **microscopically derived production law** would replace the ansatz that result 3 extrapolates; the source's own cited work
  defers that derivation explicitly.
- Neither needs new machinery, and neither is started. They are the two named next steps.
