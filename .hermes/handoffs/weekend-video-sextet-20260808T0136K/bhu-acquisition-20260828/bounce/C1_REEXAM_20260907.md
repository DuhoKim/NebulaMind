## C1_FLUID: recovery of its own smooth isotropic bounce

Test: after setting shear and production to zero in the earlier Bianchi I extension, do the constraint, acceleration, thermal evolution, and number relation agree with a flat isotropic solution with H=0 and ä>0?

The mean equations of that extension, using GRG Eq. (1)'s source and Eq. (31)'s Raychaudhuri equation, are

\[
3H^2=\kappa\rho_F+\sigma^2,\qquad
\dot H+H^2=-\frac23\sigma^2-\frac\kappa6(\rho_F+3P_F).
\]

At σ²=0 these coincide term by term with the flat FLRW equations for **the same fluid source**:

\[
3H^2=\kappa(h_*T^4-\alpha_Fh_n^2T^6),\qquad
\frac{\ddot a}{a}=-\frac\kappa6(2h_*T^4-4\alpha_Fh_n^2T^6).
\]

Both compared-equation residuals in the script are zero. GRG [Eq. (34)](https://arxiv.org/html/2007.11556v2#S0.E34) with β=0 gives ṅ+3Hn=0. With n=h_nT³ this yields Ṫ=−HT, agreeing with its [Eq. (37)](https://arxiv.org/html/2007.11556v2#S0.E37) at β=0. Independently, effective energy conservation reduces to

\[
\dot\rho_F+3H(\rho_F+P_F)
=(4h_*T^3-6\alpha_Fh_n^2T^5)(\dot T+HT)=0.
\]

No division by the prefactor is needed: number conservation fixes Ṫ=−HT even when the prefactor vanishes. Integration gives a_F(T)=C/T and na_F³=h_nC³, exactly constant.

The positive-temperature H=0 root is

\[
T_{b,F}^2=\frac{h_*}{\alpha_Fh_n^2},\qquad
a_{b,F}=C\sqrt{\frac{\alpha_Fh_n^2}{h_*}}.
\]

Substitute this root into the acceleration equation, without dividing by H:

\[
\left.\dot H\right|_b=\left.\frac{\ddot a}{a}\right|_b
=\frac\kappa3\epsilon_b
=\frac{\kappa h_*^3}{3\alpha_F^2h_n^4}>0.
\]

The evolution equations for (a,H,T), namely ȧ=aH, Ṫ=−HT and the acceleration equation above, are regular there. This is a local smooth minimum in proper time. It does **not** require da/dT=0: here da_F/dT|_b=−Cα_Fh_n²/h_*≠0 while Ṫ_b=0. **C1_FLUID=PASS** for the specified flat extension.

**Literal GRG geometry qualification.** GRG's metric is Kantowski–Sachs, Eq. (6). Eq. (25) implies that shear identically zero requires X=λY with constant λ. Substituting this into its spatial equation (11),

\[
\frac{\ddot X}{X}-\frac{\ddot Y}{Y}
+\frac{\dot X\dot Y}{XY}-\frac{\dot Y^2+1}{Y^2}=0,
\]

leaves −1/Y²=0, impossible at finite Y. Thus a test of the **literal KS system** would fail exact isotropic recovery for a geometric reason within the FLUID row. It is not the Bianchi I test retained here and cannot be silently called a PASS. Setting shear to zero at one instant is insufficient to produce an isotropic solution over an interval. This distinction follows directly from [GRG Eqs. (6), (11), (25)](https://arxiv.org/html/2007.11556v2#S0.E11).

## C1_DIRAC: recovery of PRD Eqs. (14)–(17)

Test: using the DIRAC total density and pressure, does the published conservation law integrate to the published a(T), critical temperature and minimum scale factor? No fluid-row number law is imposed.

PRD Eqs. (11)–(13), with the thermal substitutions accompanying Eq. (14), give

\[
(4h_*T^3-6\alpha_Dh_n^2T^5)\dot T+4h_*HT^4=0,
\]
\[
\frac{d\log a_D}{dT}=-\frac1T+\frac{3\alpha_Dh_n^2}{2h_*}T.
\]

This is precisely Eq. (14). Integrating independently gives

\[
a_D(T)=\frac C T\exp\left(\frac{3\alpha_Dh_n^2T^2}{4h_*}\right),
\]

matching Eq. (15), with C denoting its multiplicative integration constant (printed a_rT_r). Solving da_D/dT=0 and substituting the positive root gives

\[
T_{cr,D}=\sqrt{\frac{2h_*}{3\alpha_Dh_n^2}},\qquad
a_{cr,D}=C\sqrt{\frac{3e\alpha_Dh_n^2}{2h_*}}.
\]

These match Eqs. (16) and (17), respectively. The script checks all four equation matches, the zero derivative, and the positive second derivative. **C1_DIRAC=PASS** for this published cusp recovery. This verifies the specified algebra, not differentiability of a proper-time solution through the cusp.

Two distinctions remain true **within the DIRAC row**, but neither defeats this recovery test:

   Imposing Ṫ=−HT on the DIRAC conservation equation instead leaves 6α_Dh_n²HT⁶, generically nonzero. Thus Dirac thermal evolution plus conserved na³ would fail together; na³ conservation is not part of PRD Eq. (14)'s recovery test. This calculation alone supplies no microscopic production mechanism.

   Its H=0 root instead has T_st²=h_*/(α_Dh_n²)=(3/2)T_cr,D², reproducing Eq. (23). For exact k=1, Eq. (11) subtracts 1/a_cr,D² from the displayed H²; the cusp algebra does not generally set that expression to zero either. A contracting/expanding cusp joins branches with a change in the sign of ȧ; it is not certified here as a smooth solution of both Friedmann equations at the joining event.

The old temperature-law mismatch and nonconstant na³ were comparisons against fluid-row assumptions. The cusp-versus-stationary distinction survives as a distinction, not as a failure to recover Eqs. \[
