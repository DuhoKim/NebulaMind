# Lane method note — two engines agreeing is not independence when they share an input
**Tori, 2026-09-07 11:50 KST. Written because it cost a filed claim today.**

At 11:00 KST this lane recorded that two published papers disagreed about the sign of a torsion pressure correction. Codex's
derivation gate (C1=FAIL) and kimi's blind check (SIGN_CHECK=DISCREPANCY) agreed. Both were wrong in the same way, and they
agreed *because* they were wrong in the same way: both read `SOURCES_20260907.md`, an extraction that carried Eq. (10) and the
declared conventions but not the prose one paragraph above it, where the author distinguishes the Weyssenhoff spin-fluid case
from the Dirac case and gives both. Different engines, different methods, same blind spot — because the same input.

**The rule, for this lane's practice:** independence requires a different SOURCE, not merely a different engine. For any claim
about what a paper says, at least one check must go to the publisher page or the version of record, read as a page and not as an
extraction. An extraction is a convenience for calculation; it is not evidence about a text. When the publisher page cannot be
reached, that limit is stated in the claim rather than papered over with a second engine reading the same extract.

**What the pattern rhymes with:** this is the same shape as the census's own repeated finding — a control that inherits the
blindness of the thing it is checking. V31's both-seat-orders controls passed while a seat-order defect was live, because they
were built from the counterexample rather than from the property. Here two referee seats passed the same misreading, because
they were built from the extract rather than from the paper.

## Second instance, same day (13:00 KST) — and the sharper form of the rule
Five hours after this note was written, the lane filed `B1_UNDERDETERMINED` on the claim that an energy-transfer relation was
absent from the sources. Both papers print it: GRG 53, 18 Eq. (14) with its entropy restatement (17), and PRD 85, 107502
Eq. (13). The extraction had jumped from GRG (11) to (15). Worse: PRD (13) sits three lines below the equation I went to that
page to check, and I read the page myself and did not see it.

**The rule, sharpened by that:** reading a page for one purpose is not reading the page. An absence claim — "no source prints
X" — is the most dangerous claim a lane can make from an extraction, because extractions are built by asking for specific
things. Before asserting that a source does NOT contain something, one seat must read the FULL text of that source end to end
with the absence question as its only task, and say which text it read. A claim of presence can rest on a quotation; a claim of
absence cannot rest on anything less than a complete read.
