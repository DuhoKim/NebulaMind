## Card 01 — assertion heading

**This route closed because the idea did not provide a calibrated, identifying sky test**

Could our universe be inside a black hole? This is Duho's standing personal interest, not a NebulaMind research frontier. We read the primary sources to ask: what does this family actually predict? The answer has two parts. One branch makes a clean number that pulsars can test. But our galaxy-spin route closed: its spin claim is real, yet uncalibrated, and a spin difference would not identify black-hole-universe cosmology. This is a closure record, not a verdict that the idea is true or false.

Source: C01, C03, C04, C09, C11, C12, C16; packet lines 3–17, 143–167, 259–278, 319–342, 351–366, 386–401.

## Card 02 — assertion heading

**“Black-hole universe” names a family of different programmes, not one model**

The first surprise was structural. The sources do not describe one theory with one forecast. Lana found at least five programmes: an identification between a closed universe and a black hole; a torsion bounce; a rotating-parent axis; cosmological natural selection; and other baby-universe work. They do not make one shared observational prediction.

Source: C02; packet lines 143–167 and 368–384.

## Card 03 — assertion heading

**A scientific test needs a predicted target and a result that can count against it**

A story about origins becomes a test when it commits to an observable target: a number, a range, or a distinctive pattern. Then a measurement can land inside or outside that target. The family Lana surveyed contains one especially clean example of that structure. It points not to the sky, but to neutron stars.

Source: C04; packet lines 143–152, 259–278, and 305–317.

## Card 04 — assertion heading

**Cosmological natural selection puts a low ceiling on neutron-star mass**

In Smolin's cosmological natural selection, universes reproduce through black holes with slightly changed constants. In the Brown–Lee–Rho chain surveyed by Lana, the Brown–Bethe maximum neutron-star mass is about one point five solar masses. Their paper says a neutron star at approximately two solar masses or above would put that chain in “serious doubt or simply falsify” it. That is a real, falsifiable number.

Source: C04, C05; packet lines 259–278, 307–310, and 351–355.

## Card 05 — assertion heading

**Measured pulsars have entered the source’s approximately two-solar-mass regime**

The measurements now reach that regime. Demorest and colleagues measured PSR J1614 minus 2230 at one point nine seven, plus or minus zero point zero four solar masses. Its central value does not cross two. Fonseca and colleagues measured PSR J0740 plus 6620 at two point zero eight, plus or minus zero point zero seven solar masses. The quoted sixty-eight point three percent interval lies above two. But at ninety-five point four percent credibility, its lower bound is one point nine five. It does not clear two at that stronger level.

Source: C06, C07; packet lines 47, 91–97, and 268–277.

## Card 06 — assertion heading

**The packet does not decide between “serious doubt” and “falsified”**

That distinction matters. Brown, Lee, and Rho gave a disjunction: a heavy neutron star would put the chain in serious doubt, or simply falsify it. Lana's closing record does not decide which side applies, and neither will this video. The honest finding is narrower: observations have entered the regime named by the source.

Source: C05, C08; packet lines 91–97, 259–278, and 351–355.

## Card 07 — assertion heading

**The rotating-parent paper explicitly says clockwise and counterclockwise counts should differ**

A different branch led to our sky work. In the rotating-parent scenario, a child universe inherits the parent black hole's rotation axis. The current source explicitly says that clockwise- and counterclockwise-spinning galaxy counts should be different. The spin lane was not chasing a phantom. It was following an explicit, source-backed qualitative claim.

Source: C09, C13; packet lines 143–167, 219–257, 330–342, and 386–401.

## Card 08 — assertion heading

**The handedness claim was added after the cited observations and never calibrated**

But it was not a calibrated or pre-data forecast. The handedness material entered the paper in a 2025 revision, after the galaxy studies it cites. The paper contains mechanics and rotating-frame equations. What it does not provide is a predicted handedness amplitude, a scale or redshift law, a parent-spin prior, a transfer function, an independently predicted axis, a finite lower bound, or a numerical acceptance region.

Source: C09, C10, C11; packet lines 73–90, 116–140, 153–167, 232–257, 330–342, and 386–401.

## Card 09 — assertion heading

**A spin difference would not identify a black-hole origin**

Now the second problem. Even if a carefully controlled survey found a real spin-handedness difference, that signal would not identify black-hole-universe cosmology. The qualitative effect is not unique to this origin story. A positive generic anisotropy could fit BHU and other rotating cosmologies. The measurement would be interesting, but it could not tell us which explanation produced it.

Source: C12; packet lines 159–167, 319–328, 351–366, and 386–401. No named rival model appears in the public narration because the packet marks external freezing of that comparison as requiring primary citations.

## Card 10 — assertion heading

**The spin route failed twice: there was no numerical target and no unique signature**

Put those failures together. A small result or a finite-precision null cannot be numerically scored against a source-defined range, because the paper gives none. A positive result would still not identify BHU. So a perfect measurement pipeline could produce a trustworthy number and still not test this idea. The hunt had a source. It did not have a target that could answer the question.

Source: C11, C12, C13, C14; packet lines 153–167, 330–366, and 386–401.

## Card 11 — assertion heading

**Reopen this line only when the theory supplies a calibrated target or a unique signature**

What would change the verdict? First, a published calculation connecting parent-black-hole parameters to a handedness amplitude, scale, or redshift dependence, with a numerical acceptance region. Or second, a signature unique to birth behind a parent horizon, rather than a generic consequence of rotation or a bounce. A confirmed spin asymmetry alone would still not test BHU. That is the verdict: this route stays closed until the theory supplies a calibrated target or a unique signature.

Source: C15, C16; packet lines 403–418.
