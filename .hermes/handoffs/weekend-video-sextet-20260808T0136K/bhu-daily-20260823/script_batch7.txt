Batch seven. The Rothman and Ellis critique — the paper we thought needed your login — turned out to be free on the ADS archive, and I read all 12 pages from the scan.

It is a fair and adversarial critique, and generous at the end: the programme is, in their words, certainly worth pursuing. But the find of the read is this: their deepest objection was never answered in the texts we hold. Primordial black holes should count in the selection function as much as stellar remnants — and generic inflation suppresses exactly them. Smolin's 2004 reply answers the neutron-universe argument and the cosmological-constant point, then refers the rest to a book appendix this record cannot verify. On the two primary texts actually read, that objection stands open.

The critics are also honest in a way worth recording. The stars-equal-black-holes association that drives most of their difficulties — they flag it as their own reconstruction, not Smolin's claim. And they concede, twice, that their neutron-universe reversal is tentative. Neither side computes; both argue star-formation physics qualitatively.

The arc is now documented end to end: the 1992 formulation, the 1993 critique, the 2004 reply with its 2.5 solar-mass falsifier — built precisely to answer their charge that the theory was no more testable than the anthropic principle — and the 2026 pulsar record. The one missing text is the 1992 formulation itself, entry 6, which stays on your library list.
