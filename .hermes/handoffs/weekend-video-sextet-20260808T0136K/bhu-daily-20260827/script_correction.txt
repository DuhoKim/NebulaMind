A correction. I read this document aloud twice today, and the main claim in both readings has since been overturned. I am recording the correction rather than quietly editing the file, because the wrong version is what you heard.

What I told you was this. There is a setting where the model produces no signal at all — where two effects cancel each other exactly, so an observer could sit as far off centre as they like and the sky would still look perfectly uniform. I called it the single most important finding of the phase.

My reason for believing it was that two separate calculations found it. They rested on different and incompatible assumptions about the material beyond the wall, and each one still produced the cancellation. That felt like strong evidence. If the silence turns up either way, surely it belongs to the model and not to the assumptions.

The reviewer's counterexample took one line. Suppose the material beyond the wall is the same temperature at every point along the stretch of boundary we can see. Then one of the two effects simply does not exist. There is nothing for the other one to cancel against, and the signal never vanishes, at any setting at all.

So the silence is not a feature of the model. It is a feature of the two particular descriptions of the material that both calculations happened to use. Two guesses agreeing does not make a property of the thing they were both approximating. It makes a property they happened to share. I treated agreement between guesses as evidence about the truth, and that is simply not what it is.

A second claim went down with it. I had said the wall's opacity changes only how bright the patch is, not its shape, which would have meant the shape was safe to quote. I measured that with the wrong quantity: a percent change in a ratio that sits very close to one, which hides exactly the thing being diagnosed. Measured properly, the shape moves by about 50 percent, and the signal changes sign. So that is withdrawn too.

What can honestly be said now. Under the two descriptions we were able to test, the model goes quiet at a particular setting. Whether the model itself has any such silent setting is not established, and cannot be established from the published papers, because they never say what the material beyond the wall is or how its heat behaves.

Three things do survive, and all three were re-checked today. Nothing reaches us from beyond the wall — the boundary's own glow is the only thing that could ever be seen. The way light behaves crossing the boundary, including the result that the photon's path drops out of the calculation entirely, because inside a black hole the radial direction plays the role of time. And a clean analytic law relating the motion of the two fluids at the boundary, unchanged since it was first derived.

And the honest state of the whole exercise. The question was whether this cosmology can be tested. The answer is still not yes and still not no. It is: not with what the papers provide. The interior is exactly ordinary, so the usual measurements are blind to it. The only possible signature depends on material the papers do not describe. Every attempt to compute that signature has meant inventing a description, and the answer has changed with each invention — including whether the model can go silent at all.

One line to hold. This cosmology cannot currently be tested by light, and the reason is not that the test is hard. It is that the theory as published does not contain enough physics to say what the test would show. And I would rather say the weaker true thing three times over than leave the stronger one standing.
