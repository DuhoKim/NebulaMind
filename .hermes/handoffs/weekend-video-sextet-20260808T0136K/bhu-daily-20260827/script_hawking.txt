You asked what about Hawking radiation, and whether it could prove this. It is the right instinct. It is the one quantum handle a black hole cosmology plausibly has, and it is the obvious thing to reach for. I have now done it properly, and the answer is no. It fails 4 separate ways, and 2 of them are structural — meaning no telescope ever built, or ever imagined, changes them.

First, the literature. The authors of the model never mention it. The name Hawking appears twice in their paper, both times in the list of references at the back. Nowhere in the argument itself.

But two things I found in their text matter more than the absence. The horizon in their model is a white hole horizon, not a black hole horizon. Hawking's calculation is for a black hole formed by collapse, and a white hole is the time reverse of that — the opposite case. And they fix the direction of time deliberately, by an entropy condition, in the direction that rules Hawking's setup out.

The second thing. They feed the temperature of the background light into their solution as a given. It is an input to the model. And a model that is handed the temperature cannot also predict it.

Now the two arguments that actually kill this. The first is that it does not distinguish anything. If you work out the Hawking temperature of the mass inside our horizon, you get 1.3 times 10 to the minus 30 degrees above absolute zero. But ordinary cosmology, with no black hole anywhere in it, already predicts a temperature for its own horizon — and that number is 2.7 times 10 to the minus 30. The ratio between them is a factor of exactly 2. Not approximately 2. Exactly 2. So even a perfect thermometer, of unlimited sensitivity, could not tell the two pictures apart. And that argument does not only kill this model. It kills the whole approach, for any black hole cosmology whose horizon sits at roughly the size of the visible universe.

The second argument is simpler, and I like it more. The wavelength of that radiation — the length of a single wave — comes out at 15.9 times the radius of the observable universe. Not one wave fits inside the part of the universe we can see. That is not a sensitivity problem anyone can fund their way out of. There is no apparatus, even in principle, sitting inside our horizon, that can measure a wave longer than our horizon. And that figure of 15.9 is the same whichever value of the expansion rate you use, so the current disagreement about that rate does not touch it.

The easy failures come after those. The energy in this radiation is 10 to the minus 122 of the energy in the background light we actually see. Evaporation would take 10 to the 125 times the age of the universe. And to give you a sense of how far off it is: for the background light to actually be Hawking radiation, the source would have to be about 0.6 of the mass of the Moon, with a horizon 67 millionths of a metre across.

And then the part that should bother us. To write that temperature down at all, you have to assume there is vacuum outside the wall. This model has fluid out there. So quoting a Hawking temperature for it means adding physics the papers do not contain, and then reporting a property of what you added. That is exactly the error I was failed for this afternoon. The same hole in the same papers defeats the light test and the Hawking test, by the same mechanism. That is not a coincidence. That is the finding getting harder.

So the line is unchanged, and it now rests on more. It is not that these tests are difficult. It is that the theory as published does not contain enough physics to say what any of them would show.
