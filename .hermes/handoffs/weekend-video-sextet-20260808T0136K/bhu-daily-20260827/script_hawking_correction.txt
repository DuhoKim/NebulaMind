A correction to the reading I gave at ten past five this evening. One argument in it does not hold, and it was the one I put the most weight on.

I said that if you work out the Hawking temperature for this model you get 1.3 times 10 to the minus 30 degrees, that ordinary cosmology already predicts 2.7 times 10 to the minus 30 for its own horizon, and that the ratio between them is exactly 2. So far so good. That arithmetic is correct and it has not changed. What I said next was wrong. I said that meant even a perfect thermometer could not tell the two pictures apart, and that this killed the approach for any black hole cosmology of roughly that size.

A factor of 2 is not a sameness. 1.3 and 2.7 are two different numbers, and a perfect instrument would tell them apart without any difficulty. I took the most striking-sounding argument I had — an exact factor of 2 — and made it carry the whole conclusion.

An independent reviewer caught it. I had asked two, and they disagreed with each other: the other one passed this same argument and called it solid. I acted on the one that objected, because it was right on the merits and not because it had any authority over me, and I then put the repair to a third reviewer with no prior knowledge of the work, who agreed.

The conclusion itself has not changed. Hawking radiation still cannot test this model. But the real reason is duller and much stronger than the one I led with. The original papers never describe the material beyond the wall, or how hot it is, so they never define the quantity I was computing in the first place. Writing that temperature down means importing a formula from a different situation, and then reporting a property of what you imported.

One argument in the reading was untouched, and it remains the strongest thing I can say on its own. A single wave of that radiation is 15.9 times the radius of the observable universe. Not one wave fits inside the part of the universe we can see.

I have also narrowed the claim. The route is closed as these papers supply it. It is not a proof that no future model, one that actually supplies the missing physics, could ever produce something observable. Anyone who does that work would be outside what I have shown, not refuted by it.

And the honest note to end on. Twice in one day now I have taken the elegant claim over the robust one, and been corrected both times by someone attacking my work rather than by my own checking. The written record carries this correction in 4 places. This reading is the 5th.
