An update to this morning's statement of what is at stake. Everything in it still stands. One thing has got worse, and it is the thing the whole document turns on.

First, briefly, where we were. One published model says the Big Bang was an explosion inside a black hole, with a shock wave standing as a wall around everything we can see. From the inside, that model looks exactly like an ordinary universe, so the usual measurements can never detect the wall. Its only possible fingerprint is a single patch of sky where our line of sight crosses the boundary. And this morning's finding was that there is a setting in which two effects cancel exactly, so the model produces no signal at all while being entirely correct.

Which raises the obvious question. Which setting? Where, exactly, does the model go silent? I had one answer from my own calculation and a different answer from an independent one, so I asked two reviewers to decide between them.

They disagreed with each other. And the disagreement resolved against both answers.

The first ruled that the rival construction was geometrically forbidden. Varying which direction you look sweeps across a family of different pieces of matter on the boundary — not one piece of matter evolving through time. The rule that construction relied on only applies to a single piece of matter following its own history.

The second ruled that the published theory does not answer the question at all. The papers specify how the pressure of the material beyond the wall relates to its density. They never specify its temperature. Not its composition, not its heat content, none of it. And my own answer, it pointed out, quietly assumed that material is pure radiation — which would fix a property of it to a value contradicted by the very settings where the silence occurs. I had been caught making that same assumption once before, fixed it in one place, and left it standing in another.

So the honest position, stated plainly. The model has a setting where it produces no signal at all, and the theory as published cannot say which setting that is. Not that we have failed to calculate it yet — the information needed is absent from the source material. To find the silent setting you would have to add physics the original papers never wrote down: what the material beyond the wall is made of, how hot it is, how its heat is distributed. And then the answer would be a property of what you added, not of the model being tested.

Why this is a result rather than a disappointment. It moves the finding from "here is a gap in the test" to something sharper: this cosmology contains a blind spot, and cannot tell you where its blind spot is. A test you cannot perform is a nuisance. A theory that cannot specify the circumstances under which it becomes untestable is a different and more interesting object — and demonstrating that took more work than confirming or refuting it would have.

What survives, and what I have taken back. The silence itself survives, and is stronger than before: two calculations, resting on different and incompatible assumptions about the material beyond the wall, both produced the cancellation. That it appears under either set of assumptions is better evidence than agreement on a single number would have been. Also surviving: nothing reaches us from beyond the wall, and the wall's own glow is the only thing we could ever see. Both calculations agree on that.

Taken back: every claim about where the silence falls, mine included. They are not merely unreconciled now. They are unsupported. And taken back also: part of my own treatment of how the wall's temperature varies across the sky, for assuming a composition the model excludes at the settings that matter.

The line to hold, if anyone quotes this work. Before, it was: the model can produce no signal at all without being wrong. Now it is: the model can produce no signal at all without being wrong, and the theory cannot say under what circumstances that happens. Any claim that this is a test of black hole cosmology has to get past both halves.
