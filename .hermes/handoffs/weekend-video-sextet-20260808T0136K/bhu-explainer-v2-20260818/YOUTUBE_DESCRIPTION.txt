Black-hole-universe cosmology is a personal side-interest of the author, not a NebulaMind research programme.

Could our universe exist inside a black hole? One version of that idea — Smolin's cosmological natural selection, argued through the Brown–Lee–Rho chain — offered two clean neutron-star tests of itself. This video walks the whole ladder in plain language: what the black-hole-universe idea is, what cosmological natural selection claims, why that predicts a low neutron-star mass ceiling, and what published pulsar measurements say. We sealed the checking rule before looking, then applied it to the pulsar community's published numbers.

The result: the famous heavy-star test stops at serious doubt — but the less-watched second test, a double neutron star pair differing by more than 4% in mass, had already finished in the published literature. PSR J1913+1102 differs by 19.3 ± 0.7%.

What this does NOT say: not "the black-hole-universe idea is falsified" (at least 5 disagreeing programmes exist); not "Smolin's hypothesis is refuted" (it loses this flagship prediction, which is different); and nothing here was measured by us — the numbers belong to the pulsar community.
