## The claim under test

The chain exactly as Brown, Lee & Rho (PRL 101, 091101 (2008); arXiv:0802.2997) state it in
their abstract, quoted verbatim:

> "It is argued that a well measured double neutron star binary in which the two neutron stars
> are more than 4% different from each other in mass or a massive neutron star with mass
> M ∼> 2 M⊙ would put in serious doubt or simply falsify the following chain of predictions:
> (1) nearly vanishing vector meson mass at chiral restoration, (2) kaon condensation at a
> density n∼3n₀, (3) the Brown-Bethe maximum neutron star mass M_max≈1.5 M⊙ and (4) Smolin's
> 'Cosmological Natural Selection' hypothesis."

The source therefore supplies its own falsifier set with **two limbs**, and its own thresholds.
This criterion adopts the source's thresholds — the fair test of "the chain as the source
states it" — and fixes only what the source leaves open: the statistical standard, the
measurement class, and the robustness requirement.

Interpretation rules, fixed now: "M ∼> 2 M⊙" is read as 2.00 M⊙; the chain's own maximum as
1.50 M⊙; "more than 4% different" as fractional mass difference |m₁−m₂|/max(m₁,m₂) > 0.04. The
source's "∼" approximations are handled by the two-tier structure below, never by moving a
threshold after seeing data.

## Measurement class (what counts as "well measured" / "a firm observation")

Qualifying: dynamical masses from radio-pulsar timing (Shapiro delay or other post-Keplerian
parameters) or comparably direct orbital dynamics, published in refereed venues.
Non-qualifying for the verdict (reportable as context only): photometric/light-curve-model
companion masses (e.g., black-widow spectrophotometry), gravitational-wave events or binary
members whose neutron-star nature is itself unresolved, and any measurement retracted or
materially revised downward by its own collaboration. Rationale: the source says "well
measured" and "a firm observation"; a verdict on someone else's theory must not hinge on a
measurement class whose systematics are actively contested.

## Criterion

**Limb 1 (maximum mass) — FALSIFIES standard:** at least one qualifying neutron star whose
mass posterior exceeds 2.00 M⊙ at ≥ 95.4% credibility, corroborated by a second independent
qualifying system exceeding 2.00 M⊙ at ≥ 68.3%; OR a single qualifying system exceeding
2.00 M⊙ at ≥ 99.73%. Why 95.4%: the ledger's own C07 discipline already fixed 95.4% as the bar
Fonseca fails — adopting a weaker bar now would be tuning toward falsification. Why
corroboration: one system's inclination/companion systematics must not decide it alone.

**Limb 2 (DNS asymmetry) — FALSIFIES standard:** a double-neutron-star binary, both masses
from radio timing, whose fractional mass difference exceeds 4% with the 95.4% credible
interval excluding ≤ 4%, and whose double-NS nature is not contested in the refereed
literature. One such system meets the source's stated falsifier; two independent such systems
make it decisive.

**SERIOUS DOUBT (outcome 2):** neither limb meets its FALSIFIES standard, but at least two
independent qualifying systems exceed the chain's own maximum 1.50 M⊙ at ≥ 99.73% each AND at
least one qualifying system's central value is ≥ 2.00 M⊙. Rationale: the source set ~2 M⊙ "to
be safe" above its 1.5 M⊙ maximum (their Section V wording); evidence robustly above the
chain's stated maximum while brushing the safety threshold is what "serious doubt" means.

**NEITHER (outcome 3):** no qualifying system exceeds 1.50 M⊙ at ≥ 99.73% and no limb-2
system exists.

**CANNOT BE SETTLED (outcome 4):** the verdict between outcomes 1 and 2 hinges solely on
measurements excluded by the measurement class (contested systematics, unresolved object
nature) — stated with the specific blocking measurements.
