## 1. Two-Limb Reading

The two-limb reading is correct. Brown, Lee & Rho's abstract states one disjunctive trigger: either a well-measured double neutron star binary whose component masses differ by more than 4%, or a neutron star with mass around 2 solar masses, would put in serious doubt or simply falsify the named four-link chain. The wording is not merely a side remark about binaries; it is part of the abstract's source-supplied falsifier set.

That supports using limb 2 as sufficient under the pre-registered criterion. The verdict does not need to collapse to SERIOUS DOUBT merely because limb 1 fails the stronger 2.00 M_sun credibility bar.

Source checked: Brown, Lee & Rho 2008, arXiv:0802.2997 / PRL 101, 091101.

## 2. PSR J1913+1102 Nature

The asymmetry between J0453+1559 and J1913+1102 is real.

J1913+1102 is consistently treated in the literature as a double neutron star system. The 2020 Nature paper reports the pulsar and companion neutron-star masses from a dedicated timing campaign, and the 2026 accepted A&A update calls it a highly asymmetric double neutron star system, gives improved masses `m_p = 1.599(8) M_sun` and `m_c = 1.290(8) M_sun`, and discusses formation of the second neutron star. I searched specifically for dispute/white-dwarf/contested treatments of J1913+1102 and found no refereed counterclaim analogous to the J0453+1559 case.

J0453+1559 is different: Tauris & Janka 2019 explicitly challenge the neutron-star interpretation and propose that the lower-mass component could instead be a white dwarf from a thermonuclear electron-capture supernova. Excluding J0453+1559 while using J1913+1102 is therefore principled, not convenient.

The deciding J1913+1102 mass asymmetry is large: `(1.599 - 1.290) / 1.599 = 19.3%`, far beyond the 4% limb-2 threshold.

Sources checked: Ferdman et al. 2020 Nature; Miao et al. 2026 arXiv:2606.19276; Tauris & Janka 2019 arXiv:1909.12318; Living Reviews in Relativity review section on J1913+1102; searches for contested J1913+1102 companion nature.
