## Panel 01 — This specific chain fails its own second neutron-star test

Could our universe exist inside a black hole? That is Duho's personal side-interest, not a NebulaMind research programme. We did not measure a new star. We sealed a rule, then applied it to published pulsar measurements. The result: the Brown–Lee–Rho chain examined here fails by its own second neutron-star test. That does not mean every black-hole-universe idea is false. It means this chain lost the clean test its authors offered.

## Panel 02 — This cosmic chain offered 2 checkable neutron-star tests

Neutron stars matter here because they make an enormous cosmological story risk being wrong. Their masses can be measured through pulsar timing. In the chain examined here, ideas about particle physics and ultra-dense matter lead to a proposed neutron-star mass ceiling, which is then tied to cosmological natural selection. The authors named 2 observations that could break that chain: a sufficiently heavy neutron star, or a well-measured pair of neutron stars whose masses differ by more than 4%. Either test was meant to count.

## Panel 03 — The famous mass-ceiling test reaches serious doubt, not strict falsification

The better-known test is the mass ceiling. The chain gives a Brown–Bethe maximum of about 1.5 times the Sun's mass, then says that approximately 2 times the Sun's mass would be the safe threshold for a serious obstacle. The strongest qualifying single-star measurement is PSR J0740+6620 at 2.08 ± 0.07 times the Sun's mass. It clears 2.00 at the quoted 68.3% level, but not at the stricter 95.4% level used by our sealed rule. So this limb reaches serious doubt, not strict falsification. The literal 1.5 ceiling is still contradicted: 3 qualifying timing systems sit securely above it.

## Panel 04 — The same paper gives an independent 4% binary test

The second test is easier to miss. The same source says that a well-measured double neutron star system also breaks the chain if the 2 stellar masses differ by more than 4%. This is not a backup calculation invented later. It appears in the source's own falsifier sentence, joined to the heavy-star test by the word "or." That matters. The single-star evidence does not have to cross our strict 2.00 threshold if the independent binary test has already crossed its own threshold.

## Panel 05 — PSR J1913+1102 exceeds the 4% limit by a wide margin

PSR J1913+1102 is the decisive pair. Pulsar timing gives masses of 1.599 ± 0.008 and 1.290 ± 0.008 times the Sun's mass. Relative to the heavier star, the difference is 19.3 ± 0.7%. The source's limit was 4%. So the observed difference is nearly 5 times the threshold, and its 95.4% interval stays above 4% by a wide margin. The system is treated in the refereed literature as a double neutron star. The asymmetry was published in 2020 and sharpened by the 2026 timing update.

## Panel 06 — A sealed rule keeps the result from following the answer

The order of work matters. The criterion was written and hash-sealed before the evidence harvest. It admitted dynamical pulsar-timing masses, required an uncontested double neutron star system, and fixed the credibility standards in advance. Measurements outside that class were not used to win the verdict. A source caveat about a small extra shift from stellar evolution was found after sealing, so the rule was not narrowed. Even on the most source-generous reading, 19.3% cannot be absorbed into a small correction around 4%. The verdict stays the same.

## Panel 07 — The neutron-star prediction dies; broader ideas do not

The Brown–Bethe maximum near 1.5 times the Sun's mass is dead as a literal prediction. The earlier particle-physics links are under serious doubt through this chain; the heavy-star limb did not individually falsify them at our strict standard. Cosmological natural selection loses the flagship neutron-star prediction used here, but the hypothesis itself is not thereby refuted. It can leave this Brown–Bethe route behind, at the price of no longer being tested by this channel. And "black-hole universe" names several disagreeing programmes, so saying the whole family is falsified would be false.

## Panel 08 — The forgotten test had already finished

These numbers belong to the pulsar community. We did not discover the stars or remeasure their masses. We applied a pre-registered rule to published posteriors and stated the result per link. The famous mass-ceiling test came close to the strict bar and stopped at serious doubt. The less-watched binary test had already cleared its bar in the published literature. That is why neutron stars matter here: they turned a sweeping cosmic story into a test with an answer. This specific chain fails by the second rule its own authors wrote.
