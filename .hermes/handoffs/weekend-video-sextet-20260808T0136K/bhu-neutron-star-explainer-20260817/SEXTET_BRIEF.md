## The story, and why it is a good one

The chain's author named **two** ways to falsify it. Everyone — including our own closing video —
watched the first: neutron stars should not exceed about 1.5 suns, and "to be safe", 2 suns would
do it. Measured stars got heavy, but the heaviest well-measured one (2.08 ± 0.07) doesn't clear 2.00
at the strict standard. So on that limb: serious doubt, not falsified.

**The second limb was sitting unwatched.** The same author said: find two neutron stars orbiting
each other whose masses differ by more than 4%, and that also falsifies it. PSR J1913+1102 differs
by **19.3 ± 0.7%** — nearly five times the threshold — and has been in the refereed literature since
2020. The prediction fails by the author's own rule, through a door nobody was looking at.

That is the spine: **the famous test nearly worked, and the forgotten test had already finished.**
