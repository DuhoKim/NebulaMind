Black-hole-universe cosmology is a personal side-interest of the author, not a NebulaMind research programme.

Last video, we showed no survey could ever see the galaxy-spin lean predicted by the rotating-parent black-hole-universe idea. This video reports what happened when we went further overnight: we put the source paper itself on trial, claim by claim — and then rebuilt its one surviving idea as a strict model with every step derived and machine-checked.

The trial: 23 claims audited. The paper's own equation identifies dark energy with cosmic rotation — but measured dark energy then demands a spin a billion times over what the CMB allows, and the allowed spin supplies less than a billionth of a billionth of dark energy. Independently, the paper's own conservation premise makes its "rotational dark energy" behave like radiation (w = +1/3) — which cannot accelerate expansion, and sits about 46 standard deviations from the measurement the paper cites as support. One idea survives: a spinning universe should tilt galaxy spins.

The strict model: parent black hole, explicit inheritance assumptions, spin-fade derived per cosmic era from pinned sources, and the previously missing transfer coefficient derived for the first time (about 7, honestly bracketed). The strict answer: 0.027 standard deviations across the whole sky — about 12,000 observable universes of galaxies for statistical confidence. The earlier closure is strengthened, and as a bonus the CMB now constrains the parent black hole itself.

What this does NOT say: not "the black-hole-universe idea is false" — one paper failed its audit and one route stays closed. A new amplifying mechanism or a published derivation with a number would reopen the question. Our own galaxy-spin measurement continues independently. The strict model awaits external theoretical review.
