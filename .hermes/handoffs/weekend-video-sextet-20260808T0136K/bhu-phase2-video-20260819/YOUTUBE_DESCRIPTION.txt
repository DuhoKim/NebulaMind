Phase 2 put the published torsion-bounce chain itself on trial — four papers, 77 audit rows, equation by equation — then derived the piece the literature never wrote: a ceiling on how much of a parent black hole's spin could survive the bounce.

What the audit found (the papers' own words where quoted): the later bounce paper disavows the earlier one's foundation; the two bounce mechanisms disagree by about 730x on bounce density and cannot both be true; the averaging step under the headline torsion number is derived in neither paper (a factor-6 swing); the parent's spin appears in exactly one sentence across all four papers, with no equation.

What we derived, receipts at every step: the torsion density as an honest bracket (the published value reproduces at one edge); a self-consistency ceiling on spin inheritance near 10^-27 under the published homogeneous-bounce reading; and a frozen-ratio theorem — the bounce neither creates nor erases an axis memory.

The verdict, gated by cross-engine adversarial review at every step: no finite-amplitude signature survives at observable magnitude. The most generous stack lands around 10^-5 of what counting every observable galaxy could ever detect. The route stays closed — now with its own number.

Caveats carried throughout: both bounce states sit at or above the Planck scale treated classically; one journal correction notice remains unread behind a paywall (our recomputations stand in its place); the strict chain awaits external theorist review before any publication claim.
