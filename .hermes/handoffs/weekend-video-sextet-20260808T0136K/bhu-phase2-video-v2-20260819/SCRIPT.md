# Born inside a black hole? The four papers explained

## Panel 01 — The inheritance route now has a ceiling, and it stays closed

Could our universe have been born inside a black hole, and could it remember which way its parent spun? We audited the 4 published papers in that chain, then derived the missing limit. The result comes first: no observable signature survives. Even the most generous signal is about 10,000 to 100,000 times below the best possible galaxy-counting test. The inheritance route now exists as a ceiling, and the route stays closed.

## Panel 02 — Paper 1 replaces the bang with a spring-like bounce

Picture spacetime as fabric. Particles have spin, a quantum property like a compass arrow. In Einstein-Cartan gravity, an extension of Einstein's theory, spin gives that fabric a slight twist called torsion. The 2010 paper says that, at crushing density, torsion pushes back like a compressed spring. Collapse stops and reverses in a bounce. This paper contains no plots—the entire argument is equations—so our labeled graphic shows the spring-like turn. Its famous estimate says today's torsion density is roughly a negative 1 shifted 70 places past the decimal point: a whisper.

## Panel 03 — Paper 1's tiny number rests on an unproved average

How does the paper turn countless particle spins into 1 cosmic number? That averaging step is cited, not derived. It squares the total density of 6 neutrino types as if their spins line up. Treating the species independently makes the torsion estimate exactly 6 times smaller. The printed value sits near the lined-up edge, so we carry both. A journal correction notice exists, but its content remains unread behind a paywall. We quarantined the affected printed numbers and recomputed them. The spring-like picture survives as a proposal; its exact strength is not unique.

## Panel 04 — Paper 2 draws a cusp where the universe changes direction

Now swap particles for fermion fields, quantum fields that make matter particles such as electrons and quarks. The 2012 paper uses their spin as a different torsion engine. Its Figure 1 plots the scale factor, a ruler for the universe's size. Follow the blue curve like a ball rolling downhill: the ruler shrinks toward time zero, reaches a sharp bottom, then grows again. This cusp is a pointed corner, not a smooth U-turn. This is the only chain paper with plots; its curve makes the bounce visible.

## Panel 05 — Paper 2's hot spike exposes a different, inserted bounce

Figure 2 is the same event seen as a thermometer. The blue line rises into a narrow spike at the critical temperature, then falls. The peak nears the Planck scale, where quantum gravity should matter and classical physics is untested. The paper calls Paper 1's foundation “not self-consistent” and says it “violates the cosmological principle,” the idea that the large-scale universe looks the same in every direction. Our audit found the 2 engines disagree by about 730 on bounce density. These equations never slow the universe to a stop: the inward speed is prescribed to jump outward at the cusp.

## Panel 06 — Paper 3 gives a black hole a mass-to-baby-universe map

Picture each black hole as a seed pod: collapse inside the pod reaches a bounce and opens into a baby universe. At a fixed compactness—how tightly the starting matter is packed—the parent's mass enters a map to the baby's initial size and temperature. This is the 1 inheritance channel the papers actually calculate. The paper contains no plots—the entire argument is equations—so our labeled graphic traces mass into those starting conditions. The map carries size and heat, not the direction of the parent's rotation.

## Panel 07 — Paper 3's mass fingerprint fades after the starting line

A birth certificate can list a baby's size without predicting its whole life. Likewise, Paper 3's cited computer results say later motion barely cares about starting size; it mainly follows a particle-production setting. Mass enters the start but leaves no claimed later fingerprint. The join between black-hole interior and baby universe is also a conjecture, meaning a proposed connection rather than a worked calculation. No horizon matching—no calculation showing how the inside joins the black hole's one-way boundary—is provided. And the headline number block silently works only by choosing a starting size of 1 meter.

## Panel 08 — Paper 4 mentions a spinning parent only once

The 2025 paper replaces the seed-pod sketch with a more star-like calculation: a ball of matter collapses while torsion is switched on. This paper also contains no plots—the entire argument is equations—so our labeled graphic follows the shrinking star into its proposed bounce. Across all 4 papers, no equation carries the parent's spin through the bounce; the collapse papers mention it in exactly 1 sentence: “It would still be valid for a more realistic gravitational collapse of an inhomogeneous and rotating fluid.” That sentence has no equation, no rotating model, and no calculation carrying an axis through the bounce.

## Panel 09 — A spinning parent's bounce allows almost no spin to survive

That sentence is no bridge, so we tested what the published bounce could carry. Angular momentum means the amount of rotation. If a 10-solar-mass parent kept all of it, the bounce would demand motion 6.6 times 10 to the power 26 beyond light's speed limit. Requiring the paper's uniform bounce caps inherited spin near 1 part in 10 to the power 27, with the treatment branches spanning roughly 1 order of magnitude. And if a spinning parent can't make their bounce at all, there is even less to see. Shear, meaning uneven squeezing, fades at the same rate as torsion, so their ratio stays frozen: the bounce neither smooths nor creates lopsidedness.

## Panel 10 — Ancient helium allows the torsion whisper because it cannot hear it

Think of early helium as a fossil thermometer. Big-bang nucleosynthesis, the first few minutes when light elements formed, records how fast space expanded. Dutta and Scherrer's Figure 1 compares helium, deuterium, and lithium when extra fast-fading energy is added. Figure 2 turns the helium change into a bound. Their stiff component—energy that thins faster than radiation as space grows—could be as large as 30 times radiation at 10 megaelectronvolts. The chain's torsion whisper is about 45 orders of magnitude below that bound. Their component is positive and torsion's negative, but at that gap either sign is invisible: perfectly allowed and unseen.

## Panel 11 — Even the most generous signal is 10,000 times below the floor

Finally, stack every step in the signal's favor, like turning every volume knob to maximum. The survival budget still reaches only about 1 part in 10,000 to 100,000 of the counting floor. That floor is the theoretical best from counting all 2 trillion observable galaxies, not an instrument. It is the smallest possible random wobble left by having a finite sample. One honest caveat: both bounces sit in the Planck regime treated classically, and the strict chain awaits external theorist review. Even before that review, the ceiling is far below the strongest possible counting test.

## Panel 12 — The strongest inheritance route ends at a closed ceiling

We began with a spinning black hole and asked whether its baby universe could remember an axis. Paper 1 supplied a spring-like bounce but not a unique strength. Paper 2 replaced it with an incompatible cusp. Paper 3 passed along mass, then said later dynamics forget it. Paper 4 offered only 1 unsupported sentence about rotation. The reality check found no visible trace, and the most generous stack still missed the absolute counting floor by about 10,000 to 100,000 times. The strongest inheritance route now exists on the record as a ceiling, and the ceiling says the route stays closed.
