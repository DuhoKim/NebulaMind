Could our universe have been born inside a black hole — and would it remember its parent's spin? The four published papers behind the idea, each explained from scratch in plain language, with the papers' own plots walked through on screen, and what a strict equation-by-equation audit found in each.

Built to be followed without any physics background: every term is defined the moment it appears, every mechanism gets an everyday analogy first, and every number comes with a comparison you can feel.

Chapters:
1. Cosmology with torsion (Phys. Lett. B 694, 2010; arXiv:1007.0587) — particle spin gives spacetime a tiny twist; crush matter far enough and the twist pushes back like a spring: a bounce instead of a bang. No plots exist in this paper — the whole argument is equations (we say so on screen). The audit: the key averaging choice is never derived (a factor-6 swing we carry openly), and the journal's correction notice sits behind a wall no one can read — so every number was recomputed from scratch.
2. The spinor-torsion bounce (Phys. Rev. D 85, 2012; arXiv:1111.4595) — same author, different engine, and this paper's own two plots on screen: the universe shrinking to a minimum size and rebounding, and the temperature spiking near the Planck scale. The audit: this paper calls the first paper's foundation "not self-consistent" — the two bounce engines disagree by about 730 times and cannot both be true.
3. The universe inside a black hole (ApJ 832, 2016; arXiv:1410.3881) — every black hole as a nursery; the parent's mass sets the baby's starting size and heat, and even that fingerprint washes out by the papers' own numerics. No plots in this paper. The audit: the horizon matching is a conjecture, and the flagship numbers quietly assume a one-meter starting size.
4. Collapse with torsion (IJMPA 40, 2025; arXiv:2509.11468) — a real star collapsed with the twist switched on. Across all 4 papers, no equation carries the parent's spin through the bounce; the two collapse papers mention a spinning parent in exactly 1 sentence — shown on screen. The audit adds what the literature never wrote: demand their bounce of a spinning 10-solar-mass parent and self-consistency caps the inherited spin near 1 part in 10^27 (conserving it outright would break the cosmic speed limit by a factor of 6.6x10^26), and a frozen-ratio theorem shows the bounce neither smooths nor creates lopsidedness.

Reality check with the helium fossil record (Dutta & Scherrer, PRD 82, 2010; arXiv:1006.4166 — their plots on screen): the chain's torsion whisper is 45 tenfold steps below the measured bound — perfectly allowed, and therefore perfectly invisible. Stacking every generosity, the best possible sky signal sits 10,000 to 100,000 times below what counting every observable galaxy could detect.

Caveats carried on screen throughout: both bounces are Planck-scale events treated classically; the unread correction notice is replaced by our recomputations; the derived chain awaits external theorist review.
