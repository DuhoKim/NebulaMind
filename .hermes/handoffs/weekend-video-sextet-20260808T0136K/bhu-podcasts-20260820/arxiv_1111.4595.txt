NONSINGULAR, BIG-BOUNCE COSMOLOGY FROM SPINOR-TORSION
COUPLING
Nikodem Poplawski

arXiv:1111.4595v2 [gr-qc] 4 Jul 2012

Department of Physics, Indiana University, Bloomington, Indiana, USA∗
The Einstein-Cartan-Sciama-Kibble theory of gravity removes the constraint of general relativity
that the affine connection be symmetric by regarding its antisymmetric part, the torsion tensor, as
a dynamical variable. The minimal coupling between the torsion tensor and Dirac spinors generates
a spin-spin interaction which is significant in fermionic matter at extremely high densities. We show
that such an interaction averts the unphysical big-bang singularity, replacing it with a cusp-like
bounce at a finite minimum scale factor, before which the Universe was contracting. This scenario
also explains why the present Universe at largest scales appears spatially flat, homogeneous and
isotropic.

Kerlick has shown that Dirac fields in the ECSK gravity satisfy the strong energy condition for the HawkingPenrose singularity theorems [19], and that the torsion-induced spin-spin interaction (the second term on the right
of (9)) enhances that condition [6]. Such an interaction is thus attractive. O’Connell has obtained the same result
using the spin contributions to the Lagrangian density [12]. Writing σik = Tik + Uik , the strong energy condition
holds if W = (σik − 12 gik σ ll )ui uk ≥ 0. For a homogeneous Universe, in the comoving frame of reference of an
irrotational cosmological fluid, the tensor (9) and the Dirac equation give W = 12 mψ̄ψ + 32 κs2 , suggesting that torsion
enhances the formation of singularities [6]. This result agrees with the contribution to W from the torsion-induced
spin-spin interaction, ˜ + 3p̃ = 2αn2 > 0. The singularity theorems, however, have another important component:
the Raychaudhuri equation, which describes the time evolution of the expansion scalar for timelike congruences [20].
The expansion scalar θ = ui:i measures the fractional rate at which a small volume of matter changes with time as
measured by a comoving observer. Because of the Raychaudhuri equation, timelike geodesics for the matter satisfying
the strong energy condition converge (θ decreases), resulting in a caustic, θ → −∞ (if θ decreases continuously), and
thus in a singularity within a finite proper time [20].
√
For the cosmological fluid in the comoving frame, the FLRW metric gives θ = (ln −g),0 = 3aȧ . Since the Universe
at the minimum scale factor acr undergoes a bounce from ȧ = −v to ȧ = v, the expansion scalar has a discontinuity
there, increasing from θ = − a3vcr to θ = a3vcr . As the Universe before the big bounce contracts, θ decreases from some

value given by the initial condition of the contraction to a value − a3vcr . At a = acr , θ jumps to a3vcr . As the Universe
after the bounce expands, θ decreases towards zero. The discontinuity of the expansion scalar at the cusp-like big
bounce therefore prevents θ from decreasing to −∞ and reaching a curvature singularity, and guarantees that timelike
geodesics in the Universe with Dirac fields coupled to torsion continue through the bounce. A similar discontinuity
completes timelike geodesics in the gravitational field of an Einstein-Rosen bridge [21].
