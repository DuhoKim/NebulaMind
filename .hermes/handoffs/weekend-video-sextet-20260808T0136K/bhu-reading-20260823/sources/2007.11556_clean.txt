### Abstract

We consider a universe formed in a black hole in the Einstein–Cartan theory of gravity.
The interior of a Schwarzschild black hole can be represented by the Kantowski–Sachs metric that describes a closed anisotropic universe.
We use this metric to derive the field equations with a relativistic spin fluid as a source.
We show that torsion may prevent a singularity and replace it with a nonsingular bounce if particle production dominates over shear.
Particle production after the last bounce can generate a finite period of inflation, during which the universe expands and isotropizes to the currently observed state.
Our universe might have therefore originated from a black hole. 

 Introduction. 
A recent evidence suggests that the universe may be closed closed .
Such a universe might have originated as a baby universe from a bounce in the interior of a black hole existing in a parent universe Pat ; ER ; cosmo ; ApJ .
A universe created by a black hole would be located on the other side of the event horizon of the black and connected to the parent universe through an Einstein–Rosen bridge ER .
Accordingly, its formation and expansion could not be observed outside the black hole because of the infinite redshift at the horizon.
This scenario, which we call Black-Hole Cosmology or Black-Hole Genesis, could naturally solve the black-hole information paradox cosmo .
A physical law that turns a black hole into a bridge to a new universe must avoid the black-hole singularity. 

The simplest and most natural mechanism for preventing gravitational singularities is provided by spacetime torsion within the Einstein–Cartan (EC) theory of gravity.
In this theory, expanded by Sciama and Kibble, the Lagrangian density for the gravitational field proportional to the Ricci scalar, as in the general theory relativity Lord ; EC ; Niko .
The conservation law for the total (orbital plus spin) angular momentum of fermionic matter in curved spacetime must be consistent with the Dirac equation that allows for the spin-orbit interaction.
This consistency requires that the antisymmetric part of the affine connection: the torsion tensor Schr , is not constrained to zero req .
Instead, torsion is determined by the field equations obtained from varying the action with respect to the torsion tensor Lord ; EC ; Niko .
The resulting torsion tensor is proportional to the spin tensor of fermionic matter.
Consequently, EC is equivalent to general relativity with the symmetric Levi-Civita connection, in which the energy–momentum tensor of matter acquires terms that are quadratic in the spin tensor.
The multipole expansion of the conservation law for the spin tensor in EC gives a spin tensor which describes fermionic matter as a spin fluid (ideal fluid with spin) NSH . 

Hehl Hehl , Trautman Tra , and Kopczyński Kop1 found that torsion can generate gravitational repulsion and prevent the formation of a cosmological singularity in a homogeneous and isotropic universe described by the Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker (FLRW) metric FLRW ; Lord ; LL2 ; GR when spins of fermions are aligned.
Hehl et al. HHK found that macroscopic averaging of the spin terms in the energy–momentum tensor gives a nonzero value even for randomly oriented spins.
Consequently, the effective energy density and pressure of a spin fluid are given by

At lower densities, the effects of torsion can be neglected and EC effectively reduces to general relativity.
At extremely high densities, much greater than nuclear density, the negative corrections from the spin-torsion coupling in ( 1 ) violate the strong energy condition and manifest themselves as repulsive gravity that may prevent the formation of a gravitational singularity in a black hole.
Instead, the collapsing matter undergoes a nonsingular bounce and then expands as a new, closed universe cosmo ; ApJ ; iso whose total energy is zero energy . 

Quantum particle production immediately after the bounce can generate a finite period of exponential inflation ApJ that is consistent with the Planck 2015 observations of the cosmic microwave background radiation SD .
A bouncing scenario also occurs if the spin tensor is completely antisymmetric spin .
Torsion may also explain the matter-antimatter asymmetry in the Universe anti and the present cosmic acceleration exp .
Furthermore, it may eliminate the ultraviolet divergence of loop Feynman diagrams in quantum field theory toreg . 

The interior of a Schwarzschild black hole can be represented by the Kantowski–Sachs metric (KS) Ewing .
This metric describes a closed, anisotropic universe KS ; exact .
Without further phenomena, such a universe would be singular and oscillatory osc , and would not exceed the size of the parent black hole.
If our universe was formed in a black hole, then it must have increased its entropy ApJ ; ent ; Garcia and isotropized in the past to match the currently observed FLRW universe isotro .
Both processes can be generated by cosmic inflation infl .
Without cosmic acceleration, anisotropy in a closed KS universe would be increasing pert . 

An anisotropic universe may have past and future singularities that can be avoided under specific conditions cond ; def .
Kopczyński Kop2 and Kuchowicz Kuc1 found that a spatially flat, homogeneous, and anisotropic universe of the Bianchi type I LL2 has no cosmological singularity if the effect of torsion is greater than that of shear.
Tafel Taf found that the avoidance of singularities in EC may also occur for flat, homogeneous universes of the Bianchi types I–VIII.
Further analysis was done in other for the Bianchi types with torsion, in Kuc2 for some particular functions representing the scale factors, and in Lor for a spin fluid satisfying the equation of state of stiff matter. 

A closed, homogeneous and anisotropic universe is either of the Bianchi type IX LL2 or described by the KS metric.
In this article, we consider a KS anisotropic universe, formed in a black hole and filled with a spin fluid.
We investigate its dynamics if the spin fluid is created by quantum particle production prod .
After reviewing the interior of a black hole, an anisotropic universe, and the shear tensor, we will demonstrate that such a universe can be nonsingular because of torsion and particle production acting together to violate the strong energy condition.
Furthermore, we will show that its dynamics after the bounce can have a finite period of inflation, during which it produces more matter and isotropizes, tending to the closed FLRW geometry.

Quantum particle creation in strongly changing gravitational fields can provide a mechanism that ensures that torsion dominates over shear in the Raychaudhuri equation.
Consequently, torsion and particle production together can replace a singularity in a universe in a black hole with a nonsingular bounce.

 Summary. 
Our universe may be closed closed and slightly anisotropic evi .
The closed geometry and anisotropy may indicate that the universe was born in a black hole.
We analyzed a closed Kantowski–Sachs universe satisfying the Einstein–Cartan field equations with a spin fluid.
We showed that torsion and particle production may together violate the strong energy condition and give the universe a nonsingular bounce that stops contraction and starts expansion.
Furthermore, particle production in an expanding phase can generate a finite period of inflation, during which the universe produces enormous amounts of matter and entropy, and isotropizes.
The universe may have several bounces and cycles of expansion and contraction, until it reaches the size at which the cosmological constant takes over and the universe expands indefinitely.
