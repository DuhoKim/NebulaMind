1 Introduction


The hierarchy problem—the immense gulf between the electroweak scale and Planck scale—may be
resolved by theories with extra dimensions (EDs) that lower the fundamental 𝐷-dimensional scale of gravity,
𝑀D, to the TeV range. In the Arkani-Hamed, Dimopoulos, and Dvali (ADD) model [1, 2], gravity propagates
through 𝑛flat EDs (where 𝐷= 4 + 𝑛), while Standard Model (SM) fields are restricted to four-dimensional
space-time. Alternatively, the Randall–Sundrum (RS) model [3] employs one warped extra dimension
to generate this scale hierarchy. These theories predict a plethora of new phenomena, some of which
are accessible at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC). One such prediction is the production of quantum
black holes (QBHs) [4–8] at threshold masses 𝑀th ∼𝑀D. In this benchmark framework, it is postulated
that the QBH parton–parton cross-section follows a power-law increase with partonic centre-of-mass
energy, with the power determined by the number of extra dimensions. For higher proton-beam energy,
the suppression of the partonic cross-section arising from the parton distribution functions (PDF) at high
Bjorken-𝑥(the fraction of the proton’s momentum carried by the interacting parton) is reduced, resulting
in a significant increase in the QBH production cross-section, 𝜎QBH. Unlike semi-classical black holes,
which decay thermally via Hawking radiation into high-multiplicity final states [9–13], these QBHs decay
into two-particle final states, where lepton- and baryon-number conservation may be violated. This search
focuses on diquark production of QBHs that can decay to lepton+antiquark (ℓ+ ¯𝑞) final states:

         𝑢𝑢→QBH4/3 →¯𝑑ℓ+,  𝑢𝑑→QBH1/3 →¯𝑢ℓ+,  𝑑𝑑→QBH−2/3 →¯𝑑ℓ−,            (1)

where the superscript indicates the QBH electric charge and the processes are sorted by descending 𝜎QBH,
left-to-right. The notation 𝑢and 𝑑marks the up-type and down-type quarks. The branching ratios for the
processes ordered as in Eq. (1) are 11%, 5.6% and 6.7%, to each lepton flavour [4].

A key feature that sets this model apart from virtually all other new-physics scenarios, and motivates
this search already with a partial run dataset, is the production cross-section’s strong dependence on the
proton–proton centre-of-mass energy, √𝑠. This dependence causes the increase in √𝑠from Run 2 of the
LHC (13 TeV) to Run 3 (13.6 TeV) to enhance 𝜎QBH by approximately 100% at 𝑀th = 6 TeV up to about
1000% at 𝑀th = 10.5 TeV [4].
This analysis supersedes the previous ATLAS search performed by ATLAS at √𝑠= 13 TeV using the
full 140 fb−1 Run 2 dataset [14]. The same search was done by ATLAS at √𝑠= 8 TeV with 20.3 fb−1 of
data [15]. Complementary searches were also performed in dijet, dilepton and photon+jet final states by
ATLAS [16–21] and CMS [22–25]. In comparison with the other final states, the lepton+jet ones benefit
from significantly lower backgrounds, which compensates for their smaller cross-section times branching
ratio. Lepton+jet final states are of course not unique to QBH production, and other searches probing
similar regions of phase space do not necessarily provide an explicit interpretation in terms of QBHs. For
example, ATLAS has published a lepton+jet search for leptoquarks with a combination of the full Run 2
dataset and a partial (56 fb−1) Run 3 dataset [26].
The present search uses 164 fb−1 of √𝑠= 13.6 TeV proton–proton collision data, recorded by the ATLAS
detector during the 2022–2024 period of Run 3. Even with a Run 3 dataset similar in size to the full Run 2
dataset, this analysis yields considerably higher QBH exclusion limits thanks to the significant increase in
𝜎QBH. Electron+jet and muon+jet final states with an invariant mass above 3 TeV are targeted separately
in this search. Explicit contributions from QBH decays with 𝜏-leptons are not considered, since they
are expected to be negligible compared to those from the other two lepton+jet final states, owing to the
𝜏→ℓ¯𝜈ℓ𝜈𝜏branching ratio and the softer electron and muon transverse momentum (𝑝T) spectra due to

the presence of neutrinos in 𝜏decays. The main background processes include 𝑉+jets (𝑉= 𝑊, 𝑍), 𝑉𝑉
and events with top quarks. These are modelled using Monte Carlo (MC) simulations. Events containing
non-prompt electrons, or electrons from photon conversions or jets misidentified as electrons, collectively
referred to as ‘fakes’, are estimated using data-driven techniques. This is the dominant background in the
electron channel, while 𝑊+jets production contributes the most background in the muon channel.


2 ATLAS detector


The ATLAS experiment [27, 28] at the LHC is a multipurpose particle detector with a forward–backward
symmetric cylindrical geometry and a near 4𝜋coverage in solid angle.1 It consists of an inner tracking
detector surrounded by a thin superconducting solenoid providing a 2 T axial magnetic field, electromagnetic
and hadronic calorimeters, and a muon spectrometer (MS). The inner tracking detector covers the
pseudorapidity range |𝜂| < 2.5.  It consists of silicon pixel, silicon microstrip, and transition radiation
tracking detectors. Lead/liquid-argon (LAr) sampling calorimeters provide electromagnetic (EM) energy
measurements with high granularity within the region |𝜂| < 3.2. A steel/scintillator-tile hadronic calorimeter
covers the central pseudorapidity range (|𝜂| < 1.7). The endcap and forward regions are instrumented
with LAr calorimeters for EM and hadronic energy measurements up to |𝜂| = 4.9. The MS surrounds
the calorimeters and is based on three large superconducting air-core toroidal magnets with eight coils
each. The field integral of the toroids ranges between 2.0 and 6.0 T m across most of the detector. The
MS includes a system of precision tracking chambers up to |𝜂| = 2.7 and fast detectors for triggering up
to |𝜂| = 2.4. The luminosity is measured mainly by the LUCID–2 detector which is located close to the
beampipe [29]. A two-level trigger system was used to select events [30, 31]. The first-level trigger is
implemented in hardware and used a subset of the detector information to accept events at a rate close to
100 kHz. This is followed by a software-based trigger that reduced the accepted rate of complete events to
3 kHz on average, depending on the data-taking conditions. A software suite [32] is used in data simulation,
in the reconstruction and analysis of real and simulated data, in detector operations, and in the trigger and
data acquisition systems of the experiment.


3 Data and simulation


Data and simulated event samples used in this study must satisfy a requirement that all detector systems
operate normally [33]. The data were collected with single-electron or single-muon triggers [31]. The 𝑝T
threshold is 26 (24) GeV for isolated electron (muon) triggers and 60 (50) GeV for non-isolated triggers.
As the analysis targets very high mass QBHs whose decays produce a lepton with 𝑝T > 150 GeV, virtually
all events were triggered by high-𝑝T lepton triggers without isolation requirements whose 𝑝T threshold is
140 (50) GeV for electrons (muons). The application of data-quality requirements [33] results in a sample
corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 164 ± 3 fb−1.

QBH MC signal samples were generated using the QBH v3.02 generator [7] to compute the production
cross-sections and model the hard-scatter process, assuming zero angular momentum. Events were then
interfaced to Pythia 8 [34] to model parton showering and hadronisation. As in previous iterations of
searches for QBHs, the CTEQ6L1 [35] PDF set was used and the QCD factorisation scale set to the inverse
gravitational radius [7]. Requiring the QBH mass to be in the range (1−3)𝑀th (with 𝑀th = 𝑀D) ensures
that QBHs are produced in a region where quantum effects are significant, excluding thermal decays. In
the ADD model, 𝑛= 2, 4, or 6 EDs are assumed, whereas 𝑛= 1 is used in the RS model. The kinematic
distributions of the decay products are governed by the QBH mass; consequently, the signal acceptance
and reconstruction efficiency are found to be independent of the specific model type or the number of
extra dimensions. The production cross-section is significantly lower for the RS model than for the ADD
model. Samples were produced in 0.5 TeV steps through threshold mass ranges of 𝑀th ∈[6, 8.5] TeV and
[8, 10.5] TeV for the RS and ADD models, respectively.

Background events from 𝑊+jets, 𝑍+jets and 𝑉𝑉(𝑊𝑊, 𝑍𝑍, 𝑊𝑍) production were modelled with the
Sherpa 2.2.14 generator [36] using the NNPDF3.0nnlo PDF set [37]. The 𝑡¯𝑡and single-top processes
were modelled using the Powheg Box v2 [38–41] generator at NLO with the NNPDF3.0nlo [37] PDF
set. The 𝑡¯𝑡𝑊process was modelled using Sherpa 2.2.14, while other 𝑡¯𝑡𝑉samples were produced with
MadGraph5_aMC@NLO 2.3.3 [42] interfaced to Pythia 8 [43]. Dijet MC samples were produced
with Pythia 8 [34]. EvtGen 2.1.1 [44] was used to perform bottom- and charm-quark decays except
for the backgrounds modelled using Sherpa, for which the decays were performed internally. Non-
prompt electrons are removed from all MC samples to avoid double-counting their contribution, which
is obtained from data.  For the muon channel, this background, estimated from dijet MC events, is
negligible. The same dijet MC sample is used in the electron channel to cross-check the data-driven
fake-background estimate. All generated MC events undergo the full ATLAS detector simulation [45]
using Geant4 [46]. The fully simulated MC events are reconstructed in the same manner as the data events
and corrected to match the number of interactions per bunch crossing (in-time pile-up) observed in data.
The effect of multiple interactions in the same and neighbouring bunch crossings (pile-up) was modelled
by overlaying [47] the simulated hard-scattering event with inelastic proton–proton events generated from a
mix of Epos 2.0.1.4 [48] and Pythia 8.308 [34].


4 Event selection and background estimation


The physics objects used to reconstruct the lepton+jet final states in this analysis include electrons, muons,
jets, and missing transverse momentum. The definitions of these objects as well as the event selection
criteria are given below.

Signal electron candidates are reconstructed from energy clusters in the electromagnetic (EM) calorimeter
that are matched to an inner-detector track. They are required to have |𝜂| < 2.47, excluding the transition
region 1.37 < |𝜂| < 1.52 between the barrel and endcap EM calorimeters. To ensure that these electrons
originate from the primary vertex, the longitudinal impact parameter 𝑧0 of their track relative to the primary
vertex must satisfy |𝑧0 sin 𝜃| < 0.5 mm, where 𝜃is the track’s polar angle. Similarly, their transverse impact
parameter 𝑑0 must have a significance satisfying |𝑑0|/𝜎(𝑑0) ≤5, where 𝜎(𝑑0) is the uncertainty in 𝑑0.
Electrons are required to have 𝑝T > 150 GeV and satisfy the Tight identification and HighPtCaloOnly
isolation criteria [49, 50]. They must also match the corresponding trigger-level electron object.

Signal muon candidates are reconstructed by matching inner-detector tracks in the region |𝜂| < 2.5 to
tracks reconstructed in the MS. For muons, the transverse impact parameter requirement is tightened to



                                         4

|𝑑0|/𝜎(𝑑0) ≤3, whereas the longitudinal one is the same as for electrons. Muons must satisfy 𝑝T > 150 GeV
and also the High-𝑝𝑇identification criterion and PflowTightVarRad isolation requirement [51]. This
identification criterion is used to ensure optimal muon momentum resolution at high 𝑝T. It requires at least
three hits in each of three layers of precision tracking chambers in the MS, while specific regions of the MS
where the alignment is suboptimal are vetoed. Selected muons are also required to match the trigger-level
muon objects.

Jets are reconstructed from particle-flow objects [52] using the anti-𝑘𝑡algorithm [53, 54] with a radius
parameter of 𝑅= 0.4. All jets are required to have |𝜂| < 2.5. Signal jets must satisfy 𝑝T > 130 GeV. To
resolve ambiguities between reconstructed objects, a standard overlap-removal procedure is applied to
leptons and jets to avoid the double-counting of energy deposits [26].

All reconstructed objects in simulations have their reconstruction efficiency, energy scale and energy

Run 3 conditions. Moreover, all events are required to contain a primary vertex built from at least two

with the highest sum of squared transverse momenta Σ𝑝2T of its associated tracks [57].
Candidate events in the signal region (SR) require exactly one signal lepton and at least one signal jet
with invariant mass 𝑚ℓ𝑗> 3 TeV. The angular separation between the lepton and the jet is required to
satisfy Δ𝜙ℓ𝑗> 2.8 and Δ𝜂ℓ𝑗< 3.25 to exploit the back-to-back topology of the QBH decay. To estimate
the shape and/or normalisation of the dominant backgrounds, the analysis defines several control and
validation regions (CRs and VRs, respectively). The normalisation of contributions from 𝑊and 𝑍events is
estimated in single-bin CRs (𝑊CR and 𝑍CR), while their shapes are taken from MC simulation, following
confirmation that no shape correction from data is needed. These estimates are validated in the respective
validation regions (𝑊VR and 𝑍VR), by examining the ratios of the total background to data and checking
that they are within the uncertainty. Smaller backgrounds from 𝑡¯𝑡, single-top, 𝑉𝑉and 𝑡¯𝑡𝑉events are
estimated directly from MC simulation. The background due to fake/non-prompt electrons is estimated
from data by using the matrix method (MM) [58]; the rates determined in a dedicated fake-enriched CR
( 𝑓CR) are applied to all other analysis regions to obtain the fake-electron background contribution there.
The behaviour of this background is examined in a corresponding VR ( 𝑓VR). In the muon channel, the
corresponding contribution is estimated from the dijet MC sample and found to be negligible. All CRs
and VRs are orthogonal to the SRs and to each other via selections on the 𝑚ℓ𝑗and S(𝑝missT   ) variables.
The region definitions and event selection are summarised in Table 1. The final background estimate is
obtained from a simultaneous fit to data in the aforementioned regions. The fit procedure is described in
Section 6.

In the electron channel, the dominant background in the SR arises from fakes, estimated via the MM. The
MM requires the definition of ‘real’ and ‘fake’ rates, 𝑟and 𝑓, respectively. These probabilities are defined
as the ratio of events coming from two different electron populations: signal and baseline. The signal
criteria are defined above. The baseline criteria are defined by relaxing the electron identification from
Tight to Medium and removing the isolation requirement [49, 50]. These criteria are only used in order
to (i) estimate 𝑓in 𝑓CR, and (ii) build the respective background template in the different regions. The
estimation of 𝑟is performed in a ‘real’-enriched region using 𝑍(→𝑒+𝑒−) +jets MC events. The estimation
of 𝑓is performed in a ‘fake’-enriched region that is both orthogonal to the SR and contains a high-enough
event yield. This is achieved by requiring that 𝑚ℓ𝑗be in the range 1–3 TeV. To increase the fakes’ purity
in that region, contributions from the dominant 𝑊+jets and 𝑍+jets backgrounds are reduced by placing
a requirement on S(𝑝missT   ) or 𝑚𝑒𝑒, respectively. The resulting fraction of events with fakes in the 𝑓CR

In the electron channel prior to the fit, the fakes component constitutes approximately 56% of the total
SR background, followed by 𝑊+jets at 32%, with 𝑍+jets, top, and diboson processes contributing the
remaining 12%. In the muon channel, 𝑊+jets dominates at approximately 80%, with 𝑍+jets, top, and
diboson processes accounting for the remaining 20%. The signal acceptance times efficiency ranges from
76% to 86% (52% to 58%) for the electron (muon) channel, for the signals considered in this analysis. The
factors responsible for the difference in yield between the two channels are the lepton acceptance times
efficiency and the lepton momentum resolution. The muon trigger has a lower geometric coverage than
the electron counterpart. Furthermore, very tight identification requirements are necessary to maintain a
reasonable muon momentum resolution at high 𝑝T. Uncertainties associated with the modelling of high-𝑝T
muon identification are taken into account as discussed in Ref. [51]. The limited momentum resolution
leads to a significantly worse muon+jet mass resolution as compared to the electron+jet case and thus
broader signal shapes. The lepton+jet mass resolution in the electron channel is consistently below 5% for
all signal 𝑀th points considered in the analysis. The muon-channel mass resolution varies between 25%
and 30% for signal 𝑀th values between 6 TeV and 10 TeV, respectively. The resolution is obtained from
the quadrature sum of the standard deviation and the small bias in the mean of the respective lepton+jet
mass reconstruction response distribution.

5 Systematic uncertainties


Systematic uncertainties affecting the signal and background expectations arise from various experimental
and theoretical sources and are evaluated using the methods described below. Theoretical uncertainties are
only calculated for the two leading MC backgrounds, 𝑊+jets and 𝑍+jets, since the others are significantly
smaller and their uncertainties have negligible impact on the analysis. These uncertainties, which originate
from missing higher-order corrections, contribute via the choice of QCD renormalisation and factorisation
scales, strong coupling constant and PDFs [37]. These sources are collectively referred to as ‘theory
uncertainties’ and amount to ∼30% of the 𝑉+jets background yield in the SR. Following previous
publications, no theoretical uncertainties are assigned to the QBH benchmark signals. Experimental
uncertainties are evaluated for all MC signal and background samples. These uncertainties include
those estimated by varying the reconstruction, identification, isolation and trigger efficiencies, and the
energy/momentum calibration of electrons [59], muons [51], jets [60] and 𝑝miss [55], as well as uncertainties                                                                  T
in the integrated luminosity [61] and pile-up. The theory and experimental uncertainties are propagated to
the fakes background through the prompt-electron MC event yield that is subtracted from data in the 𝑓CR,
as explained in the previous section. Collectively, they correspond to an uncertainty of approximately 18%
of the fakes background in the SR, with the dominant contribution being from QCD scale uncertainties.
Additionally, a separate normalisation uncertainty due to the fakes in the 𝑓CR and SR not having the
same mix of origins is considered. In this analysis, almost all fakes originate from light-flavour decays
or photon conversions, according to the dijet MC sample. The two origins populate the 𝑓CR and SR
differently.  Light-flavour decays account for 92% (71%) of fakes in the 𝑓CR (SR), whereas photon
conversions account for 8% (29%). The corresponding uncertainty is found by calculating the weighted

6 Results

A signal-plus-background model is used to fit the observed 𝑚ℓ𝑗spectra simultaneously in the SR, 𝑊CR
and 𝑍CR using the profile-likelihood method [62]. The expected and observed event counts in each
𝑚ℓ𝑗bin are described by Poisson-distributed variables, where the uncertainties are accounted for via
nuisance parameters. The sensitivity of the analysis is driven by the highest 𝑚ℓ𝑗bin of the SR, 5–13 TeV.
Contributions above 13 TeV are included in this bin. In that bin, the number of background events in the
electron channel is predicted to be 1.46 ± 0.65stat. ± 0.38syst., where the leading systematic uncertainty is
from the fakes estimation. In the muon channel, the number of background events in the highest bin is
predicted to be 3.10 ± 0.95stat. ± 0.47syst., where the leading systematic uncertainty is due to the modelling
of the 𝑊+jets background. Normalisation factors for the leading MC backgrounds, 𝑊+jets and 𝑍+jets, are
found to be 1.2 ± 0.5 and 1.1 ± 0.4 (1.1 ± 0.5 and 1.0 ± 0.3) for 𝑊+jets and 𝑍+jets in the electron (muon)
channel, respectively. A moderate 20% (25%) non-closure is observed in the 𝑍VR in the electron (muon)
channel; given the small 𝑍+jets fraction of 5% (12%) in the SR, the potential impact on the background

yield in the SR is at most 1%–2% and is negligible. It is therefore not included in the fit as a separate
uncertainty.

7 Conclusion

This ATLAS analysis is performed using 164 fb−1 of data collected from √𝑠= 13.6 TeV 𝑝𝑝collisions
at the LHC. The search for QBH-like signals yields no evidence of an excess in the lepton+jet invariant
mass distribution in electron+jet or muon+jet final states. Exclusion limits are placed on the production
cross-section times branching ratio in a few ADD and RS QBH scenarios. The experimental sensitivity is
driven by the statistical uncertainty. The highest exclusion limits are for ADD QBH scenarios with six
extra dimensions, observed at 9.4 TeV (9.0 TeV) in the electron (muon) channel. Owing to the much better
momentum resolution and larger acceptance times efficiency in the electron channel, the sensitivity is
significantly higher. These limits represent the world-best sensitivity for the signals considered.
