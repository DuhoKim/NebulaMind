A Peek Outside Our Universe

Enrique Gaztanaga *    Pablo Fosalba
Abstract

According to general relativity (GR), a universe with a cosmological constant Λ\Lambda, like ours, is trapped inside an event horizon, r<3/Λr<\sqrt{3/\Lambda}. What is outside? We show, using Israel (1967) junction conditions, that there could be a different universe outside. Our universe looks like a black hole for an outside observer. Outgoing radial null geodesics cannot escape our universe, but incoming photons can enter and leave an imprint on our CMB sky. We present a picture of such a fossil record from the analysis of CMB maps that agrees with the black hole universe predictions but challenges our understanding of the origin of the primordial universe.

1 Introduction

An event horizon (EH) r∗r_{*}, or trapped surface, for a given observer can be defined as the distance beyond which this observer will never see: r<r∗r<r_{*}. The most famous EH is, of course, that of a black hole (BH) of mass M. Dimensional analysis tells us that a relativistic (cc) gravitational (GG) system of mass MM has an associated EH: r∗≃G​M/c2r_{*}\simeq GM/c^{2}. The same dimensional analysis indicates that a cosmological constant Λ\Lambda is associated with a relativistic EH, r∗≃1/Λ​c2r_{*}\simeq 1/\sqrt{\Lambda c^{2}}, which, in principle, is independent of GG. We will show first how these EHs appear as solutions to Einstein’s field equations and next how the different EHs are related in our universe expansion.

The most general form for a metric with spherical symmetry in proper coordinates xμ=(t,r,δ,θ)x^{\mu}=(t,r,\delta,\theta) with c=1c=1 can be written as Padmanabhan 2010:

d​s2=gμ​ν​d​xμ​d​xν=−A⁡(t,r)​d​t2+B⁡(t,r)​d​r2+r2​d​Ω2ds^{2}=g_{\mu\nu}dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}=-A(t,r)dt^{2}+B(t,r)dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2} (1)

where we have introduced: d​Ω2=cos2⁡δ​d​θ2+d​δ2d\Omega^{2}=\cos^{2}\delta d\theta^{2}+d\delta^{2}. Einstein’s field equations for that metric in empty space ρ=p=Λ=0\rho=p=\Lambda=0 result in the Schwarzschild (SW) metric:

d​s2=−[1−2​G​Mr]​d​t2+d​r21−2​G​Mr+r2​d​Ω2ds^{2}=-[1-\frac{2GM}{r}]\penalty\ dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{1-\frac{2GM}{r}}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2} (2)

where MM can be interpreted as a singular point mass at r=0r=0. As it is well known, the EH at r∗=2​G​Mr_{*}=2GM prevents us from seeing such a naked singularity Penrose 1969. Outgoing null radial geodesics cannot leave the interior of r∗r_{*}, whereas incoming null radial geodesics can cross inside r∗r_{*} in proper time, even when, for an observer outside, this takes t=∞t=\infty in her time.

1.1 deSitter Metric

The solution for Equation (1) with ρ=p=M=0\rho=p=M=0 but Λ≠0\Lambda\neq 0 is the deSitter (dS) metric:

d​s2=−[1−r2​HΛ2]​d​t2+d​r21−r2​HΛ2+r2​d​Ω2ds^{2}=-[1-r^{2}H_{\Lambda}^{2}]\penalty\ dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{1-r^{2}H_{\Lambda}^{2}}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2} (3)

which is also static and has an EH, or trapped surface, at r∗=1/HΛr_{*}=1/H_{\Lambda}, where HΛ2≡8​π​G​ρΛ/3H_{\Lambda}^{2}\equiv 8\pi G\rho_{\Lambda}/3 and ρΛ=Λ/(8​π​G)+ρv​a​c\rho_{\Lambda}=\Lambda/(8\pi G)+\rho_{vac}. We include a constant ρv​a​c=V=V⁡(ϕ)=−pv​a​c\rho_{vac}=V=V(\phi)=-p_{vac} to account for vacuum energy (or the potential of a trapped scalar field), which is physically degenerate with Λ\Lambda. The inside of r∗=1/HΛr_{*}=1/H_{\Lambda} is causally disconnected and corresponds to an EH: radial null events (d​s2=0ds^{2}=0) connecting (0,r0)(0,r_{0}) with (t,r)(t,r) take t=∞t=\infty to reach r=r∗r=r_{*} from any point inside.

1.2 The FLRW Metric and Λ\Lambda

The Friedmann–Lemaitre–Robertson–Walker (FLRW) flat (k=0k=0) metric in spherical comoving coordinates (τ,χ,δ,θ)(\tau,\chi,\delta,\theta), corresponds to a homogeneous and isotropic space-time:

d​s2=−d​τ2+a​(τ)2​[d​χ2+χ2​d​Ω2]ds^{2}=-d\tau^{2}+a(\tau)^{2}\left[d\chi^{2}+\chi^{2}d\Omega^{2}\right] (4)

where the scale factor a⁡(τ)a(\tau) describes the expansion/contraction as a function of time. For a comoving observer and a perfect fluid, the field equations reduce to:

3​H2≡3​(a˙a)2=8​π​G​(ρm​a−3+ρR​a−4+ρΛ)3H^{2}\equiv 3\left(\frac{\dot{a}}{a}\right)^{2}=8\pi G(\rho_{m}a^{-3}+\rho_{R}a^{-4}+\rho_{\Lambda}) (5)

where ρm\rho_{m} and ρR\rho_{R} are the matter and radiation density today (a=1a=1) and ρΛ=−pΛ\rho_{\Lambda}=-p_{\Lambda} is the effective cosmological constant density introduced in the dS metric. Given ρ\rho and pp at some time, we can find a=a⁡(τ)a=a(\tau) and determine the metric in Equation (4). Observations show that the expansion rate today is dominated by ρΛ\rho_{\Lambda}. This indicates that the FLRW metric describes the interior of a trapped surface of size r∗=1/HΛr_{*}=1/H_{\Lambda}, like the dS metric. In fact, both metrics are equivalent in that regime Mitra 2012; Gaztanaga 2021. They also reproduce the steady-state cosmological principle O’Raifeartaigh and Mitton 2015.

Interpreting Λ\Lambda as a boundary term to the GR equations and imposing the so-called zero action principle, r∗=1/HΛr_{*}=1/H_{\Lambda} can be given in terms of ordinary contributions to the energy density, ρΛ=<ρm/2+ρR>\rho_{\Lambda}=<\rho_{m}/2+\rho_{R}> Gaztañaga 2021; Gaztanaga 2020.

1.3 SW–FLRW Perturbation

The Schwarzschild (SW) metric is commonly used to describe the outside of BHs or stars and it should be understood as a perturbation inside a larger background. An example of this is the Schwarzschild–deSitter (SW–dS) metric:

d​s2=−[1−2​G​Mr−r2​HΛ2]​d​t2+d​r21−2​G​Mr−r2​HΛ2+r2​d​Ω2ds^{2}=-[1-\frac{2GM}{r}-r^{2}H_{\Lambda}^{2}]\penalty\ dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{1-\frac{2GM}{r}-r^{2}H_{\Lambda}^{2}}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2} (6)

which corresponds to an exact solution with both non-zero Λ\Lambda and MM. More generally, the FLRW metric is the background to the SW solution Kaloper et al. 2010, e.g., it replaces r=a⁡(τ)​χr=a(\tau)\chi in Equation (2). For large rr, we recover the dS metric, as in Equation (6) above, which is equivalent to the FLRW metric dominated by Λ\Lambda Mitra 2012. Close to the BH at r∗<r<1/HΛr_{*}<r<1/H_{\Lambda}, we recover the SW metric.

2 Outside Our FLRW Universe

In proper coordinates r=a​χr=a\chi, the FLRW metric with H=H⁡(τ)H=H(\tau) is also trapped inside the same EH as the dS metric, r∗=1/HΛr_{*}=1/H_{\Lambda}, because H⁡(τ)>HΛH(\tau)>H_{\Lambda}. We can see this by considering outgoing radial null geodesics in the FLRW metric (Equation (4)):

ro​u​t=a⁡(τ)​∫τ∞d​τ′a⁡(τ′)=a​∫a∞d​ln⁡a′a′​H​(a′)<1HΛ=r∗r_{out}=a(\tau)\int_{\tau}^{\infty}\frac{d\tau^{\prime}}{a(\tau^{\prime})}=a\int_{a}^{\infty}\frac{d\ln{a^{\prime}}}{a^{\prime}H(a^{\prime})}<\frac{1}{H_{\Lambda}}=r_{*} (7)

which shows that signals cannot escape from the inside to the outside of the EH. However, incoming radial null geodesics a⁡(τ)​∫0τd​τa⁡(τ)a(\tau)\int_{0}^{\tau}\frac{d\tau}{a(\tau)} could be larger than r∗r_{*} if we look back in time long enough. This shows that observers living in the interior are trapped inside the EH, but they can, in principle, observe what happened outside.

What is outside r∗=1/HΛr_{*}=1/H_{\Lambda} in the FLRW metric? The FLRW comoving coordinates (τ,χ)(\tau,\chi) can be matched to the SW proper coordinates (t,r)(t,r). The joint metric is what Gaztañaga 2021; Gaztanaga 2021 call a BHU solution. The particular case where the inside is dS (and the outside SW) is called BH.fv (where fv stands for false vacuum). The BHU metric is also a solution to Einstein’s field equations. To prove this, we simply need to show that the junction follows the Israel matching conditions Israel 1967. The two metrics can be matched on a timelike hypersurface Σ\Sigma of constant χ\chi:

d​sΣ2=ha​b​d​ya​d​yb=−d​τ2+a2​(τ)​χ§2​d​Ω2ds^{2}_{\Sigma}=h_{ab}dy^{a}dy^{b}=-d\tau^{2}+a^{2}(\tau)\chi_{\lx@sectionsign}^{2}d\Omega^{2} (8)

and the extrinsic curvature KK at Σ\Sigma is the same in both sides. The matching conditions h−=h+h_{-}=h_{+} and K+=K−K_{+}=K_{-} reduce to Gaztanaga 2021

r=R⁡(τ)=a⁡(τ)​χ§;R˙2=R2​H2=R∗Rr=R(\tau)=a(\tau)\chi_{\lx@sectionsign}\penalty\ \penalty\ ;\penalty\ \penalty\ \dot{R}^{2}=R^{2}H^{2}=\frac{R_{*}}{R} (9)

where R∗≡2​G​MR_{*}\equiv 2GM. Staring from small aa, as we increase τ\tau, both RR and R˙=H​R\dot{R}=HR grow until we reach H​R=c=1HR=c=1, which corresponds to the event horizon R∗=2​G​M=1/HΛR_{*}=2GM=1/H_{\Lambda}. It takes t=∞t=\infty in SW time to asymptotically reach R∗R_{*}. This proves that the joint BHU metric is also a solution to Einstein’s field equations with no surface terms in the junction. This is equivalent to stating that the Λ\Lambda term corresponds to a trapped surface R∗=1/HΛR_{*}=1/H_{\Lambda} in the FLRW metric which matches the EH of a BH. Generalization to k≠1k\neq 1 is straightforward (see §12.5.1 in Padmanabhan 2010; Stuckey 1994; Gaztanaga 2021).

Recall that the SW metric is a perturbation of a larger FLRW metric, i.e, a BH-like metric embedded in a background described by the FLRW metric. This means that outside r∗=1/HΛr_{*}=1/H_{\Lambda}, we have another FLRW metric, like in a Matryoshka doll. From the outside, the inner FLRW metric looks like a BH. There could be many other BHUs inside and outside r∗=1/HΛr_{*}=1/H_{\Lambda}, so the structure could be better described by a fractal.

2.1 Causal Structure

In the FLRW universe, the Hubble horizon rHr_{H} is defined as rH=c/Hr_{H}=c/H. Scales larger than rHr_{H} cannot evolve because the time a perturbation takes to travel that distance is larger than the expansion time. This means that r>rHr>r_{H} scales are ”frozen out” (structure cannot evolve) and are causally disconnected from the rest. Thus, c/Hc/H represents a dynamical causal horizon that is evolving (blue line in Figure 1).

We can sketch the evolution of our universe in Figure 1. A primordial field ψ\psi settles or fluctuates into a false (or slow-rolling) vacuum which will create a BH.fv with a junction Σ\Sigma in Equation (8), where the causal boundary is fixed in comoving coordinates and corresponds to the particle horizon during inflation, χ§=c/(ai​Hi)\chi_{\lx@sectionsign}=c/(a_{i}H_{i}), or the Hubble horizon when inflation begins. The size R=a⁡(τ)​χ§R=a(\tau)\chi_{\lx@sectionsign} of this vacuum grows and asymptotically tends to R∗=c/HR_{*}=c/H following Equation (9) with H=HiH=H_{i}. The inside of this BH will be expanding exponentially, a=eτ​Hia=e^{\tau H_{i}}, while the Hubble horizon is fixed at 1/Hi1/H_{i}. When this inflation ends Starobinskiǐ 1979; Guth 1981; Linde 1982; Albrecht and Steinhardt 1982, vacuum energy excess converts into matter and radiation (reheating). This results in BHU, where the infinitesimal Hubble horizon starts to grow following the standard Big Bang evolution. The observable universe (or particle horizon) after inflation, χO\chi_{O}, is:

2.2 A Peek Outside

A recent analysis of the Planck temperature anisotropy data, Ref. Fosalba and Gaztañaga 2021, shows that the distribution of best-fit dark energy density ΩΛ\Omega_{\Lambda} exhibits three distinct regions across the CMB sky (marked by the three large grey circles in Figure 2). These regions have radii ranging from 40 to 70 degrees. The sizes of these structures are in agreement with the scale of the causal boundary χ§\chi_{\lx@sectionsign} for Λ\Lambda dominated universes. As shown in Fosalba and Gaztañaga 2021, the size of each of these regions is correlated with the mean value of ΩΛ\Omega_{\Lambda} over that portion of the sky, in good agreement with the BHU prediction. The same large-scale anisotropic patterns are observed for the distribution of other basic Λ\LambdaCDM cosmological parameters. This represents a very significant break-down of the main hypothesis of the Big Bang model: the assumption that the universe is isotropic on a large scale. The observed anisotropy has a tiny probability (of order 10−910^{-9}) of being a Gaussian statistical fluctuation of an otherwise isotropic universe Fosalba and Gaztañaga 2021.

In summary, Figure 2 shows that: (a) regions with a given value of ΩΛ\Omega_{\Lambda} have a corresponding angular size θ\theta that agrees with the BHU prediction (see Figure 31 in Fosalba and Gaztañaga 2021), (b) causally disconnected regions of the sky (larger than χ§\chi_{\lx@sectionsign}) fit the same physical model of acoustic oscillations very well, and (c) the background is similar but with parameters that are significantly different across disconnected regions. This suggests that the underlying physical mechanism sourcing the observed anisotropy encompasses scales beyond our causal universe. This is in apparent tension with simple models of inflation (as sources of perturbation for the largest scales) and opens the door to revisiting our basic understanding of the origin of the primordial universe Gaztanaga 2022.
