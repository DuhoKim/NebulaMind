Baby universe metric equivalent to an interior black-hole metric 
Pedro F. Gonz~ilez-Diaz 
tnstituto de Optica "Daza de Fald~s'" CSI C, Serrano 121, E-28006 Madrid, Spain

It is shown that the maximally extended metric corresponding to a large wormhole is the unique possible wormhole metric 
whose baby universe sector is conformally equivalent to the maximal inextendible Kruskal metric corresponding to the interior 
region of a Schwarzschild black hole whose gravitational radius is half the wormhole neck radius. The physical implications of 
this result in the black hole evaporation process are discussed. 
The Hawking effect [ 1 ] by which an external ob- 
server detects thermal radiation coming from a black 
hole is just a semiclassical process. The approxima- 
tion is only justified when the mass of the black hole 
is much greater than the Planck mass. What happens 
in the latest stages of the evaporation process is not 
quite clear, however [2]. A reasonable conjecture 
could be that black holes disappear completely, tak- 
ing with them all the particles that fell in to form the 
black hole and the antiparticles to emit radiation. 
Hawking has conjectured [ 3 ] that all these particles 
disappear by going off through a wormhole into a lit- 
tle closed universe, which branches off from our re- 
gion of spacetime. According to Hawking [ 3 ], the 
other end of the wormhole would appear to be an- 
other black hole which formed from the collapse of a 
star made up of massive antifermions, and evapo- 
rated, giving off the antiparticles to the particles 
forming the thermal radiation emitted by the first 
black hole in our region. 
Thus, starting from an initial state which is just fiat 
space, the final state of the black hole evaporation 
process will be fiat space plus a number of baby closed 
universes. Such a topology change is only allowed 
when one is dealing with a spacetime manifold pro- 
vided with a positive definite metric, where the four 
directions of spacetime are considered at the same 
footing, i.e. for a euclidean manifold with imaginary 
time. 
Difficulties with the above guess appear when one 
tries to implement it in a four-dimensional manifold. 
If we would consider any classical lorentzian mani- 
fold, the Geroch theorem [ 4 ] simply forbids any to- 
pology change to occur. Since the spacetime struc- 
tures of four-dimensional euclidean black holes and 
wormholes are considered to be different, it is not 
quite clear how a black hole spacetime could be even- 
tually converted into a wormhole spacetime, or vice 
versa, at a given stage of their evolution, even though 
they are not classical lorentzian entities provided with 
a globally hyperbolic metric. 
For microscopic black holes, this transition could 
still be justified under the claim that the spacetime 
geometry does not have an objective existence, but 
has become an essentially observer-dependent quan- 
tum construct. However, it is very difficult to be- 
lieve, in principle, that such a justification might be 
kept for macroscopic black holes where the space- 
time geometry ought to be sufficiently well defined 
for black hole and wormhole geometries to be 
distinguishable. 
On the other hand, according to current ideas about 
wormholes [5] (see, however, ref. [6]), it appears 
that the probability for a macroscopic wormhole (i.e., 
a wormhole whose throat size is of the order of the 
solar radius) to exist in the whole universe is over- 
whelmingly close to zero. Therefore, if in the case of 
handles of macroscopic size, it is possible for a closed 
universe the size of a macroscopic black hole to 
branch off, and one would interpret [3] such a 
branching as a black hole that formed and evapo-

Once we have established the existence and 
uniqueness of a conformal equivalence between the 
region beyond the event horizon of a black hole and 
the baby universe spacetime associated with an n = 1 
wormhole, we turn to a brief discussion of the physi- 
cal consequences. We first notice that, since the larger 
the neck radius of the n--2, 4 wormholes, M, the 
smaller the nucleation rate per Planck volume per 
Planck time, P~ exp (- action), it appears that this 
radius can by no means play the role of the radius of 
a black hole; for this identification would violate the 
second law of black hole thermodynamics. Neverthe- 
less, in the case of an n--1 wormhole, it turns out 
[ 6,11 ] that the larger M, the larger P, so that the sur- 
face area of the n = 1 baby universes could well play 
the role of entropy, without any violation of the sec- 
ond law. This would also allow a nonnegligible occur- 
rence of macroscopic black holes whose formation 
and evolution is due to the branching of a closed uni- 
verse of the size of the black hole. 
The classical causal structure of submanifolds Mbu 
and Mibh is the same. Nevertheless, since r=0 is a 
boundary ideal point [ 18 ] for Mibh, one should cut it

yond r--0 [i.e., all those points taking place at nega- 
tive values of the time z appearing in eq. (6) ]. Now, 
as r=0 is just an ordinary point [18] for Mbu , and
