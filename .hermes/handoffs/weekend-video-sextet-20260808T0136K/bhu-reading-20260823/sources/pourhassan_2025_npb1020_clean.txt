a b s t r a c t
We propose a novel mechanism by which supercritical black holes formed via vacuum bubble or 
domain wall nucleation during the inﬂationary epoch not only give rise to baby universes, but 
also contribute to a generalized entropy accounting in the multiverse. We show that the interiors 
of such black holes inﬂate eternally and become new domains within the multiverse, connected 
via non-traversable wormholes. We conjecture that these disconnected regions remain quantum 
mechanically entangled with the parent universe. This entanglement modiﬁes the Bekenstein-
Hawking entropy bound, implying a multiversal generalization of the second law of thermody-
namics. Our results suggest that the total entropy of the multiverse increases not only due to local 
thermodynamic evolution, but also via creation of entangled inﬂating domains beyond causal 
contact.
1.  Introduction
The interplay between black hole physics and cosmology has long been at the heart of our understanding of the universe’s structure, 
thermodynamics, and ultimate fate. In particular, the synthesis of quantum ﬁeld theory, general relativity, and inﬂationary cosmology 
provides a fertile ground for exploring profound questions related to the origin of the multiverse, the nature of singularities, and the 
conservation of information. The seminal discovery that black holes emit radiation due to quantum eﬀects near the event horizon [1] 
initiated a cascade of developments probing the thermodynamic and quantum mechanical behavior of horizons. These insights have 
been further sharpened by the formulation of the generalized second law [2,3] and the holographic principle [4–6].
In the context of early universe cosmology, the inﬂationary paradigm [7–9] not only resolves key puzzles such as the horizon and 
ﬂatness problems, but also predicts a stochastic background of quantum ﬂuctuations that seed cosmic structure [10,11]. Moreover, 
inﬂation generically gives rise to eternal inﬂation [12,13], in which diﬀerent regions of spacetime inﬂate at diﬀerent rates or exit 
inﬂation at diﬀerent times, producing a multiverse of causally disconnected domains. The mechanism for this proliferation is rooted 
in the quantum tunneling between metastable vacua in the landscape of scalar ﬁeld potentials–a framework further developed in 
string theory and the so-called string landscape [14–17].
Recent work by Garriga, Vilenkin, and Zhang [18] has introduced a compelling extension to this picture, demonstrating that 
vacuum bubbles or spherical domain walls nucleated during inﬂation can evolve into black holes after inﬂation ends. Depend-

framework provides a novel perspective on the information paradox [29,30], suggesting that rather than information being destroyed, 
it is transferred nonlocally across the multiverse through entangled baby universes. The implications of this proposal are vast, ranging 
from reﬁned entropy bounds in cosmology [31,32] to testable signatures in primordial black hole distributions [33–35] and possible 
quantum gravitational echoes [36].

law. In Section 5, we discuss observational prospects and the broader implications for quantum gravity. Finally, Section 6 summarizes 
our conclusions and outlines directions for future work.

The internal inﬂation of the baby universe proceeds eternally if the vacuum 𝜌𝑏 is metastable or absolutely stable. The geometry can 
be described by a black hole with a non-traversable wormhole, whose throat connects to a spacelike hypersurface on the inﬂating side. 
This scenario bears resemblance to previous models where black hole interiors were conjectured to harbor new universes [41–43]. 
However, the novelty in this mechanism lies in the natural cosmological production of such objects from quantum tunneling processes 
during inﬂation, as opposed to engineered or speculative initial conditions.
The baby universe, once nucleated, becomes causally disconnected from its parent spacetime. Nonetheless, quantum correlations 
established during the bubble’s nucleation and collapse persist. These correlations are encoded in the entanglement structure of 
the initial state and motivate our later proposal that such black hole-induced baby universes contribute to the generalized entropy 
of the multiverse. The presence of a wormhole structure, even if classically non-traversable, supports the possibility of a quantum 
channel–akin to the ER=EPR conjecture [28]–whereby the parent and child regions remain entangled across the horizon.
Conversely, if the mass is subcritical (𝑀< 𝑀cr), the bubble fails to reach suﬃcient expansion and instead collapses entirely. The 
interior contracts, the bubble wall recedes, and the entire conﬁguration evolves into a singularity within a Schwarzschild black hole. 
No baby universe is formed in this case, and the geometry is indistinguishable from that of a primordial black hole. The distinction 
between these outcomes hinges on the initial nucleation time, since bubbles nucleated earlier during inﬂation have more time to 
grow and are thus more likely to become supercritical [18,20].

5.  Implications and observational prospects
The framework developed in this work oﬀers not only a theoretical uniﬁcation of entropy dynamics across disconnected cosmo-
logical domains, but also a pathway toward observational probes of the multiverse through black hole physics. In particular, the 
existence of black holes whose masses cluster near the critical threshold 𝑀cr may hint at internal inﬂating geometries. These super-
critical black holes could serve as seeds of baby universes, and their abundance and properties may leave imprints in astrophysical 
and cosmological data.
5.1.  Gravitational wave echoes and wormhole geometries
A promising avenue involves the analysis of post-merger gravitational wave signals from black hole collisions. Several studies 
have proposed that exotic compact objects–such as those containing internal structure or modiﬁed horizon physics–may exhibit 
gravitational wave echoes following the main ringdown phase. If the remnant black hole hosts a wormhole leading to an inﬂating 
baby universe, then partial reﬂections of perturbations oﬀ the wormhole throat may give rise to delayed secondary signals.
These echoes would be quasi-periodic and damped, with characteristic timescales determined by the wormhole geometry and 
throat location. The absence of classical reﬂectivity at the horizon in standard general relativity makes any such echo signature 
a compelling signal of quantum gravitational structure. Advanced LIGO, Virgo, and upcoming detectors such as LISA or Einstein 
Telescope could place stringent bounds or potentially detect such echoes.
5.2.  Primordial black hole populations and critical mass peaks
Another observational window comes from the mass distribution of primordial black holes (PBHs). During inﬂation, quantum 
ﬂuctuations in the energy density can lead to the formation of vacuum bubbles, some of which collapse into black holes. The model 
predicts a critical mass threshold 𝑀cr separating those that collapse entirely from those that inﬂate internally. A statistical excess or 
sharp feature in the PBH mass spectrum near 𝑀cr could indicate a phase transition between these two regimes.
Surveys across gravitational lensing, microlensing, and accretion signatures (e.g., via Subaru-HSC, EROS, or upcoming Roman 
Space Telescope) can constrain the PBH population. Any observed clustering of black holes near a particular mass scale, especially 
in regions of low baryonic feedback, may reﬂect the multiversal dynamics described in this paper.
5.3.  Entropy bounds, holography, and quantum non-locality
From a more fundamental perspective, the conservation of generalized entropy across universes implies a form of quantum non-
locality–a global structure that persists beyond causal disconnection. If baby universes remain entangled with their progenitor regions, 
then violations of traditional entropy bounds (such as the covariant Bousso bound) might occur locally, while still respecting the 
multiversal second law globally.
In principle, this could manifest in cosmological observables. Deviations from expected holographic scaling of entropy in cos-
mological voids or horizons might be observable via CMB constraints on late-time entropy production, thermalization of relics, or 
mismatch between energy ﬂux and entropy change. Moreover, tensor modes from inﬂation could carry subtle imprints of the bubble 
nucleation network, especially if wormhole geometries brieﬂy aﬀect horizon-crossing modes.
5.4.  Quantum correlations across universes
If the ER=EPR framework is correct, then quantum entanglement between parent and baby universes could have implications 
for the structure of quantum ﬁeld correlations near black hole horizons. While direct detection of such correlations is likely beyond 
current technology, holographic dualities–such as AdS/CFT analogs or tensor network models–may allow simulation of entangled 
multiverse branches in controlled setups.

6.  Conclusion
We have proposed a framework in which supercritical black holes serve as conduits for the creation of new inﬂating domains–
baby universes–embedded within a multiversal structure. These regions, though causally disconnected after wormhole pinch-oﬀ, 
remain quantum mechanically entangled with their progenitor universe. This entanglement modiﬁes the traditional notion of entropy, 
requiring the inclusion of hidden correlations in the total entropy budget.
By incorporating the entanglement entropy between parent and child universes, we derived a generalized second law of thermo-
dynamics that remains valid even in the presence of horizon-crossing degrees of freedom. This multiversal second law ensures that 
global entropy increases due to both conventional thermodynamic processes and the formation of entangled inﬂating domains beyond 
causal contact.
Our analysis extends the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy to include entropic contributions from quantum entanglement across space-
time regions, and places the dynamics of black hole interiors in a broader cosmological context. Black hole-induced inﬂation, in this 
view, is not an endpoint but a branching mechanism through which the multiverse grows and stores information.
We also outlined potential observational signatures of this framework, including gravitational wave echoes, critical mass distribu-
tions of primordial black holes, and violations of local entropy bounds–each of which may serve as indirect probes of the underlying 
multiversal structure.
This perspective uniﬁes gravitational thermodynamics, quantum information, and cosmological inﬂation under a single principle 
of generalized entropy conservation. It oﬀers a novel resolution to the black hole information paradox and opens a pathway toward 
testable predictions linking entanglement, geometry, and the origin of the multiverse.
Future work may focus on more precise modeling of baby universe Hilbert spaces, the dynamics of wormhole geometries in semi-
classical gravity, and embedding this scenario within a holographic or quantum gravitational framework. The multiversal second law 
may ultimately be a foundational principle in the thermodynamics of quantum spacetime.
