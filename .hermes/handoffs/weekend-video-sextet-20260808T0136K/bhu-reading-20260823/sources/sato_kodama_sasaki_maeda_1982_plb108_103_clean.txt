MULTI-PRODUCTION OF UNIVERSES BY FIRST-ORDER PHASE TRANSITION OF A VACUUM 
Katsuhiko SATO, Hideo KODAMA, Misao SASAKI a and Kei-ichi MAEDA 
Department of Physics, Kyoto University, Kyoto 606, Japan 
a Research Institute for Fundamental Physics, Kyoto University, Kyoto 606, Japan 
Received 8 June 1981 
Revised manuscript received 6 October 1981 
Gauge theories with spontaneously broken symmetries give rise to a cosmological phase transition of a vacuum. We show 
that if the phase transition is strongly of first order, such gauge theories combined with general relativity yield a surprising 
prediction; although the Creator might have made a unitary universe, many mini-universes are produced sequentially after- 
ward as a result of the phase transition. 
The first-order phase transition of a vacuum has 
been a subject of great interest because of its notable 
implications to cosmology [ 1-8]. Since the universe 
is expected to expand exponentially during the period 
of the phase transition, the existence of this period 
makes it possible to form the seeds of galaxies [1], to 
explain the homogeneity of our universe [1-3], and 
to suppress the over-production of monopoles [5-7]. 
Furthermore, a matter-antimatter domain structure 
of the universe may be realized without observational 
conflicts [1,9]. 
The first-order phase transition is triggered by the 
nucleation of true vacuum bubbles [10]. At the early 
stage of the phase transition, these bubbles are isolated 
from each other, since their volume fraction to the 
background false vacuum is sufficiently small. At the 
late stage, however, they begin to overlap and finally 
cover most of the space. Thus false vacuum domains 
surrounded by true vacuum appear here and there. 
Now a paradoxical situation arises: Since a trapped 
false vacuum domain still expands exponentially, the 
area of the boundary surface of this region keeps 
increasing, while the very same surface is pushed in- 
ward at the light velocity due to the expansion of sur- 
rounding bubbles. Noting that, besides such a situation, 
there exists a high contrast between the energy densi- 
ties of true and false vacua, one surely expects the uni- 
verse to have a quite intricate space-time structure 
during this stage. Let us first briefly discuss the space- 
time structure at this stage and show that a false vacu- 
um region surrounded ~y true vacuum bubbles be- 
comes eventually a bla'ck hole or a wormhole (for de- 
tail, see ref. [11] ).

In reality, the trapped false vacuum regions are 
highly asymmetric and new bubbles are further nu- 
cleated therein. However, the essential feature of the 
above model is expected to be still applicable to the 
actual situation, provided that the nucleation rate is 
sufficiently small. The very reason why black holes 
and wormholes are created is that false vacuum do- 
mains trapped by true vacuum are formed. In case the 
relative size of a trapped domain to the surrounding 
bubbles is small when it is formed, the domain wall 
continues to contract at light speed and the energy re- 
leased by the phase transition may well be concen- 
trated in a very small region. Hence a black hole is gen- 
erally created regardless of the initial shape of domain. 
On the other hand, in case the relative domain size is 
large, the domain wall continues to expand to infinity 
and consequently the universe attains to have two 
asymptotic (fiat) regions. This anomalous structure oc- 
curs independent of the domain shape and strongly in- 
dicates the general formation of wormhole-bridge 
structure, though it is not exactly proved yet. It 
should be noticed that the nucleation rate of bubbles 
must be smaller than the characteristic value 1-4 in or- 
der that the trapped domain can expand sufficiently 
without being destroyed by new bubbles nucleated 
therein. Thus one naturally expects these wormholes 
(and black holes) to play an important role in the sub- 
sequent evolution of the universe.

In summary, we have shown that gauge theories in 
current particle physics predict multi-production of 
universes if the phase transition is of first-order. There 
are, however, a few problems yet to be solved. One is 
on the thermalization mechanism of the kinetic energy 
of bubble walls at their mutual collisions. In particular, 
if the size of a bubble is much greater than 1, it seems 
unlikely that the kinetic energy is transformed to radia- 
tion quickly. Another is on the baryon-entropy ratio. 
When black holes and wormholes evaporate, a large 
amount of entropy is generated, which consequently 
reduces the baryon number to entropy ratio consider- 
ably. Thus, even if the ratio is initially of order unity, 
the evaporation may dilute the baryon excess too 
much to agree with observations. In order to avoid this 
difficulty, some new baryon-number generation mecha- 
nism might be necessary [ 15]. To judge the validity of 
the present result, more precise investigations based on 
the further development of particle physics will be re- 
quired.
