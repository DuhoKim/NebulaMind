The black-hole-universe line is closed for Phase 2, and the honest headline is not that it passed. Across the two equation-by-equation audits there are 77 rows, and 48 of them check out — 62%. But 7 of those rows are load-bearing: they carry the papers' actual conclusions. All 7 fail. The arithmetic is sound almost everywhere, and the argument fails exactly where the mission needed it.

What we did was audit 4 published papers line by line against their own sources, then build an in-house strict model from derived transfer functions rather than the authors' words, and confront it with real data.

The verdict from that confrontation: the most generous stack we could justify lands 10,000 to 100,000 times below the all-galaxy floor.

The caveat belongs here, not at the end. That gap is not a measurement. Our number is a ceiling, taken from assumptions chosen to flatter the theory, and the floor is a theoretical best case — every galaxy in the sky counted, with no noise. No instrument achieves it. So the effect is too small to matter. It is not that our telescopes are too weak.

Underneath that sit two structural problems. The chain's two core papers are mutually incompatible, and the later one disavows the foundation the earlier one's bounce is built on. And the averaging step, the known critique target, is never derived in either — it is asserted by citation, and the whole magnitude rests on it.

On process: 4 gates, 4 passes, every receipt rerunning byte-identical. The erratum was chased through 8 routes and never found, but that chase won the published Elsevier text of the original, which is a better source than we had.

Where it leaves the line: as a personal research interest it is intact and still interesting. As a detection program it is finished at present sensitivity, and the next honest move is not a bigger stack.
