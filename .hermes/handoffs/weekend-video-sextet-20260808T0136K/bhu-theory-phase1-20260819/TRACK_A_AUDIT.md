## 0. Headline findings

1. **The dark-energy section is internally inconsistent with the paper's own isotropy claim,
   and the inconsistency is a dilemma with no escape inside the paper.** The paper writes
   (T 210–211): *"the corresponding Hubble parameter H=(Λ/3)^{1/2}c would thus be equal to Ω"*
   and *"The cosmological constant generated by the rotation of the universe would not be
   constant: Λ=3Ω²/c²."* Taking the observed Λ (computed from Phase 0's pinned Planck values:
   Λ = 3(1−Ω_m)H₀²/c² = 1.09×10⁻⁵² m⁻², R1), this **fixes** Ω = c√(Λ/3) = 1.81×10⁻¹⁸ s⁻¹ =
   0.828 H₀ — i.e. (Ω/H)₀ = 0.83, which exceeds the Planck Bianchi VII_h bound
   ((ω/H)₀ < 7.6×10⁻¹⁰) by a factor **1.09×10⁹** and the Saadeh vector-mode bound by
   **1.76×10¹⁰** (R1). The reading was verified before claiming it (§2.2): signs and units are
   fine, and the paper's own "would thus be equal to Ω" sentence plus its DES-consistency claim
   and closing "different black holes form different universes, which have different dark
   energies" (T 222) commit it to identification, not analogy. Conversely, if Ω is retreated to
   respect the CMB bound, rotation supplies at most **8.4×10⁻¹⁹** of the observed Λ (R1) and the
   dark-energy section is quantitatively empty. Either the rotation is Hubble-scale
   (contradicting the abstract's *"the cosmic microwave background radiation is isotropic on
   large scales"*, T 16, by nine orders against data) or it is bound-compliant (and explains
   none of dark energy). The paper never confronts the two.

2. **Second, independent kill: the equation of state.** The paper's own premise — *"Because the
   angular momentum of the universe is conserved, the angular velocity of the universe decreases
   as the universe expands"* (T 214) — fixes the scaling: L ~ M R²Ω = const with R ∝ a gives
   Ω ∝ a⁻², so ρ_Λ,eff ∝ Ω² ∝ a⁻⁴, i.e. **w = +1/3** (R5, sympy). The paper cites DES as
   support (T 216); the DES abstract it cites measures **w = −0.948 +0.028/−0.027**. Δw ≈ 1.28,
   ~46σ at the quoted uncertainty (R5) — and w = +1/3 cannot produce acceleration at all
   (acceleration needs w < −1/3). "Dark energy weakens" agrees in sign only; the magnitude is
   not close, and a rotational ρ ∝ a⁻⁴ would have dominated the early universe, not the late
   one — the observed onset of acceleration is backwards for this mechanism.

3. **Third: the same force is used with two incompatible symmetries.** The bulk-flow section
   needs the centrifugal force axisymmetric — *"perpendicular to the preferred axis and away
   from it"* (T 183) — while the dark-energy section converts it, in one unsupported sentence
   (*"Because the universe is closed … The magnitude of this force becomes mΩ²r … instead of
   mΩ²ρ"*, T 207–208), into a spherically symmetric force mimicking Λ. No derivation is given
   for the conversion, and both symmetries cannot hold for one force. §2.7.

What survives: the textbook mechanics (Eqs. 1–6 all verify by sympy, R2), the spherical-trig
arithmetic (R3), the dimensional form of the Kerr radius (R4), and the **qualitative**
CW/CCW-asymmetry claim as pure symmetry reasoning — which, exactly as Phase 0 concluded, carries
no amplitude (§2.5, §3).

## 2. Detailed findings

### 2.1 The Kerr-radius "correction" (brief item 1)
The full content is one sentence (T 116–117): the FLRW+Gödel combination "should" carry
corrections containing a = M/mc. Dimensionally a is a length (R4; extremal magnitude GM/c²:
1.5×10⁴ m for 10 M☉, 1.5×10¹² m for 10⁹ M☉ — R4). But no perturbed metric, no expansion
parameter, no observable is derived from it anywhere in the paper; the Gödel citation (T 116)
is never used mathematically. Verdict: dimension CHECK, connection to any metric perturbation
UNSUPPORTED. (Notation hazard for any successor work: the paper uses M for angular momentum and
m for the hole's mass at T 117, then m for a *particle's* mass from T 120 on.)

### 2.2 Λ = 3Ω²/c² — the priority target (brief item 2), verified before claimed
Reading verification, per the kickoff's caution:
- **Units/signs:** [3Ω²/c²] = m⁻² ✓, positive ✓; H = (Λ/3)^{1/2}c is the standard empty-de
  Sitter relation ✓ (row 17 CHECK — the algebra is not the problem).
- **Identification vs analogy:** the chain at T 209–211 runs: same *form* as the Λ acceleration
  (analogy) → "the corresponding Hubble parameter … **would thus be equal to Ω**" → "The
  cosmological constant **generated by the rotation** … Λ=3Ω²/c²" → §-title "Dark energy as
  centrifugal force" → "The origin of dark energy may therefore be explained…" (T 218) →
  "different black holes form different universes, which have **different dark energies**"
  (T 222) → abstract: "may be the origin of dark energy, in accordance with recent DESI
  observations" (T 23). The "may" hedges rhetoric, not content: consistency-with-DES and
  universe-to-universe variation of dark energy are claims *about the observed Λ*. The
  identification reading stands.
- **Arithmetic (R1):** Λ_obs = 3(1−Ω_m)H₀²/c² = 1.091×10⁻⁵² m⁻² (from pinned S3 values;
  matches the brief's 1.1×10⁻⁵²). Ω_req = c√(Λ/3) = 1.808×10⁻¹⁸ s⁻¹, i.e. (Ω/H)₀ = 0.828 =
  √Ω_Λ. Against the pinned bounds: **1.089×10⁹ ×** the Planck Bianchi VII_h limit,
  **1.761×10¹⁰ ×** the Saadeh vector-mode limit.
- **Bound applicability caveat, stated honestly:** S1/S2 constrain vorticity within
  Bianchi-type expanding models. The paper's universe is expanding (FLRW + rotation), which is
  that class; the paper's proposed Gödel ingredient is stationary and non-expanding and is
  never actually combined. No published rotating-expanding model that evades CMB rotation
  bounds is cited by the paper, and it offers none.
- **The dilemma (headline 1):** identification ⇒ 10⁹ conflict with isotropy data *and* with
  the paper's own abstract sentence T 16; retreat ⇒ Λ_rot/Λ_obs ≤ 8.4×10⁻¹⁹ (R1) and the
  section asserts nothing about observed dark energy. There is no third reading in the text.

### 2.3 Newtonian rotating frames at cosmological scale (brief item 3)
Eqs. (1)–(6) are Landau–Lifshitz §39, verified symbolically (R2, all six checks True). The
application is the problem:
- A rigidly rotating frame is definable only for ρ < c/Ω (frame speed Ωρ < c). At the
  dark-energy Ω of §2.2, c/Ω = c/(0.828 H₀) = **1.21 Hubble radii** — the "closed universe"
  argument (T 207) applies the frame beyond its own validity boundary.
- **What a GR treatment replaces this with:** rotation in an expanding universe is a Bianchi-
  type cosmology (or vector/vorticity perturbations on FLRW). There, (i) "centrifugal force"
  is not a global force but anisotropic expansion + shear sourced by vorticity; (ii) the CMB
  responds to that shear/vorticity — which is exactly what S1/S2 bound; (iii) galaxy dynamics
  follow geodesics of the perturbed metric — the local physics is tidal, not a global
  E = E₀ − M·Ω bookkeeping. The energy relation (6) is frame bookkeeping, valid where the
  frame is; it does not by itself produce a torque (§2.5).
- **What survives the replacement:** a nonzero cosmological vorticity along a fixed axis is a
  genuine parity-odd background, and *some* coupling to structure formation is generic. The
  qualitative asymmetry claim survives; every quantitative statement in the paper's mechanism
  does not — which is precisely the Track B derivation target (transfer function from vector
  perturbations + tidal-torque theory, MODEL_SPEC.md).

### 2.4 The fitted axis and its data (brief item 4)
R3 reproduces the paper's numbers exactly and exposes what they are: the mean of **three**
fitted dipole axes — Longo 2011 SDSS (217°, 32°), Shamir 2012 SDSS (132°, 32°), Shamir 2025
DESI (243°, 39°) — with ±47°/±3° being the *population standard deviation of three values*
(std of the mean would be ±33.5° on α). The JADES entry (T 180) contributes no axis. POST-HOC
is confirmed from the text: every dataset predates the 2025 revision that introduced the axis
material (packet Rev 4/5 finding, now verified against the full text directly). Additional
methodological notes, receipt-backed: (a) the first two axes are not independent samples (both
SDSS-based); (b) per-coordinate averaging of axial quantities is improper — the proper 3D
axial mean of the same three axes lands ≈16° away (R3: (215.6°, 41.2°) after sign
convention); (c) the paper itself flags the DESI axis as a possible Milky-Way-motion artifact
(T 179) and then averages it in anyway; (d) the bulk-flow apex is likewise imported from the
contested SMAC/dark-flow catalogs (T 184–185). None of this was needed to *reproduce* the
numbers — the arithmetic is fine; the construction is what the verdict labels.

### 2.5 The core qualitative claim and its logical dependencies (brief item 5)

S1/S2] → E = E₀ − M·Ω [row 6, CHECK] → "galaxies tend to decrease their energies" [row 9,
UNSUPPORTED: energy minimization is an equilibrium argument requiring a dissipation channel and
a rate; no torque, relaxation time, or efficiency is computed — an isolated galaxy's M is

*mechanism* by which M reorients is exactly the missing physics] → CW/CCW counts differ

the Λ identification (row 18), or even the specific force law — only Ω ≠ 0 plus row 9's
relaxation assumption. Therefore: the failures in §2.2 and §2.7 do **not** kill the qualitative
claim; it survives as symmetry reasoning (a parity-odd background should bias a parity-odd

And the failures are not free of consequence for it either — if Ω must respect S1/S2, the
surviving claim's driver is bounded to (Ω/H)₀ < 7.6×10⁻¹⁰, which is the regime Phase 0 already
showed is unmeasurable by any survey. The full-text audit *strengthens* Phase 0's closure.

### 2.7 The symmetry contradiction (headline 3, expanded)
T 183 (bulk-flow section): centrifugal force acts "in directions perpendicular to the preferred
axis and away from it" — axisymmetric, magnitude mΩ²ρ, ρ = distance from axis. T 207–208
(dark-energy section): "Because the universe is closed, the centrifugal force … manifests
itself as a force acting in all directions away from the primordial white hole. The magnitude
of this force becomes mΩ²r … instead of mΩ²ρ." No equation supports the conversion — closure
of spatial sections does not isotropize an axisymmetric force; a rotation axis remains a
rotation axis in a closed Bianchi-IX-type space. The two sections make mutually exclusive
symmetry claims about the same force, and each section's observable (perpendicular bulk flow;
isotropic acceleration) requires its own version. If the force is axisymmetric, it mimics an
anisotropic stress, not Λ, and would itself be CMB-bounded; if isotropic, the bulk-flow
"prediction" (T 183) loses its mechanism. ERROR as written; a corrected version would have to
derive the anisotropic-stress form from a Bianchi metric — at which point it is bounded by
S1/S2 and headline 1 returns.
