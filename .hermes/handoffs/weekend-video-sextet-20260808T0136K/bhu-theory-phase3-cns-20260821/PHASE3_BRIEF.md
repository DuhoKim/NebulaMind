## The three tracks

**Track A — equation-by-equation audit (both papers).** The adjudication read BLR's *claims*;
nobody has audited its *derivations*. Verdict every step of links (1)→(2)→(3): nearly-vanishing
vector-meson mass at chiral restoration → kaon condensation at n ≈ 3n₀ → M_max ≈ 1.5 M⊙. Same
row schema as Phase 2 (`verdicts.json`), including the `load_bearing` flag at authoring time —

Smolin 1992 is the harder acquisition (CQG, 1992, likely paywalled); if the full text cannot be
obtained, say so and audit what is published-verifiable rather than substituting a preprint.

**Track B — strict in-house model: re-derive M_max ourselves.** The adjudication accepted
≈1.5 M⊙ as given and tested it observationally. Derive it instead, from the kaon-condensate
equation of state at n ≈ 3n₀, and confront it with the *modern* constraints the 2008 paper could
not use: NICER radii, GW170817 tidal deformability, and the J0740+6620 mass-radius posterior.
The question is not "is 1.5 wrong" — observation already says yes — but **how much of the chain
has to move** to accommodate 2.08 M⊙, and whether links (1)–(2) survive that move.

**Track C — the load-bearing question, and the reason this is worth a night.**
*Does CNS actually entail the Brown–Bethe maximum, or is that a contingent attachment?*
The adjudication says CNS "can retreat from the Brown–Bethe EOS route, at the price of no longer
being tested by this channel". That sentence is doing enormous work and nobody has checked it.
If Smolin's argument *requires* a soft EOS, CNS is in real trouble; if the M_max prediction is a
detachable rider that Brown–Bethe supplied, then falsifying the chain says much less about CNS
than the headline suggests. **Either answer is a genuine finding**, and it is exactly the shape
of the Phase 2 result: the arithmetic was fine, and the load-bearing step was never derived.
