Vector Manifestation of the Chiral Symmetry

Masayasu Harada and Koichi Yamawaki Address: Department of Physics, Nagoya University, Nagoya, 464-8602, Japan.
Abstract

We propose “vector manifestation (VM)” of the Wigner realization of the chiral symmetry in which the symmetry is restored at the critical point by the massless degenerate pion (and its flavor partners) and rho meson (and its flavor partners) as the chiral partner, in sharp contrast to the traditional manifestation á la linear sigma model where the symmetry is restored by the degenerate pion and scalar meson. The application to the chiral phase transition of the large NfN_{f} QCD is performed using the hidden local symmetry Lagrangian. Combined with the Wilsonian matching proposed recently, the VM determines the critical number of massless flavors Nf≃5N_{f}\simeq 5 without much ambiguity.

]

Chiral phase transition in QCD is discussed in various contexts such as the large NfN_{f} QCD, QCD at finite temperature and/or density, etc.. In many situations the traditional linear sigma model-like Wigner realization for the chiral restoration is assumed. However, the Wigner realization does not necessarily require the massless degenerate pion and scalar meson at the critical point. The linear sigma model is merely consistent with the Wigner realization. It, therefore, is natural for us to ask the following question: Is there a manifestation of the Wigner realization other than that of the linear sigma model? The answer is yes, which we demonstrate in this paper.

In this paper we propose “Vector Manifestation (VM)” of the chiral symmetry as a novel manifestation of the Wigner realization in which the vector meson denoted by ρ\rho (ρ\rho meson and its flavor partner) becomes massless at the chiral phase transition point. Accordingly, the (longitudinal) ρ\rho becomes the chiral partner of the Nambu-Goldstone (NG) boson denoted by π\pi (pion and its flavor partners).

The essence of VM stems from the new matching of the effective field theory (EFT) with QCD (“Wilsonian matching”) recently proposed by Ref. [1] in which bare parameters of the EFT are determined by matching the current correlators in the EFT with those obtained by the operator product expansion (OPE) in QCD, based on the renormalization-group equation (RGE) in the Wilsonian sense including the quadratic divergence [2]. The quadratic divergence was identified with the presence of a pole of ultraviolet origin at n=2n=2 in the dimensional regularization. Several physical quantities for π\pi and ρ\rho were predicted by the Wilsonian matching in the framework of the Hidden Local Symmetry (HLS) [3, 4] as the EFT, in excellent agreement with the experiments for Nf=3N_{f}=3, where NfN_{f} is the number of massless flavors [1]. This encourages us to perform the analysis for larger NfN_{f} up to near the critical point based on the Wilsonian matching.

Actually, the chiral symmetry restoration in Wigner realization should be characterized by the equality of the vector and axialvector current correlators. When we approach to the critical point from the broken phase (NG phase), the axialvector current correlator is still dominated by the massless π\pi as the NG boson, while the vector current correrator is by the massive ρ\rho. The crucial ingredient of the Wilsonian matching is the quadratic divergence which yields the quadratic running of (square of) the decay constant Fπ2​(μ)F_{\pi}^{2}(\mu) [2], where μ\mu is the renormalization point. It was actually shown [2] that the order parameter Fπ​(0)F_{\pi}(0) can become zero for larger NfN_{f} even when Fπ​(Λ)≠0F_{\pi}(\Lambda)\neq 0, where Fπ​(Λ)F_{\pi}(\Lambda) is not the order parameter but just a parameter of the bare Lagrangian defined at the cutoff Λ\Lambda where the matching with QCD is made. Then the π\pi contribution to the axialvector current correlator at μ≠0\mu\neq 0 persists, Fπ​(μ)≠0F_{\pi}(\mu)\neq 0, even at the critical point where Fπ​(0)=0F_{\pi}(0)=0. Thus the only possibility for this equality to hold at any μ≠0\mu\neq 0 is that the ρ\rho contribution to the vector current correlator also persists at the critical point in such a way that ρ\rho yields a massless pole with the current coupling equal to that of π\pi. Then this restoration, VM, is accompanied by the degenerate massless π\pi and (longitudinal) ρ\rho (transverse ρ\rho is decoupled from the current correlator at the critical point, see later discussions).

This is sharply contrasted with the traditional manifestation of the linear sigma model where the equality of the current corrrelators is trivially satisfied, since the axialvector correlator goes to zero due to Fπ​(μ)≡0F_{\pi}(\mu)\equiv 0 independently of μ\mu (in the absence of the quadratic divergence), while the vector correlator has no contribution from the scalar meson and hence is simply zero. Thus the Wilsonian matching (which leads to Fπ​(Λ)≠0F_{\pi}(\Lambda)\neq 0) excludes the linear sigma model manifestation in QCD.

In VM we have degenerate massless π\pi and (longitudinal) ρ\rho at the phase transition point, which are the chiral partners in the representation of (Nf2−1, 1)⊕(1,Nf2−1)(N_{f}^{2}-1\,,\,1)\oplus(1\,,\,N_{f}^{2}-1) of the chiral SU(NfN_{f})L×{}_{\rm L}\timesSU(NfN_{f})R. This representation corresponds to (8, 1)⊕(1, 8)(8\,,\,1)\oplus(1\,,\,8) for Nf=3N_{f}=3. This is contrasted with the linear sigma model-like manifestation in which π\pi is in the representation of pure (3, 3∗)⊕(3∗, 3)(3\,,\,3^{*})\oplus(3^{*}\,,\,3) together with the scalar meson. This can be understood in the good-old-day saturation scheme of Adler-Weisberger sum rule for the zero helicity mesons [5]: π\pi and the (longitudinal) axialvector mesons denoted by A1A_{1} (a1a_{1} meson and its flavor partners) are admixture of (8, 1)⊕(1, 8)(8\,,\,1)\oplus(1\,,\,8) and (3, 3∗)⊕(3∗, 3)(3\,,\,3^{*})\oplus(3^{*}\,,\,3), since the symmetry is spontaneously broken:

|π⟩\displaystyle|\pi\rangle =\displaystyle= |(3, 3∗)⊕(3∗, 3)⟩​sin⁡ψ+|(8, 1)⊕(1, 8)⟩​cos⁡ψ,\displaystyle|(3\,,\,3^{*})\oplus(3^{*}\,,\,3)\rangle\sin\psi+|(8\,,\,1)\oplus(1\,,\,8)\rangle\cos\psi\ ,
|A1⟩\displaystyle|A_{1}\rangle =\displaystyle= |(3, 3∗)⊕(3∗, 3)⟩​cos⁡ψ−|(8, 1)⊕(1, 8)⟩​sin⁡ψ,\displaystyle|(3\,,\,3^{*})\oplus(3^{*}\,,\,3)\rangle\cos\psi-|(8\,,\,1)\oplus(1\,,\,8)\rangle\sin\psi\ ,

where the experimental value of the mixing angle ψ\psi is given by approximately ψ=π/4\psi=\pi/4 [5]. On the other hand, the longitudinal ρ\rho belongs to (8, 1)⊕(1, 8)(8\,,\,1)\oplus(1\,,\,8) and the scalar meson to (3, 3∗)⊕(3∗, 3)(3\,,\,3^{*})\oplus(3^{*}\,,\,3). Then the conventional linear sigma model-like manifestation corresponds to the limit ψ→π/2\psi\rightarrow\pi/2, while the VM to the limit ψ→0\psi\rightarrow 0 in which case A1A_{1} goes to a pure (3, 3∗)⊕(3∗, 3)(3\,,\,3^{*})\oplus(3^{*}\,,\,3), now degenerate with the scalar meson in the same representation (3, 3∗)⊕(3∗, 3)(3\,,\,3^{*})\oplus(3^{*}\,,\,3), but not with ρ\rho in (8, 1)⊕(1, 8)(8\,,\,1)\oplus(1\,,\,8).

Next we consider the expression of the current correlators in the EFT which is valid in the low energy below the matching scale Λ\Lambda. As an EFT to describe the VM we need a model having both π\pi and ρ\rho fields. Here we use the HLS model [3, 4] which includes π\pi and ρ\rho consisitently with the chiral symmetry and actually reproduces experiments nicely through the Wilsonian matching [1]. The axialvector and vector current correlators in the HLS are well described by the tree contributions with including 𝒪⁡(p4){\cal O}(p^{4}) terms when the momentum is around the matching scale Λ\Lambda [1]:

ΠA(HLS)​(Q2)\displaystyle\Pi_{A}^{\rm(HLS)}(Q^{2}) =\displaystyle= Fπ2​(Λ)Q2−2​z2​(Λ),\displaystyle\frac{F_{\pi}^{2}(\Lambda)}{Q^{2}}-2z_{2}(\Lambda)\ ,

Let us now obtain constraints on the bare parameters of the HLS in the VM through the Wilsonian matching. At the critical point the quark condensate ⟨q¯​q⟩\left\langle\bar{q}q\right\rangle vanishes, while the gluonic condensate ⟨αsπ​Gμ​ν​Gμ​ν⟩\left\langle\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi}G_{\mu\nu}G^{\mu\nu}\right\rangle is independent of the renormalization point of QCD and hence it is expected that it does not vanish. Then the right-hand-side (RHS) of Eq. (4) is non-zero, implying that Fπ2​(Λ)F_{\pi}^{2}(\Lambda) is non-zero even at the critical point.

Then how do we know by the bare parameters defined at Λ\Lambda whether or not the chiral symmetry is restored ? As we discussed before, a clue comes from the fact that ΠA(QCD)\Pi_{A}^{\rm(QCD)} and ΠV(QCD)\Pi_{V}^{\rm(QCD)} in Eq. (2) agree with each other for any value of Q2Q^{2} when the chiral symmetry is restored with ⟨q¯​q⟩=0\left\langle\bar{q}q\right\rangle=0. Thus, we require that ΠA(HLS)\Pi_{A}^{\rm(HLS)} and ΠV(HLS)\Pi_{V}^{\rm(HLS)} in Eq. (3) agree with each other for any value of Q2Q^{2}. This agreement is satisfied only if the following conditions are met:

g⁡(Λ)→0,a⁡(Λ)→1,\displaystyle g(\Lambda)\rightarrow 0\ ,\quad a(\Lambda)\rightarrow 1\ ,
z1​(Λ)−z2​(Λ)→0.\displaystyle z_{1}(\Lambda)-z_{2}(\Lambda)\rightarrow 0\ . (5)

This is nothing but the VM of the chiral symmetry in terms of the HLS parameters. Note that a⁡(Λ)≃1a(\Lambda)\simeq 1 is satisfied in QCD already for Nf=3N_{f}=3 in the broken phase [1]. The first two in Eq. (5) are the values in the Georgi’s vector limit [7], which was simply assumed in Ref. [2] to be a consistent way to incorporate the chiral phase transition of the large NfN_{f} QCD into the HLS. Thanks to the Wilsonian matching it is now clear that Eq. (5) is the precise HLS expression of the Wigner realization in QCD.

The VM in the HLS is similar to the Georgi’s “vector realization” [7], but is different in an essential way: The “vector realization” is claimed to be a different realization than either the Wigner or NG realizations in such a way that the NG boson does exist (Fπ​(0)≠0F_{\pi}(0)\neq 0) while the chiral symmetry is still unbroken. On the contrary, our VM is precisely the Wigner realization having Fπ​(0)=0F_{\pi}(0)=0. Technically, the bare HLS Lagrangian in the VM coincides with the parameter choice of the Georgi’s “vector realization”; g⁡(Λ)=0g(\Lambda)=0, a⁡(Λ)=1a(\Lambda)=1 and Fπ​(Λ)≠0F_{\pi}(\Lambda)\neq 0. However, an essential difference comes from the Wilsonian RGE’s whose quadratic divergence leads to the Wigner realization with Fπ​(0)=0F_{\pi}(0)=0 at the low-energy limit (on-shell of NG bosons). On the other hand, the “vector realization” lacking the quadratic divergence leads to Fπ​(0)=Fπ​(Λ)≠0F_{\pi}(0)=F_{\pi}(\Lambda)\neq 0. In contrast to the Georgi’s “vector realization”, the VM in the Wigner realization is consistent with the chiral Ward-Takahashi identity [8, 9].

We now examine the chiral symmetry restoration in the large NfN_{f} QCD (Nf<112​NcN_{f}<\frac{11}{2}N_{c}) which was implied by the fact that the coupling at the infrared fixed point becomes very small [10]. Such a restoration was indeed observed by various methods like lattice simulation [11], ladder Schwinger-Dyson (SD) equation [12, 13], dispersion relation [14], instanton calculus [15], etc.. As pointed out in Ref. [13] the phase transition for large NfN_{f} QCD may be characterized by the “conformal phase transition”. In such a case the Ginzburg-Landau effective theory (linear sigma model-like manifestation) simply breaks down. The VM may be a manifestation of the chiral symmetry restoration consistent with the “conformal phase transition”.

The chiral restoration in terms of HLS was obtained in Ref. [2] without VM and Wilsonian matching. What was shown in Ref. [2] is that the RGE for Fπ2F_{\pi}^{2} including the quadratic divergence reduces the value of Fπ2​(0)F_{\pi}^{2}(0) (the pole residue of the massless pion pole) from Fπ2​(Λ)F_{\pi}^{2}(\Lambda) (the bare parameter of the HLS Lagrangian defined at a cutoff scale Λ\Lambda) in such a way that the larger NfN_{f}, the smaller the value of Fπ​(0)F_{\pi}(0) is. It eventually goes to zero, the chiral restoration, at a certain critical number of NfN_{f}.

Now in the VM, the bare parameters are characterized by the vector limit g⁡(Λ)=0g(\Lambda)=0 and a⁡(Λ)=1a(\Lambda)=1 [see Eq. (5)] which is actually the fixed point of RGE [2]. Then the VM justifies the previous derivation of the RGE for FπF_{\pi} in the vector limit which relates the order parameter with the bare parameter as [2]:

Fπ2​(mρ=0,Nf)Λf2=Fπ2​(Λf,Nf)Λf2−Nf2​(4​π)2,\frac{F_{\pi}^{2}(m_{\rho}=0;N_{f})}{\Lambda_{f}^{2}}=\frac{F_{\pi}^{2}\left(\Lambda_{f};N_{f}\right)}{\Lambda_{f}^{2}}-\frac{N_{f}}{2(4\pi)^{2}}\ , (6)

where we expressed the matching scale by Λf≡Λ⁡(Nf)\Lambda_{f}\equiv\Lambda(N_{f}) since it generally depends on NfN_{f}. It should be noticed that this equation holds only at the critical flavor NfcrN_{f}^{\rm cr}, and the left-hand-side vanishes at the critical point.

The value of NfcrN_{f}^{\rm cr} is determined in terms of the parameters in the OPE by combining Eq. (6) and Eq. (4) with taking ⟨q¯​q⟩=0\left\langle\bar{q}q\right\rangle=0. The resultant expression is given by

Nfcr=4​[1+αsπ+2​π23​⟨αsπ​Gμ​ν​Gμ​ν⟩Λf4].N_{f}^{\rm cr}=4\left[1+\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi}+\frac{2\pi^{2}}{3}\frac{\left\langle\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi}G_{\mu\nu}G^{\mu\nu}\right\rangle}{\Lambda_{f}^{4}}\right]\ . (7)

Here we estimate this by using the parameters for Nf=3N_{f}=3 [1]: (Λ3,ΛQCD)=(1.2, 0.35)(\Lambda_{3}\,,\,\Lambda_{\rm QCD})=(1.2\,,\,0.35) GeV, αs=0.56\alpha_{s}=0.56 and ⟨αsπ​Gμ​ν​Gμ​ν⟩=0.012​GeV4\left\langle\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi}G_{\mu\nu}G^{\mu\nu}\right\rangle=0.012\,\mbox{GeV}^{4}. The result is given by

Nfcr≃4.9.N_{f}^{\rm cr}\simeq 4.9\ . (8)

The precise estimation of this will be done by determining the NfN_{f}-dependences of the QCD coupling αs\alpha_{s} and Λf\Lambda_{f} in the forthcoming paper [9]. Here we just quote the result Nfcr≃4.8N_{f}^{\rm cr}\simeq 4.8, which is consistent with the above estimate.

To study the critical behaviors of the parameters when approaching to the critical point, we need to know how the bare parameters g⁡(Λf,Nf)g(\Lambda_{f};N_{f}) and a⁡(Λf,Nf)a(\Lambda_{f};N_{f}) approach to the vector limit Eq. (5). Comparing the difference between vector and axialvector correlators in Eq. (2) with that in Eq. (3), we know that the critical behavior of g2​(Λf,Nf)g^{2}(\Lambda_{f};N_{f}) is given as g2​(Λf,Nf)∼⟨q¯​q⟩2g^{2}(\Lambda_{f};N_{f})\sim\left\langle\bar{q}q\right\rangle^{2}. Since we do not know the scaling of ⟨q¯​q⟩\left\langle\bar{q}q\right\rangle except for the ladder SD approach[16], we here tentatively adopt the following ansatz on the behavior of the HLS gauge coupling approaching to zero:

Several comments are in oder:

In the VM both the axialvector and vector current correlators in Eq.(3) take the form of Fπ2​(Λ)/Q2−2​z2​(Λ)F_{\pi}^{2}(\Lambda)/Q^{2}-2z_{2}(\Lambda). For the axialvector current correlator, the first term Fπ2​(Λ)/Q2F_{\pi}^{2}(\Lambda)/Q^{2} comes from the π\pi-exchange contribution, while for the vector current correlator it can be easily understood as the σ\sigma (would-be NG boson absorbed into ρ\rho)-exchange contribution in the RξR_{\xi}-like gauge. Thus only the longitudinal ρ\rho couples to the vector current, and the transverse ρ\rho with the helicity ±1\pm 1, which belongs to the representation (Nf,Nf∗)⊕(Nf∗,Nf)(N_{f},N_{f}^{*})\oplus(N_{f}^{*},N_{f}), is decoupled from it. This can be also seen in the unitary gauge. [9]

The parameters L10​(mρ)L_{10}(m_{\rho}) and L9​(mρ)L_{9}(m_{\rho}) defined in Ref. [1] diverge as NfN_{f} approaches to NfcrN_{f}^{\rm cr}. However, we should note that, even for Nf=3N_{f}=3, both L10​(μ)L_{10}(\mu) and L9​(μ)L_{9}(\mu) have the infrared logarithmic divergences when we take μ→0\mu\rightarrow 0 in the running obtained by the chiral perturbation theory [21]. Thus we need more careful treatment of these quantities for large NfN_{f}. This is beyond the scope of this paper.

The A1A_{1} in the VM is resolved and/or decoupled from the axialvector current near the critical flavor since there is no contribution in the vector current correlator to be matched with the axialvector current correlator. As to the scalar meson [22], although the mass is smaller than the matching scale adopted in Ref. [1] for Nf=3N_{f}=3 [23], we expect that the scalar meson is also resolved and/or decoupled near the chiral phase transition point, since it is in the (Nf,Nf∗)⊕(Nf∗,Nf)(N_{f},N_{f}^{*})\oplus(N_{f}^{*},N_{f}) representation together with the A1A_{1} in the VM.

In this paper we applied the VM to the chiral restoration in the large NfN_{f} QCD. It may be checked by the lattice simulation: The vanishing ratio mρ/Fπ​(0)m_{\rho}/F_{\pi}(0) is a clear indication of the VM.

The VM may be applied to other chiral phase transitions such as the one at finite temperature and/or density. In such a case, the position of the ρ\rho peak of the dilepton spectrum would move to the lower energy region in accord with the picture shown in Ref. [24], and Eqs. (10) and (11) would further imply smaller ρ\rho width (Γ/mρ∼gρ​π​π2∼ϵq\Gamma/m_{\rho}\sim g_{\rho\pi\pi}^{2}\sim\epsilon^{q}) and larger peak value (Γe​e​Γπ​π/Γ2∼gρ2/(gρ​π​π2​mρ4)∼1/ϵ2​q\Gamma_{ee}\Gamma_{\pi\pi}/\Gamma^{2}\sim g_{\rho}^{2}/(g_{\rho\pi\pi}^{2}m_{\rho}^{4})\sim 1/\epsilon^{2q}) near the critical point. If it is really the case, these would be clear signals of VM tested in the future experiments.

The VM studied in this paper may be applied to the models for the composite WW and ZZ. Our analysis shows that the mass of the composite vector boson approaches to zero faster than the order parameter, which is fixed to the electroweak symmetry breaking scale, near the critical point. The VM may also be applied to the technicolor with light techni-ρ\rho.
