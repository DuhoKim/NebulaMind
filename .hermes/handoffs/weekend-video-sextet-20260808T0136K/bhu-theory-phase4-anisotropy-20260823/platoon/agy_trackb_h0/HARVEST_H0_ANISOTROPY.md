# H0 Anisotropy Bounds Harvest

## 1. Migkas et al. (2020)
* **Verbatim quote(s):**
  "The apparently maximum acceleration direction is found toward $(l,b)=(274^{\circ}, -22^{\circ})$ with $H_0=66.20\pm 1.72$ km/s/Mpc while the most extreme opposite behavior is found at $(l,b)=(17^{\circ}, -9^{\circ})$ with $H_0=75.17\pm 1.81$ km/s/Mpc. Their deviation from each other is at 3.59$\sigma$ ($13\pm 4\%$). The most extreme dipole is centered at $(l,b)=(263^{\circ}, -21^{\circ})$ with a $3.15\sigma$ significance." (Section 6: Cosmological constraints).
* **Citation:** Migkas, K., Schellenberger, G., Reiprich, T. H., Pacaud, F., Ramos-Ceja, M. E., & Lovisari, L. (2020). Probing cosmic isotropy with a new X-ray galaxy cluster sample through the $L_{\text{X}}-T$ scaling relation. *Astronomy & Astrophysics*, 636, A15. DOI: 10.1051/0004-6361/201936602. (Peer-reviewed journal)
* **arXiv ID / URL:** [arXiv:2004.03305](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03305)
* **What the number bounds:** The apparent spatial variation (anisotropy) of the Hubble constant ($H_0$), expressed as a relative difference ($13\pm 4\%$) between regions, or a dipole amplitude in the X-ray galaxy cluster $L_X-T$ scaling relation normalizations across the sky.

## 2. Migkas et al. (2021)
* **Verbatim quote(s):**
  "The combined maximum anisotropy direction is found toward $(l,b)=(276^{\circ}\pm 26^{\circ},-14^{\circ}\pm20^{\circ} )$, with $H_0=63.4\pm 2.5$ km/s/Mpc, at a $4.3\sigma$ tension with the rest of the sky ($13\pm 3\%$). ... Finally, a dipole form of the anisotropy is apparent in the sigma maps." (Section 5.1.3: Joint analysis of the $Y_{SZ}-T$ relation for our sample and ACC)
* **Citation:** Migkas, K., Pacaud, F., Schellenberger, G., Erler, J., Nguyen-Dang, N. T., Reiprich, T. H., Ramos-Ceja, M. E., & Lovisari, L. (2021). Cosmological implications of the anisotropy of ten galaxy cluster scaling relations. *Astronomy & Astrophysics*, 649, A151. DOI: 10.1051/0004-6361/202140296. (Peer-reviewed journal)
* **arXiv ID / URL:** [arXiv:2103.13904](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.13904)
* **What the number bounds:** $H_0$ relative spatial variation (anisotropy) driving differences in the joint galaxy cluster scaling relations across the sky (tension of an anisotropic region vs the rest of the sky).

## 3. Dam et al. (2023)
* **Verbatim quote(s):**
  "We find unambiguous evidence for the presence of a dipole in the distribution of quasars with a direction that is consistent with the dipole identified in the CMB. However, the amplitude of the dipole is found to be 2.7 times larger than that expected from the conventional kinematic explanation of the CMB dipole, with a statistical significance of $5.7\sigma$." (Abstract/Introduction)
  "For $M_7$, where the amplitude of the dipole is free to vary, we find $D / D_\text{CMB} = 2.46 \pm 0.18$, demonstrating that, as with S21, the amplitude of the quasar dipole is significantly different to the expectation from the CMB." (Section 4.1: Posterior exploration)
* **Citation:** Dam, L., Lewis, G. F., & Brewer, B. J. (2023). Testing the Cosmological Principle with CatWISE Quasars: A Bayesian Analysis of the Number-Count Dipole. *Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society*, 525(1), 231-245. DOI: 10.1093/mnras/stad2322. (Peer-reviewed journal)
* **arXiv ID / URL:** [arXiv:2212.07733](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07733)
* **What the number bounds:** The amplitude of the number-count dipole of CatWISE quasars relative to the kinematically expected CMB dipole ($D / D_\text{CMB}$).

## 4. Hu et al. (2024)
* **Verbatim quote(s):**
  "The values of $D_\mathrm{max}$ to $\Omega_{m}$ and $H_{0}$ are $D_{\mathrm{max},\Omega_{m}}$ = 3.29$\sigma$ and $D_{\mathrm{max}, H_{0}}$ = 4.48$\sigma$, respectively." (Section 3: Results)
  "The maximum constraint of $H_{0}$ is $H_{0,\mathrm{max}}$ = 74.26$\pm$ 0.39 km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, and the corresponding constraint of $\Omega_{m}$ is 0.30$^{+0.03}_{-0.03}$... The preferred direction of cosmic anisotropy and corresponding 1$\sigma$ range are $(l, b) = ({313.4^{\circ}}_{-18.2}^{+19.6}, {-16.8^{\circ}}_{-10.7}^{+11.1})$" (Section 3: Results)
* **Citation:** Hu, J. P., Wang, Y. Y., Hu, J., & Wang, F. Y. (2024). Testing the cosmological principle with the Pantheon+ sample and the region-fitting method. *Astronomy & Astrophysics*, 681, A88. DOI: 10.1051/0004-6361/202347121. (Peer-reviewed journal)
* **arXiv ID / URL:** [arXiv:2310.11727](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11727)
* **What the number bounds:** The local cosmic anisotropy of the Hubble expansion ($H_0$ directional variation amplitude and direction) mapped using Type Ia supernovae (Pantheon+).

## 5. Cowell et al. (2023)
* **Verbatim quote(s):**
  "We constrain the eigenvalues of the quadrupole in the Hubble parameter to be $\lambda_1 =0.021\pm{ 0.011}$ and $ {\lambda_2= 3.15\times 10^{-5}}\pm 0.012$ and place a $1\sigma$ upper limit on its amplitude of $2.88\%$. We find no significant dipole in the deceleration parameter, finding constraints of $q_{\rm dip} =  4.5^{+1.9}_{-5.4}$. However, in the rest frame of the CMB without corrections, we find $ q_{ \rm dip} = 9.6^{+4.0}_{-6.9}$, a $>2\sigma$ positive amplitude." (Abstract)
* **Citation:** Cowell, J. A., Dhawan, S., & Macpherson, H. J. (2023). Potential signature of a quadrupolar Hubble expansion in Pantheon+ supernovae. *Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society*, 526(1), 1482-1494. DOI: 10.1093/mnras/stad2788. (Peer-reviewed journal)
* **arXiv ID / URL:** [arXiv:2212.13569](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.13569)
* **What the number bounds:** Bounds on the eigenvalues of the quadrupole in the Hubble parameter and the dipole in the deceleration parameter ($q_{\rm dip}$) using the Pantheon+ sample.
