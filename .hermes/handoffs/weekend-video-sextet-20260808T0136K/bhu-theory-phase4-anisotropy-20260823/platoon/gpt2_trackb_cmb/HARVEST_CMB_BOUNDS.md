## Ellis & Baldwin — test definition

**Citation:** G. F. R. Ellis and J. E. Baldwin (1984), “On the expected anisotropy of radio source counts,” *Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society* **206**(2), 377–381. DOI: [10.1093/mnras/206.2.377](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/206.2.377). **Venue class:** peer-reviewed journal article.

**arXiv / source read:** no arXiv ID; the paper predates arXiv. NASA ADS scan read: https://adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1984MNRAS.206..377E. Local: `sources/radio_ellis_baldwin_1984.pdf`, `sources/radio_ellis_baldwin_1984.txt`.[1]

> “If the standard interpretation of the dipole anisotropy in the microwave background radiation as being due to our peculiar velocity in a homogeneous isotropic universe is correct, then radio-source number counts must show a similar anisotropy. **Paper framing:** compares the velocity inferred from radio-source counts with that inferred from the microwave-background dipole, conditional on isotropic source counts in their rest frame.

## WMAP-team quadrupole posterior — low-significance position

**Citation and source receipt.** C. L. Bennett et al., “Seven-Year Wilkinson Microwave Anisotropy Probe (WMAP) Observations: Are There Cosmic Microwave Background Anomalies?,” *The Astrophysical Journal Supplement Series* **192**(2), 17 (2011), DOI: https://doi.org/10.1088/0067-0049/192/2/17. Venue class: refereed journal article; official WMAP mission-team anomaly reassessment. arXiv: 1001.4758v2. URL actually read: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1001.4758v2. **What the numbers bound in the paper’s framing.** These numbers describe the WMAP quadrupole likelihood inferred with Gibbs sampling and a Blackwell–Rao estimator, including the posterior confidence ranges and the location of the best-fit ΛCDM prediction in the cumulative posterior.

> “The peak of the likelihood is at 200 µK², the median is at 430 µK², and the mean is at 1000 µK². The 68% confidence range extends from 80 to 700 µK², and the 95% confidence range extends from 40 to 3200 µK². Figure 4 shows the cumulative quadrupole distribution derived from 300,000 Gibbs samples. The mean quadrupole predicted by the best-fit ΛCDM model lies at a cumulative probability of 0.824, well within the 95% confidence region allowed by the data. We conclude that the WMAP quadrupole measurement is not anomalously low. Further, while alternative models that predict a lower quadrupole will better match this specific part of the data, it is impossible to significantly favor such models on the basis of quadrupole power alone.”

**Precise location.** PDF p. 5, Sect. 4, paragraph immediately above Fig. 3; page-tagged text lines 305–331.

## Low-quadrupole frequentist/Bayesian reassessment — low-significance position

**Citation and source receipt.** G. Efstathiou, “The Statistical Significance of the Low CMB Multipoles,” *Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society* **346**(2), L26–L30 (2003), DOI: https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1365-2966.2003.07304.x. **What the measurement numbers bound in the paper’s framing.** These are the low-multipole amplitudes in the public WMAP release and the WMAP summary paper’s foreground-modeling confidence interval, as stated by Efstathiou.

**Precise location.** PDF p. 2, Sect. 2, paragraph beginning “The WMAP quadrupole…”; page-tagged text lines 135–156.

**Precise location.** PDF p. 3, Sect. 3, paragraph immediately below Table 1; page-tagged text lines 201–208.
