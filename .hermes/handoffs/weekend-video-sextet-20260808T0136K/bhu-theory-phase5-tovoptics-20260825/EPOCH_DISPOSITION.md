# The epoch question, adjudicated — and the two verdicts combine against BOTH constructions
(2026-08-27. GATE_EPOCH_VERDICT.md (codex) and KGATE_EPOCH_VERDICT.md (kimi).)

## The split, and why it is not really a disagreement

**kimi: RULE_A_LOCAL_ANCHORING — (b) is GEOMETRICALLY FORBIDDEN.** Its argument is about the
locus: varying the crossing epoch sweeps a **spacelike family of distinct comoving elements**,
not a fluid worldline, and "no segment of it is tangent to one". Applying a single-element
evolution law along a locus that is not a single element is invalid. That kills the adiabatic
carry outright.

**codex: NEITHER IS DETERMINED BY THE PINNED MODEL.** Its argument is thermodynamic: the pinned
material supplies a *mechanical* equation of state p(ρ), which does **not** determine a
temperature field T(ρ). Chemical potential, particle density, entropy per particle, composition
and shock entropy production are all absent. (b) follows only after assuming a common
zero-chemical-potential isentrope; **(a) follows only after assuming the relevant energy density
is equilibrium radiation — which simultaneously forces w = 1/3.**

The two rulings attack different layers and both survive. Combined:

- **(b) is geometrically invalid** — kimi's locus argument stands on the pinned metric.
- **(a) is thermodynamically inconsistent at the very w values where the null sits** — my
  blackbody anchoring presumes radiation, which pins w = 1/3, while I apply it at w ≈ 0.041.

**So both constructions are wrong for generic w, and mine is wrong in a way I had already been
caught on once**: I fixed exactly this blackbody-versus-closure inconsistency for the *depth*
variation after an earlier double, and did not notice the same assumption still governing the
*epoch* variation.

## The finding

**The null exists. Its location is not a prediction of the pinned geometry.**

Codex states the consequence precisely: quoting either w = 0.0408 or w = 0.0815 as the physical
escape point "would silently choose an unpinned thermal closure". The location is
thermodynamic-model dependent and presently undetermined.

This is the third answer I explicitly asked for and said I would rather have than a guess. It
is a real result, not a failure: a specific published cosmology has been shown to contain a
setting in which it produces no observable signal, **and the theory as published does not
determine where that setting is.** Fixing it requires adding thermodynamics the papers never
supplied — composition, entropy, chemical potential — at which point the answer becomes a
property of the added assumptions rather than of the model.
