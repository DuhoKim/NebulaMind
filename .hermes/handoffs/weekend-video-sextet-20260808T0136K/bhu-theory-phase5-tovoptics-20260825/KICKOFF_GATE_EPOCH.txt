## The question

An observer inside the FRW interior looks in different directions. Each direction's sight line
meets the shock boundary at a DIFFERENT conformal epoch eta. Call the exterior's radiation
temperature just outside the junction, at crossing epoch eta, T_s(eta).

**How does T_s vary with eta?** Two constructions are in play and they disagree:

**(a) LOCAL / blackbody anchoring (mine, p6_path_transfer.py).** At each epoch the exterior's
temperature is set by its own energy density there:
    T_s(eta) proportional to rhobar(eta)^(1/4),
with rhobar(eta) = v(eta) * rho_FRW(eta) from the gated orbit.

**(b) ADIABATIC carrying (the blind seat's, platoon/gpt1_blind_p7/p7_blind_dipole.py:94-110).**
The temperature is carried from one normalisation by the adiabatic law for the imposed equation
of state p̄ = w rhobar:
    d ln T_s / d eta = [w/(1+w)] d ln rhobar / d eta.

They coincide only at w = 1/3.

## Why it matters — this is not bookkeeping

The observable dipole is the sum of two competing terms: the junction Doppler factor, and the
epoch-variation of what is seen through the boundary. Those cancel exactly at some equation of
state, producing a setting in which the model yields NO signal at all while being entirely
correct. Both constructions produce such a cancellation — the phenomenon is confirmed twice —
but they place it at different equations of state (mine w = 0.0408, theirs w = 0.0815). The
CHOICE BETWEEN (a) AND (b) THEREFORE DETERMINES WHICH PHYSICAL SITUATIONS ESCAPE THE TEST.
Until it is settled, no location can be quoted and the excluded set is unknown.

## The specific difficulty I want ruled on

The adiabatic law governs ONE fluid element evolving along its own worldline. Different crossing
epochs would ordinarily be different fluid elements, in which case (b) is unjustified and (a) is
right. BUT inside the horizon the areal radius rbar is TIMELIKE (pinned: astro-ph/0210105 sec.3,
A = 1 - N < 0), and the comoving exterior fluid moves along rbar. So "a different place on the
boundary" and "the same matter at a different time" are not cleanly separable here, which is
precisely what makes (b) arguable rather than obviously wrong.

## What I am asking for

1. **Rule: (a), (b), or neither** — and if neither, state the correct construction.
2. **Justify it from the pinned geometry**, not from convention or convenience: what is the
   locus swept by varying the crossing epoch, and is it a single fluid worldline, a spacelike
   family of distinct elements, or something else in this metric?
3. If the answer is that the pinned material does NOT determine it, say so plainly — that would
   make the null's location an irreducible modelling ambiguity, which is itself a publishable
   finding and I would rather have it than a guess.
