## The phenomenon is real — independently found, same structure

The seat searched and **found a cancellation**, unprompted. Its sign structure matches mine
exactly: the signed coefficient is **positive below the null and negative above it**. Both
implementations therefore agree that the dipole passes through zero as the exterior equation of
state is varied, and that a narrow band exists in which no offset can be constrained at all.

**That is the finding that matters, and it now stands on two independent calculations.** The
most important sentence in the stakes document — *the model can produce no signal at all
without being wrong* — survives doubling.

Its stability evidence is stronger than mine: the root is identical to twelve digits across
32/64/128-point quadrature, integrator tolerance 1e-10, and it verified an analytic endpoint
identity against the numerics to 5.1×10⁻¹⁵ in log temperature. This is not a numerical artifact.

## But the LOCATION disagrees by almost exactly a factor of two

| | mine (P7) | theirs |

| null at w | 0.0407786 | **0.0815000** |
| ratio | — | **1.99859** |

Exactly 2 would put mine at 0.0407500; mine is 0.0407786 — **0.070% away**. So the ratio is 2
to within about a seventh of a percent, which is far too close to be coincidence and far too
far to be exact. That pattern says a factor-of-two convention difference *plus* a small genuine
residual, not one or the other.

Band widths are consistent once thresholds are matched: they call a dipole unconstrainable
below 1.358×10⁻³ (a bound of 100% of the boundary radius); I used 1.358×10⁻² (a bound of 10%).
Their looser threshold gives 3.89×10⁻⁴ against my 8.9×10⁻⁵ — the right direction and roughly
the right size given both the threshold ratio and the factor-of-two in w.

Candidates, none yet tested: the definition of w itself (p̄ = wρ̄ against a factor-carrying

junction transition width, which I impose over 2% of the shock radius and they may treat
differently. **I am not guessing between these in the record — it is a contained calculation
and I will run it down rather than argue it.**
