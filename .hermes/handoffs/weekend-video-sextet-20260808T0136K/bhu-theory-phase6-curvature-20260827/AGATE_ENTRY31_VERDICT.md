### 1. Is the falsifier live or effectively reached?
At 2.35 ± 0.11 $M_\odot$, the 2.5 $M_\odot$ bar is **not effectively reached**. Smolin wrote that "sufficiently high is certainly 2.5 $M_\odot$". He acknowledged that anything over 1.5 $M_\odot$ would be "troubling" if one fully trusted the Bethe-Brown calculation, but he intentionally set 2.5 $M_\odot$ as a conservative hard threshold to definitively rule out the kaon-condensate regime. Being ~1.4 sigma short means his self-defined conservative bar stands unbreached.

### 2. Does a falsifier survive when its instrument is broken?
**No. It is now an unanchored round number.** The 2.5 $M_\odot$ falsifier derives its calibration entirely from the Bethe-Brown kaon condensation mechanism. Smolin's logic rests on the premise that Bethe and Brown's physical link between the strange quark mass ($\mu_s$) and the neutron star mass limit is correct. If the instrument limb (the 1.5 $M_\odot$ limit) is definitively broken, it severs the theoretical link that would allow us to infer anything about $\mu_s$ (and thus cosmological natural selection) from an observation of a heavy neutron star. Without the Bethe-Brown mechanism anchoring it, the 2.5 $M_\odot$ threshold loses its causal connection to the theory.

**Yes.** Cosmological Natural Selection proposes that black hole singularities bounce to create new expanding universes, and that our universe's parameters are tuned by evolutionary pressures to maximize black hole production. This fits the core definition of a black-hole-universe theory perfectly; it is not a classification artefact.
