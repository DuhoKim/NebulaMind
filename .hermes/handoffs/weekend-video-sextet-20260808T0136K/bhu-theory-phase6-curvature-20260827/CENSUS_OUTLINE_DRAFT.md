## The honest question first: is this a study or an assembly?

Duho's standing bar: *assembly of published values + systematics commentary ≠ a study.* A census
that only sorts 24 papers into four bins fails that bar, and I would rather say so now than
discover it at the gate.

**What makes it a study is the third leg below, not the first.** If Duho reads the outline and

## Leg 1 — the census (the data, and the weakest leg)

51 classified papers; **24 with pinned full text, 27 unreachable.** Coverage stated up front, not
in a limitations paragraph at the end.

Current distribution: 3 CALIBRATED-FALSIFIER (2 live, 1 fired) · 8 QUALITATIVE-DIRECTIONAL ·
3 PROSPECT · 33 CONSISTENCY-ONLY · 4 UNREAD.

The claim this supports: **a literature that is almost entirely unfalsifiable, with one surviving
live test that bears directly on a black-hole-universe cosmology** (entry 31, Smolin's 2.5 M☉
bar, currently 1.36σ short and moving *away* from firing as measurements tighten).

On its own this is a description, not a result. It is the substrate.

## Leg 2 — the method finding (measured, not asserted)

**Blind single-pass re-classification reproduces the bias it is meant to detect.**

Demonstrated with seeded controls: three entries with independently gated answers were classified
blind. Two reproduced the gated answer; **entry 54 returned CALIBRATED-FALSIFIER, quoting the
abstract's bracket — independently reproducing the exact error two gates had corrected hours
earlier.** The seat never saw our record. It made our mistake from the same source text.

Measured reliability: 2 of 3 controls. All six disagreements ran in one direction (promotion),
matching the control failure's direction.

The mechanism is specific and generalisable: **the error lives in the gap between a paper's
abstract and its hedges.** Entry 54's abstract states a bracket; §VI withdraws the magnitude four
sentences after the equation. A single pass reads the headline. So does a classifier. So did I,
on 2026-08-23.

This is a result about how literature audits fail, with a control design that would let anyone
measure it on their own corpus.

## Leg 3 — the anatomy (this is what makes it a study)

**Falsifiability claims in this literature separate into an INSTRUMENT and a THEORY, and the
instrument can break without the theory dying.** Every one of the corpus's calibrated falsifiers
turned on this distinction, and in each case it changed the verdict:

| entry | instrument | theory | what actually happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | Brown–Bethe / VM-HLS / kaon condensation, M ≳ 2 M☉ | CNS | instrument **fired**; record wrongly read it as killing CNS. Source gives CNS only "a serious obstacle". |
| 31 | Brown–Bethe M_max ≈ 1.5 M☉ | CNS | instrument **broken**; Smolin anticipated that and set his own 2.5 M☉ bar, which survives. |
| 51 | ECKS density / minimum-mass chain, LHC route | BHU | instrument **live**; fires ECKS, and constrains BHU only where a model inherits the ECKS premise. |

Three papers, three independent gate rounds, the same structure each time — and in all three our
record had collapsed instrument into theory, always in the direction of claiming more.

**The general statement:** a falsifier reported as testing a cosmology often tests a nuclear- or
gravitational-physics chain that the cosmology merely cites. Breaking the chain does not kill the
cosmology, and the cosmology may set its own, weaker threshold that survives. A census that
records "calibrated falsifier: yes" without recording *of what* is not recording anything usable.

This is transferable beyond BHU, it is not a restatement of any paper in the corpus, and it was
not visible from any single entry — it only appears across the census. **That is the argument for
publication.**

## What it cannot claim, stated in the outline so it does not get written in later

- **Not** that the family is wrong. An unfalsifiable literature is not a refuted one.
- **Not** complete: 27 of 51 unreachable, weighted unknown. Coverage is 47%.
- **Not** independently reliable: the classifications are mine, three of three re-read entries
  were wrong, and the measured control rate is 2/3. The census reports its own error rate rather
  than claiming accuracy.
- **Not** a claim that no falsifier exists — entry 31 is live, and entry 51 is live for ECKS.

# CORRECTION 2026-08-29 — leg 3 is Duhem–Quine, and the outline oversold it

Duho approved leg 3 as the thing that carries the paper. **I had not checked whether it was
already known. It is.**

## What is not novel

The general statement — that a falsifier may kill an auxiliary chain rather than the theory
citing it — is the **Duhem–Quine thesis**, Duhem 1906, Quine 1951:

> "one can never be sure that it is a given theory rather than auxiliary or background
> hypotheses which experiment has falsified"

That is my instrument/theory separation, a century old and standard. It must be **cited as
background, not presented as a finding.** My outline presented it as the study's core
contribution, and Duho approved on that framing. The framing was wrong.

## What survives, and it is narrower but better positioned

**1. Duhem–Quine says the ambiguity EXISTS. What this corpus shows is how it gets RESOLVED —
and that the resolution was systematically one-directional.** In all three calibrated falsifiers
the curated record collapsed instrument into theory, and every time in the direction of the
cosmology being *more* testable or *more* refuted than its source supported. Duhem–Quine predicts
ambiguity; it does not predict bias. The bias is the empirical claim, with n=3 and a mechanism.

**2. The census appears to be novel in kind, though this needs a real literature check.** A
targeted search surfaced no systematic count of falsifiable content across a cosmology
literature. The testability debate is live and prominent — Ijjas, Steinhardt & Loeb on inflation;
the multiverse/"Beyond Falsifiability" exchanges — but it is conducted **by argument and by
cherry-picked example, not by census.** Nobody appears to have counted.

**This answers the motivation question I flagged as unanswered and non-optional:** the field
argues about whether its theories are testable without measuring it. That is a reason someone
outside our record needs this.

**Caveat on that claim:** one search is not a literature review. Any priority claim needs a
proper check before it is written, and the paper should say "we are not aware of" rather than
"this is the first".

**3. Leg 2 is untouched.** Classifier reliability with seeded controls is not a philosophy-of-
science claim and Duhem–Quine has no bearing on it.

## Revised structure of the argument

Not: *"we discovered instruments separate from theories."*
But: *"Duhem–Quine ambiguity is unavoidable. In this corpus it was resolved the same way every
time — toward the stronger claim — and here is the measured rate, the mechanism, and a control
design that detects it."*

Smaller claim. Defensible. And now anchored in a live disagreement in the field rather than in
our own filing system.

## Standing lesson, and it is the session's own lesson turned on me

I pitched leg 3 as the novel contribution without checking the literature, and Duho approved on
that pitch. Three of three entries re-read this week were overclaims. This was a fourth, in my
own outline, about my own work — caught only because the outline said motivation must come from
the literature and I went to look.

# LITERATURE CHECK 2026-08-29 — the priority claim holds, at the strength a web search can carry

Four targeted searches. **No systematic census of falsifiable content across a physics or
cosmology literature surfaced.** What exists divides into three kinds, none of which is this:

**1. The testability debate itself — argued, not counted.** Prominent and live: Ijjas, Steinhardt
& Loeb on inflation; "A Farewell to Falsifiability" (arXiv 1504.00108); Carroll's "Beyond
Falsifiability: Normal Science in a Multiverse"; Dawid on string theory and non-empirical theory
assessment (arXiv 2105.14342); Chakrabarty, "Is Falsifiability a 'Blunt Instrument' for Modern
Physics?". Every one of these proceeds by argument and selected example. **None counts.** One
source puts the informal position plainly — that judgement on what counts as falsifiable "varies
considerably", and that there are "well tested theories, hardly tested theories, and non-testable
theories" — which is a taxonomy asserted rather than applied to a corpus.

**2. Bibliometrics — measures the wrong thing.** Citation counts, h-indices, keyword frequency,
co-authorship graphs. It measures *attention*, not *epistemic content*. Classifying a paper by
what it would take to refute it is a different measurement class and I found no bibliometric work
attempting it.

**3. The one real methodological precedent, and it should be cited as related work.**
arXiv 1810.03480 — text classification of the precursory-accelerating-seismicity corpus,
inferring theoretical trends in *earthquake predictability* research 1988–2018. Different field,
same genus: take a literature about whether something is predictable, classify its content
systematically, and report the distribution. That this exists in seismology and not in cosmology
is a point in the paper's favour, not against it — it shows the method travels.

## The honest limit on this check

**Four web searches is not a literature review.** A real priority check needs ADS full-text
search, arXiv full-text, PhilPapers and philsci-archive — the last two especially, since a census
of falsifiability is as likely to live in philosophy of science as in astrophysics, and web search
covers those poorly.

**The paper must therefore say "we are not aware of", never "this is the first".** If a proper
check later finds a precedent, the census is still worth reporting — it would simply be a
replication in a new corpus, which is a smaller but entirely respectable claim.

## Net effect on the outline

Motivation is now grounded outside our own record, which was the open requirement: **the field
argues about whether its theories are testable and has not measured it.** That is a reason for
someone who has never heard of this bibliography to care.

Positioning: not "nobody has thought about falsifiability in cosmology" — they plainly have, at
length — but "the debate is conducted by example, and a corpus-level count has not been done for
this literature."
