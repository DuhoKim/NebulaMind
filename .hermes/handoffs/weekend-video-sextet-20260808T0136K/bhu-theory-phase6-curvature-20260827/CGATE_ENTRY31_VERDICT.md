## 1. Is the falsifier live or effectively reached?

It is live, not effectively reached.

Smolin's section 4 is not soft enough to treat the present 2.35 +/- 0.11 solar-mass measurement as satisfying the bar. He writes that a sufficiently high neutron-star mass would show the strange-quark mass is on the conventional-EoS side and would refute S; then he sets the clean level:

> "Sufficiently high is certainly 2:5M..." (pinned text lines 255-257)

He separately says that any value above 1.5 solar masses would be "troubling" only if one is completely confident of Bethe and Brown's upper limit (lines 256-258). That creates two limbs:

- 1.5 solar masses: Brown-Bethe-confidence/troubling limb; already broken by modern 2 solar-mass pulsars.
- 2.5 solar masses: Smolin's conservative clean-refutation limb.

The current best cited record for PSR J0952-0607 remains below the 2.5 central threshold: Romani et al. 2022 report 2.35 +/- 0.17 solar masses, and arXiv:2512.05099 reports 2.35 +/- 0.11 solar masses. Against 2.5, the tightened number is 0.15/0.11 = 1.36 sigma short, with only about 8.6 percent of a Gaussian posterior above 2.5. That is close, but it is not a fired clean falsifier.

## 2. Does a falsifier survive when its instrument is broken?

Yes, narrowly.

The broken instrument removes the lower 1.5 solar-mass shortcut, not the 2.5 bar. Smolin explicitly recognizes that Bethe-Brown calculations may be inaccurate because of approximations such as chiral dynamics (lines 249-251). His argument then pivots to a conservative observation: if a star is sufficiently heavy, "certainly" 2.5 solar masses, it would show the world is on the high-maximum-mass side and that decreasing the strange-quark mass would produce more black holes (lines 252-260).

So the calibration is not "Brown-Bethe says M_max = 1.5, therefore anything above 1.5 kills CNS." That limb is dead. The surviving calibration is "a single neutron star at Smolin's own 2.5 solar-mass clean threshold would refute S." It is coarse, but it is author-stated, numerical, and tied to a specified observation.

This is weaker than a modern in-house EoS recalculation would be. It is still stronger than a qualitative direction, because the author gives a definite mass threshold for clean refutation.

## 3. Does footnote 6 demote entry 31 as section VI demoted entry 54?

No.

Footnote 6 does not withdraw the falsifier in the way entry 54 withdrew or qualified its magnitude. Smolin anticipates a rescue move: S could be modified by adding a hypothesis that the strange-quark mass is not independently variable and is tied to parameters that strongly affect star formation. He then classifies that as an ad hoc save unless it has independent support:

> "science proceeds by rejecting hypotheses that are refuted in the absence of special fixes" (lines 303-305)

and he says this case would warrant a fix only if a preferred fundamental theory with strong independent experimental support made the required parameter coupling real (lines 305-309).

That is materially different from entry 54. Entry 54's body text made the numeric magnitude conditional and left the hard content as a sign. Smolin's footnote preserves the falsifier under normal scientific practice and specifies the extraordinary condition under which the refutation could be blocked. A possible future independent rescue does not demote the present falsifier.

## 4. Is CNS in this BHU family?

Yes, with a classification caveat.

Smolin's CNS is explicitly a black-hole-bounce reproduction theory: black hole singularities bounce, each producing a new expanding spacetime region with slightly changed low-energy parameters. That is inside a broad black-hole-universe family. It is not the same thing as the Poplawski/Gaztanaga-style interior-cosmology mechanisms, and its neutron-star falsifier tests CNS specifically, not every BHU mechanism. But its presence is not a mere classification artifact.
