## The single question

**Does Smolin's 2.5 M_sun neutron-star falsifier remain a CALIBRATED falsifier, given that the
Brown-Bethe 1.5 M_sun calculation it references has been observationally broken?**

Nothing else is in dispute. Both prior seats agree that:
  - the 2.5 M_sun bar is NOT reached (heaviest well-measured neutron star is
    PSR J0952-0607 at 2.35 +- 0.11 M_sun, ~1.4 sigma short, ~8.6% posterior above the bar);
  - Smolin's footnote 6 does NOT demote the falsifier (he disclaims ad-hoc rescues rather
    than relying on them);
  - CNS genuinely belongs in the black-hole-universe family.

## The two readings, stated as fairly as I can put them

**A. LIVE_CALIBRATED** (CGATE_ENTRY31_VERDICT.md): the broken instrument removes the 1.5
shortcut, not the 2.5 bar. Smolin himself flags that Bethe-Brown's calculations may be
inaccurate and then pivots to a conservative threshold that does not depend on their number.
The surviving calibration is "a single neutron star at 2.5 M_sun would refute S" -- coarse, but
author-stated, numerical, and tied to a specified observation.

**B. DEMOTE_BROKEN_INSTRUMENT** (AGATE_ENTRY31_VERDICT.md): the 2.5 bar derives its calibration
entirely from the Bethe-Brown kaon-condensation mechanism. If that mechanism's link between the
strange-quark mass and the neutron-star mass limit is broken, the inference from "heavy neutron
star" to "CNS refuted" is severed, and 2.5 becomes an unanchored round number rather than a
calibrated threshold.

## The passage both readings turn on — read it yourself, in place, before deciding

From section 4, verbatim:

  "Bethe, Brown and collaborators claim that calculations show that mu < mu_c [52-54]. But
   their calculations involve approximations such as chiral dynamics and may be sufficiently
   inaccurate that in fact mu_c > mu. However, we can be reasonably sure of the existence of
   such a critical value mu_c. Then we may reason as follows. If mu < mu_c then, as shown by
   calculations [52-54] the upper mass limit is low, approximately 1.5 M_sun. If mu > mu_c
   neutron stars have the conventional equations of state and the upper mass limit is higher,
   almost certainly above 2 [55]. Therefore a single observation of a neutron star whose mass M
   was sufficiently high would show that mu > mu_c, refuting Bethe and Brown's claim for the
   opposite. Sufficiently high is certainly 2.5 M_sun, although if one is completely confident
   of Bethe and Brown's upper limit of 1.5 solar masses, any value higher than this would be
   troubling. Furthermore, this would refute S because it would then be the case that a
   decrease of mu would lead to a world with a lower upper mass limit for neutron stars, and
   therefore more black holes."

Reading A says the "However, we can be reasonably sure of the existence of such a critical
value mu_c" sentence insulates the argument from Bethe-Brown being wrong.
Reading B says the whole inference chain still runs through the Bethe-Brown mechanism.

**Disclosure of my own bias:** I read this passage as favouring A, and I am the party whose
tally claim depends on the answer. I have already been wrong once today on this entry, in the
direction of over-claiming. Discount my reading accordingly and form your own.

## Also worth your attention, if it bears

Smolin says that if mu > mu_c the upper mass limit is "almost certainly above 2". Observed
masses of 1.97, 2.08 and 2.35 M_sun all exceed 2. Does that already establish mu > mu_c on
Smolin's own logic, independently of whether the 2.5 bar is reached? If so, the status may be
neither A nor B. Rule on it if you think it applies; ignore it if you think it does not.
