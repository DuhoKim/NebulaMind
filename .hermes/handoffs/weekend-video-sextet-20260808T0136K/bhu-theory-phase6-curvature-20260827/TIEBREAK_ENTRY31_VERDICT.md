# Entry 31 tiebreak verdict

I uphold A, narrowly.

Smolin does not present 2.5 M_sun as a restatement of Bethe and Brown's approximately 1.5 M_sun upper limit. In section 4 he first attributes the approximately 1.5 value to calculations [52-54], expressly warns that the calculations use approximations, and then distinguishes two evidentiary levels: a mass above 1.5 would be only "troubling" if one were completely confident in that upper limit, whereas 2.5 M_sun is his stated certainly-sufficient threshold. The 2.5 bar is therefore an ex ante conservative cutoff, not a post hoc rescue added after the 1.5 result failed.

I do not rely on the requester's strongest version of A's "However" argument. As printed, the paper first writes `mu < mu_c` and then says approximation error might mean `mu_c > mu`; those are the same inequality, so that sentence contains an evident order/sign error rather than a usable logical contrast. The intended opposite branch is clear from the next sentences, especially the statement that a sufficiently heavy star would show `mu > mu_c`. My ruling instead rests on the unambiguous distinction Smolin actually makes between the conditional, merely troubling 1.5 shortcut and the certainly sufficient 2.5 refutation bar.

This does not make 2.5 independent of the kaon-condensation/critical-mass mechanism. Smolin's chain still requires a critical strange-quark mass, a low-maximum-mass branch on one side, and a conventional-EoS branch on the other. But the stipulated observational break of the numerical 1.5 calculation does not by itself establish that the entire branch structure or its directional link has failed. B silently upgrades "the approximately 1.5 upper limit is wrong" into "no heavy-star observation can still discriminate the two branches." Section 4 does not support that upgrade. Because Smolin himself withholds decisive force from the 1.5 shortcut and supplies 2.5 as the conservative decisive bar, failure of the shortcut does not erase the separately stated cutoff.

The surviving calibration is coarse and conditional, not a modern precision forecast: the paper gives no uncertainty band or fresh derivation around 2.5. Still, it is numerical, author-stated, prospectively tied to one specified observation, and explicitly identified as sufficient to refute S. On the tier used by this record, that is enough to remain a calibrated falsifier. The present evidence stipulated in the brief does not fire it.

The "almost certainly above 2" sentence does not make the already observed masses above 2 a separate firing. Smolin states the implication from mu > mu_c to a conventional-EoS upper limit above 2; the converse does not follow without trusting a low-side upper bound. Using the broken 1.5 bound to supply that converse would reintroduce the very instrument failure under review. Thus the status is A, not a third outcome.

After the primary-source ruling was fixed, I read both full prior gates. The result reproduces `CGATE_ENTRY31_VERDICT.md` on the narrow classification but not any suggestion that 2.5 is mechanism-independent. I reject `AGATE_ENTRY31_VERDICT.md` because it asserts, rather than demonstrates, that failure of the approximately 1.5 result severs the entire strange-quark-mass/maximum-mass directional link. That conclusion would require a source or recalculation showing that the low-mass branch can support neutron-star masses of 2.5 or higher, or that the branch structure itself is invalid; the stipulated failure of the 1.5 value alone supplies neither. On the record presented, B therefore does not carry its demotion burden.
