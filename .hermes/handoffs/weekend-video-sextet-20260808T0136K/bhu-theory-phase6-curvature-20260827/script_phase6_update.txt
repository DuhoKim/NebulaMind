This picks up where this morning's report left off. That one covered the opening finding, that our own note had the kill condition backwards. I am not going to repeat it. This is the three things that have happened since.

First, and it came from your question. You asked whether the universe being flat is not simply enough to rule this out. It was the right question, and it turned out to be the crux of the whole thing.

You are right about the number. The best measurement we have is plus 0.0007, give or take 0.0019 — flat to about a fifth of a percent. The paper's headline figure is minus 0.07. Our measurement sits 26.7 sigma away from the nearest edge of that. So the number the paper actually advertises is not merely disfavoured. It is gone, many times over.

But the number is not what the model requires. The equation gives a ceiling, not a value, and the authors themselves say in the next breath that it could be smaller. What the theory actually needs is only a direction. Negative. Closed by any amount at all, however small.

And a direction has no floor. Minus 0.001 sits within 1 sigma of the measurement. So does minus 10 to the minus 9. In cosmology, flat never means exactly zero. It means consistent with zero to some precision. So no flatness measurement, however good it gets, can ever reach a theory that only claims negative by a hair.

So the fair way to say it is this. Flatness kills the version of this model that had a number. What survives is the version with only a direction — and it survives by abandoning the very assumption that produced the number, which was also its explanation for a known oddity in the microwave background. It keeps its prediction by giving up the thing that made it one.

Second. I checked the three observations the paper offers as support. The first quotes its source correctly. I had doubted that, and I was wrong, so that one stands to their credit. But it leaves out that the same section of that source blames its own number on a known instrument effect, and shows the number disappearing once other data are added. The second is contradicted by the paper it cites, which states plainly that its data prefer a flat geometry. And the third cites a paper that assumes flatness and measures no curvature at all.

Third. Two independent reviewers passed all of this an hour ago, on different engines, neither of them knowing anything about this work. I put my own worst objection to them: that setting aside the datasets which disagree with me is the same fault I am accusing the paper of. They both rejected it, and for the same reason. I set those aside because the collaboration that took the data says they are unreliable, not because I dislike the answer.

One housekeeping note to close. This morning's report went out in my voice, but it was not written by me. It speaks about me in the third person. Its content is accurate and fair, and I have no complaint about it. But it is filed in the record as mine, and it is not mine, and that is worth putting straight.
