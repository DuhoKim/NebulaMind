## Panel 01 — No galaxy survey can reach this rotating-parent signal

Could a spinning parent black hole leave our universe turning? This is Duho's personal side-interest, not a NebulaMind research programme. We gave that published idea every numerical advantage, then compared its largest allowed galaxy signal with the biggest possible count. Even a perfect count of every galaxy would fall short. This does not kill the idea. It closes this test, under the published mechanics, unless someone supplies a powerful amplifier.

## Panel 02 — This family member links a spinning parent to galaxy spins

The wider black-hole-universe family contains different proposals. This video follows 1 member, distinct from the neutron-star chain tested in our earlier explainer. Here, our universe is born inside a rotating black hole. The parent's axis becomes our universe's preferred axis. A tiny inherited turn should then make clockwise and counterclockwise galaxies appear in slightly different numbers. The source states that direction, but gives no size for the difference.

## Panel 03 — The proposed test is a 2-jar galaxy count

Imagine sorting spiral galaxies into 2 jars: clockwise and counterclockwise. With no preferred direction, the jars should fill almost evenly. A rotating universe might tip a few extra galaxies into 1 jar. The test is therefore simple to picture: classify many galaxies, compare the 2 totals, and ask whether the imbalance is larger than ordinary counting noise. The hard part is not the counting rule. It is the predicted size.

## Panel 04 — The universe's baby picture allows almost no global turn

The cosmic microwave background is the universe's baby picture. Its extraordinary smoothness limits how much large-scale rotation can hide in the sky. We deliberately use the looser published limit, because that helps the model: global rotation may be less than about 8 parts in 10 billion of the universe's expansion rate. A tighter, more general check allows even less. The generous limit sets the largest signal considered here.

## Panel 05 — Maximum generosity leaves only 5 extra galaxies in 10 million

A 1998 paper estimated how much global rotation would be needed to dominate the origin of galaxy spins. We scale down from that published benchmark in the simplest linear way, and we assume later scrambling erases nothing. Both choices favor the model. Even then, the largest allowed handedness imbalance is about 5 galaxies in 10 million. This is an upper-edge estimate, not a precise forecast from the original rotating-parent source.

## Panel 06 — Our 100,000-galaxy design needs about 1 extra in 100

Use our own 100,000-galaxy spin-parity design only as a counting yardstick. A convincing detection there needs an imbalance of about 1 galaxy in 100. The rotating-parent allowance is about 5 in 10 million. Put those comparisons on the same ladder and the allowed signal is more than 10,000 times too small. Better software can reduce mistakes, but it cannot remove the counting noise built into a finite sample.

## Panel 07 — Even every observable galaxy is not enough

Could a vastly larger survey rescue the test? To reach 3 standard deviations at the generous signal size would require about 36 trillion galaxies. Published estimates put about 2 trillion galaxies in the observable universe. The test therefore needs 18 observable universes of galaxies. Even perfectly classifying every galaxy that exists would reach only 0.74 standard deviations. There is no larger catalogue waiting beyond all the galaxies we can observe.

## Panel 08 — Only a new amplifier can reopen the arithmetic

One escape exists in principle: a mechanism that makes tiny cosmic rotation lock galaxy spins together with a gain above 10,000 times. But the published rotating-parent mechanics provide no such amplifier. Their own language calls the forces small and says galaxies tend to align, not that they lock together. Adding an instability or resonance now would be new model-building. It would not be a prediction extracted from the scenario as published.

## Panel 09 — Galaxy-spin measurement continues without this interpretation

Our galaxy-spin measurement still stands on its own merits, independent of this closure. It can test asymmetry, classification bias, and astrophysical structure without being a black-hole-universe test. What would reopen the closed route is specific: a published derivation with a numerical amplifier, or a reachable fingerprint that only black-hole birth can leave. Until then, the key stays on the hook and the route stays closed.

## Panel 10 — The route is closed; the wider idea is not

This was not a hunt for a favorable pattern. We took the published idea's own rotation-to-galaxy connection, used the looser cosmic limit, assumed perfect transfer, and allowed every observable galaxy. The result still falls short. A sub-threshold effect could exist and remain forever invisible through this channel. So the honest ending is bounded: the rotating-parent galaxy-handedness test is closed under the published mechanics; the wider black-hole-universe idea is not.
