# Glossary of SDSS Terminology

This page contains a comprehensive glossary of SDSS-related terminology. Terms are given in alphabetical order, with definitions and links to extended descriptions where available. This version of the page is specific to SDSS-III. It is based heavily on the [equivalent glossary](http://classic.sdss.org/dr7/glossary/index.html) used in SDSS-I/II, but defines some new terms, and has removed some terms that are not relevant for SDSS-III. If you come across a term you don’t understand, or think we should include, please [contact us](https://www.sdss4.org/contact).

## A

Adaptive MomentsA method of measuring object shapes ( _i.e._, fitting those shapes to ellipses). These moments are close to optimal for measuring the shapes of faint galaxies, and have been used for weak lensing studies. See the [Adaptive Moments](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/algorithms/classify/#photo_adaptive) section of the [algorithms pages](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/algorithms/).Alfred P. Sloan FoundationA philanthropic nonprofit institution (www.sloan.org) established in 1934 by Alfred Pritchard Sloan, Jr., then President and Chief Executive Officer of the General Motors Corporation. An early award from the Sloan Foundation was recognized by naming the survey the Sloan Digital Sky Survey. The Sloan Foundation has continued to be a major supporter of all three phases of the SDSS.Ancillary Target (APOGEE)Target observed as part of an [APOGEE](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#APOGEE) ancillary program to utilize the survey's capabilities for interesting science beyond its primary survey goals. However, a small fraction of the fibers are devoted to a variety of additional projects, ranging from studies of variable stars to host galaxies of supernovae. These additional targets are referred to as BOSS Ancillary Targets. A full list of the target flags may be found in the descriptions of [ANCILLARY\_TARGET1](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/algorithms/bitmasks/#ANCILLARY_TARGET1) and [ANCILLARY\_TARGET2](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/algorithms/bitmasks/#ANCILLARY_TARGET2).ANNZA neural network technique for selecting quasars for spectroscopy in the BOSS survey. The algorithm is described in [Yèche et al. 2010](http://adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010A%26A...523A..14Y).APOApache Point Observatory, located in Sunspot, New Mexico. This is the location of the 2.5m SDSS telescope as well as the [Photometric Telescope (PT)](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#photo_tel), as well as other non-SDSS telescopes, such as the ARC 3.5m telescope. See the [APO homepage](http://www.apo.nmsu.edu/).APOGEEThe Apache Point Observatory Galactic Evolution Experiment, one of four surveys included in SDSS-III. This is using a high-resolution (R~22,500) H-band spectrograph fed by 300 fibers. The spectrograph saw first light in 2011, and the survey will observe roughly 100,000 stars, mostly in the disk of the Milky Way, to obtain detailed chemical compositions and kinematics. The first data from APOGEE was released publicly in Data Release 10.APOGEE Stellar Parameters and Chemical Abundances Pipeline (ASPCAP)The software pipeline used by APOGEE to calculate basic stellar parameters (Teff, log(g), \[Fe/H\], \[α/H\]) and other elemental abundances (Garcia Pérez et al., _in prep_.). The pipeline is described in the [ASPCAP](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/irspec/aspcap/) pages of the Infrared Spectra documentation.ARCAstrophysical Research Consortium. Incorporated in the State of Washington. Members include the University of Washington; New Mexico State University; The University of Chicago, Princeton University; Johns Hopkins University; the University of Virginia; the University of Colorado; the Institute for Advanced Study. Body that owns and operates [APO](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#APO).Asinh MagnitudeMagnitudes within SDSS-I/II are expressed as inverse hyperbolic sine (or "asinh") magnitudes, sometimes referred to informally as _luptitudes_. The transformation from linear flux measurements to asinh magnitudes is designed to be virtually identical to the standard astronomical magnitude at high signal-to-noise ratio, but to switch over to linear behavior at low S/N and even at negative values of flux, where the logarithm in the [Pogson magnitude](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#mag_pogson) fails. Details can be found in the [Photometry section](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/algorithms/magnitudes/) of the Algorithms pages, or the [Lupton et al. (1999) AJ paper](http://adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1999AJ....118.1406L).ASPCAPThe [APOGEE Stellar Parameters and Chemical Abundances Pipeline (ASPCAP)](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#aspcap)asTrans fileFITS binary table with astrometric transformations for every field in a single imaging run. It transforms frame (row,col) coordinates to great circle (μ,ν) coordinates for a given inclination. Available in the [SAS](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#SAS).AstroDAThe data acquisition and analysis system used to collect data from the survey telescope cameras.AstromThe data processing pipeline that maps CCD pixel coordinates to celestial coordinates. The detailed workings of this pipeline are described in the [Astrometry](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/algorithms/astrometry/) section of the [algorithms pages](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/algorithms/), or the [Pier et al. (2003) AJ paper](http://adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2003AJ....125.1559P).Astrometric ChipThe SDSS [camera](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#camera) contains 24 2048x400 pixel CCDs (in addition to the 30 2048x2048 photometric CCDs) which were used for astrometry and focus monitoring. Because they are smaller chips, the effective exposure time is only 11 seconds, allowing the survey to observe brighter stars without saturation. These bright star positions were necessary to match to objects present in astrometric catalogs used by the [Astrom pipeline](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#Astrom). They are no longer used for this purpose; soon after SDSS operations started, the UCAC catalog of astrometric standards faint enough not to be saturated in the main SDSS chips became available. For more details on these chips, please see the appropriate section of the [camera page](https://www.sdss4.org/instruments/camera/) or the AJ imaging camera paper ( [Gunn et al. 1998](http://adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1998AJ....116.3040G)).AstrometryThe detailed methodology utilized by the SDSS to do astrometric (positional) calibration is described in the [Astrometry](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/algorithms/astrometry/) section of the [algorithms pages](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/algorithms/).AstrotoolsA software environment in which several of the SDSS pipelines are run, including the image processing pipeline, `photo`.Atlas ImageFor each detected [object](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#object), the atlas image comprises the pixels that were detected as part of the object in any filter. These are available through the [SAS](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#SAS), as a single file for each field.

## B

Binned FrameEach file is a FITS image for one filter, 512 x 372 pixels, with WCS information. These are the [corrected frames](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#corrected_frame) with detected objects removed and binned 4 pixels by 4 pixels. All pixels that are in [atlas images](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#atlas_image) are replaced by the background level before binning, with suitable noise added. All of the header parameters from the original image are inherited as well. Available in the [SAS](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#SAS).Black BookSee [Project Book](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#project_book).BONUS quasarsThe principal science goal of the BOSS quasar sample is to measure the clustering signal in the Lyman-α forest, which does not require that the quasar sample be uniformly selected across the sky. While the [CORE quasars](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#CORE) are uniformly selected, an additional 20 quasars per square degree, the BONUS quasars, are selected using a combination of techniques and available data that is not required to be uniformly selected. See also [CORE quasars](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#CORE).BoresightThe telescope control computer keeps track of a specific point in the telescope focal plane that is called the _boresight_. The boresight is not fixed in the array but is at one of two places for the two [strips](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#strip) that compose a [stripe](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#stripe). It is the boresight that will track [great circles](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#great_circle_coords). Technically, the boresight tracks a path that is a great circle in J2000 coordinates as viewed from the solar system barycenter.BOSSThe Baryon Oscillation Sky Survey, one of the four component surveys of SDSS-III. It is obtaining redshifts of 1.5 million galaxies, and spectra of 150,000 z > 2.2 quasars, to measure the baryon oscillation signal in the correlation function as a geometrical probe of cosmology. It also obtained imaging over roughly 3100 deg2 of the Southern Galactic Cap beyond that in SDSS-I/II. It uses substantially improved spectrographs over those used in SDSS-I and SDSS-II, with more fibers per plate (1000 vs. 640), smaller fiber aperture (2", not 3"), improved throughput, and somewhat wider wavelength coverage.BRIGHT detectionsA technical term in the photometric reductions. The photometric pipeline performs a first-pass detection that searches for very significant objects, which are typically bright stars and galaxy, in preparation for the full object detection algorithm. These objects are measured and included in the final catalog, but never as [survey primary](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#surveyprimary) or [survey secondary](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#surveysecondary) objects. All such objects are redetected and remeasured in the second-pass of the pipeline, and those (non-BRIGHT) detections are the ones to actually use. BRIGHT detections have the BRIGHT bit set in their [photometric flags](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/algorithms/bitmasks/#FLAGS1).

## C

CalibrationThe process by which the photometric and spectroscopic observations are calibrated. The goal of calibration is to take the digital camera readouts and convert them to measured quantities like fluxes. The details of these procedures can be found on the [algorithms pages](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/algorithms/).CamcolA Camcol is the output of one camera column of CCDs (each with a different filter) as part of a [Run](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#run). Therefore, 1 Camcol = 1/6 of a Run. It is also a portion of a [scanline](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#scanline).CameraThe instrument used for imaging in SDSS. It consists of 30 photometric and 24 astrometric CCDs (see the [camera description](https://www.sdss4.org/instruments/camera/)). The camera is a mosaic of 54 CCD detectors in the focal plane, 30 large devices arranged in 6 columns of 5 each and 24 smaller devices around the periphery. See this [sketch of the camera focal plane](https://www.sdss4.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/camassy2.gif) or this [real life image](https://www.sdss4.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/faceplat2.jpg). The camera is described in detail in the imaging camera paper ( [Gunn et al. 1998](http://adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1998AJ....116.3040G)). The last imaging data in SDSS was released in DR8; the camera has now been decommissioned.CAS (Catalog Archive Server)The [Catalog Archive Server](http://skyserver.sdss.org/dr12/) is a searchable database that contains the measured parameters from all objects in the imaging survey and the spectroscopic survey.CasJobsA batch (asynchronous) interface to the [CAS](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#CAS) for querying the database and storing results. CasJobs is designed to handle especially long or otherwise demanding queries.CaveatsAlso known as "known problems." SDSS maintains a list of issues or imperfections of the data and/or its processing. We have a list of [imaging caveats](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/imaging/caveats/), [(optical) spectroscopic caveats](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/spectro/caveats/), and [infrared spectrosopic caveats](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/irspec/caveats/).Central MeridianThe meridian which passes through the center of the survey area in the Northern Galactic Cap, 12h 20m, defines the central meridian of an imaging scan. The great circle perpendicular to it passing through the survey center at δ = 32.8° is the [survey equator](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#survey_equator).ChildA product of the [deblending](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#deblend) process in the image processing. When two objects are near each other on the sky, their images may appear merged. The deblender tries to split this merged image; the resulting sub-images are called children. The initial merged image is called a [parent](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#parent). For more details, see the [Deblending](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/algorithms/deblend/) section of the [algorithms pages](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/algorithms/).Classification (photometric)The method by which we assign a type (star or galaxy) to each [object](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#object) in the SDSS images. The details of this procedure are described in the [Classification](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/algorithms/classify/) section of the [algorithms pages](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/algorithms/); in brief, it is based on the difference between PSF and model magnitudes.Cloud CameraA camera at the mountain that takes continuous pictures of the sky at 10 μm, a wavelength at which clouds emit. It is a sensitive measure of the photometricity of the sky as a function of time. It is described [here](http://irsc.apo.nmsu.edu/irsc_doc/).Cloud Camera StatusThe cloud camera status is built from an analysis of the 10 μm [cloud cameras](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#cloud_cam) used at the APO site. The full history is stored in two files in the [photoop product](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/software/products/):

- opfiles/opCloudcam.par (when was the cloudcam on)
- opfiles/opCloudveto.par (when was it cloudy)

The software generating these files from the raw IRCAM data is in pro/ircam.

In individual files in the reductions the CLOUDCAM flag gets set with the following possible values:

-1 UNKNOWN (cloud camera not on)0 CLOUDY (cloud camera indicates clouds)1 CLEAR (cloud camera indicates clear)CMASSA sample of galaxies targeted for spectroscopic followup in the BOSS survey. It is designed to select luminous galaxies in the redshift range 0.4 < z < 0.8, with a roughly uniform space density of 3 × 10-4 (h/Mpc3) to z ~ 0.6, with a tail to z ~ 0.8. "CMASS" stands for "Constant (stellar) MASS." See also [luminous red galaxy](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#luminous_red_galaxy) and [LOWZ](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#LOWZ).CMMCoordinate Measuring Machine, a device used to measure the positions of holes in the [fiber](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#fiber) mount [plates](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#plate) for the spectrographic survey.Cmodel magnitudeComposite model magnitude. See [magnitude, cmodel](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#mag_cmodel).CohortA set of targets in the same APOGEE field observed together for the same number of [visits](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/glossary/#visit). A given APOGEE plate may have multiple cohorts on it.Color Image PipelineA pipeline to produce 3-color JPG files of zoomed-down SDSS images; these are available from the CAS.ConvexA convex is the intersection of one or more circles, with a depth (the number of circles involved). If we have two intersection circles, A and B, then both (A) and (B) are a convex of depth 1, their intersection (A)(B) is also a convex, but of depth 2. We call these simple convexes "wedges."CoordinatesThe SDSS uses several different coordinate systems. We use standard astronomical right ascension (RA) and declination (Dec), J2000. There is also the _[survey coordinate system](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#survey_coords)_, with coordinates [λ](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#lambda) and [η](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#eta). This is just a rotation of the usual RA, Dec system. There is a variant on this known as [corrected survey coordinates](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#corrected_survey_coords) with different ranges for λ and η. There is the _[great circle coordinate system](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#great_circle_coords)_, which is actually a separate coordinate system for each [stripe](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#stripe). The coordinates of this system are [μ](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#mu) and [ν](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#nu). For APOGEE infrared spectra in Data Release 10 and later, we also use galactic coordinates: galactic longitude (L) and galactic latitude (B). (Note that our convention is to use capital letters to symbolize galactic coordinates to avoid confusion between the lowercase letter l and the number 1). The algorithms pages have [details on all these coordinate systems](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/algorithms/surveycoords).CORE quasarsIn the BOSS survey, 20 quasar targets per square degree are selected for spectroscopy using a spatially uniform technique, called "CORE," allowing science requiring a uniform selection of quasars to be done. The CORE algorithm starting in the second year of BOSS is the "extreme deconvolution" technique, [XDQSO](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#XDQSO) See also [BONUS quasars](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#BONUS).Corrected FrameEach corrected frame is a FITS image for one filter, 2048 columns by 1489 rows, with row number increasing in the scan direction. These are the imaging frames with flat-field, bias, cosmic-ray, and pixel-defect corrections applied, sky subtracted, and photometric calibration applied. A raw image contains 1361 rows, and a corrected frame has the first 128 rows of the following corrected frame appended to it. The pixels subtend 0".396 square on the sky. Header information using the [world coordinate system (WCS)](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#wcs) allows standard astronomical FITS tools to convert pixel position to right ascension and declination. Available in the [SAS](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#SAS).Corrected Survey CoordinatesA variant on [Survey Coordinates](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#survey_coords), for which the range of η is -180 to 180 and the range of λ is -90 to 90. See the algorithms page for [SDSS survey coordinates](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/algorithms/surveycoords).CSV (comma-separated variable/value)Comma Separated Variable (or Comma Separated Value) file. A file format used commonly for input to and output from the [CAS](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#CAS).cx/cy/czThe coordinates on the unit sphere utilized by the [HTM](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#htm) code; stored in the CAS.

## D

DASThe Data Archive Server, which provided access, for SDSS-I/II, to the imaging and spectroscopic products of the survey as flat files. Its functionality is replaced with the Science Archive Server ( [SAS](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#SAS)).Data ModelThe description of the structure and organization of the data in a database. The data model tells you all the table names, their contents, and how they are related to, and linked with, one another. The actual implementation of a data model is called the database schema. For the flat files available in the [SAS](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#SAS), the data models are available [here](http://data.sdss3.org/datamodel/).DeblendDeblending is the process by which overlapping objects in images are separated. The [frames pipeline](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#frames_pipeline) attempts to determine whether each object actually consists of more than one object projected on the sky and, if so, to deblend such a [parent](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#parent) object into its constituent [children](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#child), self-consistently across the bands (thus, all children have measurements in all bands). For details on how this works, and what [flags](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#flag) this procedure may set, see the [Deblending](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/algorithms/deblend/) section of the [algorithms pages](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/algorithms/).Design (APOGEE)A set of APOGEE targets selected together for drilling on a plate or plates; may consist of up to one each of short, medium, and long [cohort](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#cohort) s. A design is identified by an integer [Design ID](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#design_id). Changing a single target on a design results in a new design.Design IDA unique integer assigned to each APOGEE [Design](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#design).de VaucouleursAlso known as the r1/4 law, it describes the radial light profile of a typical elliptical galaxy. Defined as

```
I(r) = I0 exp(-7.67 [r/re]1/4).
```

An elliptical version of this profile is fit to every detected object, yielding the `deV` parameters. See the [model magnitude section](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/algorithms/magnitudes/#mag_model) of the photometry algorithm page for details.

DR1…10Data Release 1 through 10, the formal periodic releases of SDSS data to the public. A small portion of the data was released as part of the [Early Data Release (EDR)](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#EDR). The data releases are cumulative: later releases include more recent data and re-processed older data. DR7 is the final release of SDSS-II data. DR8 is the first release of SDSS-III data, and includes most (although not all) SDSS-I/II data as well. Each Data Release is associated with a journal article that describes changes from the previous release.

## E

EDREarly Data Release. The first public release of SDSS data occurred in June of 2001. The EDR is described in the [EDR paper](http://adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2002AJ....123..485S).EllipticityMeasures how elliptical an object in the imaging data is. In the SDSS, we have numerous methods to measure this:

- the [Stokes parameters](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#stokes)
- the [adaptive moments](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#adaptive_moment)
- fits to [De Vaucouleurs profiles](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#dev)
- fits to [exponential profiles](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#exp)
- the isophotal parameters (discontinued)

eta (η)Latitude in the [survey coordinate system](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#survey_coords). Eta is the angle between the survey equator and the great circle passing through the point perpendicular to the survey meridian, positive to the north. Constant latitude curves are great circles. See the description of [SDSS survey coordinates](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/algorithms/surveycoords).Exponential ModelThe radial light distribution of a disk galaxy can often be fit by an exponential profile:

```
I(r) = I0 exp(-1.68 r/re)
```

The number 1.68 is chosen so that the model radius is a half-light radius. An elliptical version of this profile is fit to every detected object, yielding the `exp` parameters. See the [model magnitude section](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/algorithms/magnitudes/#mag_model) of the photometry algorithm page for details.

ExtinctionWe observe all but the nearest stars through a foreground of dust extinction from our own Milky Way. The CAS tabulates extinction corrections in magnitudes at the position of each object, `reddening`, following [Schlegel, Finkbeiner & Davis (1998)](http://adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1998ApJ...500..525S). For the APOGEE survey, which focuses on the most dust-obscured regions of the Galaxy disk and bulge, see the discussion of extinction in the [Infrared Spectra Targets](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/irspec/targets/) pages, and references therin.Extra PlatesReobservations of main survey plates, useful for variability studies or checks. See the [scope](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/scope/) page for more information.

## G

Galaxy ZooAn international program whereby laypeople from all over the world contribute visual classifications of galaxies, based on SDSS images. These classifications are made available in DR10. More details may be found at their [website](http://www.galaxyzoo.org/).Great Circle CoordinatesOne of the main coordinate systems utilized by the SDSS. In this system, [μ](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#mu) and [ν](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/glossary/#nu) are spherical coordinates (corresponding to ra and dec) in a system whose equator is along the center of the [stripe](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/help/glossary/#stripe) in question. The stripes of the main survey in the Northern Galactic Cap are great circles which all cross at the survey nodes of (RA, Dec) = (95, 0) an (RA, Dec) = (270, 0) degrees.

The scans are defined by their inclination with respect to the equator, and are indexed by integers n such that the inclination of a stripe is -25 + 2.5n.

The stripes of the main survey in the South Galactic cap are great circles with the same nodes, where the indexing is such that the inclination relative to the equator is 2.5(82 - n). The celestial Equator therefore corresponds to stripe number 10 in the Northern Cap and 82 in the Southern Cap. Stripes 1-44 were observed in the North, and stripes 61 to 90 in the South. While the imaging scans of the SEGUE survey are also great circles, they have an assortment of different nodes and are numbered with stripe numbers > 200.

See also the description of [SDSS survey coordinates](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/algorithms/surveycoords).

## I

idlspec2dThe spectroscopic pipeline (also sometimes referred to as Spectro-2D and `specBS`), which reduces the raw 2D spectral frames to extracted 1D calibrated spectra (flux density versus wavelength) and then to redshifts and classifications.The operational goals of the extraction pipeline are to:

01. interpolate over bad pixels
02. mask all bad pixels and pixels contaminated by strong sky emission.
03. bias and dark subtract the raw 2D data frames
04. trim the frame
05. flat-field using calibration frames taken at the same telescope pointing position before and after an exposure on the sky.
06. optimally extract 1D spectra from this 2D frame.
07. apply wavelength calibration, rebin to a common wavelength solution, and sky subtract.
08. coadd the three or more individual exposures for each object.
09. put the red and blue halves of the spectrum together.
10. flux calibrate the spectrum.

Following extraction, the `idlspec2d` pipeline classifies objects and measures redshifts from the Legacy, SEGUE and BOSS surveys based on the calibrated 1-dimensional spectra. It fits spectra to templates in real space, as described in the [redshift fitting algorithms documentation](https://www.sdss4.org/dr12/algorithms/redshifts/). This aspect of the `idlspec2d` has sometimes been referred to as "specBS".

For SDSS-I/II, there was another pipeline, spec1d, which cross-correlated spectra to templates in Fourier space; that pipeline is not being run in SDSS-III.
