## The accepted grammar — nine moves

1. **Introduction first, and it is not optional.** The move that took this session ~40 exchanges to
   land. Establish the stakes before anything technical: what the two competing explanations are,
   why a viewer should care, and the question that separates them. **No disclaimer before it —
   earn the want first.**
2. **Strictly conditional motivation.** Stakes without claims: *"if one **were**… that **would**
   be."* Subjunctive in the narration **and** balanced/unselected in the visuals. A diagram can
   assert what careful wording avoids; Lana checks both channels.
3. **Why counting alone cannot settle it** — the difficulty that makes the design necessary.
4. **The discriminating idea as the PEAK** — spin's is the mirror; each lane has its own. Most
   narrative weight, real animation, the emotional centre. Not an item in a list.
5. **The frozen sample**, animated, counts attached to labelled stages. Never a giant number card.
6. **The estimator built progressively**, with the value explicitly **withheld** and the sign
   never stated without the withholding in the same breath.
7. **Predeclared controls as a matrix** — each control and the failure mode it tests. Design only,
   no outcomes.
8. **Gates as discipline, not a ticketing system.** *"We tied our own hands so the answer cannot be
   shaped by choices made after seeing it."* Never "the record is absent / stages remain open".
9. **Close by re-posing the opening question**, now armed. Land on the science, not a workflow.
