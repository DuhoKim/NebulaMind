## The two reasons, kept apart (this separation IS the deliverable)

- **BROAD — why anyone should care whether spirals have a preferred handedness.** Because it is a
  galaxy-scale test of a cornerstone assumption of cosmology: that the universe is the same in every
  direction and has no built-in left/right preference. Standard structure formation predicts the two
  handednesses should be **balanced with no preferred axis**; a genuine preference would be a departure
  from isotropy and mirror-symmetry — new physics, not a detail.
- **NARROW — why a mirror control is needed.** Because the humans and algorithms that sort spirals carry
  a demonstrated left/right bias, so an *apparent* excess could be a fact about the sorters, not the sky.
  The mirror flip discriminates the two. **This is a property of the measurement, not a reason the
  subject matters.** Conflating the two is the failure being corrected.

## Why a naive conference viewer should care — three source-grounded legs

1. **The cosmological principle.** The standard model assumes the universe is, averaged over large scales
   (≳100 Mpc), statistically the same in every direction — homogeneous and isotropic, with **no preferred
   axis and no preferred handedness**. This is foundational, not decorative.
2. **Standard structure formation predicts no preferred handedness.** Galaxies acquire their spin from the
   tidal gravitational fields of surrounding structure — **tidal-torque theory**, Hoyle (1949),
   Peebles (1969), White (1984) — a process with no built-in chirality. So the expectation is
   **N(clockwise) ≈ N(anticlockwise), with no preferred direction on the sky.** That expectation is what
   makes the test meaningful: a clean null to violate.
3. **A preferred handedness would matter, and some models even predict one.** Longo (2011): *"A preference
   for spiral galaxies in one sector of the sky to be left-handed or right-handed spirals would indicate a
   parity violating asymmetry in the overall universe and a preferred axis."* Separately, some inflationary
   models predict a faint **primordial parity violation** that galaxy spins might carry — an active,
   mainstream probe (see sources). So galaxy handedness is a direct window on whether the cosmos is
   mirror-symmetric on its largest scales.

**The one-sentence stakes a viewer should be able to repeat back:** *"They're checking whether the
universe has a preferred direction of spin — which, if it's the same in all directions, it shouldn't — and
that's hard because the people who sort the pictures have a left-right bias."* The first clause is the
broad reason; the last clause is the narrow one. The current video only has the last.

## What is genuinely open
The observational question is **unsettled and actively contested**, which is exactly why it is worth doing:
- **Signal claimed:** Longo (2011) reports a dipole in handedness (z~0.04); Shamir (2012+) reports a
  handedness asymmetry / dipole axis at z<0.3. These are **claims, disputed**, not consensus.
- **Consistent with isotropy once bias is removed:** Land et al. (2008, Galaxy Zoo 1) — after establishing
  and correcting a large human left/right classification bias — find the winding sense **consistent with
  statistical isotropy, no significant preferred handedness**; Mcadam et al. (2023) reanalysis concurs.
- **No consensus exists.** The disagreement is driven substantially by the bias-control problem, which is
  precisely what this method addresses. That is the honest bridge from the broad stakes to the method —
  *not* a substitute for the stakes.

## Primary sources (quoted / cited — verified today against the source, not memory)
- **Longo 2011**, *Detection of a Dipole in the Handedness of Spiral Galaxies with Redshifts z~0.04*,
  Phys. Lett. B **699**, 224; arXiv:1104.2815 — verbatim: *"A preference for spiral galaxies in one sector
  of the sky to be left-handed or right-handed spirals would indicate a parity violating asymmetry in the
  overall universe and a preferred axis."* (conditional "would indicate" — quote this framing, not the
  claimed detection).
- **Land, Slosar, Lintott et al. 2008**, *Galaxy Zoo: the large-scale spin statistics of spiral galaxies
  in the SDSS*, MNRAS **388**, 1686; arXiv:0803.3247 — the lane's own `data: Galaxy Zoo 1` source. Key
  facts (verified): demonstrated a large human left/right bias; proved it by having volunteers re-classify
  a subset with images **randomly left-right flipped (50%)**; after correction found results **consistent
  with isotropy, no significant dipole**. This paper is simultaneously the motivation *and* the origin of
  the mirror control — cite it for both, but for *different* sentences.
- **Tidal-torque theory** (angular-momentum origin): Hoyle (1949); Peebles (1969), ApJ 155, 393; White
  (1984), ApJ 286, 38. Established mechanism; predicts no preferred handedness.
- **Cosmological principle / large-scale isotropy:** standard-model assumption; Planck isotropy tests are
  consistent with it at the level relevant here (note: known ~3σ CMB hemispherical-asymmetry anomalies
  exist but must **not** be chained to galaxy spin as a driver — see trap below).
- **Primordial parity violation as a galaxy-spin probe** (the legitimate "exciting" motivation): e.g.
  *Enhancing the Detection Sensitivity of Primordial Parity Violation using Galaxy Spins* (arXiv:2606.19709);
  APS Physics synopsis *Cosmic Handedness Might Show Up in Galaxy Spins* (physics.aps.org/articles/v18/s126).
  Present as a **model prediction / motivation for upcoming surveys**, conditional, never as observed.

## Claim boundary — sentence level

**ALLOWED (conditional framework / established theory / attributed open question — no result):**
- State the cosmological principle as an *assumption/expectation*: "the universe is assumed the same in
  every direction, with no preferred axis or handedness."
- State tidal-torque origin as *established theory*: "galaxies get their spin from surrounding tidal
  fields — a process with no built-in preference for clockwise or anticlockwise."
- State the *null expectation*: "so the two handednesses are expected to be equal, evenly spread across
  the sky."
- Use **conditional** for the stakes: "**if** one handedness **were** genuinely more common across the
  sky, it **would** signal a departure from that symmetry — a parity-violating asymmetry and a preferred
  axis" (Longo's "would indicate").
- Report the open question **as contested and attributed**: "some analyses report such a signal; others,
  once the sorting bias is removed, find none — it is unsettled." (Attribution/meta-statement, not the
  video's own claim.)
- State the narrow reason with attribution: "the people and algorithms that sort spirals carry a
  left/right bias (shown directly by re-sorting mirror-flipped images), so an apparent excess could be a
  fact about the sorters" (Land et al. 2008).

**FORBIDDEN (result claims — hard block):**
- Any assertion that an asymmetry / excess / preferred direction / dipole / preferred axis **exists** —
  in the sky, in the literature-as-settled, or (above all) in **this study's** data.
- "Parity is violated," "the universe is anisotropic," "clockwise dominates," any **significance / p-value
  / amplitude / axis** presented as a finding of this study.
- Adopting Longo's or Shamir's contested claims **as true** (they may be cited only as *claimed and
  disputed*).
- Presenting Land's null **as the settled answer** (cite as one side of an open question, not the verdict).
- **Black-hole-universe** as a scientific driver (see trap).
- Any sentence that makes the broad reason and the narrow reason the *same* reason.

**The distinction, as a boundary rule:** a motivation sentence is *broad* only if it would still be worth
saying even if human bias did not exist. "Is the universe mirror-symmetric on the largest scales?" passes.
"An apparent excess could be the sorters, not the sky" does not — that sentence only exists because bias
exists, so it is narrow/method and may not stand in for the stakes.
