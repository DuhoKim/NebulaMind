## Current-opening diagnosis

The current opening begins with whether a published bright-end summary lacks a reconstructible object record because data are missing or the search missed them. That is a clear retrieval problem, but it does not first explain why the bright end of the early-galaxy population is scientifically consequential.

## Broad stake to establish first

The brightest galaxies at cosmic dawn are rare early systems. Their abundance is relevant because it constrains how rapidly galaxies and their host halos could assemble, as well as early star-formation efficiency, dust, and feedback. A departure from an explicitly stated model expectation would matter, but this lane cannot assert that such a departure exists.

## Result trap

Never say or imply:

- JWST found too many bright early galaxies;
- an excess, tension, or number-density discrepancy exists;
- any count, observed point, rank, or abundance has a particular value;
- Lambda-CDM has failed or is ruled out;
- this archive census confirms either a signal or a null.

The conditional stake is permitted: if bright early galaxies were more abundant than a declared model expected, early galaxy-growth assumptions would need reassessment. Any literature claim must be attributed as reported and contested.

## Planned opening beats — semantic plan, not narration

1. **Cosmic-dawn context:** establish the first few hundred million years as an early growth epoch using a symbolic time band, not a calibrated data axis or plotted object.
2. **Why the bright end matters:** identify rare luminous systems as probes of how quickly early galaxies could assemble; use equal symbolic populations with no counts.
3. **Conditional model test:** place `MODEL EXPECTATION` and `POSSIBLE OBSERVATION` as non-positional, equal cards; explain only that a genuine disagreement would be consequential. Show `NO COMPARISON REPORTED`.
4. **Open and contested question:** if used, attribute that excess claims have been reported and debated; do not show points, densities, significance, or a selected verdict.
5. **Narrow seam:** explain that rare-object claims require reconstructible object-level records and careful contamination/redshift control; transition to the existing missing-data versus missed-data archive test.
