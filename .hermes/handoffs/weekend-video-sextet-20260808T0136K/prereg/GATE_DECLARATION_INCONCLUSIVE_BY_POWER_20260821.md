### 2. BLOCKING — refusing the literal HC-6 reading is exactly the post-freeze discretion preregistration is meant to remove

The declaration presents “literal-algorithm” and “scientific” readings and deliberately refuses the former after discovering that it can PASS (lines 51–69). The strongest defense is that the literal calculation certifies the wrong scientific property. That defense succeeds scientifically and fails procedurally.

The frozen text does not name accepted-sample `Var(c)`, `SSE`, or a footprint-aware noncentrality as HC-6 inputs. It instead names the pinned harness and “the same analytical method.” The current mismatch is a defect in the frozen design, not permission to reinterpret the design after freeze. Choosing the scientifically preferable reading only after the footprint defect is known is precisely choosing between decision rules after the fact.

The direction of the choice does not rescue it. Preregistration discipline does not contain a “conservative outcome” exception, and avoidance of REPRODUCED-LONGO is not proof that the choice is motive-free. A biased actor could also prefer to avoid a costly, embarrassing, or disconfirming run. Outcome direction is therefore irrelevant to whether the frozen procedure was followed.

The alternative posed in the gate brief—recording the literal HC-6 PASS and declaring INCONCLUSIVE on other grounds—is not established by these inputs either. F-6 defines INCONCLUSIVE as a numeric residual outcome or a triggered rule in section 4/section 6 (preregistration lines 141–145). The declaration does not identify a frozen section 4/section 6 trigger authorizing a pre-run INCONCLUSIVE. This observation is an adjudication of the available text, not a proposed remedy.
