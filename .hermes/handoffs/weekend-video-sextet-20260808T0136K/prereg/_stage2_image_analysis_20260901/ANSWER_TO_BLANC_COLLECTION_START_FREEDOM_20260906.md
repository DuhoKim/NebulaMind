# Answer to Blanc's question (12:51 KST): is "no operator freedom over WHEN collection starts" achievable, and checkable by a third party without trusting us?

**Short answer: yes, but not by forcing us to act at an instant — by making the RESULT independent of the instant.** No mechanism can prove to a third party that we ran a program at time T. A mechanism CAN make the seed and the source a pure function of PUBLIC ARCHIVED BYTES plus a pinned root, so that WHEN we look changes nothing a third party would compute differently. Then the only operator freedoms left are DELAY and ABANDONMENT, both visible (a signed rule with a passed T_pulse and no filed identity is a public event), neither of which can select a seed or a source.

## Why the timing lever exists in V18 as written
V18 decides the source at build time from a LIVE fetch. Codex's counterexample simulated NIST "recovering" between two builds: unauthenticable at the first collection, authenticable at the second. If that transition can happen for a FIXED T_pulse, then choosing when to build chooses the source, and no log of ours proves we did not wait for the better one.

## Why that transition cannot happen for archived NIST bytes under the rule's own checks
The binding NIST inputs for a fixed T_pulse are (i) the pulse body served for that timestamp, (ii) the certificate named by the pulse's `certificateId`, (iii) the issuer chain, (iv) the next pulse. Under the rule: `certificate_id_is_sha512_of_leaf_der` FIXES the certificate bytes by their ID — NIST cannot serve a different certificate for the same ID without failing that check; the pulse body carries its own signature and `outputValue = SHA-512(message ‖ signature)` and is chained into the next pulse — NIST rewriting an archived pulse would break its own chain and our `precommitment_links_to_next`; the issuer chain is checked by anchoring to the PINNED root. Therefore "authenticable for T_pulse" is a property of bytes that are fixed once published, not of the moment we fetch them. A third party recomputes it from NIST's archive and the pinned root at any later date. The live-equality rule already REFUSES (NIST-LIVE-DIFFERS) if served bytes ever differ from retained bytes — that is the archive-integrity check, and it is exactly what fired in codex's simulation the moment the mock changed the served pulse. What CAN vary with time is AVAILABILITY (server down, transient errors): under V18 that is RETRY, never a seed, never a source decision — a delay only.

The drand side is stronger still: round_for(T_pulse) is a fixed round; its randomness is a BLS signature over the round number that ANY third party can verify against the chain's public key from the chain's own archive, with no trust in us or in the four relays. We do not verify BLS (disclosed); a checker can.

## So what does V19 have to say and do (if released)
1. State the property: the source decision is a function f(archived public bytes for T_pulse, pinned root) — recomputable by anyone, independent of collection time; NIST is binding iff the archived pulse authenticates; otherwise the fixed drand round. Collection time affects only whether a verdict is available yet.
2. Make the code honour it: the fallback opens only on the served, live-equal, T_pulse-timestamped pulse failing authentication (codex's item 2); a live fetch that raises or a different served pulse is RETRY; ACCEPT-NIST requires live equality. Remove any path where availability alone changes the outcome.
3. Downgrade the collection log from "the lock that removes freedom" to what it is: a record of what we did and when — useful, committed, verified by the driver, but not the guarantee. The guarantee is (1).
4. Bound the residual honestly: what remains attested is that NIST's archive and the drand chain are not rewritten in collusion with us — a public-infrastructure assumption, checkable by anyone who retains copies; and that we did not abandon after seeing a result we disliked — visible, because abandonment closes the commitment with the record on file.

## What is NOT achievable, plainly
Proof that we ran at T; proof that we did not compute the result privately before filing (a local pre-run learns the outcome but cannot change it — V15 already says so); removal of the delay-or-abandon freedom. If Duho wants the expedite only on the condition that timing freedom is REMOVED rather than made IRRELEVANT, that condition cannot be met and the expedite should be abandoned. My recommendation is (1)–(4): ship with the limit disclosed.
