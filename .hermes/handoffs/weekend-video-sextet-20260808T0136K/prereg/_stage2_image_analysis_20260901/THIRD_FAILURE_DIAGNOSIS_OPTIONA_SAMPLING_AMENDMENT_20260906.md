**What the property actually is, now stated correctly (for Duho):** the source decision is a function of archived bytes only for the inputs that are SELF-AUTHENTICATING — the NIST pulse body (carries its own signature), the certificate (its bytes fixed by the certificateId = SHA-512 of its DER), and the pinned root. For those, "authenticable for T_pulse" is a property of published bytes and is recomputable by anyone. It is NOT a function of archived bytes for the NEXT-PULSE link and the AIA-served issuer chain: those are served responses without a content address; V19's fallback opened when either failed, so an early collection could take drand where a later first collection would take NIST (codex executed it). The lane's own retained record only proves what it fetched, not what a third party would have fetched first.
**What would make the property true (a design decision for Duho, NOT drafted under the cap):** narrow the fallback predicate to the self-authenticating failures only — the fallback opens iff the T_pulse pulse's SIGNATURE does not verify under the content-addressed certificate anchored to the pinned root (the observed NIST condition today); a next-link mismatch, an unresolvable AIA chain, an unavailable endpoint, or any exception is RETRY, never a fallback trigger. Under that predicate the decision depends on (pulse body, certificate bytes, pinned root) alone — inputs fixed by content — and the first-collection time cannot choose the source. Cost: if NIST serves a pulse whose signature verifies but whose next link never becomes consistent, the rule waits (RETRY) rather than falling back — a delay, visible, never a seed.
