# CODEX referee report — proposed replacement §6, fourth pass

## Verdict

**NOT CLEAR.** The R4 table closes the original access finding at the normative level: it bans access by every person and process, makes the table the sole pre-unblinding exception surface, and lets the committee path operate without voiding a conforming run. The lock is also no longer self-dependent, and the opening authorization, final unblinding checkpoint, archive transition, BS-5f export, and P7 void boundary are materially better specified. Four mechanisms still fail the brief's no-renaming test. Most importantly, the acceptance projection removes named sign fields but is produced after inference by the same process that sees the sign and retains several writable per-object channels; no construction prevents those fields from encoding or deriving from handedness. The predecessor archive remains outside the mediated log, so prospective unauthorized reads can satisfy every stated checkpoint. The claimed complete automation inventory omits the explicitly automated archive checker. Finally, the χ-bearing cutout-completion receipt still has no fields or verifier even though Part 5 declares its authenticated read repaired.

## Numbered findings

### 1. BLOCKING — the acceptance-evidence projection is not sign-blind by construction

**Clause / table row at issue.** Scope lines 29–56; rows D and E; clauses 1–2; Part 2 items 5 and 7; Part 5 finding 3; current §2.7(3) and §2.7(5).

**Why it fails as a promise.** R4 prevents row E from receiving the serialized measurement receipt, which is necessary but not sufficient. Row D sees the full instrument result and then writes the separate projection. Its allowed fields include attempt identity, actual checksum, execution/non-finite status, and the confidence quantity. The draft names no deterministic projector, no canonical derivation of those fields from inputs that exclude handedness, and no verifier that checks those semantics. A conforming row-D implementation can therefore encode the sign in an attempt identifier or status choice while still emitting a schema with no field named `sign`; more directly, the confidence value is itself a per-object instrument output that current §2.7 calls outcome-adjacent and requires to be shown unable to read or derive from handedness, sign, or amplitude. Saying that the BS-2a gate “proves sign/amplitude are structurally unreachable” does not supply the construction that it would prove. The schema prevents an explicit sign field, not an outcome channel.

**Smallest sufficient repair.** At BS-2a, pin a named projection constructor and verifier, their digests, a canonical field serialization, and fixtures that vary handedness/sign/amplitude while holding every admissible projection input fixed. Define each field's source: attempt identity from a pre-inference attempt manifest; expected checksum/shape from frozen inputs; actual checksum/shape from cutout bytes; completion/non-finite state from a mechanically defined execution state; and confidence from a pinned function proven not to consume or derive from handedness/sign/amplitude. Row D must not freely author these fields after seeing the result: the pinned constructor must produce them, and row E must refuse any projection that the verifier cannot reproduce. If the confidence function cannot meet that information-flow contract, reason (d) must be refused rather than relabeled sign-blind.

### 2. BLOCKING — predecessor-archive access is forbidden but not prospectively detectable or mediated

**Clause / table row at issue.** Scope lines 58–68; rows A, B, Q, and R; clauses 1, 4, 5, and 7; §6.2; Part 4 R1.

**Why it fails as a promise.** The archive is expressly χ-bearing, but row B's enforceable mediator and append-only access log cover only the two new stores. The predecessor archive keeps an “existing holder roster,” and no clause removes raw read paths, puts those holders behind the mediator, or requires every archive read/refusal to append to an authenticated log. Rows A and Q read only self-reported non-content metadata. Clause 7 then checks identity and intact-state equality. A holder can read or copy archive bytes without changing those metadata fields or the declared seal state, so BS-2k, BS-2f, and BS-L can all compare equal while the universal access covenant has been violated. Part 4 R1 discloses the retrospective gap, but this is a prospective gap after BS-2k: the brief requires violations to be detectable, not merely forbidden.

The transition rule also leaves “monotonic event/epoch data” without an executable relation: it does not say whether equality or increase is required, how the predecessor-digest field is authenticated against the immediately prior receipt, or what event proves that a read necessarily changes state. Thus the new comparison mechanism does not establish the property it is used to certify.

**Smallest sufficient repair.** Either (a) bring the archive under a pinned mediator and append-only access log at BS-2k, remove every raw holder/read path, and bind that log's checkpoints into BS-2f and BS-L; or (b) define and gate a cryptographic/physical seal mechanism under which every successful or refused content access necessarily produces an authenticated monotonic event. Pin the archive-checker/verifier symbol, define the exact predecessor and monotonic predicates, and make BS-2k unfillable if the existing archive cannot enforce them. If neither mechanism exists, refuse prospective archive custody rather than claiming seal-state equality detects access. The honestly disclosed pre-BS-2k retrospective question may remain open separately.

### 3. BLOCKING — the table's “complete” automation inventory omits its own automated archive checker

**Clause / table row at issue.** Row Q; clause 2; Part 2 item 7; Part 3 C1.

**Why it fails as a promise.** Row Q is explicitly an “automated gate inspector,” but it has no code symbol or digest and names no class-P slot that will pin one. Clause 2 says every automation row is identified by its pinned symbol, then says all existing automation is exactly F, I, J, P, and the receipt envelope, and all future automation is B/O, C/D, E, and G/H. Q appears in neither inventory. Part 2's atomic code-side list likewise omits the archive checker. Consequently the archive state can be inspected and receipted by an execution-time implementation that the freeze never identified, directly contradicting requirements 5 and 8 and the draft's own closed-table promise.

**Smallest sufficient repair.** Give row Q one named checker symbol and verifier symbol (or one callable that performs both), pin their digests in BS-2k, add them to clause 2's future-work enumeration and Part 2's atomic code revision, and make BS-2f and `verify_lock()` refuse archive receipts not produced and authenticated by those bytes. Sweep the other process rows while making this edit; if the lock canonicalizer in row N is automated rather than a manually specified signing ceremony, it also needs a named, pinned serialization symbol.

### 4. BLOCKING — the cutout-completion receipt is named and readable but still has no contract or verifier

**Clause / table row at issue.** Scope lines 50–56; rows C, D, and E; clause 2; Part 5 finding 5.

**Why it fails as a promise.** Row C emits “the χ-bearing cutout-completion receipt,” and row D is allowed to read it. No clause defines that receipt's fields, canonical schema, authentication, completeness predicate, or verifier. BS-9 pins the C/D runner, but the draft never says the runner contains or invokes a completion-receipt verifier. A zero-byte token named `complete`, a receipt over a strict subset of parents, and a receipt with mismatched cutout digests all satisfy the prose. Part 5 nevertheless says the receipt's “authenticated read” was added to both D and E. In Part 1, E's exact surface says it reads **only** projections and fixed parent lists; the completion receipt appears only in E's authorization column, not its read/verification surface. That Part 5 claim is therefore false on the delivered bytes.

**Smallest sufficient repair.** At BS-9, define the cutout-completion receipt's exact in-store schema and pin a named verifier. At minimum it must bind the fixed parent manifest, exact one-per-parent closure/accounting, expected and actual cutout checksum/shape evidence, producer identity, and the C-run identity without exporting a digest forbidden by the opaque-digest rule. Require row D to authenticate and verify it before inference and halt on any missing, duplicate, extra, or mismatched parent. For row E, choose one coherent mechanism: either authorize an authenticated in-store verification and add it to E's exact read surface, or remove the receipt from E's authorization and prove complete one-per-parent projections against the independently fixed parent list. Do not claim both.

## Checks that held

1. **Central access attack failed at the normative level.** The table preamble, clauses 1–2, and row R bind every person and process; no named-holder or powerful-role carve-out reappears. Clause 5 preserves only within-row, within-surface, post-authorization acts.
2. **Committee end-to-end attack failed.** Rows G → H → I authorize allocated cutout views, constrain label entry to the pinned interface, keep the χ-bearing labels and label-set receipt inside the committee store, and permit row I to emit only BS-8f aggregates. A conforming committee act survives the void rule.
3. **Opening-authorization renaming attack failed.** Clause 6 now enumerates a body and signature envelope bound to BS-L, both stores, destination, one-use ceremony identifier, phase, signer, and schema; row O authenticates and consumes it and emits the named unblinding receipt. Missing/replayed authorization refuses unsealing.
4. **Final-checkpoint renaming attack failed.** Rows B and O plus clause 4 put the post-unblinding terminal chain checkpoint in the unblinding receipt and require the chain to extend the BS-L checkpoint through issuance, opening, and unsealing.
5. **Lock self-dependence attack failed.** BS-L is class E, follows BS-5f, certifies no set containing itself, uses a canonical body plus detached signature, and blocks unblinding rather than doubling as BS-V.
6. **Void-window attack failed.** Clause 5 and rows K, L, and M now use the pre-unblinding boundary, closing the BS-L-to-P7 gap while preserving the authorized ceremony operations.
7. **Cross-section edit-list attack mostly failed.** Part 2 moves BS-L to class E, adds class-P BS-2k, updates the class-P and DESIGN inventories, names the unblinding receipt, and requires §5's BS-L/unblinding/ledger guards. Applying those edits would produce 14 class-P and 7 class-E rows. Finding 3 is the remaining atomic-revision omission.
8. **BS-5f export attack failed in substance.** R4 adds BS-5f to the permitted aggregate surface and limits it to a Stage-C result rather than per-object labels. As a drafting nit, “the permitted Stage-C scalar output” should be replaced by the current exact field name(s), but this does not independently drive the verdict above.
