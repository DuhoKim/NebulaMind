| ## [![[NRAO]](https://www.cv.nrao.edu/icons/nraologo_ahb.jpg)](http://www.nrao.edu/)  The NRAO VLA Sky Survey ![stars](https://www.cv.nrao.edu/nvss/picture.gif)<br>* * *<br>J. J. Condon, W. D. Cotton, E. W. Greisen, and Q. F. Yin <br>_National Radio Astronomy Observatory, 520 Edgemont Road,_<br>_Charlottesville, VA 22903_<br>R. A. Perley and G. B. Taylor<br>_National Radio Astronomy Observatory,_<br>_P.O. Box 0, Socorro, NM 87801_<br>J. J. Broderick<br>_Physics Department, Virginia Polytechnic Institute and State_<br>_University, Blacksburg, VA 24061_<br>* * *<br>The NRAO VLA Sky Survey (NVSS) is a 1.4 GHz continuum survey covering<br>the entire sky north of -40 deg declination. A detailed description<br>appears in the 1998 May issue of The Astronomical Journal (Condon,<br>J. J., Cotton, W. D., Greisen, E. W., Yin, Q. F., Perley, R. A.,<br>Taylor, G. B., & Broderick, J. J. 1998, AJ, 115, 1693).<br>The principal NVSS data products are:<br>- A set of 2326 continuum image \`\`cubes,'' each covering 4 deg X 4 deg<br>   with three planes containing the Stokes I, Q, and U images. These images<br>   were made with a relatively large restoring beam (45 arcsec FWHM) to<br>   yield the high surface-brightness sensitivity needed for completeness<br>   and photometric accuracy. Their rms brightness fluctuations are<br>   about 0.45 mJy/beam = 0.14 K (Stokes I) and 0.29 mJy/beam = 0.09 K<br>   (Stokes Q and U). The rms uncertainties in right ascension and<br>   declination vary from < 1 arcsec for relatively strong (S > 15 mJy) point<br>   sources to 7 arcsec for the faintest (S = 2.3 mJy) detectable<br>   sources. The completeness limit is about 2.5 mJy.<br>  <br>- A catalog of discrete sources on these images (over 1.8 million<br>   sources in the entire survey).<br>  <br>- Processed _(u,v)_ data sets. Every large image was constructed<br>   from more than 100 smaller "snapshot" images. All of the edited and<br>   calibrated single-source _(u,v)_ data sets used to make the<br>   snapshot images contributing to each large image have been combined into<br>   a single multisource _(u,v)_ file for users who want to<br>   investigate the data underlying the images.<br>  <br>The NVSS has been made as a service to the astronomical community,<br>and the principal data products were released by [**anonymous**\<br>**FTP**](https://www.cv.nrao.edu/nvss/anonftp.shtml) as soon as they were produced and verified.<br>Users should read the postscript [**paper**](https://www.cv.nrao.edu/nvss/paper.ps)<br>(about 4.7 megabytes) or the gzip [**compressed**\<br>**paper**](https://www.cv.nrao.edu/nvss/paper.ps.gz) (about 1.2 megabytes) for an overview of the<br>NVSS. If you use the NVSS, please reference it as:<br>Condon, J. J., Cotton, W. D., Greisen, E. W., <br>Yin, Q. F., Perley, R. A., Taylor, G. B., & Broderick, J. J. 1998,<br>AJ, 115, 1693. <br>The sky positions of the large NVSS images are shown in the [**image grid**\<br>**plot**](https://www.cv.nrao.edu/nvss/bmdone.eps). These images are in FITS format and can be read by<br>the major astronomical software packages (AIPS, IRAF, etc.) as well<br>as by a stand-alone [**FITS**\<br>**viewer**](http://www.nrao.edu/software/fitsview/) for Windows 3.1, Windows 95, Macintosh, and<br>Unix/X-windows systems. The full 4 deg X 4 deg FITS image cubes are<br>available via [**anonymous**\<br>**FTP**](https://www.cv.nrao.edu/nvss/anonftp.shtml). You may also view (as either gray-scale images or<br>contour plots) and/or copy selected subimages using our [**postage stamp server**](https://www.cv.nrao.edu/nvss/postage.shtml). <br>We extracted a source catalog from each image by fitting elliptical<br>Gaussians to all significant peaks. The individual catalogs have been<br>merged into the master catalog of sources from all images. See the [**catalog**\<br>**description**](https://www.cv.nrao.edu/nvss/catalog.ps) for details about the cataloged source<br>parameters. An [**NVSS catalog browser**](https://www.cv.nrao.edu/nvss/NVSSlist.shtml) is available, but please exercise caution (don't ask for<br>a catalog of the whole sky!). The user program [**NVSSlist**](https://www.cv.nrao.edu/nvss/README.CATALOG.txt)<br>can display selected portions of the catalog. Both the catalog<br>browser and NVSSlist correct the raw catalog for known biases and<br>computes errors associated with the source model parameters (position,<br>flux density, etc.) using equations derived in Condon, J. J. 1997,<br>PASP, 109, 166. To interpret the catalog results in complex or<br>crowded fields, users should look at contour plots (available on the<br>[**postage stamp server**](https://www.cv.nrao.edu/nvss/postage.shtml))<br>showing the actual brightness distributions from which sources were<br>extracted. <br>Image flux densities at specified positions in NVSS images may be<br>obtained [**here**](https://www.cv.nrao.edu/nvss/NVSSPoint.shtml).<br>Users of the AIPS task IMAGR often need to image extra fields covering<br>nearby confusing sources. A [**RUN file**\<br>**generator**](https://www.cv.nrao.edu/nvss/NVSSAIPS.shtml) produces the required list of field offsets for<br>NVSS sources whose flux densities, attenuated by the primary beam, exceed<br>a chosen cutoff. <br>To avoid ambiguity, please refer to NVSS sources using the standard IAU<br>format NVSS EHHMMSS+DDMMSS, with E = J for equinox J2000 or E = B for<br>equinox B1950 coordinates, HHMMSS the hours, minutes, and truncated (not<br>rounded) seconds of right ascension, the declination sign (+ or -), and<br>DDMMSS the degrees, minutes, and seconds (truncated, not rounded) of<br>declination. Thus the NVSS source at J2000 RA = 00 00 00.24, DEC = -20 04<br>49.2 (= B1950 RA = 23 57 26.34, DEC = -20 21 31.5) should be called NVSS<br>J000000-200449 or NVSS B235726-202131.<br>* * *<br>A [mirror](http://www.mrao.cam.ac.uk/facilities/surveys/nvss/)<br>of the NVSS site is maintained by the University of Cambridge.<br>The NASA/IPAC<br>Extragalactic Database [**NED**](http://nedwww.ipac.caltech.edu/) also provides NVSS subimages centered on all database<br>objects north of -40 deg declination.<br>A [German](http://uhrenstore.de/blog/nraovlaskyuntersuchung)<br>version of this site was provided<br>by [A. Romanova](http://uhrenstore.de/blog/)<br>Several hundred thousand identifications of NVSS sources with<br>optical objects in the APM or USNO-A catalogs are listed in the [**QORG catalog**](http://vizier.u-strasbg.fr/viz-bin/ftp-index?J/A+A/427/387) (Flesch & Hardcastle 2004, A&A, 427, 387).<br>* * *<br>Other large-scale radio surveys which may be of interest include:<br>- The ongoing VLA Low-frequency Sky Survey ( [**VLSS**](http://lwa.nrl.navy.mil/VLSS/)) will<br>  ultimately cover the sky north of -30 deg declination at 74 MHz with<br>  80 arcsec resolution and an rms noise of about 100 mJy.<br>  <br>- Source catalogs from the Cambridge [**8C**](http://www.mrao.cam.ac.uk/surveys/8C/index.html) survey <br>  covering the polar cap above +60 deg declination at 38 MHz<br>  with 4.5 arcmin X 4.5 arcmin \* cosec (dec) resolution,<br>  the [**6C**](http://www.mrao.cam.ac.uk/surveys/6C/index.html) survey <br>  covering most of the extragalactic sky above +30 deg declination at 151 MHz<br>  with 4.2 arcmin X 4.2 arcmin \* cosec (dec) resolution, and the [**7C**](http://www.mrao.cam.ac.uk/surveys/7C/index.html) survey <br>  covering most of the extragalactic sky above +20 deg declination at 151 MHz<br>  with 70 arcsec X 70 arcsec \* cosec (dec) resolution.<br>  <br>- The Westerbork Northern Sky Survey ( [**WENSS**](http://www.strw.leidenuniv.nl/wenss/))<br>  covering the 3.14 sr north of +30 deg declination at 326 MHz with 54<br>  arcsec X 54 arcsec \* cosec (dec) resolution in total intensity and<br>  linear polarization. Two catalogs give parameters for about 230,000<br>  sources stronger than 18 mJy. Data from the WENSS CDROM have been<br>  copied to a disk in Charlottesville, where they are available via<br>  anonymous [**FTP**](http://www.cv.nrao.edu/~jcondon/wenssftp.html).<br>  <br>- The Sydney University/Molonglo Sky Survey ( [**SUMSS**](http://www.astrop.physics.usyd.edu.au/SUMSS/index.html)) 843 MHz survey will eventually<br>  cover the sky south of -30 deg declination with 43 arcsec X<br>  43 arcsec \* cosec (dec) resolution and about 1 mJy/beam rms noise. <br>  <br>- The VLA high-resolution (5 arcsec FWHM) 1400 MHz survey covering<br>  the north Galactic cap. The [**FIRST**](http://sundog.stsci.edu/top.html)<br>  survey (for Faint Images of the Radio Sky at Twenty-cm) yields very<br>  accurate (<1 arcsec rms) radio positions of faint (>1 mJy/beam)<br>  compact sources. <br>  <br>- The Green Bank [**GB6**](http://www.cv.nrao.edu/~jcondon/gb6ftp.html) survey covering the 6.1-sr declination<br>  band between 0 deg and +75 deg at 4850 MHz with 3 arcmin resolution.<br>  The GB6 catalog contains about 75,000 sources stronger than 18 mJy. <br>  <br>- The corresponding 4850 MHz survey in the southern hemisphere<br>  is the Parkes-MIT-NRAO ( [**PMN**](http://www.parkes.atnf.csiro.au/research/surveys/pmn/pmn.html)) survey made with the Parkes 64 m<br>  telescope. Both source catalogs and FITS-format sky images<br>  are available.<br>  <br>* * *<br>For more information about the NVSS, contact [`jcondon@nrao.edu`](mailto:jcondon@nrao.edu)<br>* * *<br>![](https://www.cv.nrao.edu/common/space.gif)<br>* * *<br>[Home](http://www.nrao.edu/) \| <br> [Contact Us](http://www.nrao.edu/cgi-bin/contact) \| <br> [Directories](http://www.nrao.edu/navbar/directories.shtml) \| <br> [Site Map](http://www.nrao.edu/navbar/sitemap.shtml) \| <br> [Help](http://www.nrao.edu/navbar/help.shtml) \| <br> [Search](http://www.nrao.edu/navbar/search.shtml)<br>_Modified on Monday, 04-Jun-2018 08:30:03 EDT_ |
