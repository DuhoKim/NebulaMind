## Claim 1: "No Single BHU Model"

PASS, strengthened by the adversarial search.

Lana did not conflate one coherent mature programme into four fake fragments. The literature really is fragmented:

- Pathria: Schwarzschild/closed-FRW identification, no birth mechanism.
- Poplawski/ECSK: torsion bounce in black holes; inflation/entropy/closed-universe mechanism.
- Poplawski rotating-axis preprint: preferred axis from parent black-hole rotation.
- Smolin CNS: black-hole reproduction and constants selection, with neutron-star mass as the famous falsifiable branch.
- Additional missed branch: Frolov/Markov/Mukhanov, Easson/Brandenberger, Dymnikova-style / vacuum-bubble baby-universe work, and newer entropy/entanglement baby-universe papers.

The missed branch is a real literature-completeness defect, but it supports fragmentation rather than refuting it. It does not reveal a single mature BHU model that owns all claimed observables.

## Claim 2: "Only One Observable Number"

PASS FOR THE CAMPAIGN'S BHU ROUTE; REWRITE FOR LITERATURE EXHAUSTIVENESS.

Lana is right that the CNS neutron-star mass branch is the clean, famous falsifiable number in the black-hole-universe family she surveyed. Brown/Lee/Rho explicitly tie the kaon-condensation chain, Brown-Bethe maximum mass, and Smolin CNS to a falsification threshold near `2 M_sun`; later pulsar measurements at about 1.97 and 2.08 solar masses put that chain under serious pressure.

I would not let "only one number anywhere in the family" stand without qualification. The torsion-bounce papers contain numerical model quantities, and adjacent baby-universe papers discuss critical masses, PBH abundance, entropy terms, or GW-echo prospects. Those are not a BHU-specific sky-statistics prediction for Duho's lanes, and many are speculative/prospect-style rather than a frozen observational target. But they are enough to make the global sentence too sweeping.

Safer wording:

> In the BHU literature relevant to Duho's sky-statistics routes, I found no quantitative, distinguishing prediction. The clean published falsifiable branch I found is CNS's neutron-star mass bound, which is not a sky anisotropy test and is already under strong pressure from massive pulsars.

## Claim 3: Axis Variant Has No Distinguishable Prediction

PASS, with wording narrowed.

Provenance check: Lana is correct. The inherited-axis claim is present in Poplawski `arXiv:1910.10819`, and the arXiv category is `physics.pop-ph`. I found no peer-reviewed journal version and no peer-reviewed successor deriving an inherited preferred axis with amplitude, scale, redshift dependence, or axis direction.

The abstract itself claims:

- parent black-hole rotation gives a preferred axis;
- galaxies tend to align with it;
- clockwise/counter-clockwise asymmetry and perpendicular bulk flow follow;
- data "seem to support" the motions.

What is missing is exactly what a test needs: how big, where, at what scale, at what redshift, with what uncertainty, and compared against what null. Without that, a spin asymmetry, a bulk flow, a 4PCF parity signal, or a null can all be narrated as compatible by adjusting parent spin or by saying the effect is small.

The only sentence I would tighten is "no measurement outcome is excluded." A perfect mathematical isotropy result would contradict a model that asserts a strictly nonzero preferred axis. But no finite public-data sky measurement can reach that logical limit, and the published model gives no lower bound on the effect. For our purposes, the correct closure is:

> As published, the axis variant excludes no finite-precision sky-statistics outcome and therefore gives no testable target.

That is enough to close the line without overclaiming philosophical unfalsifiability.

## Claim 4: "Uniqueness Fails Everywhere"

PASS FOR THE SKY/ANISOTROPY OBSERVABLES.

The observables that motivated this campaign -- spin-handedness asymmetry, preferred axis, bulk flow, parity-odd structure, cosmic anisotropy -- are generic to rotating cosmologies, Bianchi/Godel-type models, primordial vorticity, parity-violating early-universe physics, or survey/systematics failures. None identifies a parent black hole.

This point holds especially for the axis variant. It also holds for the 4PCF parity route: a parity-odd signal would at most be consistent with early-universe parity violation, covariance/systematics failure, or other anisotropic/bouncing cosmologies; it would not select BHU.

For torsion-bounce variants, uniqueness is also not established. They may be distinctive as a theoretical mechanism for singularity avoidance/bounce/inflation, but the publicly visible observables Lana and I checked do not separate "inside a black hole" from generic bounce or quantum-gravity early-universe scenarios.
