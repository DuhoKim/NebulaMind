## 0. Verdict up front

**There is no single black-hole-universe model. There are at least five distinct programmes that do not
agree with each other (§1.1–§1.6), and in the BHU literature relevant to this campaign's sky-statistics
routes I found no calibrated, BHU-specific prediction. The clean, famous falsifiable observable number
in the family I surveyed is CNS's neutron-star mass bound — a nuclear-astrophysics test, not a sky test,
whose cited pulsar measurements now enter Brown–Lee–Rho's approximate-2 M☉ "serious doubt or simply
falsify" regime (their disjunction; not adjudicated here — §1.4). Adjacent baby-universe papers
carry other numbers (critical masses, PBH abundances, GW-echo prospects — §1.6); none is a sky-statistics
target for the routes this campaign ran.** The variant that motivated our spin work — a preferred axis
inherited from the parent black hole's rotation — does exist in a primary source, and its 2025 revision
**explicitly states that clockwise and counterclockwise galaxy counts should differ** (§1.3): the spin
lane was not chasing a phantom — it was chasing an **explicit, source-backed qualitative claim, but not a
calibrated or pre-data forecast** (v2 was revised in 2025 *after* the handedness studies it cites). That source
(arXiv:1910.10819v2; popular-physics category, no journal version located as of 2026-08-11) supplies
mechanics and functional forms — a Kerr-radius correction to the FLRW metric, rotating-frame force laws —
and qualitative sign/orientation outcomes, but **no calibrated model prediction for the amplitude, scale,
or redshift dependence of the handedness statistic, no independently predicted axis direction (its quoted
axis is fitted post hoc from cited data), and no finite lower bound on the effect — that is, no numerical
acceptance region a finite-precision test could score against; and a positive generic anisotropy would
not identify BHU.** Stated carefully, because the distinction is the finding: we do **not** claim the
idea is untestable in principle — its qualitative directional claims are not nothing, and sufficiently
precise measurements could bear on them. What we claim, and can support, is narrower and operationally
decisive: **as published there is no calibrated target, so none of this campaign's sky-statistics routes
had a BHU-specific prediction to test.** That closes this line for a reason, not for lack of trying.

### 1.3 The axis paper — the one that matters for our lanes, and what it actually is
**arXiv:1910.10819**, N. Popławski, *"Universe in a rotating black hole and preferred axis."* Abstract
fetched today, verbatim in full:
> "If our universe was born as a baby universe on the other side of the event horizon of a black hole
> existing in a parent universe, then the corresponding white hole at rest provides the absolute frame of
> reference in the universe. In this frame, the cosmic microwave background radiation is isotropic on
> large scales. If the parent black hole is rotating, then its axis of rotation becomes a preferred axis
> in the universe. Accordingly, the absolute frame is non-inertial, although the non-inertial forces are
> small. To decrease their energies, galaxies tend to align their axes of rotation with the preferred
> axis, resulting in clockwise-counterclockwise asymmetry. The centrifugal force causes a large-scale bulk
> flow of galaxy clusters in directions perpendicular to the preferred axis. The astronomical data seem to
> support these motions. The angular velocity of the universe decreases as the universe expands … The
> centrifugal force in a rotating universe, which also decreases, may be the origin of dark energy …"
Facts about this source, established at Tori's full-text custody audit — which supersedes the
abstract-only account Kun and I both wrote: it is classified **physics.pop-ph** (primary category;
cross-listed astro-ph.CO, gr-qc), carries only an arXiv DataCite DOI, and **no journal version was
located as of 2026-08-11** — a dated search result, not an unbounded claim about all successors.
**Version history is load-bearing.** v1 (2019) is titled *Black Hole Genesis and origin of inertia* and
contains **no "preferred axis", "clockwise", or "counterclockwise" language at all**; v2, **revised
29 May 2025**, is a substantially expanded paper carrying all the axis material. Anyone reading the
abstract, or v1 — as Kun and I did — misses what the v2 body contains. Per Tori's read, v2 has real
mechanics and functional forms:
> "The preferred direction should introduce small corrections to the FLRW metric, containing the Kerr
> radius a = M/mc, where M is the angular momentum of a rotating black hole and m is its mass."
It derives rotating-frame relations, states the centrifugal-force magnitude m·Ω²·ρ, proposes
Λ = 3Ω²/c², and states explicitly: **"Consequently, the numbers of clockwise- and
counterclockwise-spinning galaxies in a rotating universe should be different."** It also imports
numerical observational material — samples of ~10², 10⁴, 10⁵, 10⁶ galaxies; redshift ranges z ~ 0.04,
z < 0.3, z < 2; an ~50% JADES difference; a 630 km/s bulk flow — and combines cited fitted axes into a
mean preferred axis α = 197° ± 47°, δ = 34° ± 3°. **Those numbers are post-hoc summaries of cited
observations, not independent model predictions made before the data** (Tori's characterisation, and the
distinction the whole assessment turns on). What a full-text audit leaves absent is narrower than
Rev 1–3 claimed, and it is the absence that matters: **no numerical value or prior for the parent
spin/Ω; no mapping from that parameter to a predicted clockwise fraction or handedness-dipole amplitude;
no angular/radial/redshift dependence for the handedness statistic; no uncertainty; no lower bound; no
parity-odd 4PCF or quasar number-count-dipole prediction; no operational estimator, tolerance, or
acceptance region for a prospective finite-precision test.** The Rev 2–3 marker "[VERIFY which datasets
the full text cites]" is resolved by Tori's read: the "astronomical data seem to support" sentence rests
on the contested spin-asymmetry and bulk-flow observations our lanes already know.

## 2. The five deciding questions, answered

**Q1 — Any quantitative prediction?** In the BHU literature relevant to this campaign's sky-statistics
routes: **none that distinguishes.** The clean, famous **falsifiable observable** number in the family I
surveyed is CNS's neutron-star mass bound (Brown–Bethe maximum ~1.5 M☉; quoted threshold M ≳ 2 M☉) — and it
is not about the sky. Numbers exist elsewhere without being testable targets for our routes: Poplawski's
Ω_S (full text: −8.6×10⁻⁷⁰) is a numerical model quantity with no sensitivity floor defined for any
verification route, and the adjacent baby-universe branch (§1.6) discusses critical masses, PBH
abundances, and GW-echo prospects — prospect-style quantities for other instruments, not frozen
sky-statistics predictions. The axis scenario (v2 full text, §1.3): **equations, force laws, and
post-hoc observational numbers — but no calibrated prediction.** Pathria: the accessible abstract asserts
a radius condition, oscillation, and bounded expansion — content, but no distinguishing observational
statistic; the paywalled body is **[VERIFY]**.

**Q2 — Any unique prediction?** **No.** The axis scenario's observables — galaxy-spin alignment with a
preferred axis, perpendicular bulk flow, CW/CCW asymmetry — are the generic phenomenology of *any*
rotating cosmology (Bianchi-type models, primordial vorticity), none of which involve a parent black
hole. A measured spin asymmetry of any size would be "consistent with" BHU and equally consistent with
half a dozen non-BHU anisotropic models; Kun's 4PCF point applies verbatim here. The one element unique
to BHU — birth through a torsion bounce behind a parent horizon — has, in the sources obtained, **no
published observable that differs from generic bounce cosmology**. Uniqueness failed everywhere I looked
— stated, per Tori, as a class-level comparative claim (Bianchi-type, primordial vorticity, generic
bounce); freezing it externally would first require citing those alternative models' primary sources
**[VERIFY]**.

**Q3 — Does a primary source derive the inherited axis?** **Yes — and this must be said honestly in both
directions.** The axis idea was *not* our invention: arXiv:1910.10819 states it explicitly (verbatim
above), including galaxy-spin alignment and bulk flow as consequences. The earlier lanes' motivating
inference has a real source — and Tori's full-text read shows v2 states the lane's exact statistic:
differing clockwise and counterclockwise counts. **But**: that source is a popular-physics preprint with
no journal version located as of 2026-08-11, and what it supplies is mechanics without calibration — a
Kerr-scale correction to FLRW and force laws, but **no calibrated amplitude, scale, or redshift law for
the handedness statistic, no independently predicted axis direction (its quoted axis is fitted post hoc
from cited data), and no finite lower bound** — so it cannot tell a 2% asymmetry from a 50% one, cannot

## 3. The structural finding

"BHU" is not one model. It is at least five: **Pathria's GR identification** (no birth mechanism, no
dynamics), **Poplawski's torsion bounce** (predicts isotropy; black-hole parent attached as
interpretation), **Poplawski's rotating-parent axis scenario** (popular-physics preprint, qualitative),
**Smolin's CNS** (constants selection; no anisotropy claim at all), and the **baby-universe branch I
missed** (Frolov–Markov–Mukhanov; Easson–Brandenberger; Dymnikova-style bubbles; the 2025 entropy paper —
§1.6), whose observational prospects, where stated, point at PBH populations and GW echoes rather than
any sky statistic. They disagree about what the scenario even implies — the torsion-bounce paper's stated
achievement is *explaining isotropy* while the axis preprint predicts *anisotropy*; CNS is silent on
both; the missed branch talks to different instruments entirely. (Precision required by Tori: the 2010
abstract explains why the universe *appears* isotropic and the 2025 axis body adds small rotational
corrections to FLRW — not strictly inconsistent statements, but developed as separate programmes never
joined into one calibrated model.) **"Test BHU" is therefore ill-posed as a
study goal: there is no single thing to test, and the members that are testable are either already tested
(CNS), aimed at instruments outside this campaign's sky-statistics routes (§1.6 prospects), or test
nothing distinguishing (the rest).**

## 5. What would have to change to reopen this line

Concrete and checkable, in ascending order of value:
1. **A published derivation attaching a magnitude** — an asymmetry amplitude, scale, or redshift
