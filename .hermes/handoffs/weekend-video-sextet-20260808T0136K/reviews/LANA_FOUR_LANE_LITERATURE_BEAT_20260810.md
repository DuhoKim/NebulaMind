produces a bias-controlled **method**, its own asymmetry withheld, so quoting the disputed asymmetry cannot

## mzr-anchor — **YES**
**(a)** Yes. **(b)** The separable finding is about the *measurement rulers*, not about evolution.
Verbatim: **Kewley & Ellison (2008)**, *Metallicity Calibrations and the Mass-Metallicity Relation for
Star-Forming Galaxies*, ApJ 681, 1183 (arXiv:0801.1849):
> "The absolute metallicity scale (y-int) varies up to 0.7 dex, depending on the calibration used."

Framing that holds the separation: report this as an established fact about the *tools* — the metallicity
calibrations disagree with each other by up to ~0.7 dex — so **whether** the relation evolves at high
redshift cannot be read off until measurements sit on one scale. That is precisely why the lane pursues
direct-temperature anchors. The lane's own output is a *null about anchor availability*; the
calibration-disagreement finding is at a different level (the rulers), attributed to Kewley & Ellison, and
cannot be read as the lane's evolution result. Foundational context (the MZR exists at z~0, Tremonti et al.
2004) may be stated plainly as established, not as a finding.

**(d) Near-misses that collapse the separation (reject on sight):**
1. "The mass–metallicity relation **evolves** at high redshift." — the evolution result, the lane's own
   forbidden subject.
2. "High-z galaxies **are** more metal-poor." — a direction asserted as fact.
3. Quoting an *evolution*-claim paper (e.g. a JWST high-z MZR-offset result) as the beat — that IS the
   lane's finding wearing someone else's name.
4. "This lane **shows** the relation shifts by …" — asserting Δ_Z.
5. Presenting the 0.7 dex calibration spread **as if it were a measured offset/evolution** — conflating
   ruler-disagreement (about tools) with a real high-z shift (the result). The 0.7 dex is a statement about
   calibrations, never about how much the relation moved.

## brightend — **YES** (and the primary source does the separating for us)
**(a)** Yes. **(b)** The excess is the *field's contested claim*; the lane's own question is the prior
**archival-reconstruction** question (can the public record even reconstruct such a measurement?). The
cleanest verbatim finding is one whose *own wording* hedges the excess as possibly observational — which is
exactly the lane's level. **Boylan-Kolchin (2023)**, *Stress Testing ΛCDM with High-redshift Galaxy
Candidates*, Nat. Astron. 7, 731 (arXiv:2208.01611):
> "Early data from JWST have revealed a bevy of high-redshift galaxy candidates with unexpectedly high
> stellar masses."
and:
> "the most massive galaxy candidates in JWST observations at z∼7-10 lie at the very edge of these limits,
> indicating an important unresolved issue with the properties of galaxies derived from the observations,
> how galaxies form at early times in ΛCDM, or within this standard cosmology itself."

Framing that holds the separation: report it as Boylan-Kolchin's — **claimed and explicitly unresolved**,
where his own three-way hedge names "the properties of galaxies derived from the observations" as one live
explanation. This lane addresses exactly that observational branch: it asks whether the public archive can
even reconstruct the bright-end measurement. So the excess is *the field's dispute*, and the lane's output
(reconstruction: missing data vs missed data) is a *different, prior question* — not a verdict on the
excess. Attribution + Boylan-Kolchin's own "unresolved issue" wording keep it from reading as the lane's
finding. This is the more fragile of the two YES lanes — a viewer conflates "bright end" with "excess" more
readily than "handedness" with "classifier bias" — so the reconstruction framing must be unmistakable and
the beat must never sit as the lane's conclusion.

**(d) Near-misses that collapse the separation (reject on sight):**
1. "There **are** more bright early galaxies than models predict" (video's own voice, unattributed) — the
   lane asserting the excess.
2. "The archive **confirms** the JWST excess" / "our census **shows the same** tension" — makes the lane's
   reconstruction a confirmation of the excess; a result claim.
3. "This lane finds the bright end **over-abundant**." — the lane asserting a UVLF result.
4. Quoting the excess as **settled** ("JWST **proved** galaxies form too fast") — drops Boylan-Kolchin's
   "unresolved," which is what keeps it disputed.
5. Dropping attribution, or placing the excess quote as the beat the video builds to (payoff) — placement
   elevates it to the lane's thesis.
6. "JWST found galaxies **too massive for ΛCDM**" as a flat claim — adopts the cosmology branch of a hedge
   the source deliberately left open; also brushes the forbidden cosmology scope.

## fesc — **NO** (say it plainly)
**(a)** No. **(c)** For fesc there is no literature finding at a level distinct from the lane's own subject.
The quotable findings — measured escape fractions, reported photon-budget deficits, "galaxies fall short /
suffice to reionize" — *are* the photon-budget shortfall the lane is built to investigate (source shortfall
vs assumption shortfall). Unlike spin (where the lane's output is a bias-controlled method, one step removed
from the asymmetry) and brightend (where the lane's output is archival reconstruction, a prior question),
fesc's own output is *directly the shortfall's reality*. Any "what studies found about the shortfall" beat
would read as the lane's answer — a result claim, forbidden while `SOURCE_FREEZE` is absent. Tori already
blocked this lane's framing once as circular unless fully conditional; a findings beat re-opens exactly
that circularity.

**What to keep instead (not a findings beat):** the lane's existing **conditional** motivation is the
honest form — reionization was the last great transition; galaxies are the leading candidate *if* enough
ionizing light escapes; f_esc is the dominant unknown. The *required* budget (that reionizing by z~6 would
need f_esc of order ~10–20%, Robertson et al. 2015 / Finkelstein et al. 2019) may be stated as a **model
requirement** ("what would be needed"), never as a measured shortfall — but that is context, not a "what
others found" beat, and it must stay conditional. **Recommendation: fesc does not get the literature beat.**

**Recommendation: mzr-census does not get the literature beat.**
