## fesc — cosmic reionization escape fraction

**BROAD (why the subject matters).** Reionization was the last great phase transition of the cosmos: the
intergalactic hydrogen, neutral since recombination, was ionized again (z≈6–10). *What did it?* The
leading candidate is ordinary star-forming galaxies — but their starlight only reionizes the universe if a
sufficient fraction of ionizing (Lyman-continuum) photons **escapes** their gas into the intergalactic
medium. The ionizing budget is UV luminosity density × ionizing-production efficiency ξ_ion × **escape
fraction f_esc**, and f_esc is the single most uncertain factor. So "how much ionizing light escapes
galaxies?" *is* the question "could galaxies have reionized the universe?"

**NARROW (why the method is hard).** At reionization redshifts you **cannot measure f_esc directly** — the
same neutral IGM the photons would ionize also absorbs the escaping Lyman continuum before it reaches us.
f_esc must be inferred from low-redshift analog galaxies and indirect proxies, whose calibration and
transport to high z are themselves uncertain — which is this lane's entire method.

**Primary sources.** Robertson et al. 2015 (ApJ 802, L19) and Finkelstein et al. 2019 (ApJ 879, 36):
an average f_esc of order ~10–20% is required for star-forming galaxies to reionize by z≈6; f_esc is the
dominant budget uncertainty. Naidu et al. 2020 (ApJ 892, 109) on which galaxies dominate. *(Framing-level
grounding verified today; pull the exact required-f_esc sentence verbatim before any on-screen attribution.)*

**RESULT-CLAIM FLAG.** The forbidden sentence is *"there is a photon-budget shortfall."* Tori already
blocked this lane's framing once as **circular unless fully conditional**: the required f_esc, the inferred
f_esc, and their difference (the shortfall, its sign, its growth with z) are the *answer*, computed under
the very assumptions (clumping, ξ_ion, proxy transport) whose validity is in question. Any "shortfall
exists / survives / rises," any crossing redshift, any "galaxies did/did not reionize" is a result. Even
the *magnitude* of the required budget must be stated as "what would be needed," not "what is missing."

**Boundary.** *Allowed:* the budget structure (UV × ξ_ion × f_esc), that f_esc is the key uncertainty
(established), and the conditional stakes ("if too little escapes, galaxies alone could not reionize").
*Forbidden:* any asserted shortfall, direction, redshift trend, significance, or reionization verdict —
including inside a figure.

## brightend — the bright end of the UV luminosity function at cosmic dawn

**BROAD (why the subject matters).** The bright end of the z>10 UV luminosity function counts the rarest,
most luminous galaxies in the first few hundred million years — the first massive systems. Their abundance
tests how fast galaxies could grow that early, against a ΛCDM expectation that massive halos are
exponentially rare at cosmic dawn. It is a direct probe of early star-formation efficiency, dust, and
feedback — and, if the counts are surprising enough, of cosmology itself.

**NARROW (why the method is hard).** Bright early galaxies are *rare* — small numbers, large Poisson and
cosmic-variance uncertainty — and photometric redshifts can be wrong (a low-z interloper masquerading as a
z>10 source). This lane adds a second layer: whether the **published measurement can be reconstructed from
the public archival record at all** (missing data vs data the search missed).

**Primary sources.** Multiple JWST teams report more bright z≳10 galaxies than pre-JWST models predicted —
Naidu et al. 2022 (ApJ 940, L14), Finkelstein et al. (CEERS), Donnan et al. 2023, Wang et al. 2023
(UNCOVER, arXiv:2312.05030, "the overabundance of UV-luminous galaxies at z>9"); the tension is framed as
"stress-testing ΛCDM" by Boylan-Kolchin 2023 (Nat. Astron. 7, 731). **Crucially these are reported and
contested claims, not settled** — and not this lane's to adopt.

**RESULT-CLAIM FLAG.** The forbidden sentence is *"JWST found too many bright early galaxies."* That is a
result (a) not established (contested by contamination/redshift systematics) and (b) **not this lane's** —
this lane is an archival-reconstruction census, not a UVLF measurement. Any asserted excess, number
density, order-of-magnitude tension, or ΛCDM verdict is out. The reported tension may be cited only as
*claimed-and-contested, attributed*, as motivation for why reconstructing the bright end carefully matters.

**Boundary.** *Allowed:* why the bright end matters (early galaxy growth vs ΛCDM), and that a tension has
been *reported and debated* (attributed), conditional ("if there were an excess, it would…"). *Forbidden:*
asserting the excess exists, any density/count, a cosmology verdict, or this lane's own archival result.

## mzr-anchor — the high-redshift mass–metallicity relation and its direct-Te anchors

**BROAD (why the subject matters).** A galaxy's metallicity is a fossil record of its entire life: stars
forge metals, outflows expel them, fresh gas dilutes them. The **mass–metallicity relation** — a tight
correlation between stellar mass and gas-phase oxygen abundance over three decades in mass (Tremonti et al.
2004) — is one of astrophysics' tightest scaling relations and encodes the **baryon cycle**: accretion,
star formation, feedback. How that relation stood in the first galaxies tests how galaxies chemically
enriched across cosmic time — *and* whether our metallicity rulers even work that early: locally-calibrated
strong-line diagnostics may break down at high z, so you need direct electron-temperature (auroral-line)
anchors.

**NARROW (why the method is hard).** Direct-Te metallicities require the faint auroral line ([O III]
λ4363), extremely hard to detect and rarer still at high z; and metallicity calibration scales **do not
cancel** across methods and surveys (a known systematic — [[reference-metallicity-calibration-scale]]).
Assembling a uniform-scale, high-z, direct-Te anchor sample is the hard part — and the reason this lane's
own result is a *null* (only ~5 anchors; see my science-adjudication packet).

**Primary sources.** Tremonti et al. 2004 (ApJ 613, 898, the MZR); Andrews & Martini 2013 (ApJ 765, 140,
the Te direct-method MZR — **verified verbatim** in my adjudication packet); and the lane's own
`ANCHOR_GAP_PAPER`, whose abstract frames it exactly: *"Whether locally calibrated metallicity diagnostics
survive at high redshift is a disputed axis of the galaxy-evolution literature."*

**RESULT-CLAIM FLAG.** The forbidden sentence is *"the mass–metallicity relation evolves"* (or "high-z
galaxies are more metal-poor," "the offset is X dex," "calibrations fail"). Δ_Z's value, sign, and any
evolution verdict are the answer. Conditional only: *"if the relation evolved, it would trace enrichment
history; whether local calibrations survive at high z is disputed — which is why direct anchors are
needed."*

**Boundary.** *Allowed:* the MZR as a baryon-cycle fossil (established), and that its high-z survival /
the calibration question is *disputed* (attributed, quotable from the lane paper). *Forbidden:* any
asserted evolution, offset value or sign, metal-poor/rich direction, or calibration verdict.

## mzr-census — archive-metadata eligibility census (NOT a physics lane)

**BROAD (why the subject matters) — and it is a different kind of stake.** This lane does not measure
metallicity. It asks a prior question: **can the public astronomical archive actually be used to
reconstruct a mass–metallicity census at all?** Modern science rests on the premise that results can be
independently reproduced from shared data — the **FAIR principles** (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable,
Reusable; Wilkinson et al. 2016, Sci. Data 3, 160018). But archive metadata is *semantically ambiguous* —
a column named "Z" may mean redshift, a spatial height, or a stellar metal fraction — so whether an
eligible three-axis (mass, gas-phase abundance, redshift) galaxy sample can even be *assembled* from the
record is a real, rarely-asked, reproducibility question. The stake is the reliability of the archive as
scientific infrastructure, not the metallicity physics. **Do not borrow a metallicity-physics opening
here — it would be wrong and unsupported** (Hwao's explicit warning).

**NARROW (why the method is hard).** The method is *semantic adjudication*: reading column names, UCD
qualifiers, and archived descriptions to decide whether a table's metadata truly refers to the right
physical quantity, phase, and parent sample. The ambiguity of metadata is the difficulty.

**Primary sources.** Wilkinson et al. 2016 (Sci. Data 3, 160018): data must be Findable, Accessible,
Interoperable, Reusable to be of enduring value; *"without well-documented … metadata … data sets are of
very limited use to other users."* Ochsenbein, Bauer & Marcout 2000 (A&AS 143, 23, VizieR — the archive
being censused); IVOA/Virtual-Observatory standards. *(FAIR/VizieR are established infrastructure
references, not result claims.)*

**RESULT-CLAIM FLAG.** The forbidden sentence is *"the archive is inadequate"* — or "N tables are
eligible," "the record cannot answer the question." That adequacy verdict / eligibility count *is* the
census's finding. Conditional only: *"if the public record could not supply a scientifically eligible
sample at uniform rigor, then the question could not be settled from it as it stands — which is worth
knowing before anyone tries."*

**Boundary.** *Allowed:* the reproducibility / FAIR-data stake (established), the semantic-ambiguity
difficulty, and the conditional "if the record can't answer it, that itself matters." *Forbidden:* any
eligibility count, adequacy verdict, recall/precision result, or **any metallicity-physics claim** (the
borrowed-physics error is specifically banned here).
