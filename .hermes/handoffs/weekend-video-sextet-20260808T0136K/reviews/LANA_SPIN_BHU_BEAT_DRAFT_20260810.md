## 1. The exact claim boundary
The permitted statement is a **fact about the author** — that Duho was personally drawn to the topic by an
interest in black-hole-universe cosmology (BHU). Forbidden is any **claim about the world or the science**:
that BHU motivates the field, that it predicts galaxy handedness, that a handedness result would bear on
it, or that this work tests it. The dividing test, to apply to every candidate sentence:

> Strip it to its verb. If the sentence links BHU to *the author's curiosity* ("got me interested"), it is
> permitted. If it links BHU to *the measurement, the field, or the result* ("motivates," "predicts,"

A disclaimer does **not** rescue a linkage verb: "though this video doesn't test it, a handedness signal

## 4. Near-miss phrasings that CROSS the line — reject on sight (for Kun and Hwao to test against)
Each is close enough to look acceptable; each fails, with the reason:
1. "This work was **motivated by** black-hole-universe cosmology." — presents BHU as a motivation for the
   *work*; drops personal attribution. (scientific motivation)
2. "Black-hole-universe cosmology is **one reason galaxy handedness is interesting**." — a claim about why
   the *subject* matters. (world-claim)
3. "Though this video doesn't test it, a handedness asymmetry **could bear on** black-hole-universe
   cosmology." — the linkage verb makes the world-claim; the hedge doesn't withdraw it. (bears-on)
4. "Duho is interested in black-hole-universe cosmology, **which predicts a preferred spin direction**." —
   attaches a scientific prediction and links it to the result. (predicts)
5. "This study **grew out of** the question of whether we live inside a black hole." — frames the *study* as
   engaging a BHU question. (the work bears on it)
6. "**Personally and scientifically**, black-hole-universe cosmology makes this compelling." — explicitly
   claims scientific motivation. (says the quiet part)
7. "Galaxy handedness **has been connected to** black-hole-universe cosmology." — passive, no personal
   attribution, asserts an established connection. (fails cond. 1; world-claim)
8. "Black-hole-universe cosmology, **a frontier** that drew the author in." — "frontier" misrepresents its
   status; it is a personal interest, not a ranked corpus frontier. (status inflation)
9. "The author's personal interest is black-hole-universe cosmology." **with no disclaimers.** — a bare
   personal mention with conditions 2–3 missing drifts to implied relevance. (fails cond. 2 and 3)
10. Correct words, **wrong placement**: the compliant sentence rendered inside the literature beat next to
    Longo/Shamir, or as the closing card. (fails cond. 4 — placement elevates it to the mainstream/driver)
11. Any of "**relates to / is relevant to / speaks to / hints at / is a signature of**" BHU. — all are
    linkage verbs; all cross.

Rule of thumb for reviewers: if you can delete the words "personal" and "the author" from the sentence and
it still reads as a statement about galaxy handedness and BHU, it has crossed. A permitted sentence
collapses into nonsense without the personal frame; a forbidden one survives it.
