## 2. The central risk — say it in the script's margin, hold it in every beat

**A video is a compression, and compression is where overclaim gets in.** Narration compresses harder than
prose: it drops qualifiers first, because qualifiers are what make sentences long. The three ways this video
will try to lie if nobody stops it, with the exact spoken wording that stops it:

**Risk 1 — the fork becomes the cause.** The selection-function fork is the tidy narrative; the papers
themselves gesture at it. It is the leading **stated** difference, not the demonstrated explanation.
*Exact narration for the fork beat (B6):*
> "The papers differ, first and most visibly, on one choice: whether to apply the catalogue's selection
> function. One writes it into the heart of its likelihood. The other leaves it out — and says so, in print.
> It is the obvious suspect. But obvious is not demonstrated: neither paper isolates its effect, so it
> remains the leading stated difference — not the explanation."
The words "suspect", "candidate", "stated difference" are safe; "cause", "reason", "explains", "because of"
in the same sentence as the selection function are forbidden (see §3).

**Risk 2 — the dipole dispute becomes a claim about the universe.** "The dipole exceeds the CMB
prediction" drifts into "the universe is anisotropic / the cosmological principle fails." The note states
nothing about that; neither may the video. The comparison-to-expectation is allowed **only** as a
description of what the test is for, and the boundary sentence is spoken **early** (B1), not at the end —
because the ending belongs to the verdict, not to caveats.
*Exact narration for the boundary (end of B1):*
> "This video reports what two such measurements published, and why their disagreement cannot yet be
> resolved. It makes no claim about the universe itself."
*If either paper's relation to the kinematic expectation comes up at all, the only safe form is attributed
and symmetric:* "how the counting dipole compares to expectation is exactly what the two papers dispute."
The video must never state, in its own voice, that any dipole is too big, too small, or anomalous.

**Risk 3 — "strongly supported same release" becomes "the same data."** "The same data" is shorter,
natural in narration, and false. Wherever data identity is spoken, use this wording or its B3 compression —
nothing simpler:
*Exact narration for the identity beat (B5):*
> "Are they even counting the same quasars? Almost certainly. One paper pins the exact catalogue release —
> version, archive record, checksums. And it states the other paper used the same release. But that other
> paper never pins its own copy. So the honest statement is: strongly supported to be the same release —
> not verified byte for byte. Keep that word: supported."
*The one-clause compression allowed elsewhere (B3, B9):* "what is, by strong support, the same catalogue
release." The two-word compression "same data" is forbidden everywhere, including on-screen text.

## 5. The hardest problem: a verdict that is a refusal, landing as a finding

The verdict here is "not attributable from the published record" — and it must land as a genuine finding,
not a shrug or an apology. My answer, in words Yui can build on:

**Frame the refusal as a measurement of the record itself.** The video did not fail to find the cause; it
*measured where the published record runs out*, precisely: which choices are coupled, which correction is
unstated, which quantities differ. A shrug says "we don't know." This finding says "we know exactly what is
missing, and therefore exactly what it would take." That is why B8 (what would settle it) sits directly
before the close: the refusal arrives already carrying its operational content.

**Closing narration (use this or equivalent-strength):**
> "Two papers. One catalogue — as far as the public record can establish. A factor of three between them.
> We traced the disagreement to the exact point where the published record stops answering: coupled choices,
> an unstated correction, two different quantities. That is the finding. Not 'we may never know' — but:
> the answer is not in the papers, and now anyone who wants to resolve this dispute knows precisely what it
> will take."

Delivery rules that keep it a finding: active voice with a concrete object ("the record does not contain
the cause" — never "we couldn't figure it out"); no "unfortunately", no "sadly", no trailing qualifier
after the last sentence; the last words on screen are the verdict heading, not a limitation. The note's own
§5 phrase — "a genuine contribution to a contested literature" — is the register to hit, though the phrase
itself is prose, not narration.

   `0.0080/0.0068`).** Uncompressible. Narrating a corrected pair plus a superseded pair invites the
