## 1. Kun’s four Popławski pins

### 1.1 arXiv:1007.0587 — resolves

**Metadata verdict: PASS.**

The arXiv record is *Cosmology with torsion: An alternative to cosmic inflation*, arXiv:1007.0587v2.[1] It gives the journal reference **Physics Letters B 694, 181–185 (2010)** and DOI **10.1016/j.physletb.2010.09.056**; it also lists the erratum in **Physics Letters B 701, 672 (2011)**, DOI **10.1016/j.physletb.2011.05.047**.[1]

The abstract says:

> “This scenario also suggests that the contraction of our Universe preceding the bounce at the minimum radius may correspond to the dynamics of matter inside a collapsing black hole existing in another universe, which could explain the origin of the Big Bang.” [1]

That supports Lana’s characterization of the parent-black-hole element as suggestive language in this paper’s abstract.[1] It does not, by itself, prove every in-paper claim about what is or is not derived.[1]

Lana’s phrase “some sixty orders of magnitude below observability” has no defined sensitivity floor in the paper and is not citation-supported.[1]

## 2. Neutron-star/CNS chain

### 2.1 Brown, Lee & Rho threshold

**Citation and threshold verdict: PASS, WITH CONSEQUENCE NARROWING.**

The source is G. E. Brown, Chang-Hwan Lee, and Mannque Rho, *Kaon Condensation, Black Holes, and Cosmological Natural Selection*, **Physical Review Letters 101, 091101 (2008)**, DOI **10.1103/PhysRevLett.101.091101**.[5]

The primary abstract says:

> “a massive neutron star with mass M > or approximately 2M[solar] would put in serious doubt or simply falsify the following chain of predictions” [5]

and the enumerated chain ends with:

> “Smolin’s ‘cosmological natural selection’ hypothesis.” [5]

The publisher full text renders the mathematical condition as $M\gtrsim2M_\odot$.[5] Its body also says a firm mass greater than the Brown–Bethe maximum, “or to be safe” approximately 2 solar masses, would present “a serious obstacle to the BB and CNS scenarios.”[5]

APS Publisher’s Note **PRL 101, 119901 (2008)**, DOI **10.1103/PhysRevLett.101.119901**, says a tagging error caused a “relation sign misprint” in the online abstract; the corrected symbol is $\gtrsim$, not strict $>$ or $\geq$.[10]

Therefore:

- “near/approximately 2 solar masses” is correct;
- “the chain includes CNS” is correct;
- “would put in serious doubt or simply falsify” is an exact paper-level characterization;
- “the paper declares CNS definitively falsified once a central estimate is at least 2” is **not** what the source says.

### 2.2 Demorest et al. mass

**Mass verdict: PASS.**

Demorest et al., *A two-solar-mass neutron star measured using Shapiro delay*, **Nature 467, 1081–1083 (2010)**, DOI **10.1038/nature09466**, reports PSR J1614−2230.[6]

The primary abstract/preprint text states:

> “pulsar mass of 1.97 +/- 0.04 M_sun” [6]

The Nature page formats the same result as $(1.97\pm0.04)M_\odot$ and says it comes from a strong Shapiro-delay signature.[6] The manuscript identifies the quoted uncertainty as 1-$\sigma$ from Markov-chain Monte Carlo analysis. The central value 1.97 is below strict 2.00 and its 1-$\sigma$ interval is 1.93–2.01; it is an approximately-two-solar-mass result, not a strict central-value crossing.[6]

### 2.3 Fonseca et al. mass

**Mass verdict: PASS.**

Fonseca et al., *Refined Mass and Geometric Measurements of the High-mass PSR J0740+6620*, **The Astrophysical Journal Letters 915, L12 (2021)**, DOI **10.3847/2041-8213/ac03b8**, reports:[7]

> “with the pulsar mass $m_{\rm p}=2.08^{+0.07}_{-0.07}M_\odot$ (68.3% credibility) determined by the relativistic Shapiro time delay.” [7]

The symmetric shorthand $2.08\pm0.07M_\odot$ is numerically accurate after rounding, but the exact convention is a model-averaged Bayesian posterior with 68.3% credibility.[7] Its 68.3% interval, 2.01–2.15, is above 2.00; the paper’s 95.4% lower bound is 1.95, so it does not establish $M>2$ at 95.4% credibility.[7]

### 2.4 Custody-safe interpretation

The measurements reach Brown–Lee–Rho’s approximate-$2M_\odot$ regime; Demorest does not strictly cross 2.00 by central value, while Fonseca clears it at 68.3% but not 95.4% credibility.[5][6][7] The evidence therefore supports **“the cited Brown–Bethe/CNS chain is under serious pressure and its approximate-$2M_\odot$ test has been reached.”**[5][6][7] It does not, without further scientific adjudication, support changing the source’s own disjunctive language into **“CNS is definitively falsified.”**[5]
