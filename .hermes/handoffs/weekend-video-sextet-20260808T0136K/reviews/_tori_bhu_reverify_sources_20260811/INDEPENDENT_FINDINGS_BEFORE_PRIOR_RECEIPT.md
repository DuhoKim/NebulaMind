The prior receipt reviews/TORI_BHU_CITATION_CUSTODY_VERDICT_20260811.md had not been opened in this restarted session when these findings were written.

1. arXiv:1910.10819 is primarily physics.pop-ph and cross-listed astro-ph.CO and gr-qc. The current record is v2, revised 2025-05-29. No journal version was located: the arXiv API has no journal-ref or publisher DOI, DataCite identifies version 2 as a preprint, INSPIRE has no DOI/publication_info, and an exact-title/author Crossref query returned no exact match. This is a dated search result, not proof that no publication can exist anywhere.

2. v1 and v2 differ materially. v1 (2019-10-23), Black Hole Genesis and origin of inertia, is 2 pages and contains no preferred-axis, clockwise, counterclockwise, or handedness discussion. v2 is 5 pages, retitled Universe in a rotating black hole and preferred axis, and adds the rotating-parent axis, generic rotating-frame mechanics, the galaxy-handedness consequence, post-hoc observational summaries, bulk-flow geometry, and an evolving-dark-energy proposal.

3. v2 explicitly says: “Consequently, the numbers of clockwise- and counterclockwise-spinning galaxies in a rotating universe should be different.” That supports an explicit qualitative source claim about the exact count statistic. Because v2 was written after the cited handedness data and uses those data retrospectively, “real but uncalibrated prediction” is acceptable only if “prediction” is understood as a source-backed qualitative consequence, not an independent pre-data or calibrated forecast. “Source-backed qualitative claim” is the least flattering and most precise wording.

4. The current full text contains equations and functional forms: a proposed Kerr-radius correction a=M/(mc) to FLRW; rotating-frame Lagrangian/force relations including centrifugal magnitude m Omega^2 rho and E=E0-M·Omega; and dark-energy relations H=Omega and Lambda=3 Omega^2/c^2. It names CMB isotropy, parallel galaxy-spin alignment/unequal handedness, perpendicular/outward bulk flow, and decreasing dark energy. The numerical axes, redshift ranges, sample sizes, 50% JADES difference, and 630 km/s flow are retrospective observational inputs or arithmetic summaries, not independent model forecasts.

5. The four narrow negative claims are supported for the galaxy-handedness statistic: there is no calibrated model amplitude, scale law, or redshift law; no independently predicted sky direction; no finite nonzero lower bound; and no estimator/tolerance/likelihood/numerical acceptance region for a finite-precision handedness test. These must not be widened into “no functional forms,” “no numbers,” “no qualitative constraints,” “no finite-precision result can bear on the prose claims,” or “untestable in principle.”

6. Brown, Lee & Rho use M \gsim 2 M_sun, where their TeX macro explicitly defines \gsim as “greater than or approx.” and the Publisher’s Note visually prints the corresponding ≳ glyph. Their abstract says such a star “would put in serious doubt or simply falsify the following chain of predictions,” ending with Smolin’s CNS; the body says it “would present a serious obstacle to the BB and CNS scenarios.” The source gives Brown-Bethe Mmax approximately 1.5 M_sun, not the packet’s unsourced 1.5–1.6 range.

7. Demorest et al. report PSR J1614-2230 at 1.97 +/- 0.04 M_sun using an MCMC posterior described as nearly normal and 1-sigma uncertainties: center 1.97, 1-sigma interval 1.93–2.01. Fonseca et al. report PSR J0740+6620 at 2.08 +0.07/-0.07 M_sun (68.3% credibility): interval 2.01–2.15, with a reported 95.4% lower bound of 1.95. The packet accurately says the measurements enter Brown et al.’s approximate-2-M_sun regime, while Demorest does not exceed strict 2.00 by center and Fonseca clears strict 2 at 68.3% but not at 95.4%. It correctly does not adjudicate Brown et al.’s “serious doubt or simply falsify” disjunction.
