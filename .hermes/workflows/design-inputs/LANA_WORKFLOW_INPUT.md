## 3. Where v1 breaks in practice: the pipeline is a line, and real lanes are not

**Named failure mode: mid-pipeline contract death, no lawful exit.** Shape-1 is the receipt:
the object-level track *died at v1's stage 3* — the eligibility census came back 12/12
census-only, and under the contract's own Class-X discipline the lawful output shrank to an
honest null while 12 direction-confrontable predictions sat unconfronted. v1 has no arrow for
this. A lane in that position has exactly three options: silently re-read the frozen contract
(goalpost-moving — the thing the whole receipts culture exists to prevent), die and deliver
silence, or do what we actually did: a **pre-result amendment ruling**, with the pre-result
property itself receipted (census sha'd before ruling, ruling sha'd before any fetch), gated
by the amended clause's own author (M-8: Kun wrote F2-d, so Kun reviews its amendment).
That happened twice in one week (Shape-2 Ruling 1, Shape-1 T2b). Twice in a week is not an
edge case; it is a stage. v1 without it will produce either quiet contract erosion or dead
lanes, and both will be discovered late.

Second break, same family: **stage 0 can come back empty after the contract froze.** T1b
returned an instrument-verified zero — VizieR simply does not hold the flagship z>8 UVLF
deposits (Harikane+2023 and Donnan+2023 absent entirely). The fix was a ruled source-scope
change (§1 of the frozen plan), not a re-survey. Freeze-then-discover ordering guarantees
this recurs.

Third break: **the budget arithmetic is wrong, and the design should say so rather than be
violated in week one.** "Three Kimi calls per paper ≈ $4.30" — the Shape-1 lane alone has
~20 `KUN_*` review artifacts; the spin-parity lane ran 8 Miru gates (contract review, reclear,
final, script review, script verify, three amendments) *before any science*; the MZR census
lane ran 4+. Real cadence is 8–15 metered calls per paper — $12–20, so the balance funds
roughly 5–6 papers, not 18. I am not arguing for fewer gates; I'm arguing v1's escalation
clause ("allowed but must be named") mislabels the norm as the exception. Amendments,
reclears, and redaction confirmations ARE the workflow. Budget them as named classes with
expected counts, or the first real paper breaks the plan and every subsequent overrun gets
hidden to protect the number.

## 4. The missing stage — Hwao's candidate, judged: he is right, and it's two half-stages

Hwao's concern as relayed: v1 never stress-tests the paper's claim against the literature it
contradicts; the anchor-block idea lives only inside one lane's contract. **Confirmed — and
it is the exact defect Duho named when he rejected all nine autopilot papers** (no
lit-grounded motivation, no defensible conclusion). But bolted on as a single late stage it
fails; it must be split:

- **Stage 1b — prediction freeze (pre-contract, pre-data).** What does the literature
  predict for this question, frozen as receipted entries BEFORE any data exists — the A1
  pattern: verbatim spans, sha-pinned, honest scarcity recorded (0/15 numeric-in-frame was
  recorded as a fact about the literature, not padded away). This is what makes the eventual
  confrontation non-circular: the predictions demonstrably predate the measurement.
  Seats: Lana authors entries from spans; Goru bulk-sweeps candidate papers (his 0.9% is the
  cheapest capacity we have); no Kimi.
- **Stage 7b — contradiction confrontation (post-draft, pre-referee).** Enumerate the
  published results the draft's claim contradicts; the draft must cite and answer each or
  weaken the claim. The anchor-block, generalized out of the one contract into the pipeline.
  Seats: Goru enumerates (mechanically quote-verified, the `verify_quotes.py` pattern), Lana
  answers-or-weakens, and **Kimi stance-verifies only the in-tension pairs** — the one place
  in this stage where being wrong is expensive, because a misread stance either kills a good
  claim or publishes a refuted one.

One half without the other fails predictably: post-draft only = post-hoc rationalization
(the claim was shaped before anyone looked at what it must survive); pre-contract only =
claim drift (the draft's final claim confronts nothing because it grew past the entries).

And from §3, the second missing stage: **2b — amendment ruling**, first-class, with the
pre-result declaration mandatory and a Kimi gate *only when the amended clause was itself
Kimi-reviewed* (the boundary's-author rule from M-8).

## 5. Cut one Kimi gate: cut Gate 2 (pre-execution script review) — demote it, don't delete it

Gate 1 protects everything downstream of it (a poisoned contract poisons every artifact;
stellar iron in `phys.abund` would have swamped a ~10⁸-row aggregate — nothing downstream
re-checks the frame). Gate 3 is the last line before the only irreversible act we have,
publication. **Gate 2 is the only gate with a downstream safety net already in the pipeline:**
stage 6, Tori's receipts, where totals are re-added from raw artifacts. The tie-drop class of
defect — 29,053 objects silently discarded — is exactly what funnel conservation catches:
require as a stage-5/6 invariant that every input row reaches exactly one terminal state and
the terminal states re-add to the input count, from raw artifacts, by a different seat than
the script's author. A silent drop cannot survive that arithmetic.

Cover the rest from flat-rate seats:
1. **Adversarial script review by a non-author subscription seat** (Goru, or the unassigned
   Codex seat — it's idle at 14% weekly) under a fixed refutation checklist distilled from
   Kun's actual catches: fail-open status paths, silent drops/ties, unpinned references,
   default-clean states, unit/scale coercion, off-contract cuts. Reviewer ≠ author, always.
2. **Mechanical lint before any run**: grep-class checks for bare `except:`/pass,
   status-defaulting, count-unconserved branches — plus the φ-leak grep over logs that M-8 C1
   proved we need (a check that costs nothing and that the metered seat is currently doing
   by hand).

**What we lose, stated honestly:** Kun-class *semantic* pre-execution catches — a selection
that is biased but count-conserving passes funnel conservation and a checklist both; and a
wrong script now costs one wasted execute-and-receipt cycle before stage 6 catches it
(subscription time and wall-clock, not dollars — the currency we're rich in). So: demote,
don't delete. Gate 2 stays Kimi **conditionally**: scripts that touch irreversible or
rate-limited external channels (fetch quotas, publisher sites — the `t_meta_fetch.py` class),
and scripts implementing a clause that carries Kun patch history. Everything else runs the
flat-rate review. That converts Gate 2 from ~1 call per paper to ~1 call per 3–4 papers and
funds the extra calls that §3 shows the amendment/reclear reality actually costs.
